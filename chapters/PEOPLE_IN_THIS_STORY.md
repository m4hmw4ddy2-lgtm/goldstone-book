PEOPLE IN THIS STORY

THE FAMILIES

Elizabeth Gouldstone, née Stock (born 1855): wife of William; mother of the five murdered children; later mother of Albert.
William Gouldstone (born 1856): whitesmith at the Falkirk Ironworks, Upper Thames Street; Elizabeth's husband.
Charles (born 1880), Herbert (born 1881) and Frederick William (born 1882), and twin sons (born 1883): William and Elizabeth's children.
Jemima Stock, formerly Ansell, née Suckling (baptised 1811): Elizabeth's mother; widowed once, and mother of eight Ansell children before Elizabeth.
Thomas Stock (baptised 1802): Elizabeth's father; an agricultural labourer at Wethersfield.
Thomas Gouldstone (baptised 1821): William's father; blacksmith, and later publican, at Great Sampford.
Emily Gouldstone, née Willett (baptised 1830): William's mother.

THE HOUSE, THE WORKS AND THE WITNESSES

Ada Hamilton: monthly nurse and lodger at 8 Courtenay Place; principal witness to the events of 8 August 1883.
Charles and Emma Clarke: the carpenter who held the tenancy of 8 Courtenay Place, and his wife.
William Graves (born 1829): William's foreman at the Falkirk Ironworks and a close family friend, who had known him since birth.
Martha Graves: wife of William Graves, in whose household Elizabeth and William lived for the first years of their marriage.
William Cheeseman: the police constable who arrested William.
George Folkard: police inspector, N Division, who charged him.
Henry Gould: divisional surgeon of police at Leyton.
Dr Lyle: Elizabeth's doctor after the murders.
Joseph Blythe: a member of the coroner's jury.
The Rev. Thomas Harrison Grantham Robinson (born 1837): vicar of St Saviour's, Walthamstow.

THE COURTS

Mr C. C. Lewis: coroner for the southern division of Essex.
Mr Justice Day: the judge at William's trial.
Mr Poland: counsel for the Crown.
Mr Grain: counsel for the defence.
Dr George Henry Savage (born 1842): physician at Bethlem Hospital; witness for the defence.

THE LATER FAMILY

Albert Goldstone, also known as Albert Madams (born 1887): Elizabeth's later son; the author's great-grandfather.
William Madams (born 1860): Elizabeth's later partner.
