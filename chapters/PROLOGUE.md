# Prologue

Albert Jennet Goldstone was born on the nineteenth of June 1887 in the Workhouse Infirmary in Brook Street, Lambeth.

His birth was registered the following day. His mother was recorded as Elizabeth Goldstone, of 2 John Street, Hercules Buildings. She had been born Elizabeth Stock. The space where a father's name should be was left blank.

Fourteen years later Albert joined the Royal Navy. The boy who presented himself for service in November 1901 gave the correct day and month of his birth, but not the year, and not the place. He made himself a year older and said that he had been born in a place called Heckenning Houzellon, in Germany.

There is no such place.

It was not a mistake that disappeared with the next form. Twenty years later, married and living in Chatham with a family of his own, Albert completed the 1921 census and again gave Germany as his birthplace. The spelling changed. The country did not. The false birthplace remained with him for the rest of his life.

He had supplied himself with a father as well. When Albert married in 1910, the marriage register named his father as William Goldstone, deceased, a commercial traveller.

There really was a William. His surname had usually been written Gouldstone, with a u, and he had married Albert's mother in 1879. He was not a commercial traveller. He was a whitesmith, a man who worked metal for a living. And in 1910 he was not dead.

He was in Broadmoor.

William Gouldstone had been there since the fifteenth of October 1883. He would remain there until his death, more than fifty-one years later. The reason lay in what had happened at 8 Courtenay Place in Walthamstow on the evening of Wednesday the eighth of August 1883.

I had begun with Albert Goldstone, my great-grandfather. I followed his mother backwards, and found William. Without whom I would not exist.
