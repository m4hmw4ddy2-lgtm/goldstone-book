## How to use this briefing

The service sheet provides a firm chronology. State its postings, ratings, qualifications and discharge directly. It is also reasonable to write that Albert was serving in a ship when a documented event occurred within his posting: **he was serving in Prince George during the October 1903 collision, in Powerful during her 1911 New Zealand visit, and in Courageous during the Second Battle of Heligoland Bight**. These statements do not need repeated qualifications about possible temporary absence.

The boundary is between that documented service and an invented personal scene. A ship's history does not tell us Albert's station, actions, words or feelings. Exact joining ports, medical causation and other specific missing facts are identified once in the research questions below. The transcription records difficult words locally in square brackets instead of making the whole career sound uncertain.

The primary sources are **TNA ADM 188/383, Albert Goldstone, official number 218159, image reference 122**, **ADM 171/103, page 131**, his campaign medal roll, and the **Applications for War Badges** register supplied by Rik. The latter spells his name “Gladstone”, but matches his official number **218159** and records the badge issue. [S1, S2, S29]

## 1. Identity and entry into the Navy

Albert Jennet was born on **19 June 1887** at the Workhouse Infirmary, Brook Street, Lambeth. His birth certificate names his mother as Elizabeth Goldston and leaves the father's columns blank. It was registered the following day. [S3]

His naval record gives **Albert Goldstone**, born **19 June 1886**, in a purported German birthplace. Thus he was actually **14** when his first recorded posting began on **11 November 1901**, while his declared birth date made him 15. His official number was **218159**. [S1]

The adult continuous-service engagement was for **12 years from 19 June 1904**. This is distinct from his boy service, which began in 1901. He became Ordinary Seaman on his actual seventeenth birthday, which the Navy understood to be his eighteenth.

The physical description records brown hair, hazel eyes and a fresh complexion. A later height entry is **5 feet 5 inches**. The distinguishing marks include a declaration of love tattooed on the right forearm, an anchor on the left forearm, and five dots and a ring on the left hand. The initials in the declaration remain a handwriting question; no significance should be assigned to them or the five dots without evidence. The original wording and earlier measurement appear in the transcription.

## 2. Career account

### Boy training, 1901-1903

Albert entered **Impregnable on 11 November 1901 as Boy 2nd Class**, moving to **Lion on 26 November**. Both belong to the Devonport boy-training context. He became **Boy 1st Class on 28 August 1902** and remained in Lion until **23 June 1903**. He then served in **Agincourt from 24 June to 8 September 1903**, in the Portland training setting. Devonport is in Devon; Portland is in Dorset. [S1, S4-S7]

### Prince George and Majestic, 1903-1905

He joined **Prince George on 9 September 1903**. On **17 October 1903**, according to the established ship-history chronology, Hannibal collided with Prince George off Spain. Contemporary reporting confirms the collision, serious damage and the ship's arrival at Ferrol. Albert was serving in Prince George at the time. The exact speed, damage dimensions and subsequent sailing date belong to the ship-history sources, rather than his service sheet. [S1, S8]

He became **Ordinary Seaman on 19 June 1904**, and moved to **Majestic on 15 July**. The Majestic service is divided into entries ending **28 December 1904** and restarting **4 January 1905**, with the latter ending **28 February**. A **14-day cells punishment** is recorded beside this part of his service. The sheet records the punishment but not the offence.

### Firequeen I, Victory and Jupiter, 1905-1908

His next entries are **Firequeen I, 1-31 March 1905**, and **Victory, 1 April-14 August 1905**. The Victory date is **1 April**, not 15 April. He was subsequently in **Jupiter from 15 August 1905 to 3 February 1908**, progressing from Ordinary Seaman to **Able Seaman on 1 January 1908**. The original Jupiter entries, including the short break in August 1907, are preserved in the transcription. **Ten-day and three-day cells punishments** are recorded in this part of the sheet. [S1]

Victory's Trafalgar centenary illumination is a separate October 1905 event. Albert had left the Victory posting in August; do not place him aboard her for those celebrations. [S9]

### Shannon, marriage and Dominion, 1908-1911

After **Pembroke, 4 February-9 March 1908**, Albert served in **Shannon from 10 March 1908 to 28 February 1910**. Shannon was part of the Home Fleet's Fifth Cruiser Squadron and then the Second Cruiser Squadron after the 1909 reorganisation. [S1, S10]

He returned to **Pembroke I on 1 March 1910**. Four days later, on **5 March**, he married at **St Mark's Church, New Brompton**. His wife is known in later documents as **Clarie Irene Setterington**; the marriage certificate's source-specific name is read in the project as “Rennie” or similar. The quarter of registration is January-March 1910, not evidence of a January wedding. His occupation at marriage is not another naval posting. [S20, S21]

He served in **Dominion from 7 June 1910 to 23 May 1911**. The family-tree timeline records him in the census aboard Dominion on **2 April 1911**, and the source image is in the repository. [S11]

### Australia Station, 1911-1913

The next sequence is **Pembroke I, 24 May-7 August 1911; Crescent, 8 August-15 September; Powerful, 16 September-16 December; Pioneer, 17 December 1911-23 May 1912; and Drake, 24 May 1912-25 February 1913**. [S1]

Albert was serving in **Powerful during her November-December 1911 New Zealand visit**. Her itinerary included arrival at Auckland on **20 November**, followed by Wellington and Lyttelton. She sailed from Lyttelton for Sydney on **6 December**. The visit included inspection of naval facilities; a naval stores organisation was recommended. [S12, S13]

During Albert's Pioneer posting she was **HMS Pioneer**, a Royal Navy ship on the Australian station. The official Australian history records her earlier recommissioning as a drill ship on **5 September 1905**, paying off at Sydney on **29 November 1912**, and subsequent commissioning as **HMAS Pioneer on 1 March 1913**. Albert left her in May 1912. [S14]

**Drake begins on 24 May 1912**, not March. Pioneer's preceding entry ends on **23 May**. Drake had replaced Powerful as Australia Station flagship in late 1911 and returned to Britain in early 1913. [S15]

### Tyne, qualifications and Leading Seaman, 1913-1916

Albert returned to **Pembroke I from 26 February to 26 October 1913**, then entered **Tyne on 27 October 1913**. The ship entry includes **“(P.B.31)”**. His service continued through the opening years of the First World War, and he became **Leading Seaman on 1 July 1916**. He moved to **Pembroke I on 28 July**, remaining until **3 November 1916**. That Pembroke posting was missing from the supplied timeline. [S1]

The sheet records his **Petty Officer educational examination on 3 December 1912** and a **professional PO qualification dated 20 October 1916**. These qualifications preceded his eventual acting promotion. The sub-ratings also record **S.G.** and later **Acting G.L. II**, indicating gunnery training. These are specialist qualifications, distinct from his substantive rating. [S1, S27, S28]

His daughter **Lily Alma Rose Goldstone** was born on **14 December 1913**, according to the family documents. “Lilian Alma Irene” in the earlier timeline is an incorrect name. [S21-S23]

### Courageous and Heligoland Bight, 1916-1917

Albert served in **Courageous from 4 November 1916 to 31 December 1917**, as Leading Seaman. He was therefore serving in her during the **Second Battle of Heligoland Bight on 17 November 1917**. The official naval history records her opening fire with her fifteen-inch guns during the action. [S1, S17]

His sub-rating entry records **Acting G.L. II from 1 November 1917**, shortly before the battle. This supports discussion of his gunnery qualification at the time, but does not identify a particular gun or battle station.

**He had left Courageous before the German High Seas Fleet's surrender in November 1918.** The ship's participation in that later event is not part of his documented Courageous service.

### Alexander, North Russia and acting promotion, 1918-1919

After **Pembroke I, 1 January-26 February 1918**, Albert entered **Alexander on 27 February**. The icebreaker operated in North Russia, between Murmansk and Archangel. Official despatches describe her arrival at Murmansk on **1 April 1918**, sailing with food ships on **17 April**, and presence at Archangel in July, when her captain averted a hostile confrontation. Albert was serving in Alexander during these operations. [S1, S18, S19]

He became **Acting Petty Officer on 1 September 1918**. The sheet's wording is **“P.O. (act)”**. His daughter **Clarie Irene Goldstone** was born on **21 October 1918**, according to the 1939 Register, during his Alexander posting. [S1, S23]

He returned to **Pembroke I on 17 January 1919**, and was **invalided on 26 March 1919**, aged 31. The record gives **“Org Heart Disease”**, organic heart disease. It records the condition, not its origin. [S1]

## 3. Character, ability, discipline and medals

The sheet records an improving pattern of character assessments, from **Good** in the middle years to predominantly **Very Good** later. It also records ability assessments such as **Superior** and **Satisfactory**. These are different assessments: “Superior” must not be presented as a conduct grade. Three recorded cells punishments and movements between conduct classes form part of the career; the offences are not stated. The complete visible sequence is transcribed below. [S1]

The medal roll identifies **GOLDSTONE, Albert, P.O., 218159** and records **St, V, B**, issued **S**. The National Archives' key identifies these as the **1914-15 Star, Victory Medal and British War Medal**, issued to **Self**, Albert himself. His documented campaign group is therefore **the 1914-15 Star, British War Medal and Victory Medal**. The column order in the roll is not a wearing-order instruction, and the page gives no issue date. [S2, S24, S25]

The medal roll abbreviates his rating as **P.O.**; the service sheet specifies **Acting Petty Officer**. Use the full service-sheet description for his September 1918 advancement.

**His Silver War Badge is now confirmed by the Applications for War Badges register:** “Gladstone A.”, official number **218159**, rating **P.O.**, badge number **36842**, issued **28 February 1919**. The matching official number establishes his identity despite the misspelling. The date is in the “Badge Issued” column, not merely an application date. [S29]

The service sheet contains a **K.C.** stamp with **“ISSUED 17/4/19”**, consistent with a King's Certificate, and **“PAID WAR GRATUITY”** stamps. His three campaign medals are independently proved by the campaign medal roll. [S1, S2]

### The P.C. annotation: recorded fact and possible meanings

The annotation reads **“P.C. 384833 / 28/2/19”**. It is handwritten directly beneath Albert's name in the top identification section, above the horizontal line separating that section from the engagement and physical-description fields. It is **outside the labelled service, badge and remarks columns**. [S1]

**Its expansion is unconfirmed. Protection Certificate and Pension Certificate are both plausible; the available evidence does not reliably favour either.**

| Interpretation | Why it remains plausible | What is still missing |
|---|---|---|
| Protection Certificate | Royal Navy men received protection and identity certificates after the Armistice. A surviving naval certificate and an Admiralty parliamentary answer establish this practice. | Evidence that P.C. on this particular service sheet denotes such a certificate, and that 384833 is its serial number. |
| Pension Certificate | A pension-related document is plausible in the context of Albert's medical invaliding. | A period-specific naval abbreviation key, the certificate, or a matching pension record establishing that expansion and number. |

The existence of naval protection certificates is documented; its application to Albert's annotation is an inference. The pension-certificate interpretation is also an inference, not a verified definition. [S30, S31]

Neither the position under his name nor the February 1919 date establishes the type of document. Do not invent an explanation for the clerk's choice of position. The annotation does not establish that **28 February was the day Albert physically left Pembroke or returned to civilian life**. His recorded final posting ran from **17 January to 26 March 1919** and ended with **“Invalided - Org Heart Disease”**, rather than an entry labelled demobilisation. Medical invaliding does not by itself rule out a protection certificate. [S1]

**Keep the two numbers separate:** P.C. **384833**, meaning unconfirmed; Silver War Badge **36842**, issued **28 February 1919**, independently confirmed by its register. The shared date does not identify the P.C. document or prove the two administrative processes were the same. [S1, S29]

Preferred research-note wording: **“P.C. 384833, dated 28 February 1919; meaning unconfirmed. Protection Certificate and Pension Certificate remain possible interpretations.”** Omit the unexplained abbreviation from narrative prose unless the uncertainty itself matters to the story.

## 4. Specific outstanding research

These are discrete questions, not doubts about the career as a whole.

1. **Exact joining locations:** establish the ships' positions on the joining dates before specifying Ferrol for Prince George, Gibraltar for Majestic, Portland for Shannon or Dominion, Sydney for Pioneer or Drake, or Archangel for Alexander. Powerful's posting date is not itself an arrival date in New Zealand.
2. **Tyne (P.B.31):** identify the attachment and when it applied. The annotation is legible; the date of that attachment is not separately supplied.
3. **P.C. annotation:** identify the separate “P.C. 384833” reference through a contemporary naval key or matching document. **Protection Certificate and Pension Certificate remain plausible, with neither established nor preferred.** The Silver War Badge itself is settled: number **36842**, issued **28 February 1919**. Obtain the full archive reference for the supplied War Badges register image for the final manuscript citation.
4. **A few handwritten details:** obtain a cleaner scan or check the source for the initials tattoo, altered ages and initial height, parts of the sub-rating dates, one examination day and administrative numbers. These are flagged at the relevant cells below.
5. **Original family entries:** recheck the 1911 census and the marriage/1921 census images when preparing final source notes; the present career work relies on the project's prior readings for those entries. The 1939 Register was inspected directly.

Ship logs in **ADM 53** are the appropriate route for exact movements and fuller accounts of the collision, overseas visits and North Russian operations. [S26]
