TRANSCRIPTION

REPRIEVE OF THE WALTHAMSTOW MURDERER.—The following letter has been received from the Home Office by the solicitor
of the convict Gouldstone's employers:—"Whitehall, Oct. 3, 1883.—Sir,—With reference to the applications forwarded by
you in behalf of William Gouldstone, I am directed by the Secretary of State to acquaint you that, having caused
special medical inquiry to be made as to the state of this man's mind, he has felt justified in advising her Majesty to
respite the capital sentence.—I am, Sir, your obedient servant, GODFREY LUSHINGTON."

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. THE HOME OFFICE DECISION IS DATED WEDNESDAY 3 OCTOBER 1883. A Home Office letter, quoted in full, signed by Godfrey
   Lushington (at the Home Office; Assistant Under-Secretary in 1883, Permanent Under-Secretary from 1885 -- TO VERIFY). This is a press printing of a primary document, not a
   reporter's summary. See the timeline file, Contradiction 80: the reprieve date.
2. "respite the capital sentence": the formal language. The respite of 26 September had put the execution back a week
   (1 to 8 October) pending the inquiry; this letter, after the inquiry, advises a respite of the sentence itself, i.e.
   no execution. Removal to Broadmoor followed on 15 October (Daily News, 16 October).
3. "the solicitor of the convict Gouldstone's employers": the first sign that the firm (Kennard's, 67 Upper Thames
   Street) had a solicitor acting, and that "the applications" (plural) went through him. Compare Graves's letters of
   5 September and 11 October: the workmen raised money for the defence. The solicitor is not named.
4. "special medical inquiry": Orange and a second physician (Sunday Dispatch, 30 September: Clarke; Contradiction 20).
