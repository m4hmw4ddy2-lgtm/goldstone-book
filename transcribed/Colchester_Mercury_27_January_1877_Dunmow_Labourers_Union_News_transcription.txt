LABOURERS' UNION NEWS.—Many of the branches of the National Agricultural Labourers' Union have established a franchise
fund, so that the promoters are endeavouring to form a political power. Union meetings have been held this week and
addressed by Mr. S. Bailey, at Takeley, on Tuesday evening; High Roothing on Wednesday; and Pleshey on Thursday
evening. Meetings were also held recently at High Easter, Lindsell, and Old Sampford. Between 50 and 60 labourers are
at present on strike at Old Sampford. The Broxted lock-out still continues. The men are very quiet. The District
Secretary paid them their lock-out pay on Friday as usual, and the men are all in good spirits. [In reference to our
correspondent's statements of last week, we have received a letter from Mr. Moxon, the District Secretary, in which he
states that the men on strike in North Essex are less than 50, and are members of the Old Sampford branch—not of the
Broxted, which does not belong to the North Essex District. Lastly, as to the contributions, he adds:—"The returns for
December, instead of being £19 17s. 10d., as stated by the writer of the paragraph, are actually £81 18s. 10d."]

NOTES: Two counts of the Sampford strikers in one paragraph: "between 50 and 60" (the correspondent) and "less than 50"
(Moxon, the union's District Secretary). Old Sampford had its own union branch.
