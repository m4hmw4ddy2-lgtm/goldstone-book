LUTON LADIES DINE.—Luton Working Men's Club are to have their own premises within the next two years. This was
revealed by the club's secretary, Mr. A. Goldstone, at the ladies' dinner at Luton Parochial Hall on Thursday. Mr.
Goldstone outlined the growth of the club from its opening in 1931. He spoke of the comparatively small membership
—there were now 80 members—and appealed for more. "In view of the unique position of the club we believe that
membership should be far greater than it actually is," he said. Mr. G. A. Field (chairman) welcomed the ladies, who,
he said, were on their own for the first time since the club was formed, and expressed the hope that the ladies'
dinner would be made an annual function. After dinner the men joined their ladies in a social and dance. Mrs. R. E.
Blatchford presided at the dinner.
