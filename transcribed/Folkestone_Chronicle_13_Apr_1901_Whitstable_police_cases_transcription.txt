Text, verbatim (the source's punctuation; "ult." = March 1901, "inst." = April 1901):
"POLICE CASES. — At St. Augustine's Petty Sessions on Saturday, Albert Goldstone, aged 14, was charged on remand with
stealing a pair of sea boots, value 10s., the property of Alfred Baker on the 28th ult. Prosecutor, an able seaman on
board the Christabel, deposed that the vessel was laden with coal, and prisoner was employed as basket boy in the work
of unloading. Witness last saw the boots on the 23rd ult. in the forecastle and missed them on the 29th. John James
Pout, boot-maker, of 46, Harbour-street, Whitstable, stated that on the 28th ult. prisoner brought the boots to his
shop and asked him to put pieces on the bottom. He said they were his own boots, they having been given to him, but
witness told him they were more suitable for his father than for him. On the 30th ult. prisoner came back and said
his father wanted 3s. for them and witness purchased them for that amount. He admitted they were worth more than
that. P.C. Lampkin deposed to arresting prisoner on the 1st inst. when he said the boots were at Mr. Pout's. On going
there Mrs. Pout handed him the boots. Prisoner pleaded guilty. Mr. Bray[;] the police court missionary, offered to
get the boy on a training ship, and the Bench adjourned the case for a week to see what he could do in the matter.
Supt. Jacobs asked the Bench to disallow Pout his expenses as he did not go the police about the boots in the first
instance. There had lately been many small thefts in Whitstable and the police had been unable to trace the articles
stolen. The Bench disallowed Pout's expenses and expressed the opinion that he was very fortunate to get off so
lightly."
(The last "he" is Pout: the sentence is about his expenses.)

WHAT IT GIVES: Albert, under the name Goldstone (not Madams), aged 14 by the paper (13 in fact), working as a basket
boy unloading a coal vessel; the first words in the record attributed to him, at second hand through Pout ("they
were his own boots ... given to him"; "his father wanted 3s."); a police court missionary's offer to get him on a
training ship.
WHAT IT DOES NOT GIVE: the outcome. Nothing here says the Bench sent him to a training ship. HMS Impregnable, where he
entered on 11 November 1901, was a boys' training ship; the connection is INFERENCE until the adjourned hearing of
13 April is found. "His father" is the boy's word as Pout reported it: who was meant is not said (Madams is the
obvious reading; not for prose as fact).
