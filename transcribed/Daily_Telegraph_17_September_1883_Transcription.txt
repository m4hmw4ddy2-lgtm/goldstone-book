TRANSCRIPTION

THE CONVICT GOULDSTONE.

TO THE EDITOR OF "THE DAILY TELEGRAPH."

SIR—We, being all the men who work in the smiths' shop in which William Gouldstone was employed, unhesitatingly
say that he was of unsound mind when he committed the murder. We base this opinion upon the intimate daily
intercourse we have had with him during the last four years and a half, and we consider it a great hardship that
we were not allowed to put this evidence before the jury. Had we had any doubt on the subject we should not have
taken up his case as we have done. We should be grateful if you would make this, our opinion, known to the
public.—We are, Sir, your humble servants, for self and fellow-workmen,
WILLIAM GRAVES, Foreman.
67, Upper Thames-street, Sept. 15.

TO THE EDITOR OF "THE DAILY TELEGRAPH."

SIR—As it was my painful duty to be on the case of William Gouldstone, I feel it only right to inform you that
the prisoner had been examined on the Thursday by the head physician from the Bethlehem Hospital Lunatic Asylum,
who had been conversing with him for, say, from twenty minutes to half an hour. When asked by Mr. Poland if he
would certify that the prisoner was insane, he distinctly stated "No," also declaring he believed the prisoner
knew at the time that he was committing the crime of murder. Trusting you will kindly insert this in your
valuable paper, in regard of your leading article of to-day, I am, yours, &c.,
Tottenham, Sept. 15. ONE OF THE JURY.
