## ATTESTATION, Army Form B. 111, Short Service ("Three years with the Colours or Termination of War")
No. "228" ("G.S." "E.K."); "William John Rowden"; Corps "The Buffs". Born in or near Whitstable, Kent; British subject
"Yes"; age "26 years [11?] months"; trade "Hair Dresser"; apprentice "No"; married "Yes"; previously rejected as unfit:
"Yes. Defective teeth". Attested at Whitstable, 24 August 1914.

## DESCRIPTION ON ENLISTMENT
Apparent age 26; height 5 ft 9 in; weight 134 lbs; chest 34 in, expansion 3 in; complexion fair [?]; eyes blue [?]; hair fair
[?]; Church of England. Marks: "Slight varicose vein [?] legs". Medical examination Whitstable, 24 August 1914: fit. Approved
Canterbury, 25 August 1914, for General Service.

## STATEMENT OF SERVICES
Attested Pte, The Buffs, 24.8.14; joined at Canterbury 25.8.14; posted 1-9-14 [battalion not certain]; discharged 16.4.15,
under K.R. para. 392 (xvi), "No longer physically fit for War Service". Home service, 24.8.14 to 16.4.15, 236 days.

## PENSION RECORD (Chelsea No. 26155/D)
Admitted to pension 13.5.15; Private, East Kent, "William John Rowden"; discharged 16.4.15, age 27; address "101 High St,
Whitstable, Kent"; children "Married / Nil"; character "Good"; grounds "No longer physically fit for War Service".
Disability "Tubercle of Lung": "Originated in Oct. 1914 at Purfleet. States that he caught cold, when out in the rain at
Purfleet. Cough ever since. Haemorrhage last Nov."; the medical board found it not the result of service but aggravated by
it; "Permanent"; with a note "No syph." Trade at the foot: "Hair Dresser". Reverse: reviews 1915-1917; by March 1917 "No
incapacity", "Earnings 14/- to 15/- per week (works for himself)".

## FINDINGS (notes, not prose)
1. **THE WEDDING: 13 August 1914, at SEASALTER, Kent** (the Ancestry tree's "Seaonlter" is this). The GRO index (Q3 1914,
   Blean) agrees. Settles the day.
2. **ELIZA MADAMS WAS A WITNESS.** Lilian's sister Eliza Jane (born 1891; later Coombs; to New Zealand 1927; Bronwyn
   Joule's ancestor) signed as witness to her marriage. Elizabeth's two Madams daughters together, eight years after her
   death: the only document so far that puts them side by side as adults.
3. **20 MIDDLE WALL, 1914.** Lilian's address as a new wife: Middle Wall was the Madams family's street in 1901 (Albert
   there, census). The household that Albert left in 1901 may have held together into 1914 (the house number in 1901 is
   uncertain: dramatis, Madams). For the notes; not shown that Albert had any contact.
4. By 1915 the Rowdens were at 101 High Street; by 1939, 97 High Street.
5. "No syph." is the medical board's routine entry on HIM; NOTHING to be read from it about the Madams household, and
   nothing in any voice.
6. For the book: Lilian's life in documents (Part Six, the "two Lils"; possibly the Coda's Elizabeth thread: a daughter
   married with her sister beside her). Rowden himself does not enter prose.
