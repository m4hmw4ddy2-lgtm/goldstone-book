TRANSCRIPTION

THE CONVICT GOULDSTONE.

The Home Secretary has addressed a letter to the Sheriff of Essex, Mr. J. Oxley Parker, signifying the Queen's
command that the sentence of death passed upon William Gouldstone for the murder of his five children at
Walthamstow be respited until Monday, the 8th day of October, in order that inquiry may be made as to the
prisoner's sanity. The execution was fixed for Monday next.

THE CASE OF WILLIAM GOULDSTONE.

TO THE EDITOR OF THE TIMES.

Sir,—The terrible case of the convict for the murder of his five children makes me tremble lest rough justice
should raise the cry of blood for blood. I beg to be heard on the side of mercy. I do not intend so much to argue
the case as simply to relate and remark by contrast on a case which I myself when a young man at the Bar was
engaged in, as sole counsel for the prosecution, and which I always thought rightly decided. A young gentleman of
Irish birth and residence, a stranger in Liverpool, arrived there by a steamboat from Dublin; he was armed with a
loaded pistol which he carried about his person out of sight. On the night of his arrival in Liverpool he went out
so armed into the street, and met at the corner of a street some men, to whom he addressed some words which
produced some corresponding words from them, on which he, instantly taking out his weapon, fired at and shot one
of the men dead. He was immediately apprehended by the police. His conduct was calm, and evinced no sign of
insanity. On board the vessel he had evinced none, but he had left his home suddenly without notice, and had made
no provision of clothes for a journey. He was tried for murder at the ensuing Lancaster Assizes before Mr. Baron
Parke. It was before M'Naghten's case. On the facts simply as above stated I should have regarded it and opened it
as a case of murder. Knowing, however, as I did from my instructions that this prima facie murderer in the eye of
the law was a quiet, peaceable, inoffensive, good liver at all times except when suffering from illnesses, which
occasionally visited him, and that the skilled witnesses to be called for him attributed his exceptional state
then of excitability and violence to mental disease, I opened the case as one raising only the question whether
the deed was to be classed with calamity or crime. My solicitor, an able and experienced man, approved of my
opening, and thought, as I did, the case one of insanity. The Judge so thought and so directed. The prisoner was
acquitted on the ground of insanity. That state of mind the skilled witnesses, the medical men who had attended
this young man in his former illnesses, attributed to mind disease rendering him when under its influence
incapable of self-control. It displayed itself actively by acts of violence. The mind disease which produces
morbid melancholy, described as "melancholia," is also as capable of producing an incontrollable action or set of
actions as the other, and the skilled evidence in this unhappy man's case has treated this appalling deed as the
act of a madman, and a letter in The Times from another medical man skilled in such inquiries confirms it. I find
no scientific evidence opposed to this evidence of the state of mind, and I find a jump from virtue at one leap to
unparalleled wickedness utterly inconsistent with all, and with old proverbial experience, expressed in the form,
"Nemo repente fuit turpissimus."

I have the honour to be, Sir,
Your obedient servant,
LAURENCE PEEL.
