"A YOUTHFUL OFFENDER. — At the St. Augustine's Petty Sessions last Saturday, the boy, Albert Goldstone, who the
previous week was brought up on the charge of stealing a pair of sea boots, was bound over to come up for judgment
when called upon, Mr. J.Bray,the police-court missionary,having got the boy on the training-ship Warspite."

DATE: "last Saturday" before Saturday 20 April 1901 = Saturday 13 April 1901, the adjourned hearing (Folkestone
Chronicle, 13 April 1901: adjourned from Saturday 6 April "for a week").

WHAT IT GIVES: the outcome. He was bound over to come up for judgment when called upon (no sentence passed), and the
missionary, J. Bray, had got him on the training ship Warspite.
WHAT IT DOES NOT GIVE: when he went aboard; what the Warspite was or where it lay (general knowledge, to be sourced
before prose); any link between the Warspite and HMS Impregnable, where he entered the Navy on 11 November 1901
(to be sourced: the Warspite's own records). Elizabeth is not mentioned. The boy is "Albert Goldstone".
