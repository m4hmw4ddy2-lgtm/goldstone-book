## Notes for the chapter (Claude, 27 October 2026; not part of the transcription)

1. **Royal Assent dates, from the Acts themselves:** Trial of Lunatics Act "[25th August 1883.]"; Criminal Lunatics
   Act "[14th August 1884.]". Both confirmed; the research note's "14 August" for 1884 stands.
2. **The special verdict's wording (1883, s. 2(1)):** "guilty of the act or omission charged against him, but was
   insane as aforesaid at the time when he did the act or made the omission". Detention "as a criminal lunatic ...
   till Her Majesty's pleasure shall be known" (s. 2(2)). Mercier's "Guilty, but insane" (1894) is shorthand.
3. **Section 2(4) of 1884 is "shall appoint", "shall forthwith examine"**: mandatory once "there is reason to
   believe such prisoner to be insane", the reason coming from two visiting-committee members' certificate "or by any
   other means". The report goes "to the Secretary of State"; nothing in it requires publication, oath or
   cross-examination: the very points Bucknill (March 1884) had demanded five months earlier.
4. **Shortt in 1922 reads one word differently.** His quotation of s. 2(4) has "inquire as to his sanity"; the Act
   reads "inquire as to his insanity". Both the held text ("Home Secretary's Statement 1922-06-13.txt", source and
   column not stated) and a second, independent printing (JMS July 1922, vol. 68, "Notes and News", pp. 313-315, doi
   10.1192/bjp.68.282.313-a, read through a summarising tool 27 October 2026) have "sanity". So the slip is in
   Shortt's statement as reported, not in our copy. Harmless unless the prose quotes his quotation; if it does, quote
   the Act instead. Hansard column still TO ADD.
5. **Section 2(1) of 1884: removal "to the asylum named in the warrant" and detention "until he ceases to be a
   criminal lunatic".** William's 1883 warrants used the older form ("until lawfully removed or discharged":
   Broadmoor_warrants_reception_and_removal_8_Oct_1883_transcription.md). Section 4(1): the superintendent reports
   on every criminal lunatic at least yearly, and the Secretary of State shall "at least once in every three years ...
   determine whether he ought to be discharged or otherwise dealt with". So from 1884 William's case was, by statute,
   to be considered at least every three years for fifty years. Those reviews, if they survive, would be in the
   Home Office or Broadmoor files (closed patient file to 2036): a LEAD, and a fact of the law usable in the Broadmoor
   movement without inventing any review's outcome.

## TRANSCRIPTION

### Trial of Lunatics Act 1883

CHAPTER 38.

An Act to amend the Law respecting the Trial and Custody of Insane Persons charged with offences.
[25th August 1883.]

BE it enacted by the Queen's most Excellent Majesty, by and with the advice and consent of the Lords Spiritual and
Temporal, and Commons, in this present Parliament assembled, and by the authority of the same, as follows:

[p. 129]

1. This Act may be cited as the Trial of Lunatics Act, 1883. [Short title.]

2. (1.) Where in any indictment or information any act or omission is charged against any person as an offence, and
it is given in evidence on the trial of such person for that offence that he was insane, so as not to be responsible,
according to law, for his actions at the time when the act was done or omission made, then, if it appears to the jury
before whom such person is tried that he did the act or made the omission charged, but was insane as aforesaid at the
time when he did or made the same, the jury shall return a special verdict to the effect that the accused was guilty
of the act or omission charged against him, but was insane as aforesaid at the time when he did the act or made the
omission. [Special verdict where accused found guilty, but insane at date of act or omission charged, and orders
thereupon.]

(2.) Where such special verdict is found, the Court shall order the accused to be kept in custody as a criminal
lunatic, in such place and in such manner as the Court shall direct till Her Majesty's pleasure shall be known; and it
shall be lawful for Her Majesty thereupon, and from time to time, to give such order for the safe custody of the said
person during pleasure, in such place and in such manner as to Her Majesty may seem fit.

[(3.) and (4.) not transcribed: settlement and maintenance; application of existing provisions on persons acquitted on
the ground of insanity to persons subject to the special verdict.]

### Criminal Lunatics Act 1884

CHAPTER 64.

An Act to consolidate and amend the Law relating to Criminal Lunatics. [14th August 1884.]

BE it enacted ... as follows:

1. This Act may be cited as the Criminal Lunatics Act, 1884. [Short titles.] ...

2.—(1.) Where a prisoner is certified, in manner provided in this section, to be insane, a Secretary of State may if
he thinks fit, by warrant direct such prisoner to be removed to the asylum named in the warrant, and thereupon such
prisoner shall be removed to and received in such asylum, and, subject to the provisions of this Act relating to
conditional discharge and otherwise, shall be detained therein, or in any other asylum to which he may be transferred
in pursuance of this Act, as a criminal lunatic until he ceases to be a criminal lunatic. [Order for detention of
insane prisoner as criminal lunatic.]

(2.) A person shall cease to be a criminal lunatic if he is remitted to prison or absolutely discharged in manner
provided by this Act, or if any term of penal servitude or imprisonment to which he may be subject determines.

(3.) Where it appears to any two members of the visiting committee of a prison that a prisoner in such prison, not
being under sentence of death, is insane, they shall call to their assistance two legally qualified medical
practitioners, and such members and

[p. 178]

practitioners shall examine such prisoner and inquire as to his insanity, and after such examination and inquiry may
certify in writing that he is insane.

(4.) In the case of a prisoner under sentence of death, if it appears to a Secretary of State, either by means of a
certificate signed by two members of the visiting committee of the prison in which such prisoner is confined, or by
any other means, that there is reason to believe such prisoner to be insane, the Secretary of State shall appoint two
or more legally qualified medical practitioners, and the said medical practitioners shall forthwith examine such
prisoner and inquire as to his insanity, and after such examination and inquiry such practitioners shall make a report
in writing to the Secretary of State as to the sanity of the prisoner, and they, or the majority of them, may certify
in writing that he is insane.

[(5.) not transcribed: who exercises the visiting committee's powers in convict and other prisons.]

4.—(1.) The superintendent of an asylum or other place in which any criminal lunatic is detained shall make a report
to a Secretary of State at such times (not being less than once a year) and containing such particulars as the
Secretary of State may require, of the condition and circumstances of every criminal lunatic in such asylum or place;
and the Secretary of State shall, at least once in every three years during which a criminal lunatic is detained in
any asylum or other place, take into consideration the condition, history, and circumstances of such lunatic, and
determine whether he ought to be discharged or otherwise dealt with. [Periodical report of criminal lunatics.]
