The case, in summary (our words): John Reid, a coalman, of Nelson Terrace, Luton, was charged at Chatham "on Wednesday
last week" (Wednesday 28 March 1945, by calendar computation) with stealing about five hundredweight of coal from his
employer, Herbert Wm. Pilcher, coal merchant, of Beacon Hill, Chatham, and delivering it to the Working Men's Club in
the Luton Road on the afternoon of 12 February 1945. The Bench dismissed the case without calling on the defence.

The Goldstone paragraphs, verbatim:
"Albert Goldstone, secretary of the Luton Working Men's Club, told the court that no coal was ordered after February
7th. The gates of the club were kept locked and only opened for the brewer and the coalman, who could only obtain the
key from the bar.
In answer to Mr. Williams, witness said that he got coal from the cellar on February 11th. There were then from five
to six cwts. there. He went to the cellar again with P.C. Smith the next evening and saw no difference in the quantity
of coal.
Mrs. Clara Irene Goldstone said that there was no one in the club when she left at 2.30 p.m. The bar was locked, and
the key of the cellar was in the bar."

What it gives: in February-March 1945 an Albert Goldstone was secretary of the Luton Working Men's Club, Luton Road,
Chatham, and a "Mrs. Clara Irene Goldstone" was also at the club and gave evidence. Albert was 57.
Identity: "Clara Irene" is the paper's form of Clarie Irene, Albert's wife (Vera's birth certificate, 1921; 1939
Register "Clarie I."). A husband and wife of these names in Chatham makes this Albert Jennet Goldstone beyond
reasonable doubt, and it strengthens the identification in the Observer of 19 October 1934.
Press-as-press: her first name is the paper's spelling; not to be "corrected" inside a quotation.
