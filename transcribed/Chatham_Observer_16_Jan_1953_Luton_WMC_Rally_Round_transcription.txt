THE ARTICLE (whole):

"RALLY ROUND"
New Year call to
Luton W.M.C. members

A PLEA that members should rally round their club and the committee this year. was made at the annual dinner and
dance of Luton Working Men's Club, held in Luton Church Hall on Wednesday.
Councillor G. W. Rhodes, who presided, said:
"Clubs and pubs are having a sticky time these days and this is one of the bad periods.
"During the war you could sell anything and ask any price you liked, but today all that has altered.
"Things will improve in your clubs, but it is up to you members whether it is a success or a failure—if you use your
club. [this paragraph in bold type]
"I suggest you advocate that every member bring a new member into the club during this year. It can be done and will
build the club up."
Mr. Rhodes concluded by wishing the club every success and congratulated it on having such an enthusiastic committee.
Club secretary, Mr. A. Goldstone said he was speaking more to the younger men when he said:
FUTURE POSSIBILITIES
"We now have a vast amount of ground at our disposal and I see no reason why we could not build our own dance hall and
billiards hall.
"I shall not worry one iota so long as Luton Working Men's Club is going ahead."
Mr. Goldstone added: "We have got to pull together; only then will we be able to pay off the debt owing now, and then
call the club our own." [this paragraph in bold type]
Other speakers were Mr. M, R. Lourie, chairman, Mr. W. Powell, past chairman, and Mr. L. D. Hills.
Mr. F. W. May, treasurer, was toast master.
Following the speeches there was dancing to music supplied by Mr. N. Baker, who was also M.C.

NOTES:
- "Wednesday": 14 January 1953 (by calendar computation).
- The article says "Luton Church Hall", the caption "Christ Church Hall": the paper differs with itself; nothing turns on it.
- The secretary is "Mr. A. Goldstone" of the Luton Working Men's Club, the club whose secretary was "Mr. A. Goldstone"
  in January 1939 and "Albert Goldstone" in March 1945 (Observer, 28 January 1939 and 6 April 1945, held). Same club,
  same office, same initial: very probably Albert, then 65 (true age), fourteen years on from 1939. Nothing on the clip
  names him in full or gives an address.
- HIS WORDS: three sentences in direct speech, the paper's own quotation marks (two paragraphs, then "Mr. Goldstone
  added:"). With 1933 and 1939, his third recorded utterance in direct speech, and the last held: nine years before
  his death. In 1939 he had said the club would have its own premises "within the next two years" (held); in 1953 it
  has "a vast amount of ground" and "the debt owing now". Placed side by side in the notes only; no sentence of ours
  is to join them.
- "Mr. M, R. Lourie" as printed (a comma for a full stop): the punctuation of the original is "M, R." on the image.
- The photograph names no one but Rhodes. Whether Albert (or Clarie) is in it is NOT shown; any identification would be
  family recognition (Part Six, Rik's voice), not the record.
