THE UNION DELEGATES AND THE FARMERS. To the Editor.
SIR,—Will you kindly insert in your next edition the following:—At the meeting of the N.A.L.U. held at Great Sampford
on the 8th February, I find Mr. Bailey, one of the Union delegates, made two false statements, which are apt to mislead
the labourers of other parishes, and also the public. In the first instance, he says the labourers in this parish are
out on strike resisting the reduction of wages from 12s. to 11s. Now he knows this statement is false, for we are at
the present time paying day labourers 11s. and beer, or 12s. and no beer; horsekeepers and stockmen 2[s]. per week
extra; and lastly he says farmers are very anxious to know when the men who are on strike are coming back to work
again. If he believes this latter assertion I beg to state he is wrongly informed. We have plenty of men. My holding
amounts to 267 acres, and with all the boasted power of Unionism I have at the present time in my employment eighteen
men and boys.—Yours respectfully,
G. H. SPOONER.
Bye Balls F[a]rm, Great Sampford, Feb. 21st, 1877.

NOTES: "2[s]." : the character after 2 is a broken "s" (not "4"). "Bye Balls": as printed; the farm's real name not
checked (a misprint is possible); the prose need not name it. Bailey is "Mr. S. Bailey" of the 27 January report.
