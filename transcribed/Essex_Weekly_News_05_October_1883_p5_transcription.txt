(1) GOULDSTONE'S FATE DECIDED.

As we predicted last week would be the case, Wm. Gouldstone, the murderer of his five children at Walthamstow, is not
to die at the hands of the newly-appointed common hangman. The temporary respite we previously announced has been
followed by a commutation "during her Majesty's pleasure," which means that he is to be detained in a criminal lunatic
asylum for the remainder of his natural life. The reasons assigned for this step are sufficiently strong, former
evidence of the prisoner's insanity being supplemented by the opinion of the eminent medical "specialists" who came
down last week to examine the man in Springfield Gaol. The case presents some very curious features for the
consideration of our criminal lawyers. The culprit was, in a sense, aware of the enormity of the crime he was about to
commit before murdering his children; yet during his incarceration at Springfield he has generally, we believe,
manifested behaviour consistent with that of the most sensible of persons. But he was obviously at the time of his
offence the object of an insane, irresistible impulse, and committed a crime before which even a hardened ruffian
would quail. Under these circumstances there is sound logic in the argument of the Law Journal, that "It is seldom
that no inducement besides an insane impulse is discoverable in the mind of a murderer; and if murder is prompted by
absolutely none of the motives which would influence a sane person, if a man kills a human being whom he loves, merely
because voices in his diseased brain whisper to him, 'The devil wishes it,' 'He must have red blood,' or 'Your loved
ones must die,' surely the prevention of a lunatic asylum is better suited to his case than the cure of the gallows."

(2) THE WALTHAMSTOW MURDERS.

FURTHER RESPITE.

The Home Secretary on Monday addressed a communication to the High Sheriff of Essex signifying the Queen's command
that the execution of the sentence of death passed upon William Gouldstone, the Walthamstow murderer, be respited
until further signification of her Majesty's pleasure. This extended respite is in consequence of the report made to
Sir William Harcourt by Dr. Clarke and Dr. Orange, who last week had an interview with the convict to ascertain the
condition of his mind. There can be no doubt the sentence will be remitted in a few days, when Gouldstone will be
removed to Broadmoor Asylum.

DR. ORANGE'S OPINION OF THE PRISONER'S INSANITY.

Monday's Evening Standard says:—"Dr. Orange (the Medical Superintendent of Broadmoor), who was recently sent down to
Chelmsford to see the convict Gouldstone, entertains, it is said, no doubt of his insanity. His mother and aunt were
both insane, and his great uncle committed suicide. He himself appears to have had a suicidal mania, and it is
believed that he went out with the intention of drowning himself on the very day that he murdered his children. His
wife, who is a very respectable young woman, says that he has shown signs of madness for the last twelve months."

NOTES (not for prose without a ruling):

- The leader says the sentence HAS been commuted "during her Majesty's pleasure"; the news item beside it says only
