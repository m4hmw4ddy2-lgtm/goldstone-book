TRANSCRIPTION

COMMUTATION OF GOULDSTONE'S
SENTENCE.

The Home Secretary has requested two physicians to examine the convict Gouldstone, and report as to his mental
condition. The British Medical Journal says that the Home Secretary intends to commute the sentence on Gouldstone to
penal servitude for life, "the evidence of insanity being sufficient to justify a modification of the sentence." We
venture to say that such a termination of the case would be absurd. If there is sufficient evidence of insanity to
justify a commutation of the sentence, it would follow as a matter of course that Gouldstone would be sent, not into
penal servitude, but to a criminal lunatic asylum, where he would be properly cared for. He is either a murderer of
the worst type or an irresponsible lunatic.

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

0. The same text as transcribed/Communtation in newspaper.txt (untitled, undated clipping): this Post page identifies it.

1. Date from the file name only; the clip shows no date. Monday 1 October 1883 was the original date fixed for the
   execution, before the respite to 8 October (Guardian, 26 September).
2. The BMJ passage it quotes is the BMJ of 29 September (transcribed/BMJ_29_September_1883_Transcription.txt), which
   reads "a modification of his punishment", not "of the sentence". The Post's quotation marks enclose its own
   variant. Quote the BMJ from the BMJ.
3. "two physicians": Orange and a second (Contradiction 20, Clarke or Gover). Unnamed here.
4. Under the press rule, usable as what one paper argued in the week between the respite and the reprieve: that
   penal servitude would be "absurd", and that he was "either a murderer of the worst type or an irresponsible
   lunatic". In the event he went to Broadmoor, which is what the paper said should happen.
