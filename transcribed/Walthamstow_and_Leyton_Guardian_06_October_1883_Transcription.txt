THE WALTHAMSTOW MURDERS.

MEDICAL EXAMINATION OF THE CONVICT.
Dr. Orange, Superintendent of the Broadmoor Lunatic Asylum, and Dr. Clarke, visited her Majesty's Prison at
Springfield on the 26th [?] ult. for a medical examination of the convict. The interview lasted nearly three hours.
Mr. R. [?] Hunt Carter, Gaol Surgeon, and the Rev. W. F. Lumley, Chaplain, were present during part of the
examination. Drs. Orange and Clarke afterwards had an interview with the convict's brother, who happened to be
visiting at the Gaol on that day.

DR. ORANGE'S OPINION OF THE PRISONER'S INSANITY.
The Evening Standard of the 1st inst. says:—"Dr. Orange (the Medical Superintendent of Broadmoor), who was recently
sent down to Chelmsford to see the convict Gouldstone, entertains, it is said, no doubt of his insanity. His mother
and aunt were both insane, and his great uncle committed suicide. He himself appears to have had a suicidal mania,
and it is believed that he went out with the intention of drowning himself on the very day that he murdered his
children. His wife, who is a very respectable young woman, says that he has shown signs of madness for the last
twelve months."

FURTHER RESPITE.
On the 1st instant the Home Secretary addressed a communication to Mr. J. Oxley Parker, the High Sheriff of Essex,
signifying the Queen's command that the execution of the sentence of death passed upon William Gouldstone, the
Walthamstow murderer, be respited until further signification of her Majesty's pleasure. This extended respite is in
consequence of the report made to Sir William Harcourt by Dr. Clarke and Dr. Orange, who last week had an interview
with the convict to ascertain the condition of his mind. There can be no doubt the sentence will be remitted in a few
days, when Gouldstone will be removed to Broadmoor Asylum.
