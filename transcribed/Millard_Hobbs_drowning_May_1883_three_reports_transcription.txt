Hackney and Kingsland Gazette, Friday 1 June 1883, p. 3 (viewer BL/0000942/18830601, p. 3, a. 088)

SAD BATHING FATALITY.
Two lads, named Richard Miilard and Henry Hobbs, residing at Courtenay place, St. James street, Walthamstow, and aged
respectively 17 and 19, lost their lives whilst bathing in the Lea, between seven and eight o'clock on Monday night.
They were cousins, and the younger boy had come up from Wales to spend a few days with his uncle at Walthamstow.
Accompanied by a younger brother, the two boys started off to bathe in the Copper Mill Stream. Neither of them could
swim, and they had not long been in the water when the youngest boy, who had been set to watch the clothes, saw them
sink after a struggle. Stricken with fear, the boy had not the presence of mind to call out for assistance from the
men and boys bathing close at hand; but, taking the watches and other valuables from his brother's and his cousin's
pockets, he hurried off home, a mile or so distant. On the alarm being afterwards given, a search was made and the
two bodies were discovered clasped to each other in four feet of water.

(2) Source image: sources/images/Media-coverage/Part-Two/Shoreditch_Observer_02_June_1883_0003_Clip.jpg
Shoreditch Observer, Saturday 2 June 1883, p. 3 (viewer BL/0001024/18830602, p. 3, a. 048)

SAD BATHING FATALITY.—On Monday evening between seven and eight, two youths, aged respectively 17 and 19, lost their
lives whilst bathing, under extremely sad circumstances. They were cousins, and the youngest of the two had come up
from Wales to stay with his uncle, who lives at Walthamstow. Together with a younger brother, the two lads started
off to bathe in the Copper Mill Stream, a narrow strip of water which flows into the Lea at Clapton. Neither of them
were expert swimmers, and they set themselves to teach each other the art. They had not been long in the water before
the young boy, brother of the eldest youth, who was set to watch the clothes, noticed a struggle between the two
bathers, and saw the younger lad firmly clasp his cousin, and the two sank out of sight. He was so stricken with fear
that he had not the presence of mind to call out for assistance from the large number of boys and men who were
bathing close handy. Coming to his senses, the boy took the watches and money out of his brother and cousin's
pockets, and hurried off to his home, a mile or so distant. Having told a boy of the occurrence, a crowd soon
collected around the spot at which the youths had sunk, but instead of searching about for the bodies the bystanders
sent for the drags, half a mile distant, and by the time they arrived some fifteen minutes had elapsed. The youths
were found clasped together in but four feet of water. Sir John Humphreys held the inquest at the Old Mermaid
Tavern, Hackney, on the bodies of Henry Hobbe, aged 16, a joiner, and Richard Millard, aged 19, a joiner, who were
drowned as above. After the evidence had been given, the Coroner said that the place in question was dangerous, and
boys should be careful of bathing at that spot. Had Millard only had the presence of mind to have kept out of the way
of his cousin's arms, and have caught him by the back of the neck both of them might have been rescued. It was a sad
case, but, according to the evidence no one was to blame for the occurence. The Jury returned a verdict of
Accidental Death.

["Hobbe", "occurence" as printed.]

(3) Source image: sources/images/Media-coverage/Part-Two/Walthamstow_and_Leyton_Guardia_02_June_1883_0006_Clip.jpg
Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian, Saturday 2 June 1883, p. 6 (viewer BL/0004053/18830602, p. 6)

SAD BATHING FATALITY IN THE RIVER LEA.
On Monday evening two youths, aged 17 and 19, lost their lives while bathing near Walthamstow. Together with a younger
brother, the two lads started off to bathe in the Copper Mill stream, which flows into the Lea at Clapton. They had
not been long in the water before the brother of the eldest youth, who was set to watch the clothes, noticed a
struggle between the two bathers, and saw the younger lad firmly clasp his cousin, and the two sank. A crowd soon
collected, but instead of searching for the bodies, the bystanders sent for the drags, half-a-mile distant, and by the
time they arrived some 15 minutes had elapsed. The youths were found clasped to each other in about four feet of
water. Instead of applying means to restore animation, the men with the drags wasted time in discussion as to where
they should take the bodies, and thus all chances of reviving life was lost.
