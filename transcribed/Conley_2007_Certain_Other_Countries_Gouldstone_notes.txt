WHAT SHE SAYS (pp. 182-183), and her notes
- Among fathers who blamed childbirth: William Gouldstone "felt his wife had been having children too quickly. When
  she gave birth to twins, he stopped speaking to her. A week later he killed their three young children and severely
  wounded the twins" (n. 82: the Times, 9 August 1883, 9f).
- Quotes him to the police as one speech: "I have done it like a man. . . . I thought it was getting too hot with five
  kids within three and a half years and I thought it was time to put a stop to it." (n. 82)
- The coworkers' fund and "We are quite sure he went out of his mind when his wife bore him twins" (n. 83: the
  Times, 6 September 1883, 5f).
- Grain's speech ("well-conducted ..."; "There was no evidence of want ..."; "unhinged") (n. 84: the Times, 15
  September 1883, 12a-c).
- The Times leader, which she reads as accepting that he was "driven by nature": "We admit that he has acted after
  his kind ..." (n. 85: the Times, 15 September 1883, 9e-f).
- Her argument: "It is striking that Gouldstone felt that his action was that of a man ... fatherhood included the
  power of life and death." Her interpretation; not ours.

CHECKED AGAINST OUR RECORD (10 October 2026)
1. "he stopped speaking to her": the 9 August wire dispatch's claim (live_lists 6: "he had not spoken to his wife
   since her confinement"), against the coroner deposition (he came to see her every night) and her statement (the
   cup of tea every morning from the twins' birth). Conley takes it from the Times's first report. NOT TO BE USED.
2. "severely wounded the twins": true of the first report only; both twins died (the first that evening, the second
   before dawn). Her account never says so.
3. The quotation is SPLICED: "I thought it was getting too hot ..." was said on the way to the station; "I did it like
   a man (too)" at the station, to Folkard (IPN 25 Aug; Times 14 Aug). Her "I have done it like a man" and "five kids
   within three and a half years" are not the wording of the sworn record ("I have had five kids in three years and a
   half": Cheeseman, IPN 25 Aug).
4. The Times leader misquoted: Conley "he has given to proof hereby"; the Times "he has given proof thereby"
   (transcribed/The_Times_15_Sep_leader_transcription.txt, image-checked).
5. CHECKED BY RIK, 10 October 2026: THE ITEM DOES NOT EXIST. Conley's note 83 is wrong. Was: n. 83 puts Graves's appeal ("We are quite sure he went out of his mind when his wife bore him twins") in
   the Times of 6 September 1883, p. 5, col. f. We hold it only from the Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian of 8 September
   (dated 5 September). A Times printing would be a second witness. Not held: to obtain if wanted.
6. CHECKED BY RIK, 10 October 2026: the Times of 6 November 1883, p. 6e, DOES NOT carry it; the note is wrong, and
   my inference that it gave Cole's reprieve is withdrawn. Cole's reprieve date remains unknown. Was: COLE (notes only; Cole is out of the book's main line, Rik 9 October): her next paragraph, a "London labourer" who
   killed his three-year-old son "while in a drunken rage", "shammed madness", a crowd shouting "Hang him!", convicted,
   "later found insane and transferred to Broadmoor after the death sentence was commuted" (n. 86: the Times, 21
   August, 10c; 27 August, 10d; 19 October, 8a; 6 November, 6e, 1883). The 19 October citation is Cole's trial (held).
   So the Times of 6 November 1883, p. 6, col. e, very probably reports Cole's reprieve and removal to Broadmoor: the
   date not held anywhere in the notes. For the notes only.
