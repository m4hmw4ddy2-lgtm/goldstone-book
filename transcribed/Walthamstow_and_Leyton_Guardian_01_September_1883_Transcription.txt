TRANSCRIPTION

The Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian.

SATURDAY, SEPTEMBER 1, 1883.

We are glad to find that public benevolence is being stimulated and directed in a variety of ways and means
for the relief of Mrs. Gouldstone, who, through the great misfortune that has befallen her, is left in a
destitute condition. The cause of her deep affliction is such a terrible reality that further allusion to it
is not needed to awaken sympathy for her, and as none can be sorry without being compassionate, there is
every reason to hope that she will reap some permanent benefit from the efforts put forth by the Vicar of
St. Saviour's. He was the first to extend a helping hand by opening a subscription, and through his timely
influence and aid the troubles which the poor woman has had to endure, if not alleviated, have not been
increased by reason of her necessities. Others are also doing something for her benefit; but would it not be
more advantageous to have the fruition of the various efforts concentrated in one channel, so that a
sufficient sum of money might be available to start her in some way of business? And what channel more
trustworthy than the fund inaugurated by the Vicar of St. Saviour's, which has already been so helpful, and
however much it may be strengthened and increased there is no fear of it being misapplied. We notice that
the Rev. T. Jackson, who is ever zealous in a good cause, turned an open air meeting to good account by
making a subscription for Mrs. Gouldstone. We observe, too, that an expression of sympathy has been
extracted from public enjoyment at a variety entertainment which was given for her benefit, by the "Club
Minstrels," at the Workmen's Hall. Whether listening to an entertainment of the kind under such auspices was
right or wrong, possibly never entered into the heads of the audience; but on reflection it does seem a
travesty of the shocking occurrence which, under no circumstances could be made an occasion for enjoyment.
No one can be sorry, compassionate, and full of enjoyment at the same time. There can be no doubt it was an
act of kindness on the part of the promoters, and as it had an amount of influence for good, possibly the
end justified the means, for charity it is said covers a multitude of sins. A week or two ago a paragraph
appeared in the Daily News which stated that a public meeting had been held at Walthamstow for the purpose
of opening a subscription in aid of Mrs. Gouldstone, and that a gentleman whose name and address was given,
had been appointed to receive subscriptions. Now, we must confess we are not aware of any public meeting
having been held, and we are generally well informed of matters of such importance; but, as it may be an
earnest endeavour on the part of some stray helpers, we would recommend them in order to escape from the
very absurd position in which they are placed, to hand over to the Vicar of St. Saviour's any subscriptions
that have been received so that the public may have every assurance that Mrs. Gouldstone will reap the
benefit of them.

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. The stated purpose of the fund is here, in the paper's own words, and it is not relief in the ordinary
   sense: "a sufficient sum of money might be available to start her in some way of business". Read against
   Graves's letter of 11 October 1883, which records £36 raised against £50 of legal costs and nothing left
   over for Elizabeth, this is the beginning of a story that ends badly.
2. Three competing efforts are named: the Vicar of St Saviour's subscription (Grantham Robinson, "the first
   to extend a helping hand"); the Rev. T. Jackson's open-air meeting collection; and a variety
   entertainment by the "Club Minstrels" at the Workmen's Hall. The paper disapproves of the third on
   grounds of taste while conceding it was kindly meant.
4. The paper flatly doubts the Daily News's report of a public meeting at Walthamstow with a named
   gentleman receiving subscriptions: "we are not aware of any public meeting having been held, and we are
   generally well informed of matters of such importance". The named gentleman is not named here. The
   Daily News paragraph itself has not been found.
