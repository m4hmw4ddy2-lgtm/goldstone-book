THE WALTHAMSTOW MURDERS.

RESPITE OF THE CONDEMNED MAN.

On Tuesday last the High Sheriff of Essex (Mr. J. Oxley Parker) received a letter from the Home Secretary signifying
the Queen's commands that the sentence of death passed on William Gouldstone for the murder of his five children, at
Walthamstow, be respited until Monday, October 8th, in order that medical enquiry may be instituted as to his sanity.
Monday, the 1st October, had been fixed for the date of his execution.

MEDICAL EXAMINATION OF THE CONVICT.

Dr. Orange, Superintendent of the Broadmoor Lunatic Asylum, and Dr. Clarke, visited Her Majesty's Prison at
Springfield on Wednesday for a medical examination of the convict. The interview lasted nearly three hours. Mr. E.
Hunt Carter, Gaol Surgeon, and the Rev. W. F. Lumley, Chaplain, were present during part of the examination. Drs.
Orange and Clarke afterwards had an interview with the convict's brother who happened to be visiting at the Gaol on
that day.

REPORTED COMMUTATION OF SENTENCE.

The British Medical Journal is informed that the Home Secretary intends to commute the sentence on the convict
Gouldstone to penal servitude for life, the evidence of insanity being sufficient to justify a modification of the
sentence.

A THAXTED SURGEON ON THE CULPRIT'S SANITY.

Mr. Wm. Sunderland, of Thaxted, writes:—

Sir,—As one of the medical witnesses in this late trial, will you please allow me, in The Times, to say that the Jury
evidently understood Dr. Savage's answer "No," to Mr. Poland's question—"From your conversation with Gouldstone, could
you certify him to be insane," to imply that he did not consider him to be insane at the time; whereas Dr. Savage's
opinion was that he was insane, but that from his interview with Gouldstone he would be unable to fill up the
certificate required by the Lunacy Commissioners. "Certifying" a lunatic in accordance with these requirements is in
most cases a difficult task, and properly. A medical man may have a strong opinion, but he must have strong facts to
satisfy the Commissioners. Dr. Savage's opinion was firm, but his facts being meagre he could not "certify" to
Gouldstone's insanity, therefore he most properly replied "No" to Mr. Poland's question. Gouldstone is as insane to the
medical profession by his massacre of his five children as if he had sat in a corner for a month with a paper crown
and called himself the Emperor of all the Russias; he is not legally insane until some medical man has been witness of
this massacre, so it appears from the verdict. The hereditary tendency to insanity, though ridiculed in Court, has a
most important bearing on the case, and will no doubt receive its due consideration from the advisers of the Home
Secretary.—Truly yours,

Thaxted, Essex.                                        WM. SUNDERLAND.

THE CHOICE OF A HANGMAN.

The Sheriffs of London and Middlesex—Alderman De Keyser and Alderman Savory—and the High Sheriff of Essex, Mr. J.
Oxley-Parker, were on Monday occupied for two hours in examining candidates for the office of public executioner.
Seventeen persons presented themselves, and five were selected for an interview, when all the details of the
appointment were discussed. One of the men, James Berry, of Thorp-street, Shearbridge, Bradford, who was sanguine that
he would be appointed, once assisted Marwood at an execution at Leeds. Another of the selected candidates is a
builder, residing at Lincoln, who has also, as he stated, "been with Marwood."—A telegram received at Bradford on
Monday night stated that Berry had received the appointment of public hangman for Newgate. Berry, who is a shoemaker,
31 years of age, formerly served in both the Bradford Borough police force, and in the County force. When he left
Bradford, on Sunday he took with him a silk rope, which had been specially plaited for him, fitted with a special
loop. He contended that death by this rope would be speedier than by the method which Marwood practised. Berry's
statement that he had been selected for the post is incorrect, the authorities having the matter still under
consideration. Berry, however, and a man named Taylor, of Lincoln, have been reserved for final selection.
