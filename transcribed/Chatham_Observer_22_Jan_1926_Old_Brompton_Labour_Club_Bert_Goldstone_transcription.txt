FILLING A GAP.
Old Brompton Leads the Way.
LABOUR CLUB FORMALLY OPENED.
Saturday was a red-letter day for adherents of the Labour Party in Old Brompton, for it marked the official opening
of the first purely Labour Club to be formed in … [not transcribed further]

[last paragraph:]
After the dinner the company was largely increased at a most enjoyable concert given by the Rainbow Concert Party,
the manager of which is Mr Bert Goldstone. The contributors to the programme included Miss V. Anderson, Mr Bert
Goldstone, Miss Morgan, Mr Bert Tobin, Mr Taffs, Mr Fisher and Norman, Mr Bert Sharp, Freda Lis Garten, Mrs Kennedy.

NOTES (for Rik): "Mr Bert Goldstone", manager of the Rainbow Concert Party and a performer in it, at the opening of
the Labour club at Old Brompton (Saturday 16 January 1926). "Bert" is a common short form of Albert; Albert was a
Labour man at Chatham by 1933, and the name is not common there. But nothing on the clip identifies him: NOT
established. The Observer also has "Mr. Bert Goldstone" among concert artistes on 4 December 1925, p. 15, and in a
party with "Mr Albert Taffs" on 30 April 1926, p. 9 (snippets only, not clipped). Family memory (a performer? a
concertina, as his grandfather William played?) is Rik's to supply; nothing is drawn here.
