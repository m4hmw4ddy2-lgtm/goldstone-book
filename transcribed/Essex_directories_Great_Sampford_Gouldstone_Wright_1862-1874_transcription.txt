NOTES (inference, NOT for prose):
- Thomas: "blacksmith" alone in 1867 and 1871; "beer retailer" and "blacksmith" in 1874. The 1871 census (April) has
  him "Blacksmith & Beer Retailer" (corrected 21 October 2026 from the image) at the White House, Moor End (dramatis_personae.md).
- George Wright, the buyer of Lot 26 ("Freehold house and blacksmith's shop ... in occupation of T. Gouldstone",
  August 1867): a furniture broker in 1867; listed also as a blacksmith in 1871 and 1874; and in 1874 also "farmer,
  White house". Whether these are one man, and whether "White house" here is the White House at Moor End where Thomas
  kept his beerhouse, is NOT shown. If it is, Thomas's landlord at the White House may have been the man who bought his
  forge. Needs: the rate books or a sale record for the White House, and the 1871/1881 census entries for George
  Wright. Until then, notes only.
- Winterflood at the Free Roberts (George, 1867; Mrs Susan, 1871): the cholera report of 9 November 1866 said the
  dead pugilist William Winterflood had "no connexion with Great Sampford". Noted; nothing drawn.
- CAUTION ON "WHITE HOUSE" (added 20 October 2026, from the 1862 list): the directories have a FARM called White
  house, farmed by William Byatt in 1862 and 1871, and by George Wright in 1874. The 1871 census has Thomas, blacksmith
  and beer retailer, at the White House, Moor End. Whether the directories' farm and Thomas's beerhouse are one building
  is NOT known; the lead that Wright was Thomas's landlord is weakened accordingly. Historic England 1322551 (the White
  House, Moor End) and the tithe/OS maps may settle it.
- 1862 also lists "Wright Hannah (Mrs.), beer retailer" and a second blacksmith, James Turpin. A Wright family kept a
  beerhouse in the village before George Wright bought the smithy; relation to George not known.
- The 1878 page is now held: see the 1878 section below. The edition of GB0652 (1862?) to confirm from the volume's title page.

NOTES (not for prose):
- No Thomas Gouldstone in 1878, as either blacksmith or beer retailer. His eldest son Robert is the village
  blacksmith. Benjamin still the shoemaker.
- The 1874 "Wright George, farmer, White house" is now "Wright Geo. furniture broker & farmer", with no farm named;
  "Wright George, blacksmith" stays a separate line. Two George Wrights, or one man listed twice: not shown.
- Catharine Stock, Board School mistress, 1877-78: no link to Elizabeth drawn (Stock is common in the district;
  Elizabeth had no sister of that name in any record held).
- Graves Robert and Graves Thomas at Great Sampford: William Graves the foreman was born at Radwinter (1829) to George
  Graves; no link drawn (dramatis_personae.md, William Graves: the "Thomas Graves, gardener" claim is DO NOT USE as
  William Graves's father).

NOTES: the list is alphabetical; this is the only Gouldstone line, between "Godward Thos. Grosvenor pl. Southend" and
"Graystone Charles, Woodford Bridge". So in 1878 there is no Thomas Gouldstone, AND no "Gouldstone Jas. Gt.
Bardfield" (listed as a blacksmith in 1874 and again in 1882, thumbnails only). Robert is the only Gouldstone smith in
the county list that year. The W pages (George Wright) were not read.
