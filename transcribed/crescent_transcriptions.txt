BIG FLEET OF WARSHIPS AT COLOMBO.
CRUISERS ARRIVE FROM THE AUSTRALIAN AND CHINA STATIONS.
Novel Spectacle in Harbour.
To-day nine war vessels are lying at anchor in Colombo Harbour, forming one of the biggest fleets that the port has
sheltered for many months past. A point of special interest in the presence here of this little army of grim
men-of-war is the fact that it consists of units in the British fleets in East Indian, Chinese, and Australian
waters. It is doubtful whether an event of such peculiar interest has ever previously occurred in the history of
Colombo as a naval port. The first arrival to augment the ships of the East Indies Squadron in port was H. M. S.
"Flora" of the fleet in Chinese waters which put into harbour shortly before sun set on Saturday. Yesterday, about
noon, four warships of the Australian Squadron—H. M. S. "Cambrian," "Psyche," "Pyramus" and "Pioneer"—were sighted.
Flying the paying-off pennant, they steamed in line, and thousands of residents along the sea shore turned out of
their bungalows to watch the stately procession of the ironclads approaching the port. The Australian warships took
up their moorings near the "Flora," close to the Breakwater The five men-of-war have come here to meet H. M. cruisers
"Gibraltar" and "Crescent" which are due in port on the 10th and 12th instant, respectively, with relief crews. They
will pay off within a day or two of the arrival of the latter, and recommission with the new crews immediately
afterwards for a further period of service on the stations on which they are employed. The "Gibraltar" is bringing
out the new crews of the "Cambrian," "Pyramus," and "Flora," while the "Crescent" has on board reliefs for the
"Pioneer" and "Pysche." After the transfer of crews, the "Gibraltar" and "Crescent," will return home with the
time-expired ship's companies, leaving about the 14th or 15th, and the other vessels will depart for their stations
about the 20th instant.
[Saturday = 2 September; "Yesterday" = Sunday 3 September 1911. The clip's foot cuts a further heading, "...H.M.S.
'FLORA'."]

NINE BATTLESHIPS IN HARBOUR.
FIVE ARRIVALS THIS WEEK END.
INTERESTING INCIDENTS.
With the arrival, this week end, of five battleships, Colombo harbour this morning presented quite an imposing and
warlike appearance, the four vessels already in the port bringing the total up to nine. The ships, which have come
here to be re-commissioned, are H. M. second-class protected cruiser "Flora," 4,360 tons, which arrived from the China
station, on Saturday night; H. M. second-class protected cruiser "Cambrian," of the same tonnage, and H. M.
third-class protected cruisers "Pysche," "Pioneer," and "Pyramus," 2,135 tons, from the Australian station, which
arrived yesterday. Steaming in line ahead, as these vessels were yesterday, they presented an interesting sight,
which was noticed by a good many between Mount Lavinia and the harbour who happened to be looking out to sea at the
time.
H.M.S. "FLORA."
H.M.S. "Flora," which had a very pleasant journey here, with excellent weather, has been out in China for about six
years now. Relief crews for her are coming out by H.M.S. "Crescent," and Captain Corbet will relieve Captain
Nicholas. The time expired crews will go back by the "Crescent." [The rest of the Flora section: her six years in
China, the Bedford salvage, Brunei, the Ragnar, a submarine towed, the Yangtze, speed trials, gunnery, the signal cup,
a whale struck between Manila and Singapore: not transcribed.]
THE AUSTRALIAN VESSELS.
The Australian vessels, also, have had a pleasant voyage, with good weather. They all came up together from Sydney.
the "Cambrian" being with the "Pysche," and the "Pioneer" with the "Pyramus." Fresh crews are being brought out by
H.M.S. "Gibraltar" and H.M.S. "Crescent," which will also take back the time-expired crews.
The three smaller vessels are doing recruiting work, each covering a certain district of Australia. They have nothing
very exciting to record, their work being mostly of a routine character. The "Pioneer" had a man overboard, in shark
infested waters, between Timor and Singapore, when a heavy sea was running, but the "full speed astern" order was
given and a boat was lowered with exceptional celerity and he was picked up in three and a half minutes.
[Then: Pyramus towed about a thousand miles for an engine defect; Cambrian's cruising since 7 September 1909, her
ports of call on the way; a shark and the target light at night firing; a murderer carried from Suva; a cassowary,
wekas, a wallaby and a dog as pets: not transcribed. All are the OLD crews' voyage, before Albert: not his.]

ANOTHER WARSHIP IN HARBOUR.
H.M.S. "CRESCENT" ARRIVES WITH RELIEF CREWS.
The number of His Majesty's warships in harbour has been augmented by the arrival of the "Crescent" shortly after 4
p.m. to-day. His Majesty's ships make an imposing array along the South-West arm of the breakwater, the total number
of vessels in harbour now being ten. The "Crescent," as already announced, is bringing relief crews for H.M.S.
"Flora," of the China station, and H.M.S. "Psyche" and "Pioneer," of the Australian station, which have been in
harbour for the past few days awaiting her arrival. The transfer of crews will probably take place on Friday, after
which the warships will return to their respective stations. The "Crescent" and "Gibralta" return to England with
time-expired crews. The "Gibralta" will probably sail on Monday, and the ships of the Australian station the day
before.
[Friday = 15 September; Monday = 18 September; "the day before" = Sunday 17 September 1911. "Gibralta" as printed,
twice.]

ARRIVAL OF H.M.S. "CRESCENT."
RECOMMISSIONING ON FRIDAY.
H.M.S. "Crescent" of the Home Fleet, arrived from London at 4-15 p.m., bringing relief crews for H.M.S. "Flora" of
the China Station, and H.M.S. "Pioneer" and "Psyche" of the Australian Station, which are here awaiting her arrival.
The recommissioning of the five ships, according to the present arrangements, will take place on Friday, followed by
the transfer of crews.
The "Crescent" was in communication with the "Cambrian" by means of wireless telegraphy during the last three days.
The "Cambrian" and other ships of the Australian Station will go back to their Station probably on Sunday. The
"Gibraltar" is expected to return home on Monday.
