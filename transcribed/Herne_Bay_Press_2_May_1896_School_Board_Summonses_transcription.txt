Text, verbatim:
"WHITSTABLE: SCHOOL BOARD SUMMONSES.
Lewis Castle was summoned for not sending his daughter, aged 12 regularly to School.
Mrs. Castle attended and said the child had not got any shoes, and that the family had only had 6/6 in three weeks
for eight of them to live upon.
The Attendance Officer said he knew the family were in very poor circumstances.
The Bench decided to adjourn the case for a fortnight to see if the child will attend more regularly in the
meantime.
William Madams was similarly summoned in respect to his son aged eight.
The mother of the child pleaded poverty as the cause, and the Bench dealt with the case on the same conditions as
the previous one."

DATE OF THE HEARING: not given in the clip (before Saturday 2 May 1896; the court not named). Albert was eight until
19 June 1896.

IDENTITY: "William Madams ... his son aged eight", Whitstable, 1896: the age is Albert's (born 19 June 1887); Madams's
own children by Elizabeth were girls (Lilian, b. April 1890; Eliza Jane, b. September 1891). Very probably Albert and
Elizabeth; neither is named.

WHAT IT GIVES: the summons is in Madams's name, "in respect to his son" (five years after the 1891 census called Albert
his "Stepson"); "the mother of the child" attended and "pleaded poverty as the cause"; the case was adjourned for a
fortnight on the same terms as the Castles', to see whether the child would attend more regularly.
HER WORDS: none quoted. "Pleaded poverty as the cause" is the reporter's summary of what she said in court: reported
speech, not her words (thread file, section 2: no addition).
ONE JOB PER VOICE / NO BORROWING ACROSS FAMILIES: the shoes, "6/6 in three weeks" and "eight of them" are Mrs Castle's
evidence about the Castle household. Nothing of it may be carried over to Elizabeth's. The Attendance Officer's "very
poor circumstances" is said of the Castles only.
Wanted: the adjourned hearing (about a fortnight later, mid-May 1896) and the first hearing's date and court.
