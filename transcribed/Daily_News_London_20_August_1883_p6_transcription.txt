TRANSCRIPTION

THE MURDER OF A FAMILY AT WALTHAMSTOW.

COMMITTAL OF THE PRISONER.

At the Stratford Petty Sessions on Saturday, William Gouldstone, 26, described on the charge-sheet as a blacksmith, of
8, Courtenay-place, St. James's-street, Walthamstow, was brought up in custody by Inspector Craggs, of the N division,
and charged on remand with murdering his five children at 8, Courtenay-place, St. James's-street, on the 8th
inst.—Charles Gouldstone, aged three years and a half; Herbert Gouldstone, aged two years and a half; and Frederick
Gouldstone, aged sixteen months, by drowning them in a cistern; and two twin infants, seven days old, who died from the
effects of blows on the head with a hammer.

Mr. H. Batchelor, from the office of the Solicitor to the Treasury, appeared to conduct the prosecution, Mr. J. W.
Atkinson, solicitor, of Stratford, being present on behalf of the prisoner.

Mr. Nathaniel Powell (the Chairman) and Mr. Arthur Lister were the magistrates.

Mr. Batchelor said that the evidence he should give was very short, and, after intimating the course he proposed to
take, he said that he should then ask that the prisoner be committed for trial.

Mrs. Ada Hamilton, who has been already examined at this court and at the inquest, was recalled, and detailed the
evidence given by her on the last-named occasion.

William Cheeseman, police-constable 86 N, said, in reply to Mr. Batchelor: I was on duty at St. James's-street,
Walthamstow, on the day in question, and was called to 8, Courtenay-place. When the prisoner saw me he said, "Good
evening, policeman. I have done it. Now I am happy, and am ready for the rope." I told him that what he said would be
used in evidence against him, and then he put on his hat and coat, and asked me to let him see his wife. I then took him
into the bedroom to his wife, and when I got there his wife said, "Oh! you wicked man, what have you done?" The prisoner
said, "I have killed all your children, you will be single again, and I shall be hanged." His wife asked him for money,
and he gave it to her. The prisoner then kissed his wife, wished her "good-bye," and I took him away. On the way to the
station, prisoner said, "When I took my money last week I thought of buying a revolver to do it with, but I altered my
mind as I thought it would make too much noise." I made a note of the conversation a few minutes afterwards. He also
said, "I had a hard job with the two biggest, but the other little ——, I soon settled him. I thought it was getting
too hot, for I have had five —— children in three years and a half, so I thought it time to put a stop to it." When I
got him to the station he said, "I did it like a man." When the charge was read over to him at the station he said,
"Quite right, sir." When I saw the three children at the house I satisfied myself that they were dead.

By Mr. Atkinson—He seemed excited at the time, but not such an excitement as from drink. He had no appearance of drink.
When he spoke of about being "ready for the rope" he had not a piece of rope in his hand. When in the bedroom I did not
hear the wife say she wished the children dead. She did not supplement her request for money with the wish that the
children were dead. The whole of the children, as a matter of fact, were not dead.

By Mr. Powell—When the wife asked for money he gave her some—a two-shilling piece, or a half-crown, and a shilling; I
could not say which.

James Honey, police-constable, 41 N Reserve, George Folkard, inspector of N division, and Henry Wheatly, police-constable
208 N, having given corroborative evidence,

Henry Gould, examined by Mr. Batchelor, said—I reside at Lea Bridge-road, Leyton. I am a M.R.C.S. and L.A.S., London,
and divisional surgeon of police for the district. On the 8th inst., at six o'clock, I was called by Inspector Folkard,
and went with him to 8, Courtenay-place. I went into the front room on the first floor, and there on a bed saw two
children, only a few days old. They were male children. I examined them, and found they had both received injuries to
the head. I felt that the skull of each was fractured, and that blood and froth was issuing from them. I thought it
impossible that the children could live. I went into the kitchen and there saw three children lying in a row side by
side, their feet being all one way; they were quite dead and their clothes were saturated with water. One of the
children was naked, Dr. Twining having taken the clothes off. I saw no marks of violence on two of them, but on the
eldest (Charles) I found on the neck an indentation mark of a stout cord; it had been put around the neck. At seven the
same evening I again went to the house, and saw that one of the two younger infants had died. Soon after seven o'clock
the next morning I went and found that the other one was dead. Since that time I have made a post mortem examination of
the bodies of the whole of the five children. As to the two younger I found that their death had been caused by the
violence used that fractured the skull of each. As to the three others, from the condition of the heart and lungs I
came to the conclusion that they died from suffocation, caused by drowning. All the bodies of the three children were
well nourished. On the three there were no marks of violence, except the indentation on the neck of the elder.

By Mr. Atkinson—He had only seen the prisoner in connection with this case. He would not like to say that he was
perfectly acquainted with the "Cyclopædia of Practical Medicine."

Mr. Wells (the Clerk) said this was scientific evidence of the doctor; he was not well up in questions of insanity, if
it was to be referred to, as that was a specialty.

Mr. Batchelor, having concurred with Mr. Wells that insanity was not a question for the consideration of this Court, Mr.
Atkinson said he would not press the question, saying his only desire was to get something on the depositions before it
went to a judge and jury.

This was the whole of the evidence.

The prisoner was committed for trial, Mr. Atkinson saying he would reserve his defence.

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. THE SECOND MAGISTRATE: "Mr. Nathaniel Powell (the Chairman) and Mr. Arthur Lister were the magistrates." Settles
   Contradiction 61 (Lister, previously the likely reading from the Times's "re-called by Mr. Lister").
2. "By Mr. Powell": the question about the money was the Chairman's, not Atkinson's (the IPN/Guardian copy runs it on
   under Atkinson's cross-examination).
3. Cheeseman: "I made a note of the conversation a few minutes afterwards." Not in the IPN/Guardian copy; the Old
   Bailey account has a similar statement.
4. "the other little ——" and "five —— children": two words printed as dashes in the station statement. The Times of
   20 August also has "five ——". Bears on the Chapter Nine decision on "bloody".
5. "Now I am happy, and am ready for the rope": full stop after "I have done it", "Now" opening the sentence
   (Contradiction 37).
6. Atkinson's own stated reason for the insanity questions: "his only desire was to get something on the depositions
   before it went to a judge and jury" (the Times: "to get a few words on the depositions"). This is the defence's
   stated reason for the Gould questions only; it does not license a reason for the questions to Ada (standing caution).
7. "Wheatly", "Batchelor", "L.A.S." (for L.S.A.): as printed.
