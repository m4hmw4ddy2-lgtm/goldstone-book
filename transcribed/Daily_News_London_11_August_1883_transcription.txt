TRANSCRIPTION

THE MURDER OF A FAMILY AT
WALTHAMSTOW.

INQUEST.

At the lodge of the Walthamstow Cemetery yesterday Mr. C. C. Lewis held an inquiry touching the death of the five
children of William Gouldstone, a blacksmith, living at 8, Courtenay-place, St. James's-street, Walthamstow, on
Wednesday night last. The children were—Charles, aged three years and a half; Herbert, aged two years and a half; and
Frederick, aged 16 months, who were drowned by being placed in a cistern; and two infant boys, aged seven days,
unnamed, and who were each struck on the head with a hammer.

Inspector Craggs, of the N division, watched the case on behalf of the Commissioners of Police, Inspector Foulkard
and Sergeant Smith, of the N division, being also present.

The first witness called was Ada Hamilton, of 8, Courtenay-place, St. James's-street, Walthamstow, the wife of a
jeweller, who said—I have been with my husband living in the house three weeks. I had been engaged to attend Mrs.
Gouldstone in her confinement as nurse. She was attended by a medical man, and was confined on August 1. She was
delivered of twins, two boys. All went on well till last Wednesday night. Gouldstone frequently saw his wife after
her confinement. He went to see her every night when he came home from work. He had never, however, asked to look
at the two newly-born boys. He slept in the kitchen. Last Wednesday night he returned home at about a quarter past
five o'clock. Mr. Gouldstone came into the bedroom and stood by the bedside of his wife, but he never spoke. His
wife said to him, "How is it you are so early home to-night?" He said, "I left when the manager went for his
dinner." He worked at 67, Upper Thames-street. She then asked, "Have you told them at the shop that I have been
confined?" She meant the workshop. He replied, "No." She had at this time her little boy Freddy, 16 months old, on
the bed at the foot, and Gouldstone picked it up and took it into the kitchen. By that time I had finished washing
and dressing the first baby, and had laid it down by its mother's side. I had occasion to go out into the kitchen,
and saw all the three children playing on the floor there, the father sitting on the fireguard. I returned to the
bedroom, and said to Mrs. Gouldstone, "Now, I'll make your bed if you get out." I got Mrs. Gouldstone out of bed
and I put her back again. I then asked Gouldstone if I should go out of the room, but he did not answer. I went
downstairs, leaving Mrs. Gouldstone with her two infants by her side. When I reached the yard door, I heard cries of
"Murder," and I recognised Mrs. Gouldstone's voice. I ran upstairs and tried to open the bedroom door, but it was
locked on the inside. She said, "Open the door, he is murdering my children." I tried to force the door, but could
not. I called "Help," and Mrs. Clarke (the landlady of the house) came running upstairs, and we both tried to force
the door. Gouldstone opened the door. I said, "Oh, you wicked man; what have you done?" I had not then seen
anything done to the children. Mrs. Gouldstone was at the foot of the bedstead. She said, "Come and look at my
babies, and see if he has killed them." I passed into the room, and saw the two babies on a pillow behind her—she
was down at the foot—where I had laid them. They were each bleeding from the nose and the mouth, and one had got a
bruise right over one of its eyes. They were both breathing. When I said, "Oh, you wicked man," he said, "No, I am
not a wicked man; I am a happy man." I passed in and stood by Mrs. Gouldstone's side. She told me that he had
murdered the other three children, and she asked me to go and see after them. I had no previous idea that they were
dead. As I entered the kitchen door I saw Gouldstone bringing little Frederick in his arms from the scullery. His
clothes were wringing wet. I put my head out of the door, and saw a policeman standing in the kitchen. Gouldstone
and the constable went into Mrs. Gouldstone's bedroom. She said, "Will you give me all the money you have? I have my
doctor to pay;" and he gave her all he had in his pocket. He then kissed her, and said "Good-bye," and the constable
then removed him from the house.

The next witness called was Emma Clarke, wife of James Clarke, a carpenter, of 8, Courtenay-place, St.
James's-street, Walthamstow, who said—My husband hired this house. William Gouldstone, his wife, and family had
lodged with us eleven months. He was generally a steady man. I only saw him the worse for drink once—that was on
Bank Holiday. He was a calm, placid man, and seemed to be very fond of his wife and kind to his three children.

William Cheeseman, police-constable 86 N, stationed at Lea-bridge-road, said—On Wednesday afternoon last at about 20
minutes to six o'clock I was on duty at St. James's-street, Walthamstow, when I received information that a man was
killing his children at No. 8, Courtenay-place. I immediately went to the house. I went upstairs on the landing, and
I saw William Gouldstone in the kitchen. He was in a stooping position over three children side by side lying on the
floor. The three children appeared to be dead, and were thoroughly drenched with water. William Gouldstone had his
coat and hat off and had his shirt sleeves tucked up. As soon as he saw me he came towards the door, and said, "Good
evening, policeman. I have done it. Now I am happy. I am ready for the rope." I cautioned him that whatever he said
would be used in evidence against him. He then put on his coat and hat, and said, "Let me see my wife." I took him
into the bedroom, and his wife said "Oh, you wicked man, what have you done?" He said, "I have killed all your
children. Now you'll be single again, and I shall be hanged." His wife said, "Have you any money?" He said, "Yes,"
and gave her all he had. He then kissed her and bade her good-bye. On the way to the police-station he said, "When
I took my money last week I thought of buying a revolver to do it with; but I altered my mind, as I thought it would
make too much noise. I had a hard job with the two eldest, but the other little ——, I soon settled him. I thought it
was getting too hot. I have had five children in about three years and a half, so I thought I would put a stop to
it." At the police-station he said, "I've done it like a man;" and when he was charged—the charge being read over to
him by the inspector—with having murdered these three children, he said, "Quite right, sir." The hammer produced I
received from Mrs. Hamilton.

The Coroner said that if they must adjourn they might as well adjourn now, if it met with the approbation of the
jury.

The inquiry was then adjourned, and after Mr. Lewis had made out the orders for the burials of the children he and
some of the jurymen proceeded to view the premises where the sad affair occurred.

1. THE NOTES' CLAIMS FROM THIS PAPER, CHECKED AGAINST THE IMAGE (30 September 2026):
   - "three weeks": confirmed as printed. Shared with the Evening News: one reporter (Contradictions 21, 58).
   - "She was attended by a medical man": confirmed. No longer a discrepancy: Mr Sutton delivered the twins
     (Contradiction 23, resolved 22 September).
   - "Now, I'll make your bed if you get out": confirmed.
   - Elizabeth's "I have my doctor to pay": confirmed, but it comes AFTER the constable is in the house ("Gouldstone
     and the constable went into Mrs. Gouldstone's bedroom"), NOT "on the door opening" as MASTER_NOTES has it.
   - MASTER_NOTES says this report and Ada's witness statement both have William telling Elizabeth where the other
     three children were. THIS REPORT DOES NOT: "She told me that he had murdered the other three children." The
     words "Your other three children are in the cistern" are in the witness statement (and the Guardian of 11 August,
     reporting the first hearing), not here. Corrected in MASTER_NOTES.
   - Cheeseman's greeting: "Good evening, policeman. I have done it. Now I am happy. I am ready for the rope." Full
     stops, the "now" opening the second sentence (Contradiction 37; the Evening News transcription has "I have done
     it; now I am happy"). One text, same placing of "now" either way.
2. "the other little ——, I soon settled him": the Daily News prints a dash for the word; the Evening News
   transcription has "the other little one—". Compare the Chapter Nine decision on "bloody" (MASTER_NOTES,
   22 September). A newspaper suppressing a word is evidence that there was a word to suppress.
3. "with having murdered these three children": the charge of the Wednesday night, correctly three (the twins
   were still alive). The Evening News transcription reads "the five children"; one of the two is a slip, and the
   Daily News reading agrees with the sworn record.
4. "The hammer produced I received from Mrs. Hamilton": Cheeseman's, at the inquest. Also in the Times and the
   Telegraph of the same day.
5. "wife of James Clarke": the press error already on the DO NOT USE list (Charles Clarke).
6. "At the lodge of the Walthamstow Cemetery": where the inquest sat on the Friday.
7. "I then asked Gouldstone if I should go out of the room, but he did not answer": in every sworn version she asks
   Elizabeth, who says "Yes, do, my dear". Reporter's compression.
