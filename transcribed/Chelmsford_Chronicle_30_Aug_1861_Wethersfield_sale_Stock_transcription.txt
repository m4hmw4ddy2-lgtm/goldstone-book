WETHERSFIELD.
TO BE SOLD BY AUCTION,
By Samuel Newman,
On Monday, the 23rd of September, 1861, at the Dog Inn, Wethersfield, at Three for Four o'clock punctually,
A DWELLING-HOUSE and Premises, now used as a Draper's and Grocer's shop, with out-buildings, warehouses, &c., and a
Cottage near.
A DOUBLE TENEMENT, near the "Brewery," in the occupation of Thomas Stock and Harriet Love.
A DOUBLE TENEMENT, situate on the Causeway, in the occupation of Matthew Mountford and Daniel Patient.
EIGHT COTTAGES, situate near "Russell's Farm," in the occupation of Matthews, Robinson, Root, Fryer, Newman, Hitching,
Mead and Rust, with a large garden occupied by Goldstone.
A DOUBLE TENEMENT, situate in Grays Lane, in the occupation of Sams, and one untenanted.
THREE TENEMENTS, situate near "Brand's Farm," occupied by George Pasfield, Joseph Pasfield, and George Poulton.
A DOUBLE TENEMENT, near the Washway, in the occupation of Joseph Freeman and Joseph Poulter.
A DOUBLE TENEMENT, situate at Blackmore End, in the occupation of Schooling and Newman.
A FIELD called "Trumpets," situate near "Wortofts Farm, containing Five Acres.
Particulars and Conditions of Sale will be shortly ready.

NOTES:
- "Three for Four o'clock" as printed. The closing quotation mark after "Wortofts Farm" is missing in the print.
- "A DOUBLE TENEMENT, near the 'Brewery'": the 1861 census (7 April) lists the Stocks and Elizabeth Love (78, widow)
  with her son George among four dwellings under "Brewery House". Who "Harriet Love" was is not known.
- The sale was fixed for five weeks before Emma Ansell's death (27 October 1861). The result (buyer, price) not found.
- The auctioneer, Samuel Newman (also of the 1855 Wethersfield Place sales). Elizabeth's grandmother was a Newman, and
  Susan Newman was her schoolfellow: a common surname in the district; no link drawn.
- "a large garden occupied by Goldstone": unidentified.
