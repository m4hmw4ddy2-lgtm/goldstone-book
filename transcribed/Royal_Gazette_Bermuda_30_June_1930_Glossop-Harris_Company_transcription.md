## Advertisement (whole)
COLONIAL OPERA HOUSE / Last Few Performances of / MISS FLORENCE GLOSSOP-HARRIS / AND HER ENGLISH REPERTORY COMPANY / In
the Latest London and New York Successes / TONIGHT / By Public Vote / "THE BIRD IN HAND" By John Drinkwater / STILL
RUNNING IN NEW YORK / Farewell Performance Tuesday, July 1st. / By Popular Request / "THE WRECKER" By the Author of "The
Ghost Train" / Book your seats early at The Wm. Weiss Co., Reid St. / Prices: Reserved Seats 4/-, 3/-, 2/6 / Evening
Performances Doors open at 8.00 Commence 8.30 prompt / 4322†w.

## "TONIGHT'S PLAY. 'Bird in Hand'" (passages)
"John Drinkwater's brilliant comedy "Bird in Hand," gives the Glossop-Harris Co. an excellent vehicle for providing a
delightful evening's entertainment at the Colonial Opera House tonight."
Cast named: Dolys Stevens; Roger Livesey (the innkeeper); Trevor Ward; John Tanner; "King's Counsel (Mr. E. Rayson-
Cousens) a young novelist (Mr. J. Perry) and a commercial traveller (Mr. T. Gordon Blyth)"; Miss Rita Ricardo.
Ends "—(From a Review.)"

## "'FLOSSOPS' FAREWELL. Final Performance of Visiting Company Tomorrow Night. 'THE WRECKER'" (passages)
Cast named: John Tanner; Dolys Stevens; "Mr. Roger Livesey, without make-up, as Roger Doyle"; "Mr. Raysons-Cousens"
[sic; "Rayson-Cousens" in the other review]; "The rest of the cast particularly Mr. Arthur Holland and Mr. John Perry as
Sir Gervase Bartlett and Chester Kyle respectively ...". (Heading's "unprecendented" in the body [sic].)

## NOTES (not prose)
1. CORROBORATES the Orbita list (transcribed/Orbita_inward_passenger_list_14_July_1930_...): Florence Glossop-Harris,
   "Actress"; Gordon Blyth, "Actor Manager" (the review's "T. Gordon Blyth"); Arthur Holland, "Actor"; all embarked at
   Bermuda. The company closed at the Colonial Opera House, Hamilton, on Tuesday 1 July 1930 (the advertisement's
   farewell; "Tomorrow Night" on 30 June). The Orbita landed at Plymouth on 14 July. Its sailing date from Bermuda is NOT
   on either document. Anne Glossop and Daphne Carr ("Stage" on the list) are not named in these reviews.
2. It explains why THE COMPANY was aboard. It says NOTHING of why ALBERT was in Bermuda (live_lists 4, item 64, still
   open). Nothing shows that he met, saw or knew of them; no sentence may suggest it.
3. A COINCIDENCE, FOR THE NOTES ONLY: in "Bird in Hand" Gordon Blyth played "a commercial traveller", the trade Albert
   gave his invented father in 1910. Pointing of the purest kind; never in the narrator's voice. Rik's to know, and to
   leave.
4. Roger Livesey (later the film actor) was in the company; whether he sailed on the Orbita is not shown (only one
   page of the list is held; L would fall on another page). Not to be said unless a page shows him.
5. Val Larg (later Morley): the Bahá'í histories page (Liz Emerson, posted 30 Nov 2012) says she toured Bermuda with
   the company as Celia and married in it in 1935, but gives no year for the tour. Not on the page of the list held;
   NOT shown to be the 1930 company. Second-hand family memoir of another family: colour at most, and only if a document
   ties her to 1930.
