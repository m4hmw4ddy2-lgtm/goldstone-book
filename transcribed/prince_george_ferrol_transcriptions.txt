BATTLESHIP MISHAP.

COMMANDER OF THE PRINCE
GEORGE ACQUITTED.

The battleship Prince George, which was recently in collision with the battleship Hannibal off Ferrol, arrived safely in Portsmouth for repairs yesterday. She experienced heavy weather during the passage.

She has sustained extensive damage on the port side forty feet from the stern and about fifteen feet abaft the armour-belt. The damage extends from the upper deck right down the side of the ship to the keel, the steel plates being ripped up like tin and bent.

The two-days court-martial upon Captain Campbell, of the Prince George, has resulted in the officer’s acquittal. The trial of the captain of the Hannibal has not yet taken place.

It transpires that the special ventilators on board the Prince George, which should have closed automatically when she was in collision, failed to act, wires the Central News Ferrol correspondent, and instead of the water being prevented from entering the torpedo departments of the ship, they were immediately flooded.

According to the official report, the Prince George had over 800 tons of salt water in her when she reached Ferrol, and was so low in the stern that the admiral’s gallery was quite close to the sea-level.

She was down so much at the stern that some doubts were entertained as to whether there would be sufficient water for her to enter Ferrol Harbour, and it appears that H.M.S. Prometheus took the precaution of sounding Ferrol River opposite the Government forts. This action has caused some commotion in Spanish official circles, a law in Spain prohibiting the taking of any kind of soundings in front of the Government forts without first obtaining permission.

THE PRINCE GEORGE COLLISION.

[From Our Own Correspondent.]
Ferrol, Monday.

The whole of the British Channel Fleet arrived here last night. As the result of further examination of the hull of the Prince George, it has been decided that it will not be necessary to dock her here for repairs, her injuries not proving so extensive as was at first supposed.

She will be temporarily repaired afloat, and will then proceed under escort to Portsmouth to be docked. The Hannibal is scarcely marked.

WARSHIPS IN COLLISION.

How the Prince George was Struck.

(CENTRAL NEWS TELEGRAMS.)
Corunna, Monday Night.

The collision between the Prince George and the Hannibal occurred on the night of the 17th. The ships of the fleet were manoeuvring at a distance of 80 miles from Corunna. They all had lights extinguished, and as far as can be ascertained the Hannibal struck the Prince George on the port side near the stern. The torpedo compartments of the Prince George immediately filled with water.

9 30 p.m.

At the last moment the Vice-Admiral commanding the Channel Fleet at Ferrol has resolved to attempt to have the repairs required in the battleship Prince George effected while she is afloat, instead of beaching her. If this should prove to be impracticable the vessel will be beached as a last resource. It is confirmed that the torpedo and propeller sections of the ship are filled with water to such an extent that the prow of the vessel is submerged seven feet below the water line.

THE CHANNEL FLEET.

The Secretary of the Admiralty has received the following telegram, dated October 18, from the Vice Admiral commanding the Channel Fleet at Ferrol:—

“I have put into Ferrol with the Channel Fleet, on account of Hannibal having collided with Prince George during turning movement on the night of the 17th October, considering it desirable to proceed to Ferrol to ascertain full extent of the damage, as aft submerged torpedo flat of Prince George filled with water. No damage to Hannibal.

“I will report further when damage has been ascertained, and I will take necessary steps for repair and inquiry into cause of the accident. No one hurt.”

(FROM OUR CORRESPONDENT.)
MADRID, MONDAY NIGHT.

British and Spanish official reports to-day state that the British Squadron entered Ferrol on Sunday evening, with the battleship Prince George, which had been injured in a collision. The Spanish Naval authorities volunteered every possible assistance, and it is believed that the repairs will be soon completed.

LATER.

Spanish telegrams from Ferrol report that the repairs to the battleship Prince George have already begun, with a prospect of prompt completion, though the damage extends to several compartments, and at first caused fears of an explosion of the boilers after the collision.

It is stated that the Fleet will remain until the repairs are completed. The Spanish Captain General at Ferrol, who, acting under instructions from the Madrid Government, has tendered every assistance, has been thanked by the British Admiral.

(THROUGH REUTER’S AGENCY.)
FERROL, Oct. 19.

His Majesty’s battle-ship Prince George suffered great damage in the collision with his Majesty’s battle-ship Hannibal. She is taking in an immense amount of water, and the pumps are working continually.

The dock here not being large enough to accommodate her, she has been beached to enable temporary repairs to be made such as will enable her to be towed to some dock abroad.

(CENTRAL NEWS TELEGRAM.)
CORUNNA, Oct. 19.

The collision between the Prince George and the Hannibal occurred on the night of the 17th inst. The ships of the fleet were manoeuvring at a distance of eighty miles from Corunna. They all had lights extinguished, and, as far as can be ascertained, the Hannibal struck the Prince George on the port side, near the stern. The torpedo compartments of the Prince George immediately filled with water, and the ship herself sank six feet astern. It is said that the collision was caused by an unexpected movement on the part of the Hannibal.

BRITISH WARSHIPS.

SERIOUS COLLISION.

H.M.S. PRINCE GEORGE BADLY
DAMAGED IN MANŒUVRES.

From Our Own Correspondent.
MADRID, Sunday.

Telegrams from Ferrol this morning announce that the British battleship Howe entered that port and asked permission from the authorities for the entrance into the docks of the warships Hannibal and Prince George, which had suffered heavy damages by collision during the night near Finisterre, whilst manoeuvring without lights.

The injured vessels, accompanied by others of the squadron, were expected to arrive this afternoon.

Alarming Account.

Later.

At Ferrol it is stated that the Prince George has a large hole beneath her water-line, and that nine feet of water are reported on board.

She is being towed here, being unable to use her own steam or engines.

The cruiser Prometheus arrived first for the purpose of ascertaining the buoys which mark the entrance to the port.

It is feared that the Prince George will be too large to enter the dock, in which case it may be necessary to beach her to save her sinking.

An immense multitude are on the pier awaiting the squadron.

FLEET AT FERROL.

Lloyd’s agent at Ferrol telegraphed last evening as follows:—

“The British men-of-war Prince George and Hannibal have been in collision, and have put in here. The former vessel is badly damaged, latter slightly damaged. All the fleet have put in.”

THE TWO BATTLESHIPS.

The first-class battleship Hannibal was built at Pembroke, and was launched on 28 April, 1895 [sic]. She is of 14,900 tons displacement, and is of 10,000 indicated horse power on natural draught, with a speed of 16.5 knots. Her engines were built by Messrs. Harland and Wolff.

She is 413 ft. length over all, with a beam of 75ft., and a mean draught of 27ft. 6in. She is provided with a belt of Harveyed steel for a length of 220ft., and 16ft. in breadth, its thickness being nine inches. The vessels of this class contain 150 compartments and 206 [or 208] watertight doors.

The armament consists of four 12in. 46-ton (wire-wound breechloading guns); 12 six-inch quick-firing guns in casemates; 16 12-pounder quick-firers and two 12-pounder boat guns, besides Maxims and other small guns. The ship’s crew consists of some 750 officers and men, all told.

The first-class battleship Prince George was laid down at Portsmouth in 1894, and was launched in August, 1895. She is of the same class, generally speaking, as the Hannibal, having a displacement of 14,900 tons, and being similarly armored and equipped in the matter of guns.

Her engines were built by Messers. Humphrys and Tennant. At her trial, drawing a mean 25½ft., she steamed with natural draught 16.52 knots per hour, the indicated horse power of her engines being 10,464. Drawing 25ft., she did, with forced draught, 18.3 knots, with an indicated horse power of 12,253.

Like the Hannibal, the Prince George is twin-screw, with hooded barbettes heavily armored, and she is fitted with two funnels abreast, and two military masts, each with two fighting tops.

THE BATTLESHIP COLLISION.

“PRINCE GEORGE” BEACHED AT FERROL.

EXTENSIVE DAMAGE.

His Majesty’s battleships Prince George and Hannibal, two of the most powerful vessels of the Channel Fleet, were towed into Ferrol yesterday after having been in collision off Finisterre.

The following Reuter telegram was received from Ferrol to-day:—

H.M. battleship Prince George suffered great damage in the collision with H.M. battleship Hannibal.

She is taking in an immense amount of water and the pumps are working continually.

The dock here is not large enough to accommodate her. She has consequently been beached to enable temporary repairs to be made such as will enable her to be towed to some dock abroad.

The British Squadron, consisting of eight battleships and three cruisers, will remain here until the repairs to the Prince George have been completed.

Officers in Command.

The Channel Fleet is under the command of Lord Charles Beresford and Rear-Admiral the Hon. Hedworth Lambton.

Captain Frederick L. Campbell, who is in command of the Prince George, took over his duties a month ago. He has had his present rank for over six years, has had wide experience, and has recently relinquished the command of the cruiser Indefatigable on the North America station. Captain George A. Giffard, who gained the C.M.G. for his services as second-class commodore on the Newfoundland fisheries, has had the Hannibal since May, 1902. He is the senior captain of the Fleet, and next for promotion to rear-admiral.

THE KING AND HIS NAVY.

Message from His Majesty to
the Prince George’s Crew.

The First Lord of the Admiralty this afternoon assembled the crew of H.M.S. Prince George and conveyed to them a message from the King.

Lord Selborne said that his Majesty desired him to convey to the officers and men of the Prince George his appreciation of the calmness and courage they displayed after the collision with H.M.S. Hannibal off Ferrol. They displayed perfect discipline and a splendid bravery which was quite in keeping with the best traditions of the British Navy.

His Majesty’s gracious message was received with enthusiasm by the ship’s company of the Prince George, which is now in dock having her damaged hull repaired.

H.M.S. PRINCE GEORGE.

NARROW ESCAPE FROM A GREAT
DISASTER.

His Majesty’s battleship Prince George, which was recently in collision with the Hannibal off Ferrol, reached Portsmouth yesterday, having been escorted by the cruiser Sutley. The Prince George was taken into the South Dock for examination, and to-day will be placed in the repairing dock and operations commenced immediately. She is extensively damaged on the port quarter, having been rammed forty feet from the stern and twelve feet abaft the armour belt. The accident is described by those on board as nearly approaching a repetition of the Victoria disaster. The accident occurred at ten o’clock on the night of October 17th, while the Fleet was carrying out night manoeuvres off Cape Finisterre.

The Prince George was third of the line, and her opposite number in the division was the Hannibal, which crashed into the battleship’s side. Remarkable coolness was exhibited by those on board; collision quarters were sounded, the crew rushed below to close the remaining water tight compartments, and the Hannibal, whose engines were reversed at the moment of the collision, stood by with the Hogue, ready to render assistance. The manoeuvres were at once stopped, and orders issued for the ship to light up. Within ten minutes Lord Charles Beresford was on board and ordered the ship to proceed to Ferrol.

Meanwhile Commander Nicholson, the ship’s carpenter, and other officers rushed below, and assisted in getting out the collision mats. It was found that the ship’s side was ripped from the upper deck to the keel, the worst part being below the water line. “It was the narrowest escape that could have been possible, though we didn’t realise it at the time,” said one. It was a beautifully fine night, and the 80 mile trip to Ferrol was safely accomplished. There however, it was found that the ship was making water so rapidly that her pumps and the Hogue’s were insufficient to reduce it, and orders were given for her to be beached. The beach was eminently unsuited for this risky operation, but it was successfully accomplished, and then the whole Fleet’s carpenters worked for nearly a week constructing a copper dam on the inside of the ship. On Friday she was towed off by other ships of the Fleet, and found water-tight.

A Court of Inquiry decided that Captain Campbell, of the Prince George, should be immediately tried by court-martial. After sitting for two days, under the presidency of Rear-Admiral Hon. Hedworth Lam[b]ton, the Court acquitted him. It is probable that another court- marial [sic] will be held in a few days for the trial of the captain of the Hannibal.
