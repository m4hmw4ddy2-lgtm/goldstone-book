TRANSCRIPTION

THE WALTHAMSTOW TRAGEDY.
To the Editor of the WALTHAMSTOW AND LEYTON GUARDIAN.

SIR,—As vicar of the parish in which Wm. Gouldstone resided, I enclose you a copy of a letter I have received from
his fellow-workmen. I find on inquiry that he bore a most excellent character, that he had never absented himself a
single day from his work, that he was a man of remarkable sobriety, and had always been a kind husband and father.
He has on several occasions exhibited symptoms of insanity, such as talking of his desire not to live and of
committing suicide. I venture to think that this is a case which fairly appeals to the sympathy of the public, and
I shall be happy to receive subscriptions for the purposes named in the accompanying letter.—Yours, truly,
                                        T. H. GRANTHAM ROBINSON.
St. Saviour's Vicarage, Walthamstow, Sept. 5.

[The enclosed letter follows, printed directly below. Source: the Clip-2 image.]

                                        67, Upper Thames-street, E.C., Sept. 5, 1883.
Reverend Sir,—We, the fellow-workmen of William Gouldstone, the poor man now awaiting his trial for the murder of
his five children at Walthamstow, have been doing what is in our power to collect money to employ a good counsel
for the purpose of proving, what we all believe, that Gouldstone was out of his mind when he committed the act. We
are quite sure that he went out of his mind when his wife bore him twins. We think, too, that if you would kindly
write a letter to the papers something might be collected more than is required for the lawyers, to provide for
the poor wife. We can see our way to get £20 among ourselves. As he is to be tried next week, we hope you will not
mind seeing to this at once.—I am, reverend sir, your humble servant,
                                        WM. GRAVES, for Self and Fellow-Workmen.
To the Rev. T. H. Grantham Robinson,
     Vicar of Walthamstow.

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. THE ENCLOSED LETTER, found by Rik the same evening (Clip-2): signed "Wm. Graves, for Self and Fellow-Workmen",
   67 Upper Thames-street, 5 September. So Graves DID write in September, but on the 5th, printed on the 8th; not the
   15th (Contradiction 74). "The purposes named in the accompanying letter" are two: "to employ a good counsel for the
   purpose of proving ... that Gouldstone was out of his mind", and, if more came in than the lawyers needed, "to
   provide for the poor wife". The £20 is their own ("We can see our way to get £20 among ourselves"). "£20" is
   slightly blurred in the image; it agrees with the Guardian of 15 September.
2. "he had never absented himself a single day from his work": the vicar's "on inquiry", not evidence. Graves, the
   foreman, swore William was absent on Tuesday 7 and Wednesday 8 August. Presumably meant of his working life before
   that week.
3. "talking of his desire not to live and of committing suicide": compare Robert Gouldstone at the Old Bailey ("he
   wished himself dead at times; I have heard him say so"). Here, before the trial, from the vicar's inquiries.
4. The purpose of the subscription has moved (and see note 5). On 9 August (issue of 11 August) the vicar asked for "a few pounds to
   provide present necessities" for Elizabeth; the leader of 1 September hoped to "start her in some way of
   business"; here the money is for "the purposes named in the accompanying letter", which by the issue of 15
   September means his legal defence.
5. "We are quite sure that he went out of his mind when his wife bore him twins": the workmen's own dating of it,
   before any medical evidence. Compare Ada ("after that date I noticed a great difference in the prisoner") and
   Elizabeth's own later account, that the change began about twelve months before. The workmen put Elizabeth second, and conditionally:
   whatever is "more than is required for the lawyers". Graves's letter of 11 October records the outcome: £36
   raised, £50 of costs, nothing for her.
6. "Vicar of Walthamstow": Graves's address; Grantham Robinson was vicar of St Saviour's.
