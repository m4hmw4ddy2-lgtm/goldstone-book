TRANSCRIPTION

THE WALTHAMSTOW TRAGEDY.

The September Session of the Central Criminal Court opened on Monday morning last at the Old Bailey—before Mr.
Commissioner Kerr (acting for the Recorder), Sir Andrew Lusk (acting for the Lord Mayor), Sir Charles Whetham, Mr.
Alderman Nottage, and Mr. Sheriff Savory. There are four cases of murder. Mr. Commissioner Kerr, in charging the
Grand Jury, said he did not know that any of the cases called for his direction with regard to the law. The first
case of importance, and which he might call a very painful case, was that of William Gouldstone, who lived in
Walthamstow, and who stood charged with the wilful murder of his five children. After the murder of the twins the
prisoner made some observations which would induce them to hope that he was not aware of the consequences of his
acts. That, however, was not a question for the Grand Jury to consider. If a crime had been committed it would be
their duty to return a true bill, and leave the matter in the hands of the Petty Jury for examination.

On Tuesday the Grand Jury returned a true bill against the prisoner. The prosecution for the Crown will be
conducted by Mr. Poland and Mr. Montagu Williams, and the prisoner will be defended by Mr. Forrest Fulton.

William Gouldstone, 26, described as a blacksmith, was placed at the bar at the sitting of the Court on Wednesday,
before Mr. Justice Watkin Williams, to plead to several indictments charging him with the murder of his five
children. The prisoner when brought into dock presented a perfectly calm and composed demeanour, and pleaded not
guilty in a firm tone.

Mr. Elliott, for the prisoner, then applied that the trial should be postponed until Friday morning, as the
witnesses for the defence could not attend before that day.

Mr. Poland stated that, as the counsel for the prosecution, he could not oppose the application, and Mr. Justice
Williams said that although the state of the business would render it necessary for a judge to attend specially to
try the case, still, under the circumstances, he was bound to accede to the application, and the case therefore
stands fixed for this (Friday) morning.

The fellow workmen of Gouldstone, who have already presented a memorial expressing their belief in his insanity,
have raised among themselves the sum of £20 for the purpose of procuring legal assistance; but as this sum is not
sufficient an appeal is being made to the public.

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. NEW DATED EVENT: the arraignment, Wednesday 12 September 1883, before Mr Justice Watkin Williams. He pleaded not
   guilty "in a firm tone"; Elliott applied for the trial to be put off to Friday because the defence witnesses could
   not attend before then; Poland did not oppose. This falls between the true bill (Tuesday 11) and the trial (Friday
   14), so it bears on where Chapter Thirteen ends.
2. "defended by Mr. Forrest Fulton": at the trial the defence was Grain and Elliott. Elliott is already acting on the
   Wednesday. Fulton is either the paper's error or a change of counsel; not resolved.
3. The notes attribute the memorial and the £20 to "Graves's letter, Guardian 15 September". In this issue they are
   in a news paragraph, not a letter, and Graves is not named. If Graves wrote to this issue, the letter is not in this
   clip; the notes need checking (see the timeline file).
4. Commissioner Kerr's charge: the "observations" after the twins' deaths that might suggest William "was not aware of
   the consequences of his acts" were "not a question for the Grand Jury". The third body in a month to set insanity
   aside for another place (compare the Stratford Bench, 18 August, "at another place").
5. The sessions opened on Monday 10 September, as the project already has.
