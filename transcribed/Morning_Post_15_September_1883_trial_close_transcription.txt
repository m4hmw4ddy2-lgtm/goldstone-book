His lordship then summed up, and pointed out that [the] sole question the jury had to determine was whether, [when]
the prisoner killed his children, he did so under circum[stances] which amounted to wilful murder. If at the time
the man killed his children he knew the nature and qual[ity] of the act which he committed he was guilty of wil[ful]
murder.

The jury retired at 20 minutes past five to consider their verdict, the prisoner looking on as they left the box with
[a] smile upon his face.

The jury, after 15 minutes' absence, returned a verdict of guilty.

The Prisoner—I am not guilty.

His lordship then assumed the black cap and passed sentence of death in the usual form, the prisoner looking upon the
court unconcernedly.

NOTES (not for prose without a ruling):
- Contradiction 93: "The Prisoner—I am not guilty." agrees in words with the Essex Weekly News of 21 September ("The
  Prisoner: I am not guilty."), against the Times's "The prisoner replied in the negative". Whether the Morning Post
  and the Essex Weekly News share a reporter is not known (compare their wording elsewhere in the two reports before
  counting them as two witnesses).
- Contradiction 99: the direction as "the nature and quality of the act" only, with no "knew that he was doing wrong"
  (the Times), as in the Essex Weekly News.
- "15 minutes' absence": other reports' timings to compare (the jury's time out).
- The bracketed letters are supplied at the gutter; "[when]" is the likeliest reading of a fragment ("wh" visible)
  and should not be quoted as certain.
