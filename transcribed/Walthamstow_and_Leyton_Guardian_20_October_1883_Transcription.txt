TRANSCRIPTION

REMOVAL of the CONVICT GOULDSTONE.

The convict Wm. Gouldstone left H.M.'s Prison, Chelmsford, on Monday morning, in charge of Warders Lanham
and Parker, en route for Broadmoor Criminal Lunatic Asylum. They proceeded to Liverpool-street by the 7.45
a.m. train from Chelmsford Station.

                        ___________________

William Graves, a fellow-workman of Gouldstone, writes:—"I hope you will forgive me for bringing the
position of Gouldstone's poor wife to the notice of the public. By the act of her mad husband she has lost
her only support as well as her five children, and now stands alone in utter destitution. We have paid the
sum of £50 for the law expenses, but only got in £36 from subscriptions, so that not only are we short of
the bare sum wanted to meet the expenses, but we have nothing to give to the wife. We should be grateful if
you would once more allow us to appeal to the public for help by putting this letter in your paper, and Mr.
Grantham Robinson, Vicar of St Saviour's, Walthamstow, who has already kindly taken a deal of trouble in
this matter, will receive subscriptions, or they may be sent to me at this address.—W. GRAVES, for self and
fellow-workmen, 67, Upper Thames-street, Oct. 11."

TRANSCRIBER'S NOTES

1. **The removal date.** "Monday morning" in a paper of Saturday 20 October means Monday 15 October 1883,
   which is confirmed as a Monday and is the date of William's admission to Broadmoor already established
   in the project. The two fit exactly: 7.45 a.m. from Chelmsford to Liverpool Street, in the charge of
   Warders Lanham and Parker, admitted the same day. This is now the only source in the archive that
   describes the journey itself.
2. **The letter is dated 11 October**, which places it either the day after or within a day or two of the
   Home Office letter reaching the Sheriff ("on Wednesday morning", i.e. 10 October, per the Preston
   Chronicle of 13 October). Graves is writing about legal costs and destitution at the moment the reprieve
   is being settled, and he does not mention it, which is worth noting when the reprieve date is finally
   pinned down.
3. **The money.** £50 paid for the law expenses, £36 raised by subscription, nothing left for Elizabeth.
   The Guardian's own leader of 1 September had urged that the various funds be concentrated so that a
   sufficient sum "might be available to start her in some way of business". Six weeks later the fund is
   £14 short of the lawyers alone. These two transcriptions should be read together.
4. The paper calls Graves "a fellow-workman of Gouldstone". He was the foreman, and had known William since
   birth, taught him the trade and housed the couple for three years; his own signature, "for self and
   fellow-workmen", is what the paper is following. Not an error worth logging, but not a description to
   carry into prose.
5. The summary of this letter already in `notes/dramatis_personae.md` (Graves's entry, added 15 September
   2026) is accurate against the text above. The letter had never been transcribed in full.
