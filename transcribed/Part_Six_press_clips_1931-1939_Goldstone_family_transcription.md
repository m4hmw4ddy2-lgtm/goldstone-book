# THE GOLDSTONE FAMILY IN THE KENT PRESS, 1931-1939: TWENTY CLIPS (Rik's uploads, 29 October 2026)
Images: sources/images/Media-coverage/Part-Six/ (file names give paper, date and page). Uploaded by Rik 29 October 2026.
Transcription scope: the passages naming a Goldstone, verbatim, with each article's heading and enough context to date and
place it; the rest summarised in square brackets. End-of-line hyphens closed up.
IMAGE CHECK (check-once rule): 29 October 2026 (Claude): every clip read whole at full size (the two tall carnival pages
read in halves). The Goldstone passages read closely; certain unless marked. The Observer's day of issue was Friday in
these years (each date below is a Friday, computed), except 26 March 1938 (a Saturday, as Findmypast gives it).

IDENTITIES (notes): "Irene Goldstone" / "I. Goldstone" (Christ Church and Luton schools, 1931-1933) = very probably the
younger Clarie Irene (born 21 October 1918; "Rene" to the family): the Luton parish, the school, her age. "V. Goldstone"
(County School, 1934; a Luton dance, 1938) = very probably Vera. "Doris Goldstone" (Luton Council Girls' School, 1936) = very
probably Doris May (born 1928). "Mr A. Goldstone" (Labour; the Luton Working Men's Club) = Albert (settled by the
nominations: "ship's rigger, H.M. Dockyard"). "Mrs Goldstone" (Labour bazaar, 1933; the club's committee, 1939) = very
probably Clarie (wife). None is proven by a parent's name or an address on the clip.

## 1931
1. **Chatham, Rochester and Brompton Observer, Friday 18 December 1931, p. 6.** '"The Cradle King." NATIVITY PLAY AT LUTON
   CHURCH.' A nativity play "presented by girls of the senior school at Grove-road" in Christ Church, Luton, "on
   Wednesday" [16 December 1931]: "... Hetty Davis as Joseph, V. Soper, I. Goldstone, M. Burkett and A. Sargent as the
   shepherds, and Vera Brown as Gabriel were also successful in their respective roles." [The rector, Rev. A. E. Blake:
   "to-night we have a ministry of girls."]

## 1932
2. **Observer, Friday 27 May 1932, p. 6.** "Luton's May Queen. PICTURESQUE CEREMONY IN THE PAROCHIAL HALL." Crowned
   "yesterday (Thursday)" [26 May 1932] in the Parochial Hall; the new Queen, Irene Loft; the retiring Queen, Hetty Davis.
   "The children taking part in the ceremony were: Irene Goldstone (captain), Kathleen Woolward, Elsie Meacock, Hilda
   Longhurst, Violet Norris (cushion bearers), ..." [staff of Christ Church senior girls' school present; the Mayor and
   Mayoress of Chatham; Mrs Packer.]
3. **Observer, Friday 23 December 1932, p. 7.** [Heading not on the clip.] A musical play, "The Fortune Ballot," presented
   "in Luton Parochial Hall on Tuesday" [20 December 1932] under Miss M. V. Austin: "Irene Goldstone had the chief part as a
   servant girl who believed that she, like her mistress, had won first prize in a sweepstake." [Cast follows; Irene Loft
   among them.]

## 1933
4. **Observer, Friday 30 June 1933, p. 6.** "Christ Church, Luton." The midsummer fete in the Rectory meadow "on Wednesday"
   [28 June 1933], opened by the Mayor of Rochester; Sir Park Goff, M.P.; Clr. M. Packer; Clr. Mrs Freeman: "Luton's May
   Queen (Irene Goldstone) presented the Mayoress with a bouquet, and Sir Park Goff and the Mayor with buttonholes."
5. **Observer, 30 June 1933, p. 10.** The fete photograph; caption names the platform party (Goff, Leech, Blake, Freeman,
   Willis, Packer, Hawes). No Goldstone named.
6. **Observer, Friday 15 September 1933, p. 9.** "LABOUR PARTY DANCE." Chatham divisional Labour party's opening dance "at
   the Town Hall on Saturday" [9 September 1933]: "The event was arranged by Mr A. Goldstone (propaganda secretary) and a
   committee."
7. **Maidstone Telegraph, 30 September 1933, p. 8.** "THE NEXT MAYOR OF CHATHAM? Invitation To Councillor Mark Packer."
   "Councillor Packer retires in November. The Labour Party are likely to run a candidate in the person of Mr. Alfred
   Goldstone, but the final decision awaits the confirmation of the party executive." ["Alfred": the paper's error; the
   Observer, 29 September 1933, has "Mr A. Goldstone".]
8. **Kentish Express, 27 October 1933, p. 10.** "CANDIDATES FOR COUNCIL SEATS." Chatham: "Luton Ward.—*Mr. G. B. Silver
   (C.), and Mr. Goldstone (Lab.)." Also "St. Mary's Ward.—Mr. J. Bray (Ind.); and Mr. A. Gurr (Con)."
9. **Maidstone Telegraph, 28 October 1933, p. 13.** "KENT BOROUGH ELECTIONS. NOMINATIONS LIST." Chatham, Luton Ward: "A.
   Goldstone (Lab.), ship's rigger, H.M. Dockyard." St Mary's Ward: "J. Bray (Ind.), probation officer."
10. **Maidstone Telegraph, 4 November 1933, p. 2.** "KENT ELECTION RESULTS." Chatham, Luton: "*G. B. Silver (Cons.) 700";
   "A. Goldstone (Lab.) 665". St Mary's: "J. Bray (Ind.) 611", "A. Gurr (Cons.) 526". [A second paper's figures for Luton
   ward: they agree with the Observer, 3 November 1933.]
11. **Observer, Friday 15 December 1933, p. 8.** "OLIVER BALDWIN'S ADVICE TO LABOUR." The Chatham Divisional Labour party's
   Christmas bazaar and fair, Chatham Town Hall, "on Friday and Saturday" [8-9 December 1933]. Stalls: "cocktail bar, Mrs
   Goldstone, Mrs Carpenter". Officers and committee: "executive committee, Clr. A. Fray, Mesdames M. E. Stearne, J.P., B.
   Grieveson, Gorman, Pentecost, Messrs F. Hall, T. E. Reader, A. Goldstone, E. F. Cash, G. Whitelock"; "finance committee,
   Clr. A. Fray, Mesdames M. E. Stearne, J.P., B. Grieveson, Messrs F. Hall, T. E. Reader, A. Goldstone, E. F. Cash."
12. **Observer, Friday 22 December 1933, p. 4.** "Gundulph Would Be Proud of Them. GIRLS OF CHRIST CHURCH SCHOOL WHO HELP
   ST. BART'S." Gundulph House of Christ Church Girls' School, Luton, had collected "£5 1[?]s in halfpennies and farthings"
   since January for St Bartholomew's Hospital, Rochester; the Mayor (Clr. Mark Packer) presented a shield "on Friday"
   [15 December 1933]: "Irene Goldstone (house captain) welcomed the visitors, and the Mayor said it was very fitting the
   girls should receive a shield ..." [The clip's right edge is cut; some words supplied by sense only where marked.]

## 1934
13. **Observer, Friday 2 February 1934, p. 6.** "Luton Children Entertained. MAYOR AT BIG METHODIST SCHOOLROOM PARTY."
   "More than 200 children of unemployed resident in Luton and Christ Church wards" at the Methodist Schoolroom, Luton, "on
   Saturday" [27 January 1934]: "The party was arranged by a committee comprising Messrs W. Grinham (chairman), W. McCully
   (secretary), A. Hawley (treasurer), A. Goldstone, J. Letchford ("the pearly king"), Mrs Carpenter and Mrs Wray."
14. **Observer, Friday 20 July 1934, p. 3.** '"The Blue Bird." CHATHAM COUNTY SCHOOL GIRLS' PERFORMANCE.' Maeterlinck's play,
   "On Friday and Saturday" [13-14 July 1934]. Cast includes, among the Happinesses, "V. Goldstone", and among the Unborn
   Children, "V. Goldstone". [Also Sylvia Cox (Gaffer Tyl), Dorothy Bays, Nancy Pagett, Evelyn Burgess: girls of the 1934 and
   1936 items in Ch21.]

## 1936
15. **Observer, Friday 28 February 1936, p. 11.** "Children's Party." "The first children's party to be given by the members
   of Luton Working-men's Club and Institute was held in the club on Saturday [22 February 1936], when about forty children
   of the members were present. After tea the children played games, and a concert programme was presented by the Rustlers
   dance band, Miss Woods, Mrs Morris, Mr A. Moon and Mr A. Goldstone. ... The event was arranged by a committee comprising
   Messrs Moon (secretary), Goldstone and E. Smith, Mesdames Smith and Moon and Miss Woods. At the conclusion Mr Goldstone
   thanked the members for the assistance which they had given and which had contributed largely to the success of the
   party." [In 1936 the club's secretary was Mr Moon; by January 1939, Albert.]
16-17. **Observer, Friday 7 August 1936, pp. 6-7.** "Chatham's Spectacular Carnival." The first Chatham Carnival, in aid of
   the extension fund of St Bartholomew's Hospital, Rochester; the procession "on Wednesday" [5 August 1936]; the floral
   pageant of 300 schoolchildren at the Town Hall crowning: "Luton Council Girls' School (headmistress, Miss A. Emmett);
   teachers in charge, Misses M. Hickson, D. Reid and A. Gill. Dancers—Violet Pearce, Doris Goldstone, Daphne Bradley, ..."
   [The May Queen of Christ Church, Luton (Miss Dorothy Boys) attended. A trainbearer "Peter Stock": a namesake; nothing.]

## 1938
18. **Observer, Saturday 26 March 1938, p. 6.** "Ballroom Gossip. DANCE PARADE. By PAUL JONES." At Luton Football Club's dance
   "on Saturday" [19 March 1938]: "Other prizes were won by L. Rowe, C. Wallis, C. Forrester and Miss V. Goldstone."

## 1939
19. **Chatham News, Friday 27 January 1939, p. 9.** "Luton Club's Ladies' Night.—About 40 ladies, wives of members of the Luton
   Working Men's Club, attended the Club's first ladies' night dinner at the Luton Parochial Hall last evening. Mr. G. Field
   (Chairman of the Club) presided, and was supported by the Secretary (Mr. A. Goldstone). In a short speech, Mr. Goldstone
   reminded the ladies that he hoped the club premises would become the property of the members in about two years. He
   expressed his gratitude to the committee and the ladies for their support, and hoped that it would continue. Concluding,
   he said that although there were only 80 members in the club, the spirit was such that it might well be envied by much
   larger organisations. The committee responsible for the dinner were: Mrs. Blatchford, Mrs. Dudeney, senior (secretary),
   Mrs. Goldstone, and Mrs. Dudeney, junior. The dinner was followed by a dance, at which music was supplied by a band, under
   Mr. Golding. The M.C.'s were Mrs. Blatchford and Mr. Goldstone." [WHOLE ITEM.]
20. **Chatham News, Friday 10 February 1939, p. 11.** 'LUTON'S "TOUGH GUYS." Successful Inaugural Year of Working Men's
   Venture.' A penny-a-week Christmas fund club formed within the Luton Working Men's Club; its first annual dinner "On Tuesday
   of last week" [31 January 1939] at the club; the chairman, Mr G. Field; guest, the Rector of Luton (Rev. G. P. Phelps):
   "Mr. A. Goldstone (Secretary) referred to the growth of the Club, and acknowledged the support he had received from other
   officers and members. He also thanked the catering staff, under Mrs. Crump, and the Press." "The dinner was followed by a
   musical evening. Songs were rendered by Mr. Halliday, Mrs. Blatchford, Mr. Powell and Mr. Goldstone."

## FINDINGS (notes, not prose)
1. **THE LADIES' DINNER OF 26 JANUARY 1939 HAS A SECOND WITNESS** (item 19), independent of the Observer of 28 January (its
   wording differs throughout): it adds that Mrs Goldstone was on the committee that arranged the dinner; that Albert and
   Mrs Blatchford were the M.C.s; about forty ladies, "wives of members"; the "first ladies' night dinner"; and Albert's
   words in reported speech ("the spirit was such that it might well be envied by much larger organisations"). CONFLICT:
   the Observer has "Mrs. R. E. Blatchford presided at the dinner"; the Chatham News, "Mr. G. Field (Chairman of the Club)
   presided". Ch23 movement one currently follows the Observer ("with Mrs R. E. Blatchford presiding"): to resolve (both
   reported as their papers say, or neither).
2. **ALBERT SANG** (item 20: "Songs were rendered by ... Mr. Goldstone"; and item 15: "a concert programme was presented by
   ... Mr A. Goldstone", 1936). Nowhere else in the record. (Eileen's "Mick's Dad used to sing sometimes" at the RNA club
   cannot be George, who died before she met Mick: whether it was ever Albert is NOT shown, and she never met him. Notes only.)
3. **ALBERT IN LABOUR WORK, 1933-1934** beyond the elections: the party's dance (Sept 1933), its bazaar executive and finance
   committees (Dec 1933, with Mrs Goldstone on a stall), and the party for 200 children of the unemployed in Luton and Christ
   Church wards (Jan 1934). Ch21 material by date.
4. **RENE (Irene Goldstone), 1931-1933:** a shepherd in the Christ Church nativity (Dec 1931); captain of the May Queen's
   retinue (May 1932); the chief part in "The Fortune Ballot" (Dec 1932); LUTON'S MAY QUEEN in 1933 (June 1933); house
   captain of Gundulph House, which raised money for St Bartholomew's Hospital (Dec 1933). The Observer's 1933 "Irene
   Goldstone, Luton's May Queen" search hit is now CLIPPED and checked. Ch21 material by date.
5. **VERA, 1934 and 1938:** "V. Goldstone" in The Blue Bird (July 1934; Ch21); "Miss V. Goldstone" won a prize at a Luton
   Football Club dance (March 1938: Ch23, before Bath).
6. **DORIS, 1936:** a dancer from Luton Council Girls' School in the floral pageant of the first Chatham Carnival (August
   1936), which was raising money for St Bartholomew's Hospital. Ch21 material by date.
7. **ST BARTHOLOMEW'S, ROCHESTER,** where Brian died in May 1937, appears twice before it (Rene's house collecting for it,
   1933; the carnival for its extension, 1936). FACTS OF RECORD; any juxtaposition with Brian's death is pointing and is
   NOT to be made in the narrator's voice.
8. **J. BRAY, PROBATION OFFICER,** won St Mary's ward, Chatham, on the night Albert lost Luton ward (1 November 1933). The
   police court missionary who got the fourteen-year-old Albert onto the Warspite in Whitstable in 1901 was "Mr. J. Bray"
   (Herne Bay Press, 20 April 1901). SAME MAN? NOT SHOWN: a namesake until a source joins them (police court missionaries
   did become probation officers after 1907: general knowledge, TO CHECK). If ever proven, it is a fact of extraordinary
   weight and goes in only as the record states it. Notes only.
9. Maidstone Telegraph's "Mr. Alfred Goldstone" (Sept 1933): a press error, not used.
