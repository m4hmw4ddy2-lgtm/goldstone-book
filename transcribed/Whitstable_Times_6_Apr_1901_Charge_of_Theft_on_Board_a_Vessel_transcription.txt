Text, verbatim (the source's punctuation; its dashes kept as printed):
"CHARGE OF THEFT ON BOARD A VESSEL.—At the County Magistrates' Office, Canterbury, on Tuesday last, Albert Goldstone,
a boy 14 years of age, was charged with stealing a pair of sea boots, value 10s., from the brigantine Christabelle,
lying in Whitstable Harbour, on the 28th ult., the property of Alfred Baker.—The prosecutor gave evidence identifying
the boots produced as his property. He left them in the forecastle of the vessel on the previous Friday week, and
when he went for them a week afterwards they were gone.—John Pout, shoemaker, of Harbour Street, deposed that
prisoner brought the boots to him one day last week to be repaired. The next day he came again, and offered the
boots for sale, and witness bought them for 3s.—Corporal Lambkin proved the arrest and stated that when told the
charge prisoner admitted having stolen the boots and sold them.—Remanded to the Petty Sessions on Saturday."

DATE: "Tuesday last" before Saturday 6 April 1901 = Tuesday 2 April 1901 (calendar computation): the FIRST hearing,
at the County Magistrates' Office, Canterbury, the day after the arrest (Monday 1 April, Folkestone Chronicle).
"Remanded to the Petty Sessions on Saturday" = Saturday 6 April (Folkestone Chronicle, 13 April 1901).
"The previous Friday week" = Friday 22 March; "a week afterwards" = about Friday 29 March.
