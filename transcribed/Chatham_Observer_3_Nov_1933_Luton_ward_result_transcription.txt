The Luton ward return, as printed ("*" = retiring councillor, key printed below the table):
LUTON.
*SILVER, George Brown (Cons.) 700
Goldstone, Albert (Labour) ... 665
Majority ... 35
No change. Electorate 3,701.

At the after-the-poll meeting at the Labour Party headquarters, Batchelor Street, Chatham (verbatim):
"Mrs M. E. Stearne, J.P., who presided, said the party had experienced some splendid results. Mr Blackmore had won a
fine majority and Mr Goldstone had put up a magnificent fight."
"Mr Goldstone said: "I shall live to fight again. Let us try and organise a little better and we shall win next time.
If I can get a band of workers like that which assisted Mr Blackmore I can beat Clr. Harris next year.""
(Our outer marks; the paper's own opening and closing marks round his words.)

WHAT IT GIVES: Albert Goldstone, Labour, lost Luton ward on 1 November 1933 by 35 votes to the retiring Conservative,
George Brown Silver. His own words afterwards, in direct speech, as the paper prints them. He named the Luton ward's
other councillor, Harris, as his target for 1934. (In 1934 he stood instead in Christ Church ward and lost to Freeman,
505 to 832; Harris lost Luton ward to Rosenberg, Co-op: Observer, 2 November 1934.)
NAME COINCIDENCES, NOTHING TO BE MADE OF THEM: "ANSELL, Percy" (St John's ward) and "BRAY, James" (St Mary's ward)
share surnames with Jemima's first husband and with the Whitstable police court missionary of 1901, "Mr. J.Bray".
No connection is known or implied.
