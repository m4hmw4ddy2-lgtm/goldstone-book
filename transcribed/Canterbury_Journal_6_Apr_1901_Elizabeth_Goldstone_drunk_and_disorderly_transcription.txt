Text, verbatim:
"DRUNK AND DISORDERLY.—Elizabeth Goldstone, of Middle Wall, was charged at the County Police Court on Tuesday with
being drunk and disorderly at Whitstable on the previous day. She is the mother of the boy in custody charged with
stealing a pair of sea boots, and when she heard that he had been locked up she went in a state of intoxication, to
the police station and created a disturbance. She was requested to go away, which she refused to do, and as she was
very drunk the police locked her up also.—Fined 5s. and 10s. 3d. costs."

DATES (calendar computation): charged "on Tuesday" = Tuesday 2 April 1901; the offence "on the previous day" = Monday
1 April 1901, the day of Albert's arrest (Folkestone Chronicle, 13 April 1901). Albert's own first hearing was "At
the County Magistrates' Office, Canterbury, on Tuesday last" (Whitstable Times, 6 April 1901): the same day, and very
probably the same bench (inference: the two papers name the court differently).

IDENTITY: certain. "Of Middle Wall" (the household's street in the census of Sunday 31 March 1901, two days before)
and "the mother of the boy in custody charged with stealing a pair of sea boots".

WHAT IT GIVES (the court's facts): Elizabeth, as "Elizabeth Goldstone", charged with being drunk and disorderly at
Whitstable on Monday 1 April 1901; fined five shillings, with ten shillings and threepence costs.
WHAT IS THE PAPER'S ACCOUNT, AND STAYS ATTRIBUTED: the sequence and her state ("when she heard that he had been locked
up she went in a state of intoxication, to the police station and created a disturbance"; "requested to go away,
which she refused to do"; "as she was very drunk the police locked her up also"). Press as press: quoted and named,
never narrated as our own fact. No interior state or motive beyond these words.
NAME: two days after the census entered her as the wife of William Madams (surname under his),
the court and the paper call her "Elizabeth Goldstone". Who gave the name is not recorded. Rik's ruling on spellings
does not cover this (it is a different surname, not a spelling); to be ruled in the Chapter Nineteen skeleton.
Her words: none recorded.
