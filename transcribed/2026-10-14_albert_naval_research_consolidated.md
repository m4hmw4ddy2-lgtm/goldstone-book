# Albert Goldstone naval research: consolidated session note

**Date:** 14 October 2026  
**Scope:** Research completed in this session into ships and operational settings appearing on Albert Jennet Goldstone's Royal Navy service sheet, with particular attention to sources that overlap his exact dates aboard.

## 1. Working evidential rule

For Part Five, a ship's documented actions may be used as part of Albert's lived chronology when his service sheet has him continuously borne on that ship across the dated event. We should still distinguish between:

- **Albert's presence:** established by his service sheet.
- **The ship's action or movement:** established by a contemporary or official source.
- **Albert's personal action, station or thoughts:** not to be asserted unless separately evidenced.

The strongest results today are therefore events where Albert's service dates and an independently dated ship source overlap exactly.

## 2. ADM 53 at The National Archives

The National Archives Discovery catalogue confirms that the relevant Royal Navy ship logs survive in **ADM 53**, but the high-value volumes located today are not digitised and are available only at Kew.

### Prince George

- **Reference:** ADM 53/24983
- **Coverage:** 8 October 1903 to 14 July 1904
- **Albert aboard:** 9 September 1903 to 14 July 1904
- The volume begins nine days before the 17 October 1903 collision with *Hannibal* and runs through Albert's final day aboard.
- It should therefore contain the collision, damage control, Ferrol, temporary repairs, return to Britain and the rest of Albert's posting.
- Current status: **Kew only; useful later for scene-level detail, not required to establish the event.**

### Tyne / TB31

- ADM 53 logs for *Tyne* and TB31 are available, but likewise only at Kew.
- Current online evidence is already strong because neighbouring TB35 and TB36 logs explicitly name TB31 during Albert's posting.
- Kew priorities: 29-30 August 1914; 1 July 1915; 29 February 1916; 22 June 1916.

### Courageous and Alexander

- Relevant logs also survive but are Kew-only.
- *Courageous* priority: 16-18 November 1917, especially the Second Battle of Heligoland Bight on 17 November.
- *Alexander* priority: April-July 1918, especially the Murmansk-Archangel passage and July tensions at Archangel.

**Conclusion:** ADM 53 is now a defined later research layer rather than an immediate online source stream.

## 3. Old Weather / Naval-History logbook corpus

Albert-related ships present on the Old Weather/Naval-History site do **not** have transcribed logs overlapping his own service dates:

- *Jupiter*: online log begins in 1914; Albert was aboard 1905-1908.
- *Crescent*: online coverage begins in 1914; Albert was aboard in 1911.
- *Drake*: online coverage begins in 1916; Albert was aboard 1912-1913.

The major exception is indirect evidence for **TB31** in the surviving logs of neighbouring boats TB35 and TB36.

## 4. TB31 mini-chronology from TB35 and TB36

Albert's service sheet records him under **Tyne (T.B.31)** from 27 October 1913 to 27 July 1916. The following are explicit references to TB31 in contemporary neighbouring-boat logs during that period.

| Date | Source | Evidence | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 May 1914 | TB35 | Hands employed shifting TB31 to No. 3 Tier | TB31 physically present with the flotilla at Parkeston |
| 2 May 1914 | TB36 | Assisting to shift TB No. 31 | Independent corroboration of the same movement |
| 29 Aug 1914, 9.15 pm | TB35 | TB31 reported aground on Iron Craig | Start of the grounding incident in the Firth of Forth |
| Overnight 29-30 Aug 1914 | TB35 | Boats, anchors, lines and searchlights used around TB31 | Rescue/salvage effort continued through the night |
| 30 Aug 1914, 5 am | TB36 | TB31 grounded south-west of Inchkeith; collision mat and bottom line sent | Independent corroboration and direct assistance |
| 30 Aug 1914 | TB35 | Two TB31 torpedoes removed; TB31 refloated about 10 am and towed to Leith by *Malta*, *Jumbo* and *Vigilant* | Detailed contemporary account of refloating |
| 30 Aug 1914 | TB35 | TB31's torpedoes transferred into *Tyne's* boat and sent to *Tyne* | Strong practical evidence for the parent/depot relationship encoded in `Tyne (T.B.31)` |
| 1 Jul 1915 | TB36 | TB31 and TB32 operating in company; South Patrol at 13 knots | Active Forth patrol work |
| 28 Dec 1915 | TB36 | TB36 proceeded up harbour with TB28, TB31 and TB26 | TB31 still operating with the flotilla at Rosyth/Forth |
| 29 Feb 1916 | TB36 | C Patrol in company with TB27 and TB31 | Continued patrol service |
| 22 Jun 1916 | TB35 | C night patrol with TB32 and TB31, 10 knots and later 13 knots | Explicit night patrol five weeks before Albert left the posting |

The broad site-wide search did not produce further verifiable TB31 mentions in other Old Weather transcripts during Albert's service. That is not proof that none exist, but TB35 and TB36 are currently the productive online neighbouring logs.

### Organisational context

- Pre-war organisation material places **TB31 in the 8th Destroyer Flotilla at Chatham** before the war.
- Wartime listings place the flotilla in the **Firth of Forth**, with *Tyne* as depot ship.
- The surviving neighbouring logs put TB31 in operational company with other numbered boats around Parkeston, Rosyth, Queensferry, Inchkeith and the Forth.

The combined evidence makes the interpretation of `Tyne (T.B.31)` very strong: Albert was administratively borne through *Tyne* in connection with TB31, an operational torpedo boat of the flotilla.

## 5. TB31 contemporary press: the crew describes its own war

Repository source:
`sources/images/Media-coverage/tb31/Western_Times_29_October_1914_0002_Clip.jpg`

The *Western Times* of 29 October 1914 printed a letter dated **26 October 1914** from an officer of H.M. Torpedo Boat 31, signed **Harry M. L. Scott**, thanking the Mayoress of Exeter for warm clothing sent to the boat.

The officer wrote that a good proportion of the crew knew from former experience what **"winter in the North Sea means to those serving in torpedo craft"**, adding that even in peace the work was arduous and was more so at present.

This is unusually valuable evidence because it is:

- from **Albert's own boat**;
- written **during his posting**;
- contemporary, not retrospective;
- descriptive of the crew's physical service environment rather than merely the boat's movement.

## 6. Prince George, 1903-04

**Albert aboard:** 9 September 1903 to 14 July 1904.  
**Collision:** 17 October 1903.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/prince-george-ferrol/`

Eleven contemporary press clips were transcribed.

### What the contemporary coverage establishes

- The collision was with **HMS Hannibal** during Channel Fleet night manoeuvres on 17 October 1903.
- Several contemporary reports explicitly state that the ships were manoeuvring **with lights extinguished / without lights**.
- An Admiralty telegram printed in the press says *Hannibal* collided with *Prince George* during a **turning movement** and that *Prince George's* submerged torpedo flat filled with water.
- The same official message states **"No one hurt."**
- Reports place the collision off Cape Finisterre / in the approaches to Ferrol and describe the fleet putting into Ferrol after the accident.
- Contemporary reports describe severe flooding and continuous pumping. Early press accounts vary in their estimates of the amount of water and the precise damage, so those figures should not be harmonised into one false certainty.
- Temporary repairs were made at Ferrol. Later reporting says the ship returned to Portsmouth and was taken into dock.
- One later account describes collision quarters being sounded, crew going below to close remaining watertight compartments, collision mats being got out, and the fleet's carpenters constructing a copper dam against the damaged side.
- Captain Frederick L. Campbell of *Prince George* was tried by court-martial and **acquitted**.
- On 3 November the *Southern Daily Mail* reported that the First Lord of the Admiralty assembled *Prince George's* crew and conveyed a message from the King praising their calmness, courage and discipline after the collision.

### Narrative value

This is now a fully supported Albert episode: his service sheet has him aboard continuously across the collision, and contemporary British newspapers describe the event in substantial detail. ADM 53/24983 remains desirable later for exact log-level sequencing.

## 7. Jupiter, 1905-08

**Albert aboard:** 15 August 1905 to 3 February 1908.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/jupiter/`

Thirteen clips were accounted for.

### Key findings

- Contemporary papers report that *Jupiter* was recommissioned at Chatham on **15 August 1905** after a refit.
- Albert's service sheet has him joining *Jupiter* **that same day**.
- Therefore Albert joined *Jupiter* on the day of commissioning.
- *Jupiter* was intended for the Channel Fleet and later moved through Sheerness toward Portsmouth as her crew was completed.
- On 25 November 1905 the *Portsmouth Times and Naval Gazette* reported *Jupiter* at Spithead carrying out **"aim corrector" trials**.
- On 23 December 1905 the same paper reported that she had been at Spithead carrying out **aiming director trials**.
- This materially strengthens the existing archival lead to A. H. Pollen's reports on aim-correction trials aboard *Jupiter* in 1905-06.
- In January 1906 the ship was reported conducting further coal/furnace trials; one phrase in the source is too degraded to transcribe confidently.
- A sailor named **Corke** was reported killed aboard *Jupiter* at Berehaven after being jammed by a large block during operations.
- April 1906 reporting describes Channel Fleet mine experiments at Berehaven, including an E.M. minefield laid by *Jupiter*.
- June 1906 reporting describes *Jupiter* and *Ocean* off Portland taking part in experiments for transferring ammunition at sea from specially fitted pinnaces.
- An August 1905 Lloyd's List item also reports that the brigantine *Caroline* had been run into by *Jupiter*; exact incident date needs separate establishment before narrative use.

### Narrative value

The strongest material is not merely that Albert served on a battleship. He joined a newly recommissioned *Jupiter* on commissioning day and was aboard during a period of experimental fire-control work, mining exercises and ammunition-transfer trials.

## 8. Shannon, 1908-10

**Albert aboard:** 10 March 1908 to 28 February 1910.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/shannon/`

Four clips were transcribed.

### Key findings

- The *Western Mail*, 11 March 1908, says *Shannon* was commissioned at Chatham on **Tuesday 10 March 1908**.
- Albert's service sheet has him joining *Shannon* on **10 March 1908**.
- Therefore Albert again joined a ship **on the day she was commissioned**.
- *Shannon* was commissioned for the Fifth Cruiser Squadron of the Home Fleet at the Nore, as flagship of Rear-Admiral Callaghan.
- Contemporary descriptions call her the largest and most powerful cruiser then in commission and give a designed speed of 23 knots with four 9.2-inch and ten 7.5-inch guns.
- A March 1908 report lays out a programme of North Sea manoeuvres: gunnery tests at the Nore in May, a move to Queensferry for much of the summer, extensive exercises in June and July, and battle practice near the Moray Firth in August.
- On 21 March the Chatham press reported *Shannon* being taken into dock for final preparation for sea before joining the squadron.

### Narrative value

The exact commissioning-day overlap is strong and gives the beginning of the posting a clean chronological anchor. The manoeuvre programme provides a defensible operational setting for the months immediately following.

## 9. Powerful, 1911

**Albert aboard:** 16 September to 16 December 1911.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/powerful/`

Twenty source files were accounted for. Some coverage predates Albert's joining date and is context rather than an Albert event.

### Key findings within Albert's posting

- *Powerful* was flagship of the Australasian/Australian Squadron.
- Contemporary reporting places her at **Jervis Bay** preparing for battle practice.
- A naval correspondent reports *Powerful* and *Encounter*, accompanied by Australian destroyers *Yarra* and *Parramatta*, leaving Sydney for Port Phillip.
- On two nights the destroyers carried out simulated attacks on the cruisers. The cruisers were **manned and armed to resist destroyer attack, with all lights darkened**. Heavy weather prevented the destroyers from succeeding.
- *Powerful* was one of the ships scheduled actually to fire in the ensuing battle practice.
- Lloyd's and New Zealand reports trace her through Melbourne, Sydney, Auckland, Wellington and Lyttelton.
- She reached Lyttelton in early December 1911. Lieutenant H. H. L. Pennell of *Terra Nova* made an official call, and Admiral King-Hall subsequently visited Captain Scott's Antarctic expedition ship.
- Contemporary reporting states that *Drake* would replace *Powerful* as flagship and that *Powerful's* crew would be transferred to *Drake*.

### Narrative value

The October night destroyer attacks and battle practice are the standout event. They are exactly the sort of dated ship activity that can make Albert's service vivid without inventing his personal station.

## 10. Pioneer, 1911-12

**Albert aboard:** 17 December 1911 to 23 May 1912.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/pioneer/`

Nineteen clips were accounted for.

### New Zealand movements and training

The papers describe *Pioneer* as the Royal Naval Reserve warship / training ship for New Zealand, moving through Bluff, the West Coast Sounds, Dunedin/Timaru and Lyttelton, with periods devoted to reserve training.

### Search for Himitangi, March 1912

This is the strongest episode:

- The steamer *Himitangi* was overdue from the Chatham Islands.
- The New Zealand Prime Minister, Sir Joseph Ward, became involved in efforts to have *Pioneer* join the search.
- On 4 March *Pioneer* was reported taking aboard **350 tons of Westport coal** and preparing for sea.
- On **5 March 1912 at 2 p.m.**, she left her moorings at **Gladstone Pier**, Lyttelton, and sailed for the Chathams.
- The press emphasised her modern wireless installation and a stated radius of about 2,000 miles, allowing communication with passing ships and the Wellington post office.
- While at sea, *Pioneer* received word by wireless that *Himitangi* had been found stranded and that the search was no longer necessary.
- Commander Stopford turned the ship back.
- *Pioneer* returned to Lyttelton on **8 March**.

### Search for the dredger Manchester

In late April 1912, Admiralty reports said *Pioneer* and *Encounter* sailed from Auckland in search of the overdue dredger *Manchester*. A 4 May report still lacked definite news of their movements.

### Other texture

- A February report records a drunken melee involving sailors from *Pioneer* at Timaru, though only one man was identified and charged. This is shipboard/social context, not an Albert event.
- A March report records a cricket match between a *Pioneer* team and the Canterbury Druids at Sydenham Park.

### Narrative value

The *Himitangi* search is a particularly good Albert episode because the contemporary coverage provides fuel, departure time, pier, route, wireless capability, mid-ocean recall and return date.

## 11. Drake, 1912-13

**Albert aboard:** 24 May 1912 to 25 February 1913.  
**Repository folder:** `sources/images/Media-coverage/drake/`

Twenty-two source files were accounted for; one repository object is empty and cannot currently be transcribed.

### Australasian movements

- In July 1912 *Drake*, with the Admiral aboard, sailed from Sydney for a six-week island cruise.
- Contemporary reporting traces her via **Suva to Auckland**, where she arrived on 24 July.
- The Auckland coverage says the crew was preparing for **big-gun firing at Jervis Bay**.
- Later reports trace *Drake* through Auckland, Wellington, Picton and planned movements via Milford Sound.
- By late 1912 she was clearly being treated as the last Imperial flagship of the old Australian Station arrangement before the new Commonwealth naval order.

### Homeward voyage, January-February 1913

- *Drake* sailed from Sydney for Britain at the beginning of January 1913.
- A detailed naval report describes crowds at Farm Cove, the band playing as the ship slipped her moorings, and seamen aboard returning cheers from *Psyche* and *Pyramus*.
- *Drake* later left Australian waters and reached **Colombo on 30 January 1913**.
- The *Ceylon Observer* gives exceptional physical detail: very great heat between Fremantle and Colombo, **several stokers fainting**, and men working below having a trying time.
- The same report describes a **crossing-the-line ceremony** for recruits, involving a Father Neptune figure, mock prescriptions, paint, coal and ducking.
- *Drake* resumed her voyage to Europe and arrived at **Plymouth on 21 February 1913**, then proceeded to Portsmouth to pay off.
- Albert remains on the service sheet until 25 February, so he is borne aboard throughout this homeward voyage.

### Narrative value

This gives the end of Albert's Australasian period real physical texture: island cruising, gunnery preparation, the last Imperial-flagship moment, tropical heat below decks, Colombo, and the final return to Britain.

## 15. Research implications for Chapters 20-22

The Navy material no longer needs to read as a sequence of ship names. We can now give Albert a documented service arc:

**boy sailor -> major battleship collision -> commissioning-day battleship service and experimental gunnery -> commissioning-day modern cruiser service -> Australasian fleet exercises -> New Zealand search operations -> return from the Australian Station -> small torpedo-boat wartime patrols and grounding -> later fleet action on Courageous -> North Russia on Alexander -> invalided home in 1919.**

That is an interpretive structure, not prose. Each individual scene still needs to be built only from the service sheet plus the dated ship evidence.
