# William Gouldstone at Broadmoor: the 1891, 1901 and 1911 censuses

**Images (added by Rik, 24 October 2026):**
- sources/images/census-stuff/William Gouldstone 1891 census.jpg: RG 12/1008, page 12. "RETURN of all the PERSONS who
  SLEPT or ABODE in this INSTITUTION on the NIGHT of SUNDAY, APRIL 5th, 1891." Berkshire, Easthampstead district,
  Sandhurst civil parish, Crowthorne ecclesiastical parish (FreeCEN, piece 1008). The institution is not named on
  this page; every entry is "Patient", "Lunatic".
- sources/images/census-stuff/William Gouldstone 1901 census.jpg: RG 13/1164, page 8. "LIST of all PERSONS who SLEPT
  or ABODE in this INSTITUTION on the NIGHT of SUNDAY, MARCH 31st, 1901." The institution is not named on this page;
  every entry is "Inmate", "Lunatic".
That both are Broadmoor rests on the place (Crowthorne, Sandhurst), the run of patients, William's own entries, and
the same men in the same order on both pages (below). The cover pages naming the institution are NOT held.

## Notes
- **Married** on both nights. On 5 April 1891 Elizabeth was entered at 41 Harbour Street, Whitstable, as William
  Madams's wife (Chapter Eighteen); on 31 March 1901, at Middle Wall, as his wife again.
- **Age:** 34 in April 1891 is right (born 10 October 1856). 45 in March 1901 is a year over (he was 44). 64 in June
  1921 is right.
- **Birthplace:** "Great Sanford" (1891), "Great Sandford" or similar (1901), "Gt. Sanford" (1921): the asylum's
  spelling of Great Sampford, carried from one census to the next.
- **1901 gives initials only** for every inmate on the page ("I.H.R.", "C.C.", ... "W. G."). 1891 gives full names.
  The 1911 return may also be by initials: search by age, trade and birthplace, not name.
- **The same men, in the same order.** The patients listed above William in 1891 reappear, by their initials, ages
  and birthplaces, in the same order above him in 1901: Matthew Jackson Hunter (M. J. H., Skipton), James Lawrence
  (J. L.), John Mobbs (J. M., Norfolk), George Stratton (G. S., Ivinghoe), Luke Carter (L. C., Chedgeley),
  Arthur Saml Smith (A. S. S., Marylebone), Jno Norris Saunders (J. N. S.), Thomas Luck (T. L., Weston Favell),
  Jas Albert Wallis (J. A. W., Little Chart), Zebedee Abbott (Z. A.), Henry White (H. W., Bethersden), John Robert
  Price (J. R. P., Stockport), Richard Cleeve (R. C., Hockworthy), James Porter (J. P., Swaffham), William Farley
  (W. F., Liverpool), H. Powell Easton (H. P. E.), George Jas Bland (G. J. B., Clipston), Charles Geo. Rutt (C. G. R.),
  then William. The list is in admission order, it seems: ten years on, he is still among the same men. (Reading of
  the order only; the other men's names are NOT for prose without a check, and are not needed.)
- For 1911 (RG 14): look for "W. G.", blacksmith, about 54, born Essex, after "C. G. R." in the same run.

## Across all four censuses (added 25 October 2026, with the 1921 page)
The 1921 page (transcribed/Broadmoor_1921_census_William_Gouldstone_transcription.md; read whole 25 October for the
names in column (a) and birthplaces in (f); rows 1 to 9 NOT checked word by word) gives the patients in full again. Its
last four rows, above William at row 10, include two men who are on every page held, 1891 to 1921:
- **Thomas Luck**, born Weston Favell, Northamptonshire: 1891 in full ("Clerk (Commercial)", single); 1901 "T. L.",
  38; 1911 "T. L.", 48, "Clerk (Commercial)"; 1921 in full, 57, single.
- **Henry White**, born Bethersden, near Ashford, Kent: 1891 in full ("Hawker formerly a soldier", single); 1901
  "H. W.", 50, "Hawker"; 1911 "H. W.", 60, "Hawker, formerly Soldier"; 1921 in full, 69, single.
Two more on the 1911 and 1921 pages only (the 1891 and 1901 pages held do not reach them): Joseph Waller (St Mary's
Cray, Kent: 1911 "J. W.", 54, labourer; 1921, 63) and William Jones (Whitehaven, Cumberland: 1911 "W. J.", 72,
physician; 1921, 81).
The matches rest on initials, age (ten years on each time) and birthplace together: strong. What the listing shows is
that they were enumerated together, census after census; it does not show where they slept, or that they knew one
another. Before any of these names is quoted in prose: each line checked word by word on its image.
