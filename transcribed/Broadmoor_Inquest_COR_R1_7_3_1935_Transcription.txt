[typed]
Berks to wit

DEPOSITIONS of WITNESSES taken and acknowledged on behalf of Our Sovereign Lord The King touching
the death of — WILLIAM GOULDSTONE — at BROADMOOR CRIMINAL LUNATIC ASYLUM CROWTHORNE in the County
aforesaid on Monday the 21st day of January 1935 Before ROBERT SIDNEY PAYNE, Gentleman, One of His
Majesty's Coroners for the said County on view of the body of the said person then and there lying
dead.

[handwritten, first deposition]
Kenneth Oswald [?] Milne M.D. on oath saith:- I am assistant Medical Officer at Broadmoor Asylum.
Deceased Wm [the writer first began "Goldstone", struck it through, and wrote] Gouldstone aged 78
years at time of death was admitted here on 15 October 1883 from Chelmsford Prison. He lived
formerly at 2 Helen Cottages, Queens Road, West Walthamstow, Essex: he was a married man & a
blacksmith by occupation. He was charged with wilful murder & was tried at C. Crim Court on
Sept 10th 1883: found Guilty & sentenced to death: this sentence was "respited": on admission he
was suffering from confusional insanity & later became demented. He has been in poor bodily health
for the last six [?] years: during the last 3 months he gradually became weaker & died in the
Infirmary on 19 instant: at 12.5 am. A p.m. was made & showed the cause of death to be hypostatic
pneumonia following myocardial degeneration.
                                                        [signed] K. Milne.

[handwritten, second deposition]
Edward John Priest on oath saith:- I am an Attendant at Broadmoor Asylum. I was on duty when
Deceased died in the Infirmary at 12.5 am on the 19th inst. He received every attention & was
regularly seen by the Medical Officers.
                                                        [signed] E. J. Priest.
[signed, lower left] Robert S. Payne, Coroner
[endorsed, lower right] COR/R1/7/3/[3?]

NOTES ON THE TRANSCRIPTION — what is new here, and what needs checking
================================================================================

1. TIME OF DEATH. 12.5 a.m., 19 January 1935, in the Infirmary. The project has carried only the date.
   Both the typed slip and Milne's deposition give the time, and Priest swore he was on duty at it.

2. "CONFUSIONAL INSANITY & LATER BECAME DEMENTED." This is the only medical characterisation of
   William's fifty-one years in Broadmoor currently available to the project, and it sits OUTSIDE the
   sealed patient file. "Confusional insanity" was a recognised diagnostic category of the period,
   broadly an acute psychosis with clouded consciousness, and is consistent with Savage's trial
   evidence about slowness of appreciation. TO CHECK before use in prose: the term's contemporary
   definition, from Savage's own Insanity and Allied Neuroses or the Journal of Mental Science.

3. "TRIED AT C. CRIM COURT ON SEPT 10th 1883" IS WRONG. The trial was Friday 14 September 1883
   (Old Bailey transcript; The Times, 15 September). Milne, writing in 1935, is four days out. He was
   presumably copying from the admission papers or the case file. LOG AS A CONTRADICTION. It matters
   for two reasons: it is direct evidence that the Broadmoor file's own summary data contains errors,
   which is worth knowing in advance of 2036; and it is a warning against treating anything in these
   papers as independent testimony about 1883. Milne never met the William Gouldstone of 1883.

4. "BLACKSMITH", TWICE, AND ON THE TYPED SLIP. Both the inquisition and Milne's deposition give his
   trade as blacksmith, not whitesmith. Graves swore at the Old Bailey: "When he first came to work for
   this firm he was a blacksmith; we then made him a whitesmith." Broadmoor recorded the trade he
   arrived with, and kept it for fifty-one years.

5. "WIDOWER." The typed slip calls him a widower. He and Elizabeth were never divorced, so he was in
   law her widower from 29 June 1906. Her own death certificate, five weeks short of twenty-nine years
   earlier, calls her "Widow of William Madams, General Labourer" — a man she could not marry because
   she was still married to this one. Each certificate records the other's survival under a different
   man's name. Both are accurate. Neither is true.

6. "HE RECEIVED EVERY ATTENTION." Priest's formula is routine and appears at asylum inquests
   everywhere; it should not be read as a statement about this patient in particular. Noted here only
   because the sentence exists, sworn, in the file.

7. THE ADDRESS. "2 Helen Cottages, Queens Road, West Walthamstow" appears here twice. Queens Road did
   not exist under that name in 1883 (see session-note-2026-06-28), so this is either the address as
   later understood or a 1935 clerk's rendering of it. Already flagged in the notes; this transcription
   does not resolve it.

8. THE CORONER. Robert Sidney Payne, of Weedon & Payne, solicitors, 17 Market Place, Reading. The
   firm's own envelope, postmarked 1926, was reused as the wrapper for the papers nine years later.

9. "RESPITED" IS IN INVERTED COMMAS in Milne's own hand. He is quoting a word from the file rather
   than using it himself. Small, but it is the only visible trace in these papers of the 1883 documents
   that will not open until 2036.

10. THE SIXTH YEAR. "in poor bodily health for the last six years" — the numeral word is not certain
    and could be "nine". Do not use a figure in prose without re-checking the manuscript.

SEPARATE ITEM: "Broadmoor admisision register.jpg" (same folder) — IDENTIFICATION CORRECTED
================================================================================

The repository filename is misleading. This is NOT a Broadmoor admission register. It is an
alphabetical register of criminal lunatics covering many institutions: the columns are No. in order of
Admission, Name, Private/Pauper (M/F), Date of Admission 1883, ASYLUM, and Date of Discharge or Death,
and the Asylum column on this page alone names Lincoln, Leicester, Notts, S. York, Berks, Lancaster,
Wandsworth, Prestwich, Somerset, Abergavenny, Dorset, Durham, Sussex, City of London, Stafford, Colney,
Oxford, Gloucester, Burntwood, Hanwell, Rainhill and Broadmoor. Very likely a Lunacy Commission or Home
Office register. TO IDENTIFY properly: the series and repository are not recorded anywhere in the notes.

William's entry, surnames under G:
  No. 4953 | Gouldstone, Wm | pauper, male [with a superscript "c" above the tick, presumably
  "criminal"] | 15 Oct [1883] | Broadmoor | 19.1.35 | ticked in the "Died" column

Worth noting, and observable rather than inferred: on this page every other entry closes between 1883
and 1893, with three exceptions running to 1900, 1906 and 1911. William's "19.1.35" is the latest on
the page by twenty-four years, and it is written in a visibly larger, different hand from the entries
around it. Someone came back to that book half a century after the page was filled in, to close his
line.
