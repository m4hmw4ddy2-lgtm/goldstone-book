The attendance passage, verbatim:
"The following report of the School Attendance Committee was read, and legal proceedings were authorised as
recommended:—"The attendance officer reported that legal proceedings had been taken as authorised by the Board, and
that the cases were heard on July 29th. An attendance order was made in the case of Herbert Waters, and a fine of
5s. was inflicted in each case in resp[ect] of Edward Olive, Arthur Butcher, Hubert Bean, Ernest Bean, and Albert
Madams. The Committee have ordered final warnings to be served on the parents of Edward and Thomas Webb, and first
warnings on the parents of several other children. The Committee recommend that legal proceedings be taken for
irregular or non-attendance in the cases of Arthur and Robert Butcher, Edward and William Castle, Thos. Webb, Edward
Shinglston [?], and Barker Ramsey.""

DATES (calendar computation): the meeting "on Tuesday evening last" = Tuesday 15 August 1899; "the cases were heard on
July 29th" = Saturday 29 July 1899 (the court is not named; a Saturday, as St Augustine's Petty Sessions sat in 1901).

WHAT IT GIVES: in respect of Albert Madams's irregular attendance, a fine of five shillings, imposed at a hearing on
29 July 1899. Not named: on whom it fell (the proceedings run "against the parents" in the Board's practice, June
1898; this item names the children only), the court, or whether it was paid. Albert was twelve.
NOT THE SAME ROUND AS JUNE 1898: those proceedings (Whitstable Times, 25 June 1898) were ordered a year earlier; this
is the 1899 round "authorised by the Board". Whether the 1898 order led to a hearing is still not held.
Arthur Butcher is in both lists (1898 and 1899).
Wanted: the court's own report of 29 July 1899 (Whitstable Times, 5 August 1899; petty sessions), which may name the
parent who answered.
