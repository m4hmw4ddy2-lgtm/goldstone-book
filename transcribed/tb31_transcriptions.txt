COMFORTS AT THE FRONT

Acknowledgements Received by the
Mayoress of Exeter

Sometimes doubts have been expressed as to whether parcels sent to the front ever reach their destination. “You say you have forwarded twelve cases of comforts to the 1st Devons,” say the doubters; “How do you know the troops get them?” Fortunately the Mayoress of Exeter has direct evidence, which we are sure will not only give great satisfaction to those who have assisted her, but will also stimulate further generosity.

The evidence is supplied by two letters, which we quote below.

The communication from the Military Forwarding Office at the Overseas Base is a typewritten form, and therefore does not actually specify the 1st Devons, but the cases of comforts went forward properly directed, and doubtless reached the troops to whom they were addressed:—

Military Forwarding Office,
British Expeditionary Force,
Overseas Base,
24th October, 1914.

VARIOUS PACKAGES COMFORTS.

Dear Madam,—I wish to thank you on behalf of the officers and men of the British Expeditionary Force for the above-mentioned gifts, and have pleasure in informing you that same were very much appreciated, and have been distributed.

I am, Madam,
Your obedient servant,
W. E. PIPER, Lieut.
for Military Forwarding Officer.

Mayoress of Exeter,
Exeter.

The second letter is from an officer of Torpedo Boat 31, and needs no comment.

H.M. Torpedo Boat 31,
26th October, 1914.

Dear Madam,—Allow me to convey to you, and through you, to the other ladies who so kindly contributed, the heartiest thanks of the officers and ship’s company of this vessel for the very generous present of warm clothing that you sent us, and which was received safely. It is most deeply appreciated, more especially as a good proportion of us know from former experience what winter in the North Sea means to those serving in torpedo craft, even in peace time, when the work is naturally somewhat less arduous than at present. Will you please accept the very best wishes of all “31’s”?

I remain,
Yours very truly,
HARRY M. L. SCOTT.

The Mayoress of Exeter.

The point we would emphasise is that comforts despatched from a recognised depot like the Mayoress’ Depot, Exeter, stand an infinitely better chance of getting through than odd parcels sent by individuals.
