TRANSCRIPTION

ENTERTAINMENT AT THE WORKMEN'S HALL, WALTHAMSTOW.

On Tuesday evening a variety entertainment was given by the Club Minstrels in aid of Mrs. Gouldstone, who, through
the sad misfortune that has befallen her, has been left in a destitute condition. For this object the hall was
lent free of charge, and the fact of it being crowded with a very appreciative audience, showed that the occasion
commended itself to the goodwill of many people in the neighbourhood. The programme comprised minstrel songs and
choruses, plantation dances, clog hornpipes, walk round, handbell ringing, and a comic sketch, entitled "Shaving."

In the first part eleven of the coloured minstrels appeared, including Mr. Cartwright (interlocutor), Mr. C. Baker
(bones), Mr. F. Kimber (tambo), and Messrs. R. Barrington, J. Fortune, W. Bale, A. Fleming. Mrs Cartwright
presided at the pianoforte, the band comprising violins, banjo, cornopean, piccolo, and concertina. The
handbell-ringers were Messrs. Scarlet, Single, and Matthews, and the airs they rendered were "The young recruit,"
"Soldier's joy," and "Blue bells of Scotland." The clog dancers were Messrs. Fleming and Godbeer. Mr. C. Baker was
also distinguished as a dancer, also Mr. Barrington. Their impersonation of a couple of "Funny old gals" was very
amusing. Mr. and Mrs. Melrose, Mr. T. Weller, Mr. Bellinger, Mr. G. Hailey, Mr. Barton, and Mr. Wesley took part in
the entertainment; and as the whole of them gave their services, it would be invidious to make any distinction;
they combined for a good object, and were successful.
