### B. Why the twelve years began on his eighteenth birthday
- THE SHEET ITSELF: "19 June [190]4 - 12 Years", on the day the sheet makes his eighteenth birthday; Ordinary Seaman the
  same day. The coincidence of the date is the record's own evidence.
- PRIMARY (rule of the period, two witnesses either side of 1901):
  (1) Admiralty, "Regulations under which Boys are entered in Her Majesty's Navy", 11 June 1868 (transcription on
  RootsWeb, pbtyc "Navy List 1870" pages; the transcriber names no source): "All Boys who enter the Navy must
  understand that they are bound to serve continuously for ten years from the time of their attaining the age of 18".
  TEN years in 1868.
  (2) Recruiting broadside, "Wanted for the Royal Navy, Stout Healthy Boys, of Good Character, from 15 to 16 1/2 Years
  of Age" (London: W. P. Griffith and Sons, 1888): boys "must sign an engagement to serve for 12 years from the date of
  attaining the age of 18". Known only from a dealer's description (Sky Duthie Rare Books, PBFA listing): the
  broadside itself not seen.
  (3) Later, for the same rule still standing: IWM PST 2767, interwar recruiting poster (Lavery): "First engagement 12
  years from age of 18."
- NOT FOUND: the regulation in force in 1901-1904 itself (King's Regulations and Admiralty Instructions; the Navy List's
  boys' entry page for 1901). The 1888 and interwar texts bracket it and agree. ASSESSMENT: safe as fact that a boy's
  twelve-year engagement ran from his eighteenth birthday; best citation the 1888 broadside if the image or a period
  Navy List page can be obtained. A TNA guide line ("forms that were signed by boys, agreeing to serve ten years once
  they turn 18") describes the earlier, ten-year form.
