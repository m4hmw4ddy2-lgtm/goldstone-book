## SPAN AND QUESTION

1833 to July 1858. Under B: what did each of them carry into 8 Courtenay Place? For Elizabeth, this chapter is the
answer to "loss": she is born into the aftermath of six deaths and loses two more half-brothers before she is three.
For William, it is only his birth into a working forge, and the parallel it sets up.

## SHAPE (three movements)

1. **The certificate.** Open, as ruled, on Elizabeth's birth and its registration. NEW dated moment: Thomas
   registering her on the thirtieth of March, six weeks after the birth, and making his mark. Her mother's name "went
   down in full", and the three names lead straight into who Jemima had been.
2. **What the names carried.** Jemima's first family, told forward from 1833 as background (the one step back the
   chapter takes, allowed before the eighth): the wedding, the seven children, the six deaths, the 1851 household, the
   second wedding with Smee at the register again. Largely the present Chapter Two's run, which is strong; tightened
   under G, and Edward Suckling cut to a clause (DECIDE B).
3. **Two births.** Back to 1855 and forward: Nathan's death in July 1856, with Elizabeth seventeen months old. Then,
   a few miles north, William's birth in October 1856 and Emily registering it herself (the forge, the mill, the Cock
   Inn; "at least the fourth generation" in one sentence, under E). The chapter ends on George's burial in July 1858:
   of Jemima's eight, only Emma left. That hands to Chapter Two, which opens on census night 1861 with Emma at home.

## DECIDE POINTS

**A. The registration as the opening moment.** (a) Open on the birth, then the registration six weeks later, with
Thomas's mark; (b) as the present draft, birth and names only.
RECOMMEND (a): it is a dated act by a person, and the mark sets up the schooling and the 1879 signatures later.

**B. Edward Suckling** (blind, out of work, a parish pauper by 1861; Malting Yard). Bears on Elizabeth only as a
grandfather she may never have met (no source shows contact).
RECOMMEND: one clause, or the notes.

**C. The parallel registrations.** Thomas Stock registers Elizabeth with his mark; Emily Gouldstone registers William
herself. Whether Emily signed or made her mark in 1856 needs the certificate image (the present prose says only that
she "registered the birth herself"). If she signed, the two registrations can sit side by side with no comment.
CHECKED 19 October 2026 (sources/images/birth-certificates/William Gouldstone Birth Cert.jpg, informant column
only, cropped): "Emily Gouldstone / Mother / Great Sampford", with no mark, registered "Third November 1856", Henry
Caslake, Registrar. The next entry on the same page begins "X", so the copy records marks where they were made: on
this certificate Emily signed. (It is the register's copy hand, not her own signature.) Agrees with her signing the
1879 register.
RECOMMEND: set the two registrations side by side, his mark and her name, with no comment.

**D. Elizabeth Carter, "the same witness".** The present Chapter Two's title rests on Smee at both weddings; Carter
at five deathbeds is the stronger repetition. Title to be settled when the chapter is done (plan: titles last).
RECOMMEND: keep both facts; no comment on either.

**E. The ending.** (a) George's burial, July 1858, "only Emma was left"; (b) William's registration, November 1856.
RECOMMEND (a): it ends on Elizabeth's side and points at the next chapter's census night without saying so.
