## 2. RESOLVED — JEMIMA'S PARENTAGE (SUCKLING, NOT ANSELL)

### The problem
Existing MASTER_NOTES stated Jemima's parents as "Edward Suckling (1784–1864), Sarah Crawley (1790–1870), married 23 September 1810" with no traceable primary source. The 1854 Stock/Suckling marriage register itself lists her father as "Edward **Ansell**," directly contradicting this.

### The resolution — confirmed by convergent primary sources
1. **Jemima's own first marriage**, as bride, in her birth name, before any married surname existed to confuse a clerk: *Jemima Suckling* (also indexed elsewhere as "Sutling," "Luckling," all spelling variants of the same clerk's handwriting) married John Ansell, 25 December 1833, Wethersfield, St Mary Magdalene. Archive ref D/P 119/1/9. Confirmed independently in two separate marriage indexes.
2. **Baptism**: Jemima, baptised 15 December 1811, Hornchurch, Essex. Father Edwd. Suckling, mother Sarah. FHL Film 857075.
3. **Edward Suckling traced across four censuses** (1841, 1851, 1861, 1871 — the 1871 return is Jemima's own, confirming Hornchurch birthplace a second independent time): consistent household, consistent ages, wife Sarah (b. Cranham, Essex), son Robert (11 in 1841, matching "Robert Suckling," witness at Jemima's 1854 wedding to Thomas Stock — her own brother).
4. Edward's occupation shifts from Ag Lab (1841) to Gardener (1851) to Pauper (1861), alongside Sarah, both blind/elderly and on parish relief by 1861 — Malting Yard, Finchingfield.

### Conclusion
The 1854 marriage register's "father: Edward Ansell" is a **clerical conflation** — the clerk recorded Jemima's married surname (from her first husband, John Ansell) in the field asking for her father's name. A known and understandable error for a widow remarrying. It is not evidence of alternative parentage. **Suckling is confirmed as her birth surname.**

### Corrected entry for MASTER_NOTES / confirmed facts
> Jemima Suckling, baptised 15 December 1811, Hornchurch, Essex. Father Edward Suckling (b. Barking, Essex, c.1785–87; Gardener, later a parish pauper; d. after 1861), mother Sarah (b. Cranham, Essex). Family relocated from the Barking/Hornchurch area to Finchingfield, Essex, sometime between December 1811 and 1841. Jemima married (1) John Ansell, 25 December 1833, Wethersfield, St Mary Magdalene (D/P 119/1/9); widowed 19 April 1845 (John Ansell d. age 32, buried St Mary Magdalene, Wethersfield); married (2) Thomas Stock, 16 June 1854, same church (D/P 119/1/10). Witnesses at the second marriage: her brother Robert Suckling, and Frederick Smee — who had also witnessed her first wedding, 21 years earlier.

## 3. RESOLVED — JEMIMA'S FIRST MARRIAGE HAD SEVEN CHILDREN, NOT ONE

### The problem
Existing project notes state Jemima had "one son, Arthur Ansell" from her first marriage. The 1841 census shows John Ansell (28) and Jemima (29) in her father-in-law's household at Wethersfield, alongside **six children**: John (7), Eliza (6), Sarah (6), Alfred (3), George (2), Emma (8 months) — no Arthur present at all.

### The resolution
- **Arthur Ansell**, baptised 9 July 1843, Wethersfield, St Mary Magdalene (D/P 119/1/5), parents John Ansell and Jemima — born after the 1841 census and before the 1851 census.
- Arthur **died in infancy**: buried 29 April 1844, age 0, same church (D/P 119/1/7).
- This explains his total absence from both censuses: he lived and died entirely within the nine-year gap between them.
- **John Ansell (husband/father) died 19 April 1845, age 32** (b. c.1813), buried St Mary Magdalene, Wethersfield — one year and nine days after his infant son.
- **1851 census** (Jemima now widowed, Dog Chase, Wethersfield, straw plaiter): only three of the surviving six children remain with her — John Nathan (16, errand boy), George (11, scholar), Emma (8, scholar).
- **Eliza, Sarah, and Alfred are unaccounted for after 1841.** By 1851 they would be approximately 16, 16, and 13. No death or service record found this session. Genuinely open — see Section 5.
- **1861 census**: Emma Ansell (20, straw plaiter, unmarried) is living in Jemima and Thomas Stock's household at Brewery House, Wethersfield, recorded as Thomas's "daughter-in-law" — a known period usage for step-daughter, not a spouse. This is Jemima's own daughter Emma, correctly still at home. (Also present: John Ansell Sr, 85, blind, pauper, Thomas's "father-in-law" — actually Jemima's first father-in-law, carried into her second household.)

### Conclusion
**"One son, Arthur" in the existing notes was a real fragment of information, badly misapplied** — Arthur existed, but was one of seven children, not the only one, and died as an infant eleven years before Elizabeth Stock was even born. The claim should be corrected, not simply deleted.

### Corrected entry for MASTER_NOTES / dramatis personae
> Jemima's first marriage to John Ansell (25 December 1833 – his death 19 April 1845) produced seven children: John Nathan, Eliza, Sarah, Alfred, George, Arthur (b. 9 July 1843, d. in infancy 1844), and Emma. Three (John Nathan, George, Emma) remained with Jemima through her widowhood and are traceable in the 1851 and 1861 censuses; Emma was still living with Jemima and Thomas Stock in 1861, recorded under the period convention of "daughter-in-law" (i.e., step-daughter). Eliza, Sarah, and Alfred are unaccounted for after the 1841 census — not confirmed dead, not yet traced elsewhere. Elizabeth Stock Gouldstone therefore had at minimum six known half-siblings on her mother's side from this earlier marriage, not one.

## 4. NEW DETAIL — JEMIMA AND THOMAS'S DEATHS, 1873

Existing notes treat this as "died within weeks of each other." The actual death certificates give a fuller, more specific picture:

- **Jemima**: died 25 October 1873. Cause given as "Neuralgia, 6 weeks; Congestion of the Brain, 3 weeks."
- **Thomas**: died 22 November 1873, five weeks after Jemima. Cause given as "Bronchitis, Chronic, 12 months."

This means Thomas had been visibly and slowly ill for a full year before either death — not a sudden double blow, but a household in which the husband was already failing when the wife's own final illness began in the autumn of 1873, worsening over six weeks into what the certificate calls congestion of the brain. Thomas outlived her by five weeks. ("Congestion of the brain" is a Victorian catch-all term — do not render this in prose as a specific modern diagnosis such as "stroke"; state only what the certificate says.)

Elizabeth would have been about 18 in 1873 and, per the existing Stoke Newington service record (1871 census, age 16, general servant), quite possibly already living away from the household when this happened.

## 8. METHODOLOGY NOTES FOR FUTURE SESSIONS

- **1841 census**: ages 15+ rounded down to nearest multiple of 5 (a recorded "55" means 55–59); relationships to head were not recorded at all and are software-inferred from surname/sex/position in later transcriptions — treat as hypothesis, not fact, until corroborated by a later census; birthplace was only recorded as same-county Y/N, not specific parish — any parish-level birthplace attached to an 1841 record has been carried over from a different census year.
- **1851 census onward**: relationships, marital status, and specific parish of birth were recorded directly — a much stronger source for verifying household structure than 1841.
- **When two transcriptions of the same document disagree on an age or detail** (this session: Ellen Suckling age 11 vs 4 in 1841; Daniel Suckling age 3 vs 13 in 1851; George/Emma Ansell age in years vs months in 1841), check the original image before trusting either transcription, and where the image isn't available, test the disputed reading against the document's own internal pattern (e.g., Daniel's blank occupation field only makes sense at age 3, not 13, given every other child that age has an occupation or "Scholar" listed).
- **"Daughter-in-law" / "father-in-law" in Victorian census returns** were frequently used for step-relations, not only the modern legal meaning — confirmed useful in resolving Emma Ansell's 1861 listing.
