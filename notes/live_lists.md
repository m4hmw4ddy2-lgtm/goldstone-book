## 6. NOT EVIDENCE — USABLE ONLY AS PRESS (under the press rule, project instructions)
Devon_Evening_Express_Aug_9_1883.txt — confirmed fabricated as an account of events. Has William holding the hammer at the door (contradicts Ada's sworn "nothing in his hands") and has the boys' bodies found submerged rather than on the kitchen floor; also Frederick "laughed at him", "kissed and caressed", and the second twin dead at eleven. Never a source of fact. May be quoted as what one paper printed. It is a separate, more lurid text from the wire dispatch below, and the IPN of 18 August reprints its closing paragraph.

The 9 August wire dispatch. Runs verbatim in the Western Daily Press, the Guardian Journal Nottingham and the Evening Gazette Middlesbrough, with a shorter related version in the Telegraph's first report, and is ONE agency report reprinted, not three independent sources. Demonstrably unreliable throughout: it has Ada discovering the three bodies in the cistern (the identical error that condemned the Devon Evening Express file); it has William in "a very bad mood" against Ada's sworn evidence that he smiled and that she noticed nothing in his manner; it has William asking Ada to leave the house so he could discuss the doctor's money with his wife, when leaving was Ada's own suggestion and the money conversation happened after the murders with a constable in the room; and it says he had not spoken to his wife since her confinement, when the coroner deposition says he came to see her every night. It is the SOLE source for Frederick appearing "pleased at this parental recognition" when his father picked him up. That detail was cut from Chapter Seven on 20 September 2026 as fact and is not to be reinstated in any scene. Under the press rule it may be quoted as what the papers printed. Its own text dates it to the night of 8–9 August, between the two twins' deaths ("the death of the other is hourly expected").

The Spectator, 25 August 1883, on Cole ("Murders of the deliberate kind appear to breed each other"; Cole "had been reading the story of the Walthamstow case all the Sunday"). Not supported by any witness at Cole's trial. Usable in Chapter Thirteen as what the Spectator printed, named and dated; wording to be checked against the page image before quoting.

Shared copy: several papers print one reporter's or one agency's text. The Daily Chronicle (13 Aug) and Dover Express (17 Aug) are one text for the Saturday hearing; the IPN and the Walthamstow Guardian (both 18 Aug) share copy for the same hearing; the Dover Express and the Guardian share copy for the Monday inquest and part of the funeral; the Bury Free Press (23 Aug) reprints The Times (20 Aug) word for word for the committal of 18 August; the IPN and the Guardian (both 25 Aug) share copy for the committal itself, and the Daily News of 20 August prints an abridged setting of the same copy for Cheeseman, Gould and the Wells/Batchelor exchange. Count shared copy as one witness, not several.
