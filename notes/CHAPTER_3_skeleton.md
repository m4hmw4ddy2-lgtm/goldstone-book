## SPAN AND QUESTION
April 1871 to 1878. Both children out of their parents' houses: Elizabeth in service in London, alone after 1873;
William at home, then working with Robert, hurt; the old smith in the workhouse; the father gone from the directory.

## DECIDE POINTS
A. SHAPE. The chapter is short because the record is. (a) Keep as its own chapter; (b) MERGE with Chapter Four
   (1878-79: William to London, Graves, Elizabeth at Frampton Park Road, the wedding) into one chapter running from
   both children leaving home to their meeting under one roof and marrying. RECOMMEND (b): one arc, about 1,500-1,800
   words, and no run of short chapters.
B. MABEL. Her mother on the certificate is "Emily Gouldstone", no father; that she was William's sister's child is an
   inference (strong: Emily senior was married; the 1901 census has Mabel a granddaughter). Draft states the
   certificate and then "William's sister Emily was twenty-one, and unmarried", leaving the reader to join them.
   RECOMMEND as drafted; or (b) say it outright on the 1901 evidence.
C. THE GAP. One rationed line: "Nothing yet found places Elizabeth anywhere from the census of 1871 until she appears
   again, in Hackney." RECOMMEND keep (the missing record is the point; dramatis_personae: "state as an open gap").
D. ROBERT AND THE ACCIDENT. Facts only, no trial words; "wished himself dead" and the hidden knives held for the trial.
   RECOMMEND as drafted.
E. THE ENDING. The 1878 directory: George Wright and Robert Gouldstone the blacksmiths; Thomas not in it. The reader may
   take this George Wright for the buyer of the forge in 1867 (Ch2); that he is the same man is NOT shown. RECOMMEND
   keep, with this flagged; or (b) end on Joseph's burial.
F. LEFT OUT, to the notes: James's beerhouse licence (Great Bardfield, 1874); the Raven tomb and the Raven children
   (ruling D); the St James train and the lockout (D); the population figures for 1881 (they belong to the 1881
   census, in a later chapter); the plait collapse until it has a source; Chilton boys' later careers; the generic
   account of service.
