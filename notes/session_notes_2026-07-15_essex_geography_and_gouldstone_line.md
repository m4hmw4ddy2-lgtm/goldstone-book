## PART ONE: VILLAGE GEOGRAPHY

All three villages were checked against the same OS sheet layer (1892–1914, per Rik's confirmation) and cross-checked against modern maps and Street View where noted. This layer post-dates the 1840s–1890s family residence in all three villages, so building-level detail should be treated as a good proxy for the Victorian layout, not as period-contemporary proof. Named institutions (churches, working mills, breweries) are more reliable across this gap than individual building footprints.

### WETHERSFIELD (Stock/Suckling side — Jemima and Thomas Stock's own household)

- **Church**: St Mary Magdalene, with adjoining Vicarage. Confirmed via OS as the most stable, unchanged part of the village — Rik's own overlay check found that "all the buildings" around the church match between the historic OS sheet and the modern map. This is the anchor point for the whole village.
- **Brewery House** (Thomas & Jemima Stock's 1861 address): Wethersfield had a real, working brewery, run by the **Raven family** — confirmed in White's 1863 trade directory: "Thomas Raven, brewer and maltster," with the family also farming a holding called Russell's. The maltings building survives today as **Wethersfield Village Hall**. On the OS sheet, "Brewery" is labelled plainly on the southwest side of the village, immediately next to the **Windmill (Corn)**. The windmill site today is a private house called **Mill House**, with **The Old Coach House** standing beside it — working buildings gone, names of the trades preserved on their successors.
- **Dog Chase** (Jemima's own address, 1851 and 1871 census — she was there in both years, not just 1871): the street survives today, still called Dog Chase (confirmed via Rik's own visit/Street View and matching coordinates: modern pin at 51.954352, 0.490525, historic grid ref TL712313, essentially identical). On the OS 1892–1914 sheet the same street is labelled **Dog Lane** — so the name has alternated (Dog Chase 1871 census → Dog Lane, OS 1890s–1910s → Dog Chase today), not simply changed once. Both names were evidently in concurrent or overlapping use depending on who was recording it (enumerator vs. cartographer).
  - **The Dog Inn**: White's 1863 directory lists "Dog, William Shead, (and miller)" among the village's inns (alongside the Bull, the Cock, the Red Lion). The inn stood at the top of Dog Lane/Dog Chase, on the corner of the High Street. It survives today as a private house, **3 High Street, "Chase House"** — Georgian-fronted (six-over-six sashes, symmetrical five-bay front, hipped roof, two brick chimneys), confirmed via Street View (Jun 2023). The building itself dropped "Dog" from its name; the street outside kept it. Chase House fronts the High Street; Dog Chase is the lane at its side, not the road it faces.
  - The inn has a small rear courtyard with barns, visible on Street View — this is **not** the same as "Barrack Yard," which is a separate, more distant location also visible on the OS sheet (labelled near the High Street cluster, north of the church). The Barrack Yard/pub-yard identification I initially proposed was wrong and was corrected by Rik on the ground — flagging this explicitly so the error isn't repeated. Barrack Yard's own history/origin remains unresearched.
  - William Shead, licensee of the Dog in 1863, was also listed "(and miller)" — unresolved whether this means he ran a second business at the Wethersfield windmill (some distance away, near the Brewery) or whether it's a separate detail entirely. Not chased further.
- **Village boundary**: Rik's own overlay check found the stretch between the Mill (southwest end) and 7 Silver Street is entirely modern build — the historic village boundary sits at Silver Street. Everything from Silver Street inward (church, High Street, Dog Chase) matches the historic mapping closely; everything on the approach outside that boundary is new.
- **Namesake traps flagged, NOT to be used without independent evidence**:
  - "Thomas Goldstone, saddler and ropemaker" — Wethersfield inhabitant, White's 1863 directory. Same surname as the core family, in the same village Elizabeth grew up in, in a trade (ropemaker) that echoes Albert's later Chatham ropery career. Certainly a different branch/family — flagged specifically because the coincidence is tempting.
  - A Suckling working as a **blacksmith at Beazley End** (Wethersfield hamlet), same 1863 directory. Possibly a relative of Edward Suckling (Jemima's father) — his son Robert witnessed Jemima's 1854 wedding — but unconfirmed. Needs FreeCEN/FreeBMD/Ancestry access to resolve; open web search couldn't get a first name.
- **Straw Hat Makers** named directly in the 1863 directory (Ann Boyton, Caroline Butcher, Ann Westwood) — confirms straw-plait/straw-hat trade as a real, present, named occupation in this exact village at this exact time, not generalised-in colour.

### FINCHINGFIELD (Suckling side — Edward and Sarah Suckling, Jemima's parents)

- **Church**: St John the Baptist, with adjoining Vicarage and School, same anchor role as Wethersfield's St Mary Magdalene.
- **Malting Yard**: (Edward & Sarah Suckling's 1861 address, both listed Pauper): confirmed as a real, named location on the OS 25-inch sheet (1892–1914 series, up to three decades after the family's actual residence there) — sits on the **eastern edge** of the village, not north as earlier noted. **Correction, 25 July 2026**: the previous note conflated this with Duck End and the Spains Hall windmill, which are a separate, northern location entirely unrelated to this route. Finchingfield had its own working brewery (separate from Wethersfield's), confirmed via a regional brewing-history source listing Finchingfield among Essex villages with a brewery in this period — supports Malting Yard being a genuine industrial address, not a generic place-name, though note the OS sheet shows the "Malting Yard" label over apparently open ground with nearby field/parcel numbering (243, 245), consistent with a preserved field name rather than confirmed standing buildings at the time of survey, let alone in 1861.
- Relative to the church: walking from St John the Baptist, past the almshouses, past a smithy, past the Three Tuns Inn, continuing east along the same road, Malting Yard sits at the eastern edge of the village. Edge-of-village, industrial fringe, not among the residential core around the church — consistent with the family's Pauper status (receiving outdoor relief from the Braintree Poor Law Union, not workhouse-resident, since they had their own address). **No windmill on this route**.
- **Named pubs on the OS sheet**: The Swan (near Hill House, southwest of the church), the Green Man, and the Three Tuns (both nearer the church/almshouses cluster). Three working pubs — comparable density to Wethersfield's four.
- **Two smithies** shown on the sheet — one near Duck End, one near the main church/almshouses cluster.
- **Namesake trap flagged, NOT to be used without independent evidence**: **Rev. John Stock, M.A.**, Vicar of Finchingfield (White's 1848 directory), patron and incumbent of the living, ~40 acres of glebe, rebuilt the vicarage in Tudor style. Same surname, same exact parish as the labouring Stock family — but a beneficed clergyman is a wholly different social world from an agricultural family. Flagged explicitly because it's sitting in the same village records and could be mistakenly folded into the family story.
- **Documented overlap, not a connection**: by 1881, Thomas Gouldstone (William's father, see Part Two below) had moved his own family to Finchingfield — the same village as the Suckling side. This is roughly contemporary with, but does not connect to, the Stock/Suckling residence there a generation earlier. Two branches of what becomes Rik's ancestry were both, at different times, resident in the same small Essex village. Flagged as a genuine documented coincidence with no causal connection currently evidenced.

### GREAT SAMPFORD (Gouldstone side — Thomas Gouldstone's own village, William's birthplace)

- **Church**: St Michael's, with Vicarage, at the southwest end of the village near The Stow. This is the baptism and burial church for at least three documented generations of the family (see Part Two).
- **Windmill (Corn) and smithy** stand together at the northwest end of the village, near the **Cock Inn**. Only one smithy confirmed on this sheet (correcting an earlier assumption of two).
- **Bull Inn** and **Red Lion (P.H.)** further along the High Street, nearer the school.
- **Moor End**: a separate, distinct settlement roughly half a mile to a mile north of the main village core, connected by a single road. Site of the **White House** — Thomas Gouldstone's pub-and-smithy address in 1871 (see below), a Grade II listed building (Historic England list entry 1322551, National Grid Reference TL 64021 35902): C17, timber-framed and plastered, two gables on the south-west front, wings extending north-east at the rear, two storeys plus attics and cellar, sash windows to the front/casements to the north-west side, tiled roof with an original chimney stack (grouped square shafts, a heart carved into the base — this last detail long predates Thomas's tenancy, C17 build vs. 1871 occupancy, worth using carefully rather than as "his" detail).
  - Census index originally gave the pub name as "White Horse" — this is a transcription error; the Historic England listing confirms **White House** is correct.
- Two more sets of large-lettered parish-boundary markers noted on the sheets (W/F/T/H near the Wethersfield/Finchingfield edge; R/D near Moor End) — OS convention marking parish boundaries. Not yet identified which parishes the second set (R/D) represent. Worth chasing if boundary/registration-district questions come up later.
- **Not yet done for Great Sampford, unlike Wethersfield and Finchingfield**: a full trade-directory pull (White's 1848/1863). Would likely name pub keepers directly and confirm whether Thomas himself appears by name in his working years.

### Generation 2: Joseph Goldstone/Gouldstone (c.1793–1876)
- **Baptism**: 17 March 1793, St Michael's, Great Sampford. Father Robert Goldstone, mother Rebecca. Confirmed via two independently-indexed FamilySearch entries (England Essex Parish Baptisms and Confirmations 1538–1924, image 417; England Essex Parish Registers 1538–1900, image 308) — same GS film 1472680, same digital folder 4298638, different image numbers, dates and parents agree exactly across both.
- **1841 census**, Great Sampford: age ~15 (rounded per 1841 convention, actual age ~19 given baptism date — no discrepancy, standard 1841 rounding-to-nearest-5). Occupation: Blacksmith (head of household). Wife Ruth, 35. Children: Robert (15, note: same age as Joseph — worth double-checking this isn't a transcription duplicate), Betsey (12), James (11), Lydia (10), Ruth (6), Joseph (3). Also in household: William Richardson (7, relationship unstated).
- **1851 census**, Milk[?], Little Sampford (street name uncertain — index gives "Milk," original address column blank, may be recoverable from previous census page, not yet checked): age 58, Blacksmith. Wife Ruth Goldston, 59, born **Broomfield, Essex** (see discrepancy below). Children in household: Lidia (21, straw plaiter), Ruth (16, straw plaiter), Joseph (13, agricultural labourer) — three previously unknown siblings of Thomas, i.e. William's aunts/uncle, not previously in dramatis personae.
- **1861 census**, Village, Great Sampford: age 69, Blacksmith. Wife Ruth, 66, born **Bedford** (contradicts 1851's Broomfield — see discrepancy below, unresolved). Household also includes: **Ann Gouldstone**, daughter, unmarried, 42, dressmaker (b. ~1819, so older than Thomas — another previously unknown sibling); **Ruth Gouldstone**, granddaughter, 7, born **Holloway, Middlesex**; **Thomas Gouldstone**, grandson, 5, born **Holloway, Middlesex**. Parentage of these two grandchildren is unresolved — likely children of one of Joseph's own children (possibly the 1851 Ruth Jr or Joseph Jr, or an unrecorded child) who had moved to London. This Thomas Gouldstone (b. ~1856, Holloway) is a **different person** from William's brother Thomas (b. ~1859, Great Sampford) — confirmed distinct by both appearing as separate individuals in separate households on the same 1861 census night. Flagged explicitly to prevent future conflation — same name, same surname, same generation, same small parish.
- **Burial**: 1 May 1876, St Michael's, Great Sampford, age 83 (confirmed via two independently-indexed FamilySearch entries, image numbers 96/page and 513, same GS film/folder as baptism — consistent across both). Note: exact burial date should be checked against the primary image before being cited precisely elsewhere — "1 January" style exact-date defaults sometimes indicate placeholder dates in indexing systems where only the year is certainly known; this does not appear to be the case here (1 May, not 1 Jan) but the general caution about verifying exact register dates stands.

### Wife of Joseph: Ruth Goldstone/Gouldstone (c.1793/95–1866)
- Birthplace: **contradictory** — Broomfield, Essex (1851 census) vs. Bedford (1861 census). Genuinely unresolved discrepancy, not a spelling variant (different counties). Flag both, do not silently resolve.
- **Burial**: 1 January 1866, St Michael's, Great Sampford, age 71 — the "1 January" date should be treated with caution as a possible indexing placeholder for "year only known"; check primary image before using an exact date in prose.

### Generation 3: Thomas Gouldstone (c.1821/22–1899)
- **Baptism**: 1 December 1821, St Michael's, Great Sampford. Father Joseph, mother Ruth. (Already in existing notes; now additionally confirmed via the Joseph/Ruth baptism-and-burial record set above establishing the family unit.)
- **1841 census**, Great Sampford, in Joseph's household: age 15 (rounded from actual ~19).
- **1851 census**, **Cock Inn, Great Sampford** (address flagged as uncertain — index gives "Cock Inn" but the street/place column on the primary image is reportedly blank for this entry; the Cock Inn designation's actual source is unclear and may be an inference rather than a direct transcription. Do not treat as confirmed without checking the image directly): age 29, Blacksmith. Wife Emily, 21, born Wimbish. Neighbouring household: **James Goldstone**, 22, blacksmith, born **Thaxted** (not Great Sampford — birthplace doesn't match known siblings), wife Mary (b. Great Sampford), daughter Louisa (1). Very likely a relative (cousin, most plausibly) given trade, surname, and proximity, but **unconfirmed** — flagged as open.
- **1861 census**, Village, Great Sampford: age 39, **Blacksmith employing 3 men**. Wife Emily, 27. Children: Robert (7), Emily (9), William (4, i.e. b. ~1857, matches existing notes), Thomas (2). Neighbouring households: William Philpott (grocer), Edward Porter (tailor), Thomas Martin (ag. lab.), George Gray (master carpenter employing 1 man, two sons also carpenters) — confirms a genuine trade cluster around the smithy, not open farmland.
- **1871 census**, **White House, Moor End** (index originally "White Horse" — corrected via Historic England listing, see Part One): age 49, occupation **Blacksmith & Publican**. Wife Emily, age given inconsistently as 37 (household table) vs. 39 (prose summary) in the same transcription — check image, one of the two is wrong; 37 is closer to the established 1834 birth year. Full sibling list now confirmed matching existing notes: Emily (19), Robert (16), William (14, still a scholar, not yet working), Thomas (12), Bennet (9), Flora (7), Hugh C (3).
- **1881 census**, adjacent to/near **The Swan Inn, Finchingfield** (own household, Schedule 38, not the inn itself — Schedule 37 is the Cammell family running the Swan): age 60, Blacksmith, birthplace correctly "Gt Sampford" (index error gave "St Sampford"). Wife Emily, 44, occupation now **Dressmaker**. Household: Hugh (13, scholar), **Mable** (7 — previously unknown child, not in the 1871 sibling list, born ~1874), **Florence Miles** (19, married, dressmaker — almost certainly "Flora" from 1871, married name Miles; two-year age discrepancy between the two records, 7 in 1871 vs 19 in 1881 implying births four years apart when it should be ten — flag as open, names/sex/birthplace otherwise match).
- **1891 census**, **near the church** (index originally "W The Church" — corrected by Rik on the primary image to "Nr The Church," i.e. "Near," standard period abbreviation), Great Sampford: age 70, **General Smith**, self-employed. Wife Emily, 58 (closest of any census to the established 1834 birth year). Household: just the two of them, three rooms — children grown and moved on by this point.
- **Death**: 1899, Great Sampford (per Rik, exact source not yet given as a citation — age at death would be ~77/78, consistent with all census-derived birth years).

### Wife of Thomas: Emily Willett Gouldstone (c.1833/34–1913)
- Birthplace: Wimbish, Essex — consistent across all census returns.
- **Age recorded inconsistently across six census years and never resolves to a single consistent birth year**: 21 in 1851 (implies b. ~1830, arguably too young against the 1834 figure), 27 in 1861 (implies ~1834, matches), 37 or 39 in 1871 (implies ~1832–34, internally inconsistent transcription), 44 in 1881 (implies ~1837), 58 in 1891 (implies ~1833, closest match), 66 in 1901 [see below] (implies ~1835). Treat as a permanent, unresolved feature of the record rather than something to keep chasing to a single figure.
- **1901 census**, Village, Great Sampford, West Suffolk[sic — index error, document itself says Essex]: Widow, age 66, three-room household. Living with **Mabel Goldstone, granddaughter**, 26, dressmaker. Note the relationship change from "Daughter" (1881, age 7) to "Granddaughter" (1901, age 26) — same person by age (both consistent with b. ~1874), genuine discrepancy, not a transcription slip on one side alone. More likely the 1881 "Daughter" was a simplification of an actual grandchild being raised by Thomas and Emily; "Granddaughter" in 1901 is the more specific and less convenient claim, probably the more reliable. Mabel's actual parent among Thomas and Emily's children is unresolved and worth chasing.
- **1911 census**, Great Sampford: Widow, age 76, living with daughter **Emily Willett Gouldstone**, 59 (b. ~1852 — consistent with the eldest daughter first appearing as 19 in the 1871 census). Index gave "Nillett" — corrected to Willett, matches consistently elsewhere; likely an OCR misread of "W" as "N."
- **Death**: registered Q2 (Apr–May–Jun) 1913, Saffron Walden district. Age 79 at death (implies birth 1833/34 — closest single data point yet to the established 1834 figure). GRO reference: Volume 4a, Page 785.
