## 1. RESOLVED — HOW ELIZABETH REACHED LONDON

### The finding
**1871 census, 115 Albion Road, Stoke Newington St Mary:** Elizabeth Stock (18, general servant, b. Wethersfield) is in service alongside **Susan Newman (16, nursemaid, b. Wethersfield)** in the household of George Horace David Chilton (34, Solicitor & Attorney, b. London), his wife Isabella (32, b. Oundle, Northamptonshire), and their three young sons, Guy, Stanley, and Reginald.

### Why this matters
Two girls from the same small Essex village, placed in the same London household. This is not coincidence — it's the mechanism. Susan Newman was baptised 28 October 1855, Wethersfield, St Mary Magdalene, father John Newman, mother Emma. Susan's father had died by 1861 (Emma is head of household, a young widow, in that year's census, with Susan, 5, and a brother John). Confirms a plausible route: a fellow Wethersfield girl, already known to the family or the village, likely instrumental in Elizabeth's own placement in London domestic service — whether via direct family connection, shared local network, or a single placement channel serving both girls.

### The Chilton household
George Horace David Chilton — solicitor, London-born — represents a step up in class from Elizabeth's own labouring background: a professional household, not tradesman or agricultural. Worth using as concrete texture (the voice guide's "name the actor, the mechanism" principle) rather than a vague "she went into service."

**Not yet resolved:** attempts to trace the wider Chilton family (a Henry Charles Chilton, solicitor, son of George Chilton Esquire of Chancery Lane, found via a monumental inscription) did not establish a confirmed link to George Horace David Chilton specifically, nor any connection to Stoke Newington or the Graves/Falkirk Ironworks side of William's story. Treat as unconnected background unless a direct link is found later — do not assume the two Chilton threads are the same family.

### Possible Newman/Stock cousin connection — flag as colour only, not fact
A compiled genealogical record suggested Susan's father John Newman may descend from the same Newman family as Sarah Newman, Thomas Stock's own mother (making Susan and Elizabeth's children second cousins). **This is unconfirmed and should not be stated as fact.** The specific record checked (John Newman m. Emma Lewis, July 1842, Castle Hedingham) contains an internal contradiction — it also lists a "1891 residence, Castle Hedingham" for a man who should already be dead by 1861 — consistent with the kind of collaborative-tree conflation caught repeatedly earlier tonight (see Part One update, Section 7). **Usable in prose only as suggested colour** ("possibly cousins," "the two families may have been connected") — not as a sourced genealogical claim.

## 2. THOMAS STOCK'S FIRST MARRIAGE — CLOSED OFF, NOT RESOLVED

### Decision
After extensive further search this session, **Thomas Stock's first wife's identity cannot be confirmed**, and the attempt to confirm it is now closed. This is a deliberate decision, not an unfinished thread: the fact is not load-bearing for the book's argument and further search was producing diminishing returns.

### What was ruled out
At least three additional Thomas Stocks were found active in the Finchingfield/Wethersfield area in the relevant decades, none of them ours:
- "Thomas Stock, Gravel Pit, Wethersfield," 1841, with wife "Mary" and five children — already flagged in Part One update as a probable namesake.
- A Thomas Stock, single/bachelor, born c.1806, tracked consistently across the 1841 and 1851 censuses (both at "Village, Finchingfield") — never married in either return, ruling him out as the Hockley groom.
- A "Sarah Stock, 88, Finchingfield, 1841" household containing a "Thomas Stock, 20" — too young by two decades to be ours, a fourth distinct namesake.

**No census return across 1841 or 1851 shows a Sarah as wife to a Thomas Stock matching our man's known details.** This is negative evidence, not proof the marriage didn't happen — but combined with the repeated namesake collisions in this exact parish, it means the 20 October 1827 Finchingfield marriage record (D/P 14/1/10, no ages, no parents' names given) cannot be safely attributed to our Thomas Stock.

### What stands regardless
Our Thomas Stock was a **widower** at his 1854 marriage to Jemima — that status is recorded directly on the marriage register and does not depend on knowing his first wife's identity.

### Instruction for prose
Write Thomas as "a widower, though no confirmed record survives of his first wife" (or similar) — an openly flagged gap, not smoothed over or silently assigned the name "Sarah Hockley." Do not use "Sarah Hockley" as his first wife's name in any final text without a fresh corroborating record (an 1841 census entry showing her as his wife, or her 1845 death record naming him as husband) that hasn't yet been found.
