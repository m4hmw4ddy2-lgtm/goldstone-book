## NEW CONFIRMED FACTS

### Genealogy — Gouldstone family

- **Thomas Gouldstone** baptised 1 December 1821, St Michael's, Great Sampford. Father Joseph Gouldstone, mother Ruth. Archive ref: D/P 289/1/13. Blacksmith employing 3 men (1861); General Smith (1891). 1851 census: at Cock Inn, Great Sampford on census night — six weeks after his wedding. Died Q3 1899, Saffron Walden district, age 77.
- **Emily Willett** born 1834, Wimbish, Essex. Married Thomas 30 January 1851. Died June 1913, Essex, age 79.
- **William's siblings** (1871 census, Moor End White Horse, Sampford): Emily (b.c.1852), Robert (b.c.1854), William (b.c.1857), Thomas (b.c.1859), Bennett (b.c.1862), Flora (b.c.1864), Hugh C (b.c.1868)
- **Marriage certificate (15 March 1879):** William: age 22, Iron Worker, 88 Frampton Park Road, father Thomas Goldstone (Smith). Elizabeth: age 24, Spinster, same address, father Thomas Stock (decd). Witnesses: Thomas and Emily Goldstone. Note: "Goldstone" spelling throughout — registrar's variant, not significant in itself.

### Genealogy — Stock/Suckling family

- **Jemima Suckling** born 23 November 1811, Wethersfield. Father Edward Suckling (1784-1864), mother Sarah Crawley (1790-1870), married 23 September 1810. First married John Ansell, Christmas Day 1833 — one son Arthur Ansell. Ansell died April 1845. Married Thomas Stock 16 June 1854. Elizabeth their only child. Died 25 October 1873, Braintree, age 61.
- **Arthur Ansell** — Elizabeth's half-brother. Born before 1845. No confirmed contact with Elizabeth after 1873.

### Rev. T. H. Grantham Robinson (1837–1884) — fully identified

From obituary in *Church Bells and Illustrated Church News*, 1884:
- Full name: Thomas Harrison Grantham Robinson
- Born Ireland, July 1837. Educated King's College London and University of Dublin.
- Ordained to Whitechapel; Secretary to Prayer Book and Homily Society; assistant curate St Augustine's Highbury under Rev. Gordon Calthorp
- In 1865 offered living of St James's, Walthamstow. St Saviour's church built through munificence of Mr R. Foster.
- Preached Sunday 16 March 1884; visited parish Monday; unwell Wednesday; died Friday 21 March 1884, age 46.
- Almost certainly the "Vicar of the parish" (BMJ 29 Sep 1883) who wrote to the Home Secretary
- Buried the children 13 August 1883; dead by the following March
- Character: "large-hearted, good humoured, and kind to all"; respected by Nonconformists; mourned by the poor as "a departed leader and friend"
- He earns a paragraph in Part Three

Mrs Raymond Pelly, wife of the vicar of St John the Evangelist, Stratford. Founded **St Agatha's Refuge**, Stratford, **1883** — same year as murders. Church of England rescue institution for women in moral distress. ### Elizabeth's Lambeth address — fully researched

- **Albert's birth certificate:** Born 19 June 1887, Workhouse Infirmary, Brook Street, Lambeth. Registered 20 June by J Frost (infirmary staff member, present at birth). Mother: Elizabeth Goldston of 2 John Street, Hercules Buildings, Lambeth.
- Elizabeth was **not a workhouse inmate** — she had her own address and came to the infirmary as a pauper patient through the relieving officer system to deliver.
- **The infirmary:** Brook Street (later Brook Drive), Lambeth. Built approximately 1876-1879. Separate from the adjacent Renfrew Road workhouse. Admissions by means of relieving officer — pauper stigma attached. Had a dedicated Lying-in Ward. Trained its own nurses. [Mrs Emma Brewer's account, 1890, workhouses.org.uk]
- The infirmary was approximately **half a mile** from Elizabeth's address at John Street — not adjacent.
- **2 John Street, Hercules Buildings** — John Street ran parallel to the railway line, adjoining Hercules Terrace. Renamed **Newnham Terrace** in 1893 when John Street, Short Street, and Hercules Terrace were merged. The street no longer exists — the site is now the **Park Plaza Hotel Waterloo**.
- Albert's middle name **Jennet**: origin unknown. No family precedent in Gouldstone, Stock, Suckling, or Crawley lines. Open question.
- Spelling of surname on birth certificate: "Goldston" — registrar's rendering of what Elizabeth said (Gouldstone/Goldstone). The significant fact is she used her married name, not Stock. The precise spelling is the registrar's business.

### Courtenay Place — OS 25" map confirmed

- OS 25" map (1891-5 revision, National Library of Scotland) confirms Courtenay Place location and building footprints
- **Thirteen bay-fronted two-storey cottages** on east side of Courtenay Place facing St James's Street station platforms directly
- Matching bay-fronted terrace on the High Street — same speculative development, mirror image
- The cottages were built to exploit the railway — artisan/lower-middle-class commuter housing, approximately 1881
- Site now a bus depot
- "One of the first of its row" (IPN) places the house towards the southern, station end of the terrace
