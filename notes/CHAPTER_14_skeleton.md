## THE DAY, PINNED TO SOURCES

- **Morning.** "On Friday morning last ... William Gouldstone ... was placed at the bar" (Essex Weekly News). The
  case "occupied the greater part of Friday last" (Savage, Lancet). Before Mr Justice Day.
- **Counsel.** Poland and Montagu Williams for the Crown "on behalf of the Director of Public Prosecutions" (Times);
  "on behalf of the Treasury" (Essex Weekly News); Grain and Elliott for the prisoner (Old Bailey; "Mr. G. Elliot",
  Guardian; "Mr. J. P. Grain", Essex Weekly News).
- **The indictment.** "Indicted for, and charged on the Coroner's Inquisition with, the wilful murder of Charles
  Gouldstone" (Old Bailey); "first tried upon the indictment charging him with the wilful murder of his eldest child"
  (Times); "indicted first for the murder of Charles Gouldstone" (Guardian). See CHECKED, 1.
- **The dock.** "Looked carelessly round the Court, and stood calmly, with his thumbs in the sleeveholes of his
  waistcoat while the Jury were being sworn" (Essex Weekly News, image checked); "glanced carelessly around the court,
  and pleaded 'Not guilty' in a calm voice" (IPN, image checked). See Contradiction 100.
- **The Crown's case.** Poland's opening (Times, in reported speech). Ada, Emma Clarke, Cheeseman, Folkard, Wheatley,
  Gould, Honey; Cheeseman re-examined on the hammer (Old Bailey). Wheatley's "preying" corrected on
  cross-examination, here (Old Bailey).
- **Grain's opening** (Times; Essex Weekly News, which has him invoking the new Act: see CHECKED, 2).
- **The delay.** "Some delay occurred in consequence of the witnesses not being in readiness when called", and Day's
  complaint about "the arrangements" at the Central Criminal Court (Times only).
- **The defence witnesses**, in the Old Bailey's order: Thomas, Robert and Emily Gouldstone; Graves; Cakebread;
  Bennett; John Clark; Skelton; Westbrook; "Kinnaird" (Kennard in the Times); Charles Gouldstone of Epping; John
  Byford; Dr Sunderland; Dr Savage. Day's intervention during Savage's evidence (Times only).
- **Speeches.** Grain's closing; Poland's reply (Times; IPN for both, partly shared wording with the Times).
- **The summing-up**, with the Act "handed to him by the learned counsel for the prosecution—of the existence of which
  he was not previously aware" (Times, image checked) and the special verdict explained (Times; Guardian/IPN copy).
- **Retired at twenty minutes past five** (Guardian/IPN copy only); out "about a quarter of an hour" (Times;
  Guardian/IPN); "only fifteen minutes" (Essex Weekly News); "fifteen minutes' consideration" (Daily Chronicle, via
  the Guardian). Guilty.
- **After the verdict.** "Being asked in the usual course whether he had anything to say why sentence of death should
  not be passed upon him, the prisoner replied in the negative" (Times, image checked); "The Prisoner: I am not
  guilty." (Essex Weekly News, image checked). Contradiction 93.
- **Sentence.** The black cap; Day's words (Times, verbatim, image checked; Guardian/IPN paraphrase); "a good deal
  affected" (Guardian/IPN). "Calm and undisturbed demeanour throughout the trial" (Times, image checked); "perfectly
  composed" (Guardian), "perfectly calm and composed" (IPN); "appeared to be quite unconcerned" (Essex Weekly News).
- **Evening.** "Brought down by the 8.12 p.m. train" to Chelmsford "in charge of Mr. Thomson (from the office of
  Messrs. Gepp and Sons, the Acting Under Sheriffs) and two warders from Springfield Gaol", by cab to the prison,
  "placed in the condemned cell" (Guardian and Essex Weekly News, one copy; "Thompson" in the Essex Weekly News).
- **The same day, elsewhere.** Savage writes to the Lancet from Bethlem Hospital, dated 14 September (published
  22 September).

## THE CLAIMS ALREADY IN THE NOTES, CHECKED AGAINST THEIR SOURCES (as asked)

1. **Only the murder of Charles was tried; the other four deaths came in as evidence. CONFIRMED, with a
   qualification.** Old Bailey: one indictment, Charles. Times: "first tried upon the indictment charging him with
   the wilful murder of his eldest child"; Guardian: "indicted first for the murder of Charles Gouldstone". But the
   Times also opens "placed upon his trial upon an indictment charging him with the wilful murder of his five
   children", the IPN says "indicted for the wilful murder of his five children", and Day, summing up, called him
   "indicted for the wilful murder of his five children" (Times). He had been arraigned on "several indictments" on
   the 12th (Guardian, 15 September; Chapter Thirteen). Safe statement: there were several indictments; he was tried
   on the one for Charles. **MASTER_NOTES ("The trial — 14 September 1883") says "Guilty on all five counts". That is
   wrong** (corrections list, A).
2. **The Trial of Lunatics Act direction was put to the jury by Day; the Old Bailey account omits it. CONFIRMED, and
   the sourcing is stronger than the notes say.** The notes rest it on the Guardian of 22 September alone, and
   part_three_context_law_and_medicine.md (section 3 and the timeline's "RESOLVED" block) record that "the Times of
   15 September" was searched and found silent. **It is not silent.** The Times, image checked today: "He had had
   handed to him by the learned counsel for the prosecution—of the existence of which he was not previously aware, as
   no notice had been given to him—an Act of Parliament passed towards the end of last month, which might affect the
   form of their verdict." It then sets out the old law ("Not Guilty on the ground of insanity") and the new special
   verdict, and adds: "If, on the other hand, they found that he knew the nature and quality of the act when he killed
   his children, and that he was not of unsound mind, they must find him guilty, and the new Act would not affect that
   verdict." **And a third account, independent of both:** the Essex Weekly News (image checked) has Grain, opening
   the defence, telling the jury "he thought the Jury would find it their duty to say, under the provision of a new Act
   of Parliament, that the prisoner did commit the murders, but that he was insane at the time; the result of which
   verdict would be that he would be detained during her Majesty's pleasure." So: defence counsel invoked it in his
   opening; prosecuting counsel handed it to the judge; the judge, by the Times's account, had not known it existed;
   and he explained it to the jury last. **The Guardian's wording is misquoted in two notes files**: the image reads
   "a special verdict **stating** that the prisoner did commit the offence", not "finding" (corrections list, B).
3. **The jury was out about a quarter of an hour. CONFIRMED** by four printings, three independent: Times; the
   Guardian/IPN copy ("about a quarter of an hour", retired at 5.20); Essex Weekly News ("only fifteen minutes");
   Daily Chronicle via the Guardian ("fifteen minutes' consideration"). The 5.20 is one copy only.
4. **Neither could be sworn; whether William made an unsworn statement is open until the 15 September reports are
   checked. CHECKED, so far as the reports we hold allow.** No account records any statement from him before the
   verdict: not the Old Bailey, the Times, the Guardian/IPN, the Pall Mall Budget or the Essex Weekly News. **After the
   verdict there are two records, and they differ** (Contradiction 93): the Times, "replied in the negative" to the
   allocutus; the Essex Weekly News, "The Prisoner: I am not guilty." Both images checked today. The papers of
   15 September other than the Times are not held (see SOURCES WE DO NOT HAVE, 1 and 2). The legal position
   (part_three file, "CURRENT POSITION") stands unchanged.
5. **Emma Clarke gave evidence at the Old Bailey, which she had not done before the magistrates. CONFIRMED in her own
   words**, cross-examined: "I was examined before the Coroner, but not before the Magistrate." **And her drink
   evidence at the trial is the opposite of what Contradiction 6 says.** Contradiction 6 has her telling the Old
   Bailey "I only saw him once the worse for drink; that was on the Friday after the babies were born." That is
   **Ada's** answer, a page earlier in the Old Bailey account. Emma's own, at the trial: "I had not about this time or
   any time that week seen the prisoner the worse for drink." The Times ("She never saw him the worse for drink")
   agrees with the Old Bailey; it is not a "compression error". The real contradiction is between Emma at the inquest
   (the Thursday, Daily Telegraph 11 August; her coroner deposition) and Emma at the trial (corrections list, C).
6. **"Forrest Fulton" against Grain and Elliott is unresolved (Contradiction 73). STILL UNRESOLVED.** Every account of
   the 12th and the 14th gives Elliott for the prisoner and Grain leading; no trial-day source mentions Fulton.
7. **Savage examined William once, the day before. CONFIRMED, with one slip in the Old Bailey text.** Old Bailey,
   cross-examined: "I saw the prisoner yesterday for not more than a quarter or half an hour ... that was the only
   time I saw him"; a few lines later, "from what I saw of him on Saturday I would not certify him". The Times has
   "on Thursday" and "yesterday"; the juror, "on the Thursday". **Thursday 13 September.** "Saturday" is a slip in the
   website text or the Sessions Papers (Contradiction 95). Durations: "not more than a quarter or half an hour"
   (Old Bailey); "between a quarter and half an hour" (Times; BMJ 22 September); "twenty minutes to half-an-hour"
   (juror); "fifteen to thirty minutes" and "twenty minutes or half an hour" (Savage's own letters); "a ten-minute
   examination" (BMJ 6 October). Use the Old Bailey's.
8. **A juror and Savage argued in print within three days. CONFIRMED**: "One of the Jury", Tottenham, 15 September;
   Savage, Bethlehem Hospital, 17 September. Clark Bell heads Savage's reply "To the Editor of The Daily Telegraph",
   so the juror's letter was printed there. **But see FINDINGS, 6: there may have been two juror letters.** Both
   documents are dated after the trial and belong to the next chapter under the linear rule.
9. **The press divided three and three (Guardian, 22 September). CONFIRMED** as the Guardian's own count: for the
   sentence, the Times, Daily Chronicle, Morning Advertiser; for further inquiry, the Daily Telegraph, Morning Post,
   Daily News. It is the Guardian's framing, and should be given as the Guardian's. Belongs to the next chapter.

## FINDINGS FROM THE SWEEP (new to the project)

1. **After the verdict, William spoke, or did not** (Contradiction 93). The Times: "replied in the negative" when
   asked why sentence should not be passed. The Essex Weekly News: "The Prisoner: I am not guilty." They may describe
   one moment (a reply to the allocutus that the Times summarised as a refusal) or two. Neither is in the Old Bailey
   account. If the Essex reading is right, these are William's only recorded words in court that day.
2. **The Act was in the room before the judge knew of it** (CHECKED, 2): Grain cited it in opening (Essex Weekly News);
   Poland handed it to Day, who "was not previously aware" of it (Times). It was the last thing Day put to the jury
   before they retired ("in conclusion", Guardian/IPN; the last paragraph of the summing-up, Times).
3. **Day intervened in Savage's evidence** (Times only): "The witness was about to be asked what conclusion he arrived
   at after hearing the evidence of insanity on both the paternal and maternal side of the family, when Mr. Justice Day
   said it seemed to him that that was the question the jury had to decide. A doctor was entitled to give his medical
   evidence, but not to draw a conclusion which the jury had to draw." The Old Bailey records nothing of it. This is
   the courtroom fact behind Savage's later complaints ("Counsel were not allowed to ask what my opinion was",
   Lancet; "The judge opposed strongly attempts to get my opinion", 17 September).
4. **Day complained about the court** (Times only), when "some delay occurred in consequence of the witnesses not being
   in readiness when called": the arrangements for "witnesses, juries, and other persons" were "so exceedingly bad that
   they would not be allowed in any other large town". The delay was the defence's witnesses, the ones the trial had
   been put off two days to wait for (Chapter Thirteen).
5. **Savage in the box, two lines not yet in the notes** (Times): "At Bethlehem the last patient who died without being
   removed to Broadmoor was a woman who had killed her whole family." And his evidence-in-chief, Old Bailey: "the
   insanity on the mother's side having been proved, I should say the prisoner has a very great tendency to become
   insane, and if insanity, however remote, were proved on the male side, it would considerably increase the
   liability in the offspring."
6. **One juror letter or two.** The Guardian prints "ONE OF THE JURY", Tottenham, 15 September, about Savage's
   evidence. Clark Bell reports that "The Foreman of the jury published a card in the Daily Telegraph" giving the
   judge's direction: "'That if a person was proved to be of sound mind up to the time of committing a certain deed;
   if he knew the nature of that deed and the penalty it involved; and if after this he still appeared of sound mind,
   we are bound, according to this law, to say such a person was not insane.'" Savage's reply refers to "the letter"
   (Guardian) or "the letters" (Clark Bell). Whether the Tottenham juror was the foreman, and whether there were two
   letters, is open. This is the "juror's account of Day's direction" the notes say is in the Medico-Legal Journal; it
   is reported there, at second hand, not reprinted whole. Next chapter.
7. **Bucknill's version of Day's charge is the Times's, word for word** (BMJ, 15 March 1884, p. 500, read from the
   image). MASTER_NOTES (15 September session) says it "reads as a more precise, formal version than the press
   paraphrase". It is not independent: it is the Times text (corrections list, D).
8. **A lead for live_lists.md, item 29 (the London daily that first printed Elizabeth's statement).** The Dundee
   Courier of 21 September prints the statement's opening sentence and its closing paragraph under "The Daily
   Telegraph says:—". The Essex Weekly News prints the same opening sentence. So the Daily Telegraph printed the
   statement, very likely first, on Wednesday 19 September. For the statement's chapter; to confirm from the Telegraph
   itself.
9. **Thomas Gouldstone at Stamford Hill.** Re-examined at the Old Bailey: he last saw William "just after Christmas,
   at my place at Stamford Hill—I was living there then". In the Times he saw him "about eight weeks ago"
   (Contradiction 101). A London address for Thomas in the winter of 1882-83 is not in dramatis_personae.md. Notes
   only; not for this chapter unless Rik wants it.
10. **"Not in the building."** The Elizabeth thread file (section 3) says she had no right to speak "because she was not
   a party and was not in the building". Nothing sources the second clause, and her own statement says she saw him
   "once at the Old Bailey" (undated: the 12th or the 14th). The clause should come out (corrections list, E).
11. **Day's sentence, verbatim** (Times, image checked): "William Gouldstone, you have been convicted upon overwhelming
   evidence of the crime of wilful murder. The jury could not consistently with their duty have found any other verdict
   in your case. It is my invariable rule never to comment upon the facts of a case where it is my painful duty to
   sentence a prisoner to such a painful death as that which awaits you. I wish to say nothing to add to your
   sufferings, but merely to beg of you, as a fellow man, to make good use of the short time which is allowed you by
   law to prepare for your last day. Seek mercy where mercy alone is certain to be found. Seek it at the feet of your
   Redeemer. I beseech you make good use of your time. Every facility and every help will be afforded you. Do, I
   beseech you again, make use of that short time which is allowed you."

## WHERE THE NATURAL BREAKS ARE

The day has three bodies of material, of unequal weight and unequal novelty.

**1. The Crown's case.** Long in the record and almost entirely known to the reader: Ada, Cheeseman, Folkard,
Wheatley, Gould and Honey have each been heard at least once already (Chapters Seven to Twelve). Under the selection
rule of Chapter Twelve (repeat earlier evidence only where the courtroom changes what it means) it compresses hard.

coroner's for the first time; and Wheatley's "preying" correction, which was made here. Perhaps 600 to 900 words.

**2. The defence.** Fourteen witnesses, almost all of them new: the family (Thomas's cross-examination and
re-examination, Robert, Emily, Charles of Epping); the shop (Graves, with the Tuesday and Wednesday; Cakebread;
Bennett; John Clark; Skelton; Westbrook; Kennard; Byford); Sunderland, reserved for this chapter since 12 September
2026; and Savage, with Day's intervention and Poland's cross-examination. This is the chapter's new material and its

**3. The law and the verdict.** The two closing speeches, the summing-up, the Act, a quarter of an hour, the verdict,
the words after it, the black cap, the sentence, the train to Chelmsford. Perhaps 1,000 to 1,400 words.

## THE CHAPTER'S ELIZABETH THREAD

She is the absent witness at her children's trial, and the record shows it without being made to.
- **Her words are in the courtroom only in other mouths.** Ada swore to "What makes you home so early?", "Did you
  tell them I was confined?", "Didn't you tell Mr. Graves?" and "Come in; he is murdering my children" (Old Bailey;
  the last a new wording); Emma Clarke to "He has killed my babies"; Cheeseman to "Oh you wicked man, what have you

## THE SHAPE PROPOSED (two chapters; see DECIDE 1 for one)

### Chapter Fourteen: Friday morning to the close of the evidence
**1. The bar and the Crown's case.** The dock and the jury sworn (the thumbs in the waistcoat, Essex Weekly News).

**2. The delay, and the family.** Grain's opening (Times; with or without the Act: DECIDE 9). The witnesses not ready;

### Chapter Fifteen: Friday afternoon to Friday night

**2. The summing-up.** Day on the law, in the Times's words (the nature and quality of the act; "knew that he was

**4. The night.** Removed from the bar; the 8.12 to Chelmsford; the condemned cell. (DECIDE 3 for the last line.)

**DECIDE 1. One chapter or two, and where the break falls.**
(a) **Two chapters, the break at the close of the evidence** (recommended). Chapter Fourteen ends on Savage conceding,
under cross-examination, the two things the M'Naghten test turned on; Chapter Fifteen is the law applied to them. It
keeps the evidence and the verdict apart, and puts the Act, the quarter of an hour and the sentence in a short chapter
of their own, as Chapter Eleven did for the funeral and the verdict. Against: Chapter Fifteen may run short (about
1,200), and the break falls in the middle of a single day, which Chapter Ten and Eleven did not do.
(b) **One chapter**, Friday only, four or five movements, 3,600 to 4,900 words. Against: the longest chapter in the
book by some way, and Chapter Ten was split at a smaller planned length.
(c) **Two chapters, the break at the close of the Crown's case** (after Grain's opening and Day's complaint about the
witnesses). Against: Chapter Fourteen would be almost entirely evidence the reader already has, about 1,200 words.

**DECIDE 2. How the evidence chapter ends (if 1a).**
(a) On Savage's last answer in the Old Bailey account: "I believe that he knew he was killing the children, and that
the penalty for that act was death." (recommended: the record's own last line of evidence, and it hands the next
chapter its question without a word of ours).
(b) On Day's intervention ("A doctor was entitled to give his medical evidence, but not to draw a conclusion which the
jury had to draw"), with the cross-examination before it. Against: reorders the evidence, and the intervention came
in the examination-in-chief, before the cross-examination.
(c) On Sunderland, closing on the family. Against: leaves Savage to open the next chapter, cut off from the witnesses
he was summing up.

**DECIDE 3. Where the trial day ends.**
(a) On the 8.12 train and the condemned cell at Chelmsford that night (recommended: linear, plain observation, and it
leaves the Chelmsford chapter to begin on the Saturday). The shorn curls and prison dress are "since" (later) and stay
for that chapter.
(b) On "removed from the bar" / "removed to the cells".
(c) On Savage's Lancet letter, dated the same day ("If this man is hanged, I think it will be a scandal to law, or
rather justice."). Under the linear rule a letter belongs to the day it is dated (the vicar's letter of 10 August in
Chapter Eleven), so this is allowed. Against: it closes the trial on the defence's expert's verdict on it, which is
close to an editorial, and it spends the strongest line of the medical campaign before that chapter begins.
Recommended placing for the Lancet letter: the opening of the next chapter, introduced by its date.

**DECIDE 4. William's words after the verdict (Contradiction 93).**
(a) Give both, each named: the Times, that asked in the usual course whether he had anything to say, "the prisoner

(b) The Times only. (c) The Essex Weekly News only.

**DECIDE 5. "Once at the Old Bailey."**
(a) Hold it for the statement's chapter, as decided for Chapter Thirteen (recommended: the day is unknown, so it cannot

(b) State it as fact in this chapter, undated ("At the Old Bailey that week she saw him once"). Against: "that week"

**DECIDE 6. A paragraph on who could and could not speak.**
(a) A short explanatory paragraph, in the manner of the grand-jury paragraph in Chapter Thirteen: in 1883 a prisoner
could not give evidence on oath, but could address the court unsworn; his wife could be called by neither side

(b) One sentence only, at the same point.
(c) Nothing: let the record's silence stand, and let the Elizabeth thread show it.
Recommended (b): enough for the reader to know the silence was the law's and not a choice of counsel's, without an
essay; and it must say that he could have spoken unsworn, not that he could not speak.

**DECIDE 7. Thomas Gouldstone's evidence, already in Chapter Three.**
Chapter Three quotes his examination-in-chief in direct speech, with the juryman's "Grain is the Essex word for
strangle". (a) In this chapter, give only what Chapter Three does not: his cross-examination ("I have seven children

**DECIDE 8. The Tuesday and the Wednesday.**
