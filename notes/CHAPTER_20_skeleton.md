## CRESCENT AND POWERFUL: SETTLED FROM THE CRESCENT CLIPS (15 October 2026; GOVERNS over the two sections that follow)
Source: transcribed/crescent_transcriptions.txt (19 clips, Rik's upload of 15 October; each transcribed from and checked
against its image by Claude the same day). Crescent's voyage, inside his entry (8 Aug to 15 Sep 1911, no break), so his
(plan DECIDE 8):
- 10 Aug: Crescent, "taking out relief crews for ships on the Australia and China stations", came round from Portsmouth
  to Plymouth and that evening left for Malta, en route for Colombo (Western Morning News, 11 Aug). Gibraltar sailed
  from Devonport the same day (her printed programme is HERS, not Crescent's).
- 20 Aug: Malta, arrived in the morning (Lloyd's List 22 Aug; N&MR 23 Aug); resumed her voyage on the 21st (Daily Malta
  Chronicle, 21 Aug: "with relief crews for the China and East Indies Station", its wording).
- 25 Aug: Port Said (Lloyd's List 28 Aug); left Suez Monday 28 Aug (N&MR 30 Aug).
- 4 Sep: sailed from Aden (Lloyd's List 5 Sep).
- Meanwhile Cambrian, Psyche, Pyramus and Pioneer, flying the paying-off pennant, steamed into Colombo in line about
  noon on Sunday 3 Sep, watched from the shore (Times of Ceylon, 4 Sep), having come up together from Sydney
  (Ceylon Observer, 7 Sep).
- 12 Sep: Crescent arrived "shortly after 4 p.m." (Times of Ceylon, 12 Sep; "4-15 p.m.", Ceylon Observer, 14 Sep), ten
  warships "along the South-West arm of the breakwater"; in wireless touch with Cambrian for the last three days (CO).
  She brought "relief crews for H.M.S. 'Flora,' of the China station, and H.M.S. 'Psyche' and 'Pioneer,' of the
  Australian station" (TofC 12 Sep; also TofC 4 Sep and CO 14 Sep: three items, two papers. Outlier: TofC 18 Sep gives
  those reliefs to Gibraltar).
- Friday 15 Sep: recommissioning, "followed by the transfer of crews" (CO 14 Sep; TofC 12 Sep "probably ... on
  Friday"). HIS CRESCENT ENTRY CLOSES THAT DAY.
- Sunday 17 Sep: Cambrian, Pioneer and Psyche "sailed for Australia" (TofC 18 Sep). Crescent sailed for Aden on the
  20th with time-expired men (Lloyd's List 22 Sep; TofC 12 Sep).
SO: on 15 September 1911 at Colombo he became one of the new ship's company of Pioneer or Psyche (which, not shown;
Pioneer, his next book, the likelier), and sailed for Australia on the 17th. Powerful was then in New South Wales.
WHY THE SHEET SAYS POWERFUL FROM 16 SEPTEMBER (Rik: "No idea really"): NOT KNOWN. Hypothesis, not for prose: the men of
the recommissioned small ships were carried on the flagship's books, as boys at Portland were "(Borne in 'Boscawen.')"
(Navy List, July 1903, Agincourt, held) and as he was later borne on Tyne's books for T.B.31; and on 17 December the
books changed to Pioneer's own. To test: the Navy List of October 1911 to January 1912 (Pioneer and Psyche: any
"tender to" or "borne in" note), or Pioneer's ledger (ADM 117/ADM 115, Kew).
FOR THE PROSE: Crescent's voyage and Colombo are his (in his entry, no break). Powerful's doings are not (he was in a
drillship). From 17 September to 16 December he was in Pioneer or Psyche, and neither is certain: so neither ship's
doings in those months are his either, unless the ship is settled. From 17 December, Pioneer's are.
The Pioneer man overboard (CO 7 Sep) was on the old crew's passage, before him: OUT.

## SETTLED FIRST: WAS HE ABOARD POWERFUL IN OCTOBER 1911? [SUPERSEDED IN PART by the section above: he was in a drillship from 15 Sep]
**Answer: no, as far as the record goes; and he is not shown aboard her at any time.** Recommendation: no Powerful event is

1. A MATERIAL TRANSCRIPTION ERROR, found and corrected today against the image (header of powerful_transcriptions.txt;
   propagated to albert_naval_research.md and CHAPTER_19_skeleton.md; grep shows no other copy). The Lyttelton Times of
   23 September 1911 does NOT say the drillships were "each bringing out men from training at Colombo". It prints:
   "H.M. ships Cambrian, Pyramus, Pioneer and Psyche are engaged bringing out new crews landing at Colombo from H.M.
   ships Gibraltar and Crescent." The Crescent's men were the drillships' OWN new crews.
2. The drillships left Sydney for Colombo on 2 August 1911, "viâ the North of Australia", calling at Singapore rather than
   Java because of plague (Naval & Military Record, 6 September 1911, letter of 2 August; image-checked today).
3. Cambrian reached Melbourne from Colombo "after recommissioning" the day before Powerful and Encounter arrived there;
   Powerful had left Sydney on Wednesday 4 October, and the destroyer night attacks were on the Wednesday and Thursday
   nights at sea; "the Psyche and Pioneer will not arrive as early as was expected" (Naval & Military Record, 22 November
   1911, letter of Sunday 8 October; image-checked today).
4. The sheet: Crescent to 15 September, Powerful from 16 September. On 16 September Powerful was in New South Wales
   (coaling at Sydney on the 11th; Jervois Bay from the 20th: Naval & Military Record 18 October and 1 November 1911,
   NOT image-checked), and Crescent's men were landing at Colombo. So on the day the Powerful entry opens he cannot have
   been in her. INFERENCE (notes only): his name passed to the flagship's books at Colombo while he came out in one of the
   recommissioned drillships; which one is not recorded. Even the first of them reached the flagship only after the night
   attacks. That his next entry is Pioneer (17 December) fits his having come out in her, but does not prove it.
5. Consequence for the chapter: the October night attacks, the battle practice, Cup Week, Auckland, Wellington, Lyttelton
   and the Terra Nova visit are Powerful's, not shown to be his. Lloyd's Weekly's "returning to Devonport to take the place
   of the old training ship Impregnable" (10 December 1911) is a fact about a ship he is not shown to have been in: its
   pull as a juxtaposition is now weaker, and it still points. Recommended OUT (DECIDE A).
6. Kew (albert_naval_research.md, section 17, item 7): the drillships' logs, or Powerful's, would settle which ship he
   came out in. Not needed for the chapter as recommended.

### POWERFUL, RE-EXAMINED (Rik, 15 October 2026: "3 months, then the Pioneer")
The sheet (image-checked today): Powerful, 16 September to 16 December 1911, one entry, no break; Pioneer from the 17th.
What the evidence allows, by stretch of the entry:
- 16 Sep to about 13 Oct (Sydney, Jervois Bay, the night attacks of 4-5 Oct, the run to Melbourne): NOT aboard. Crescent's
  men were landing at Colombo in late September; no drillship had reached the flagship before the attacks; Psyche and
  Pioneer were still behind on 8 Oct.
- About mid-Oct to 16 Dec: POSSIBLE, NOT SHOWN. Psyche was at Melbourne with Powerful by 7 Nov (N&MR 13 Dec 1911, letter
  of 7 Nov; NOT checked); "The Powerful, Encounter and Pioneer are due at Lyttelton on December 5" (LT 20 Nov 1911; NOT
  checked), so by then Pioneer was with the squadron. A man who came out in a drillship could have crossed to the
  flagship at Melbourne and gone round New Zealand in her; or stayed in his drillship, borne on the flagship's books.
- 16-17 Dec: Powerful left Lyttelton on 7 Dec for Sydney, to coal and store, sailing again on 19 Dec (LT 6 Dec 1911); Pioneer
  is "from Australia" in January (LT 10 Jan 1912). So the change of books on the 17th fits a change of ship at Sydney:
  CONSISTENT with his having been in Powerful in December, and equally with his having been in Pioneer throughout.
- What would settle it: Powerful's or Pioneer's log (Kew, ADM 53) or ship's ledger for October to December 1911.
THE DATES THAT DECIDE IT (Rik's reading, 15 October 2026: barracks, Crescent 8 Aug, "transferred to the Powerful" 16 Sep,
Pioneer 17 Dec). The Crescent entry closes on 15 September, about the time the Lyttelton Times (23 Sep) has Crescent
landing new crews at Colombo; the Powerful entry opens the next day, while Powerful was in New South Wales (coaling at
Sydney 11 Sep; Jervois Bay from the 20th). Colombo is several thousand miles from Sydney (GK, not recorded). So the
transfer on the 16th was a change of BOOKS made while he was at or near Colombo, not a move into the ship herself. Where
he was in body from then until he reached Australian waters is not recorded.
NEW, Rik 15 October 2026 (SOURCE TO SAVE: paper, date and image not yet in the repo): Crescent "arrived in Colombo at
4pm local time 12 September" 1911, "bringing relief crews for the Psyche and Pioneer". With the sheet (Crescent to 15 Sep;
Powerful from 16 Sep; Pioneer from 17 Dec): he came out to Colombo in Crescent as one of the relief crews for Psyche or
Pioneer, and was carried on the flagship's books from the 16th while in one of those two ships (INFERENCE, which of the two
not shown; Pioneer, his next ship, is the likelier). Psyche and Pioneer had not reached the squadron by 8 October (N&MR
22 Nov 1911): the October events are certainly not his. Crescent's arrival at Colombo IS inside his Crescent entry with no
break: usable as his once the source is saved and checked (movement one).
RESULT FOR DECIDE A: the October events stay out in any case. The New Zealand circuit (Auckland 20 Nov, Wellington,
Lyttelton 5-7 Dec, the Terra Nova) is formally inside an unbroken entry (plan DECIDE 8's condition) but under the "borne is
not always aboard" caution: Rik to rule (A, options revised below).

## FINDINGS
1. The drillship line was misread (above, 1). Material: it is the evidence for how he got to Australia.
2. Powerful: borne, not shown aboard (above).
3. The first good conduct badge (4 December 1911) falls in the Powerful entry: it is the sheet's, and may be given by date
   without placing him in her.
4. The twelve years. By the sheet his engagement ran from 19 June 1904 for twelve years: to 19 June 1916. The sheet has no
   re-engagement line; twelve days later he is rated Leading Seaman (1 July 1916), and he serves on to 1919. Why he stayed
   is not on the sheet. That time-expired men were kept for the war is GENERAL KNOWLEDGE, NOT SOURCED (DECIDE G).
5. The badges and the character. Twice in the war one badge is taken away and restored (15 Oct 1914 to 15 Apr 1915; 31
   May 1917 to 31 May 1918). The year-end character falls to "G"/"Good" in exactly those two years (1914, 1917) and is
   "V.G." in every other year from 1910 to 1918. Visible on the sheet; no cause given. The second column ("Supr.",
   "Sat.") is an ability mark: its expansions ("Superior", "Satisfactory") are GENERAL KNOWLEDGE, not on the sheet; not to
   be expanded in prose without a source (DECIDE E).
6. TB31 before the war: TB35's log (lead, through a summarising tool: naval-history.net, OWShips-WW1-10-HM_TB_35, read 15
   October 2026) puts TB35 at Parkeston (Harwich) from January to August 1914, and TB31 shifting berth there on 2 May
   1914; its header has TB35 in "the Forth Patrol Flotilla" "from outbreak of war". This sits against the research note's
   unsourced "8th Destroyer Flotilla at Chatham": not to be used until a source is named.
7. Lily's date comes only from the 1939 Register (held; read 2 October). Dramatis gives "Baptised 11 Jan 1914, Luton,
   Kent" with no source named: not for prose until sourced (live_lists 4, new item). The GRO birth index would give the
   quarter and district.
8. Clarie Irene the daughter: 21 October 1918, 1939 Register only (likewise).
9. Drake came home to Plymouth on 21 February 1913 and his entry closes on the 25th; Pembroke I from the 26th to 26 October;
   Lily born 14 December. The order of dates is in the sheet and the register. No sentence of ours.
10. The photograph (sources/images/albert-goldstone/Albert Goldstone_Lily_Clarie.jpg): Albert in a seaman's jumper and
   collar, no cap, a baby on his knee, Clarie beside. UNDATED (the index's "~1915" is a guess from the baby's age); no
   cap tally, no ship. Not a source for any fact in prose (DECIDE L).
11. "K.C. ... ISSUED 17/4/19" and the medal roll fall after 26 March 1919. Under the linear rule they belong after the
   discharge; the chapter may end on 26 March and give them, or leave them for Chapter Twenty-Two's opening (DECIDE K).
12. Dates the sheet gives with a cancelled line ("Act G.L.2", 26.12.16): out.
13. Sources held for this chapter are much thinner after 1913: no saved press or official source for Dominion beyond the
   census, for Courageous or for Alexander. Each needs one saved source before its event can be his (DECIDE 8's
   condition). Leads: naval-history.net's transcription of Newbolt, Naval Operations V (WW1Book-RN5a, found by search
   today; LEAD, not read), the Admiralty staff monographs index there, Wikipedia's "Second Battle of Heligoland Bight"

### J. ALEXANDER IN THE OFFICIAL DESPATCH (researched 15 October 2026)
PRIMARY, OFFICIAL: Rear-Admiral T. W. Kemp's despatch on naval operations in North Russia, 1918, Fifth Supplement to
the London Gazette, issue 31970, 8 July 1920, pp. 7331-7335 (thegazette.co.uk). READ ONLY THROUGH A SUMMARISING TOOL
(short verbatim excerpts); the PDFs could not be saved from the session (host not allowed): Rik to download pp. 7331-7335
into sources/pdfs/, and every line checked before it is quoted. What it gives:
- Alexander is "a lightly-armed icebreaker", Acting Captain Henry A. le F. Hurt, R.N.; "arrived April 1st" (place not
  given in the excerpt; Murmansk by context).
- Kemp "left in 'Salvator' on July 2nd" and "arrived at Archangel on July 4th", and found there "H.M.S. 'Alexander'
  and two transports with food supplies", which had left Murmansk in April and made their way "with much difficulty
  through the ice"; the food was to be exchanged for Allied war material, and the negotiations had broken down.
- "Two days before my arrival" [so about 2 July 1918] "an unprovoked hostile demonstration had been made against"
  "Alexander." "She had been surrounded by armed vessels and field batteries on the shore." Hurt is praised for his
  "courage and address". "To save further trouble of this nature I sent her" to Murmansk, her armament being too light
  to be of practical use.
- The capture of Archangel, 1-2 August 1918: the ships named are Attentive, Nairana and Amiral Aube (Modjuga battery
  silenced, island taken by 8 p.m. on 1 August). ALEXANDER IS NOT NAMED: she had been sent to Murmansk.
- "two large icebreakers, 'Sviatogor' and 'Mikula,' had been sunk"; "The two icebreakers were raised shortly
  afterwards and refitted." Hurt is in the list of officers mentioned for good services, 1918.
SECONDARY, SCHOLARLY (corroborates the scuttling, from Russian sources): George Bolotenko, "The Icebreaker Mikula
Selianinovich (1916-1937): To Russia and Back", The Northern Mariner XII:3 (July 2002), 17-42, pp. 32-35: the Bolsheviks
sent out Sviatogor and Mikula against the Allied approach on 31 July-1 August, then scuttled both in the mouth of the
Dvina to block it (Kedrov, Za Sovetskii sever, 1927; Mints and Gorodetskii, 1941); the cruiser Attentive was off
Archangel on 3 August notwithstanding; raised afterwards (Kemp, London Gazette). It does NOT mention Alexander.
WHAT THIS DOES TO THE SUMMARY RIK SUPPLIED: (1) Alexander was NOT in the August advance and Sviatogor was not sunk "to
keep HMS Alexander out": the official account sends Alexander to Murmansk before it. DO NOT USE that version. (2) The
real confrontation is EARLIER and better: about 2 July 1918, at Archangel, Alexander was surrounded by armed vessels and
shore batteries in an "unprovoked hostile demonstration", and came away. historicalrfa's "10 July 1918 ... came face
to face with the heavily-armed SVIATOGOR ... conflict was avoided" is very probably the same incident, with a different
date and the armed vessel named; the despatch names no vessel. Prose follows the despatch (date "two days before" 4
July; no Sviatogor) unless a second source names her. (3) Albert's Alexander entry runs from 27 February 1918 with no
break: her presence at Archangel with the food transports by 4 July, the demonstration about 2 July, and the return to
Murmansk are all his (plan DECIDE 8). Whether she brought the transports through the ice in April is NOT in the excerpt
read: to check on the saved pages before it is said.
