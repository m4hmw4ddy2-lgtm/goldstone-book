## THE WEEK, PINNED TO SOURCES FIRST

The shape proposed on 22 September was written from the notes. Every date in it has now been checked
against a named source, per standing rule one, and the sequence holds with one correction and one new
contradiction.

- **Wednesday 8 August.** The murders. The arrest. Both twins dead by dawn. (Chapters Seven to Nine.)
- **Thursday 9 August.** 8.30 a.m., charged with all five (Folkard). Stratford Petty Sessions, first
  appearance. Ada gives evidence. **"Then the prisoner will be remanded till Saturday, and in the
  meantime further inquiries will be made"** (Mr Lister, Daily Telegraph, 10 August; Pall Mall Gazette,
  9 August: "The prisoner was remanded till Saturday"). Ada's JP witness statement is sworn this day.
- **Friday 10 August, afternoon.** The inquest opens, **at the board room of the Walthamstow Cemetery**,
  before Mr Charles Carne Lewis, coroner for the southern division of Essex. The Times and The Evening
  News, both 11 August: "held an inquest yesterday afternoon at the board room of the Walthamstow
  Cemetery". Three witnesses: Ada Hamilton, Emma Clarke, PC Cheeseman (Morning Post, "on the last
  occasion three witnesses were called"). Ada "repeated the evidence she gave on the previous day before
  the magistrates" (The Times), which independently confirms the Thursday hearing. Ada's coroner
  deposition is sworn this day, the day after her witness statement. Then the view of the house, with
  Elizabeth in the front room. Adjourned. Blyth is not at the view.
- **Saturday 11 August.** Second Stratford hearing, on the remand set by Lister. The crowd; the walk from
  the station; Ada recalled and her deposition read over; the cord held up for identification and William
  pointing to his throat; the hammer produced in a neck handkerchief; Inspector Craggs asking a week
  because "The Treasury will take up the prosecution"; Atkinson deferring his cross-examination;
  remanded a week; the escape through the coffee shop. Reported in the Daily Chronicle of 13 August, the
  Dover Express of 17 August and the IPN of 18 August.
- **Sunday 12 August, morning.** Blyth at Elizabeth's bedside.
- **Monday 13 August.** The funeral at noon, moved forward three hours. Then, **that same afternoon, in
  the same cemetery**, the resumed inquest: Lyle's protest, Blyth's admission, Smith, Bryant, Folkard,
  Gould, the summing-up, the verdict of wilful murder, the coroner's warrant.

**CORRECTION to carry forward.** The proposed shape had the inquest opening and the view happening
together on the Friday and the resumption on the Monday, which is right, but the project's notes have
nowhere recorded that the opening was in the AFTERNOON and at the BOARD ROOM, while the resumption was at
the LODGE. Whether these are two descriptions of one building or two different rooms is **open**; do not
have the prose move them around without settling it.

**NEW CONTRADICTION (44): when the inquest resumed.** The Dover Express of 17 August: "The adjourned
inquest was resumed the following morning at the lodge of the cemetery." The Morning Post: "At the Lodge
of the Walthamstow Cemetery yesterday afternoon, Mr. Charles Carne Lewis... resumed the inquiry."
Afternoon is the better reading and the Dover Express is wrong, for two independent reasons: the Morning
Post is a daily reporting the previous day, and the funeral was moved forward from three o'clock to noon
precisely "in order to prevent the assemblage of a large crowd while the proceedings at the inquest were
progressing", which only makes sense if the inquest sat after the burial on the same day. The Dover
Express is a weekly stitching agency copy together and its "following morning" follows nothing that
works. **Use the afternoon. Log and move on.**

## THE FACT THE CHAPTER IS BUILT ON

They buried the five children at noon on Monday the thirteenth of August, and that same afternoon, in the
lodge of the same cemetery, a jury returned a verdict of wilful murder against their father. The crowd
moved between the two. Dover Express, of the resumed inquest: "Crowds of people congregated in the
cemetery, many inspecting the flower-covered grave of the five children, and others lingering outside the
small lodge while the inquest was going on."

The grave was already covered in flowers when the jury sat down. That is the chapter, and it needs no
commentary whatever.

## THE SHAPE — six movements, chronological

**1. Thursday 9 August: Stratford.** He is charged with all five at half past eight in the morning, and
by the afternoon he is in a dock. The book's first sight of the machinery. Ada gives evidence for the
first of what will be four times, and this is the version sworn earliest. Lister: remanded till Saturday,
"and in the meantime further inquiries will be made."
DECIDE: whether the post-mortems belong here or at the inquest, where Gould gives their findings. Gould's
own deposition says "Next day I made a post-mortem examination of the babies", and the babies died on the
evening of the 8th and at two on the morning of the 9th, so the post-mortem is the 9th or the 10th and
the notes should not state which. Recommend: hold the findings for Gould's evidence on the Monday, where
they are sourced to a moment, rather than staging a scene we cannot date.

**2. Friday 10 August: the inquest opens, and the jury walks through the house.** Ada, Emma, Cheeseman,
in the board room of the cemetery. Then twelve men and a coroner in the rooms where it happened, testing
whether three children could have been drowned in that cistern. Elizabeth is through the wall. The
coroner's own defence of the visit, given three days later, is the line to end the movement on: "so that
Mrs. Gouldstone, lying ill in the front room, really did not know what was going on."

**3. Saturday 11 August: the crowd.** The first appearance of the public as a mob rather than a readership.
Made to walk half a mile from the station because nobody thought to order a cab; not recognised at first,
then hooted the whole way. The cord held up, and William pointing to his own throat. The hammer, produced
in a neck handkerchief. The Treasury taking over. The cab, the coffee shop, the short cut to the station.
DECIDE: the Dover Express's "By some thoughtlessness the police, instead of bringing Gouldstone from the
station to the court in a cab, made him walk the distance of about half a mile" is press, not evidence.
Under the press rule it can be quoted as a newspaper. Recommend quoting it as the Dover Express, since
the paper's judgement of the police is itself part of what the public was told.

**4. Sunday 12 August: Blyth.** A juryman at the bed of a woman eleven days from a confinement, telling
her the verdict is decided, that her husband will hang or be as good as dead to her, and that the jury
blames her. He calls it a visit "of a kindly nature, to see if he could be of any service". Set it down
flat. No adjectives.

**5. Monday 13 August, noon: the funeral.** Five elm coffins, one grave in the third-class portion.
Grantham Robinson officiating, his first appearance beside Elizabeth. Mrs Graves; the wife of one of
William's uncles, and her daughter. The hour moved forward to outrun the crowd, and the crowd arriving
anyway. Children carrying flowers in baskets. Elizabeth is not mentioned in any account of it.
DECIDE: the grave's depth is Contradiction 42 (6ft against 10ft) and attendance is Contradiction 7 (1,500
against several hundred). Recommend: give neither figure. The elm coffins and the third-class portion
carry the class point without a number that we would have to defend.

**6. Monday 13 August, afternoon: the verdict.** The same cemetery. The flower-covered grave outside the
window. Lyle's letter read aloud by Lyle himself. Blyth: "Well, they might have been. I do not know."
The coroner repudiating it on behalf of the court, and then: "We will now proceed with the case." Smith,
Bryant, Folkard, Gould. The coroner's direction that sanity "was a subject that the jury had nothing to
do with, but which concerned another tribunal". The jury returning wilful murder "at once".
**End the chapter on the last line of the Dover Express's report, which is the paper's own closing
sentence after the verdict on five murdered children: "It is stated that Mrs. Gouldstone is on the whole
progressing satisfactorily towards recovery."** Under the press rule, attributed. It does the Elizabeth
thread's work in one borrowed sentence and the prose adds nothing to it.

## ELIZABETH THROUGH THE CHAPTER

She appears three times and speaks none of them. Through a wall on the Friday, while twelve men establish
that she did not know they were there. In her bed on the Sunday, being told by one of them what the jury
thinks of her. And on the Monday, absent from her children's funeral and spoken for at the inquest by her
doctor, in a letter, read aloud by him, in a room she is not in.

Chapter Eight has already said the thing once, plainly: "Nobody who was in the house that night described
under oath what Elizabeth went through." It is not repeated. This chapter demonstrates it instead.
