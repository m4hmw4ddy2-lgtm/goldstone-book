## 1. THE HEADLINE FINDING — EVERY ANSELL CHILD PREDECEASED JEMIMA

Continuing from the 14 July resolution (Jemima's parentage, first marriage), this session traced the fate of every one of Jemima's children by John Ansell, using a compiled secondary tree as a lead list, then independently confirming (or correcting) each entry against St Mary Magdalene, Wethersfield burial register D/P 119/1/7.

**Result: eight children, not seven or six as previously recorded. All eight died before Jemima did.**

| Child | Born | Died/buried | Age at death |
|---|---|---|---|
| John Nathan | 19 May 1834 | buried 19 Jul 1856 (as "Nathan") | 22 |
| Sarah | 4 Aug 1835 | buried 6 Mar 1844 | 8 |
| Elizabeth ("Eliza") | 4 Aug 1835 | buried 11 Aug 1845 | 10 |
| Alfred | c.1838 | buried 1 Jun 1846 | 8 |
| George | 19 May 1839 | buried 11 Jul 1858 | 19 |
| Emma | c.1840 | buried 2 Nov 1861 | 21 |
| Arthur | 9 Jul 1843 (bapt.) | buried 29 Apr 1844 | 0 |
| Edith | b. posthumously, ~Apr–Jun 1845 | buried 24 Feb 1847 | 1 |

**John Ansell (husband/father) died 19 April 1845, aged 32** — between Sarah/Arthur's deaths (spring 1844) and Elizabeth's (August 1845). Edith was born after his death.

**The "Eliza" resolution**: the 1841 census entry for "Eliza, 6" was long treated as a possible eighth, unaccounted-for child, or a transcription duplicate of Sarah. Resolved this session via a nonconformist baptism record (RG4/1768, Congregational or Independent, Wethersfield): Elizabeth Ansell, born 4 August 1835 — the exact same date as Sarah's own baptism entry, one line below Sarah's on the same register page (52). Same birth date does not necessarily mean twins were the case; treat as same-day births or same-date recording, not confirmed twins. "Eliza" is a diminutive of Elizabeth, not a ninth or separate child. This closes the "Eliza, Sarah, and Alfred unaccounted for" note from the 14 July session update.

**Note on Elizabeth Ansell's name**: Jemima's first daughter with John Ansell was baptised Elizabeth. Her second daughter, by Thomas Stock, born 20 years later, was also Elizabeth — the Elizabeth this book is about. Possibly coincidence (a common name), possibly not. No evidence either way. Flagged as a fact worth having, not a claim to make in prose.

### Why this matters for the book
By November 1861 (when Elizabeth Stock, the second Elizabeth, was six years old), every one of Jemima's eight children by her first marriage was dead. Elizabeth was the sole survivor of nine births across Jemima's two marriages. This gives real potential weight to the Liverpool Mercury's 1883 line, "her father and mother are both dead, and she has no near relatives whatever" — previously treated as likely journalistic shorthand, now potentially closer to literally true, at least as regards the entire Ansell branch. (Caveat: Jemima's own Suckling birth family had multiple living siblings across the relevant decades — this finding does not establish Elizabeth had *no* living blood relatives anywhere, only that this specific, closest branch was extinct.)

## 2. BREWERY HOUSE — FULL HOUSEHOLD MAPPED, 1861

Confirmed this session: Brewery House, Wethersfield (where Thomas, Jemima, six-year-old Elizabeth, and old John Ansell Sr lived in 1861) was a shared dwelling housing **four separate working families**, not the Stock household alone:

- **Thomas Raven** (46, b. Cressing), farmer of 100 acres, maltster, and brewer, all three trades combined in one operation — with two sisters (Sarah, 53; Jane, 44) keeping house, a nephew (Thomas, 19, already a brewer), and a visitor.
- **George Love**, journeyman brewer (working under Raven), with Elizabeth Love, a widow, very likely his mother (not explicitly stated in the record — treat as probable, not confirmed).
- **William and Ann Westwood** — residents, not visitors (corrected this session from an earlier draft that mistakenly called William a visitor). William, master shoemaker, independently confirmed in White's 1863 Directory of Essex ("Boot and Shoemakers... William Westwood"). Ann, straw hat maker, also independently listed in the same directory.
- **The Stocks**: Thomas, Jemima, Elizabeth (6, scholar), plus John Ansell Sr (85, blind, mislabelled "father-in-law").

### The brewery itself, and why it is NOT connected to John Ansell
Thomas Raven's Wethersfield Brewery (Braintree Road) was founded **c.1855–58** — independently confirmed via the Brewery History Society wiki ("founded by 1855") and a Wethersfield local history newsletter article ("about 150 years ago" from a 2007/8 publication; the brewery was owned by the Maberly family, "Mrs Maberly['s] maiden name being Raven"; the surviving Village Hall building was the maltings; Thomas Raven recorded as maltster from 1848). **This postdates John Ansell's own brewing occupation (recorded 1841) by over a decade**, and Ansell had already lost his own trade by the 1851 census (recorded as Ag Lab, no longer Brewer). The two brewing operations are unconnected. "Brewery House" almost certainly takes its name from Raven's operation specifically, not from any general village brewing tradition, and not from Ansell.

A separate, later "Dog Brewery" (High Street, recorded 1895, run by a Thomas William Raven — a different, later Raven) was also found and ruled out as connected to John Ansell for the same chronological reason.

### An AI-sourced theory that did NOT hold up
A speculative narrative (from a different AI tool) proposing that John Ansell ran an independent "beerhouse" on Dog Lane, distinct from Brewery House, was checked and found to rest on no actual source beyond a modern real-estate listing page (used to support a claim about 1841 geography, which it cannot do) and an unsourced, invented date. One factual element in it ("Wethersfield Brewery... founded around 1855") turned out to be independently correct once checked against the Brewery History Society wiki — but the specific claim about Ansell's own beerhouse remains entirely unsupported. Do not use.

### Where exactly John Ansell Sr's own brewing business was located: still unresolved
His 1841 household sits on a census page where the address shifts from "Village" to "Dog Lane" — suggestive of the general area, not a confirmed address. No trade directory from the 1840s has been found (trade directories of this period only list tradesmen/gentry with occupations given, not addresses, and rarely cover small-village labourers or minor brewers in enough detail).

## 3. JOHN ANSELL SR — FULL ARC NOW SOURCED

- **1841**: head of his own household, Wethersfield, "Village"/Dog Lane area — Brewer, wife Elizabeth (59), son John Jr (28, Ag Lab, Jemima's husband), Jemima (29), and six of their eventual eight children.
- **1845**: son John Jr dies, aged 32.
- **1851**: widower (wife Elizabeth had died in the interim), occupation now Ag Lab — trade and household both lost — living in Jemima's household (Dog Chase, Wethersfield) rather than she in his.
- **1861**: 85, blind, living in Jemima and Thomas Stock's household at Brewery House, recorded as "father-in-law" though he shared no blood with Thomas Stock at all — he was the father of Jemima's *first* husband.

A man who went from head of his own brewing household to blind dependent, inside twenty years, all of it (as far as the record shows) within the same small area of the village.

## 4. DANIEL SUCKLING — UNRESOLVED, FLAGGED

Possible child or grandchild of Edward and Sarah Suckling (Jemima's parents). 1851 census, Finchingfield, "Son," age transcribed as 3 (giving implied birth ~1848) or possibly 13 (giving ~1838) — a possible stroke before the digit in the primary image was reported but is genuinely ambiguous.

- If born 1848: Sarah (b. c.1790) would have been ~58 — biologically implausible.
- If born 1838: plausible, but his own 1871 census return (self-reported, age 23) independently corroborates birth ~1848, the "implausible" reading, not the "plausible" one.
- No birth or baptism record for a Daniel Suckling has been found in Finchingfield under either date, despite every other child in this extended family turning up in a parish or nonconformist register this session.

**Working theory, unconfirmed**: Daniel is a grandson, not a son, with the 1851 relation label simplified by the enumerator — directly paralleling the John Ansell Sr "father-in-law" mislabelling above. Not resolved. Not load-bearing for Elizabeth's story — flagged for a future session only if useful.

## 5. SARAH (EDWARD SUCKLING'S WIFE) — "CRAWLEY" FORMALLY RETRACTED

Extensive further checking this session (a compiled tree offering a full 11-child sibling list under "Sarah") did not turn up any source for the surname "Crawley." The compiled tree itself shows signs of merging two different mothers under one "Sarah" profile (the Daniel Suckling age problem above being the clearest symptom). **"Sarah" alone, birthplace Cranham, Essex, is the confirmed position.** Do not use "Crawley" going forward.

Individually cross-checked and confirmed as real, distinct people from this same compiled sibling list (via primary census records pulled independently across this and the 14 July session): Robert (b. 1829, the 1854 wedding witness), Ellen, and John Suckling. The remaining names on the list (Jane, George, William, Sarah, Elizabeth, Martha, Edward Jr, Daniel) are not independently confirmed as Sarah's children specifically and should be treated with the same caution as Daniel above unless/until checked.
