## A. WHITSTABLE

Searched: "madams" in the Whitstable Times and Herne Bay Herald, 1885-1910 (40 results, all read) and the Herne Bay
Press, 1890-1906 (57, all read); "madams" in Kent papers, April-July 1896 and June 1898 to August 1899; "goldstone"
with "whitstable", 1885-1910 (60) and "goldstone" in Kent, April 1901 (5).

Already held, found again (no change): Herne Bay Press, 2 May 1896, p. 6 (the attendance summons, "William Madams
was similarly summoned in respect to his son aged eight"); Whitstable Times and Canterbury Journal, 25 June 1898
(proceedings ordered); the Band of Hope, February 1899; 19 August 1899 (final warnings; "Albert Madams"); 6 April 1901
(Elizabeth, "of Middle Wall"; the Canterbury Journal's text is the same copy, one witness); Folkestone Chronicle and
Herne Bay Press, 13 April 1901, and Herne Bay Press, 20 April 1901 (the sea boots; the Herne Bay Press of 13 April
misprints "Alfred Goldstone, aged 14").

NEW:
1. **No. 42 Island Wall, "in the occupation of Mrs. Madams", sold at auction, August 1899.**
   - Whitstable Times, 12 August 1899, p. 1 (BL/0000338/18990812, p. 1, art. 067), the sale notice (Mr J. T. Reeves):
     Lot 2, a messuage "with Glasshouse, and the Appurtenances thereto, situate and being No. 42, Island Wall,
     Whitstable, in the occupation of Mrs. Madams, at the weekly rent of 3s. 3d."
   - Whitstable Times, 19 August 1899, p. 8 (BL/0000338/18990819, p. 8, art. 055), "PROPERTY SALES BY MR. J. T.
     REEVES": "Lot 2, a timber built messuage, with glass house, etc., No. 42, Island Wall, in the occupation of Mrs.
     Madams at the weekly rent of 3s. 6d., was purchased by Mr. T. Gill for £105."
   - The two give different rents (3s. 3d.; 3s. 6d.): to read on the images.
   - WHICH MRS MADAMS is NOT shown. Elizabeth was "Mrs Madams" in Whitstable (1891 census; Lilian's certificate,
     1890), at 41 Harbour Street in 1891 and in Middle Wall in 1901; Beach Alley (1890) runs between Island Wall and
     Middle Wall (CHAPTER_19_skeleton.md). But William Madams was living in 1899, and the occupier is the wife, which
     is odd unless he was away; and there were other Madams households in the town (George Madams, 1891 and 1894;
     John Madams, 1901). A lead only. To settle it: the 1899 rate book or electoral register for Island Wall; the
     Whitstable directory.
   - If it is Elizabeth's home, it is the third family home in the book sold over the occupiers' heads (the Stocks'
     cottage, 1861; Thomas Gouldstone's house and smithy, 1867). Noted, not concluded.

## B. CHATHAM: ALBERT GOLDSTONE AND THE COUNCIL ELECTIONS (live_lists.md, section 4, item 43)

Searched: "goldstone" in the Chatham, Rochester and Brompton Observer, 1905-1962 (all results, 141 to mid-1934 and 70
after, read in snippet form; football, wrestling, F. W. Goldstone MP, Goldstone Farm, Ash, Hove and Gravesend
athletes set aside).

1. **1933, Luton ward.**
   - 29 September 1933, p. 7 (BL/<Observer>/19330929, p. 7, art. 180): "The only Labour candidate at present selected
     is Mr A. Goldstone, the propaganda secretary, who will oppose Mr. Packer at Christ Church."
   - 13 October 1933, p. 7 (art. 162): Packer having become Mayor-elect, "Mr Goldstone will contest Luton ward".
   - 27 October 1933, p. 7 (art. 114), the nominations: "LUTON WARD. GOLDSTONE, Albert (Labour), 46, Edinburgh road,
     Chatham, ship rigger in H.M. Dockyard: proposed by Robert Elliott Russell, seconded by William Thomas McCully"
     (OCR "MeCully"). AGE 46 in October 1933: born 1887, the true year (Lambeth, 1887), NOT the naval 1886. To read on
     the image before anything rests on it.
   - 3 November 1933, p. 7 (art. 144): result, Silver (Cons.) 700, Goldstone (Labour) 665 (already held, "lost by 35").
     And after the count: "Mr Goldstone said: 'I shall live to fight again. Let us try and organise a little better
     and we shall win next time. If I can get a band ...'" (OCR; the rest to read).
2. **1934.** 7 September 1934, p. 6: "Another chosen Labour candidate is Mr. Albert Goldstone, who last year was
   beaten by 35 votes in a close contest with Clr. G. B. Silver, in Luton Ward." 19 October and 2 November 1934:
   already held (Christ Church, 505 to 832).
3. **No candidacy found for November 1935 to 1938, or 1945 to 1949**, in this paper's OCR (it is weak; not proof).
4. **The Luton Working Men's Club.**
   - 28 January 1939: "the club's secretary, Mr. A. Goldstone", at the ladies' dinner at Luton Parochial Hall,
     "outlined the growth of the club from ... 1931" and new premises "within the next two years".
   - 6 April 1945, p. 3 (art. 105): "Albert Goldstone, secretary of the Luton Working Men's Club, told the court that
     no coal was ordered after February 7th" (a coal case).
   - 16 January 1953, p. 4: "Club secretary, Mr. A Goldstone" (which club not in the snippet).
   - 2 November 1945, p. 8: "A. Goldstone, 155, Dale St Chatham", in a notice about a transfer "to the new proprietor"
     (a licensing or business notice; to read).
   Identification: "Albert Goldstone" of Luton (Chatham), 1945, is very probably Albert (Luton ward, 1933); the 1939
   and 1953 "A. Goldstone" are likely the same; 155 Dale Street to compare with his known addresses (Edinburgh Road,
   1933). NOT established.
5. **Possibly the family, not identified:** "Lily A. R. Goldstone, Luton Council [School]" (1 August 1924, p. 8,
   a scholarship or school list: Lily Alma Rose was born 1913-14); "Vera Goldstone" (Luton Council School and
   elocution lists, 1931-1937); "Irene Goldstone", Luton's May Queen (1933); "Mr. Bert Goldstone", manager of a
   concert party (22 January 1926, p. 2; 4 December 1925, p. 15; 30 April 1926, p. 9: "Bert" may be Albert, or
   another); "Messrs A. Goldstone and B. B. Burkett, recorders" (3 August 1928, p. 3).
   Vera Georgina Goldstone (born 1 March 1921) is Rik's grandmother; the "Vera Goldstone" of the Luton school lists
   is very likely she. Lily Alma Rose ("Auntie Lil") likewise. For Rik to judge; none of it is checked.
6. **No obituary or death notice for Albert, 1962**, found in this paper's OCR.
