## The text, as written

This woman, my great-great grandmother who had survived every possible torment and abjecture that life
could throw at her: the loss her siblings before she turned x, her whole family gone but he time she was
y, giving birth to five children, raising them as best she could despite her poverty and their demands. A
woman who saw them all murdered, some right before her eyes in the most vicious ways imaginable. She
survived the public approbation, the navigated her lost years in Lambeth, there giving birth to the boy
that would survive, father unknown. Eventually finding a life an a home in a marriage never made under the
law and raising a family to adulthood. Her partner taken from her, ending his days in yet another asylum
before she herself succumbed to the ravages of syphilis.

This woman may have been obliged to live and eventually die in silence, her own history buried and
reinvented by her son, out of fear and shame. And the crime that shocked a nation faded with the ink on the
pages of the Victorian newspapers.

She could not tell her own story at the time, so I am telling it for her now. Elizabeth Stock was a
survivor, a mother, and deserves to be remembered.

## Where this belongs

Claude's reading, for Rik to accept or reject: this is Part Six or the Coda, in his own voice, and the last
three sentences are a candidate for the closing of the book. The causal-chain argument is deliberately
cold. It turns five murdered children into a mechanism, which is what gives it its force. This paragraph is
the counterweight, and without something doing its job the book risks being admired for its structure. Note
that it makes a different claim from the standing one. "William Gouldstone is not my ancestor, he is the
reason I exist" says why the book exists. This says why it was worth writing.

## The two numbers

- **x = six.** Elizabeth's mother Jemima had eight children by her first marriage, to John Ansell. Every one
  of them predeceased her. The last, Emma, was buried on 2 November 1861, when Elizabeth (b. 18 February
  1855) was six years and eight months old. Elizabeth was the only child of nine births across Jemima's two
  marriages to survive into adulthood. Sources: St Mary Magdalene burial register D/P 119/1/7, all eight
  entries; see dramatis_personae.md, Elizabeth's entry.
- **y = eighteen.** Jemima Stock died 25 October 1873; Thomas Stock died 22 December 1873. Both GRO death
  certificates. Elizabeth was eighteen at both, and they died within two months of each other.

Also available, and stronger than a number: the Liverpool Mercury of 10 August 1883 wrote that "her father
and mother are both dead, and she has no near relatives whatever." The project's own research suggests this
may have been literally true rather than reportorial shorthand.

## Four points of fact, for Rik, not corrections to the prose

1. **"succumbed to the ravages of syphilis" is sourced.** Her death certificate gives syphilis, of two
   years' standing, certified by Jno. W. Hayward, M.R.C.S. **But** the sentence as ordered — Madams dying
   of General Paralysis of the Insane, then her dying of syphilis — assigns the direction of transmission
   by adjacency without stating it. The project's own standing position is that direction cannot be
   determined and the book must not assign one: syphilis has a latency of years to decades, Madams could
   have been infected long before he met her, and Elizabeth has four undocumented years, 1883 to 1887.
   State only that both died of the same disease after thirteen years together. This is a note about the
   ordering of two clauses, not about whether the fact is true.
2. **She did not die alone.** The informant on the certificate is Lilian Madams, her daughter, "present at
   death", same address, Beach Alley, Whitstable. The paragraph ends in isolation, and this one detail in
   the record cuts against it. Worth knowing before deciding whether to use it.
3. **"father unknown" is exactly true of the document** — the space on Albert's birth certificate is
   blank — but the project's own position is stronger than that: William James Madams is "almost certainly"
   Albert's biological father. If the prose wants both, the honest form is the blank space itself rather
   than the phrase.
4. **The daughter emigrated and the DNA came back.** Eliza Jane Madams left for New Zealand on 29 June
   1927. Her descendant Bronwyn Joule is the MyHeritage match, found 10 September 2026, that proves the
   Stock/Gouldstone/Madams identity chain genetically rather than documentarily. The woman whose history
   was buried and reinvented is the one the DNA confirms.

## Two typos, flagged and deliberately not fixed in the text above

- "public approbation" — approbation is approval; the word wanted is opprobrium.
- "but he time she was y" for "by the time she was y"; "the navigated" for "then navigated"; "a life an a
  home" for "a life and a home".
