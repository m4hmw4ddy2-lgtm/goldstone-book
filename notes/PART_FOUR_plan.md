## FINDINGS FROM TODAY'S READING
1. **Elizabeth acts in the record, once, in her own name.** On Eliza Jane's certificate the informant is "E. Madams,
   Mother": she registered the birth herself and gave her own history as "Elizabeth Madams late Goldstone formerly
   Stock". After 1883, the only document in which she speaks for herself. Whether she signed or made a mark cannot be
   told from a certified copy (the registrar's hand). This is the Part's turn, as the statement was Part Three's.
2. **Eliza Jane's certificate names William Madams as her father** (column 4). Albert's names no one. The 1891 census
   calls Albert "Stepson". Nothing new on Albert's paternity; set side by side, no comment (Contradiction 88).
3. **"Harbour Place", not Harbour Street,** is Eliza Jane's birthplace and Elizabeth's address in September and
   October 1891, five months after the census at 41 Harbour Street. UPDATED 10 October 2026 (Rik's lead): Harbour Place
   was a separate street. Postcards Then and Now, "Whitstable, Kent, Harbour Place c1910" (blog, 2012): "now called
   Woodlawn Street"; a reader's comment, quoting an unnamed book, gives "Sydenham Street" instead and places Waverley
   House at "15 Harbour Place", with the New Inn and St Peter's Sunday School beside it. So the family very probably
   MOVED between April and September 1891. MAP, 10 October 2026 (Rik; NLS, OS 25-inch 1892-1914 layer, screenshot saved as
   sources/images/Whitstable_OS_25inch_1892-1914_Harbour_Place_NLS.png): HARBOUR PLACE is a terraced street east of the
   old town, between Fountain Street and Sydenham Street, crossed by St Peter's Road; HARBOUR STREET is the old street
   running down to the harbour by Sea Street, some way west. Two different streets: the move is confirmed by the map.
   Sydenham Street is shown as a separate street, so the blog comment's "now Sydenham Street" is wrong. Harbour Place
   appears to continue east of St Peter's Road as WOODLAWN TERRACE, which fits the blog's "now called Woodlawn Street"
   (inference from the map; a modern map check would settle it). Beach Alley and Middle Wall are not on this crop.
   MIDDLE WALL (Rik, 10 October 2026; same layer, saved as
   sources/images/Whitstable_OS_25inch_1892-1914_Middle_Wall_High_St_NLS.png): the narrow street running parallel to
   High Street on its west side, its name lettered spaced out along it (M..D..E..W..A readable), with a chapel on it and
   Gladstone Road opposite on the east side of High Street. So it is in the old town centre, back from the main street.
   BEACH ALLEY (Elizabeth's death address, 29 June 1906): Rik, 10 October 2026, marked an UNLABELLED alley on the
   same layer with a black line (saved as sources/images/Whitstable_OS_25inch_1892-1914_Beach_Alley_marked_NLS.png;
   map readout about OS 610559, lat 51.3595). It runs east-west between the diagonal street on the west (P.H. on it)
   and the street running parallel to High Street. Rik: it joins ISLAND WALL (the diagonal street) and MIDDLE WALL. So
   the house where she died in 1906 was a few yards from the Middle Wall house of the 1901 census. The map does not
   name the alley: TO CONFIRM from a directory, the 1911 census or a local-history
   source before prose; and confirm that the parallel street on this crop is Middle Wall. Earlier note, kept: a second
   crop (same layer, saved as
   sources/images/Whitstable_OS_25inch_1892-1914_Beach_Walk_Road_NLS.png) shows BEACH WALK and BEACH ROAD running
   north to the shore east of the harbour and station, either side of the almshouses, towards Tankerton. Beach Alley is
   not labelled there. Do NOT equate Beach Alley with Beach Walk: an alley may be too small for the 25-inch map to name,
   and it may lie elsewhere (the old town had many named alleys). TO CONFIRM from a Kelly's directory, the 1901 or 1911
   census, or a local-history source. The modern name is disputed (Woodlawn or Sydenham Street): TO CONFIRM from
   a directory or OS map before prose (live_lists 4, item 33). Prose needs only "Harbour Place".
