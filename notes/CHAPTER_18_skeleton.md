## FINDINGS

1. **Is there enough for a chapter? Just, if it is short.** The documentary material is one day and its morrow, from
   two documents, of which the Prologue has already spent the headline facts (date, place, registration next day,
   the mother's name and address, the blank father's column). What is new: the register opening and its counts; the
   form of the lines; "Illegitimate"; "Albert Jennet"; the informant, S. Frost, "Present at the birth", whose
   residence is the infirmary; the registrar; no maiden surname for the mother. Honest length under the governing
   test: **about 400 to 650 words**, against the Part plan's 700 to 1,200 and Chapter Seventeen's 870. Not "far too
   short" in my judgement (DECIDE A), because the brevity is the record's and the thread asks for it.
2. **The count:** 17 entries, 11 illegitimate. Albert is one of two births entered on 19 June, both illegitimate.
3. **The form of the line is the form of every illegitimate line.** On this opening each illegitimate entry gives the
   mother's name alone, with no former surname and no remark; each legitimate entry gives both parents, the mother's
   former surname, and the father's trade under Remarks. Albert's line has nothing that any other illegitimate line
   lacks. The prose must not make the blanks in his line particular to him.
4. **"When Baptized" is blank for all 17**, so it says nothing about Albert; "In what Name Baptized" is where every
   child's given names stand. Prose must not say he was unbaptised, or baptised. (Correction C1.)
5. **No maiden surname on the certificate.** Column 5 asks for "Name, surname and maiden surname of mother" and gives
   "Elizabeth Goldston" only: no "formerly Stock". It is the form of Mabel Gouldstone's 1873 entry (timeline file,
   Contradiction 16), which the project reads as the usual form for an unmarried mother. Elizabeth was not the
   informant, so the entry cannot tell us who said what. Distinct from the ruled spelling point (that is about the
   letters of a name; this is a whole column's content). It sets up Chapter Nineteen, where she herself registers
   Eliza Jane as "late Goldstone formerly Stock". DECIDE D.
6. **S. Frost:** the informant's "residence" on the entry is the infirmary itself. That is all: no sex, no post, no
   full name. In prose: no pronoun for Frost, no "nurse", no "she" (the archive's "a woman ... named Frost" is an
   assumption). The certified copy is in the copying clerk's hand, so "signature" means nothing here.
7. **Elizabeth is not the informant.** Her son's birth goes into the civil register through someone else, "Present at
   the birth". In 1883 men spoke about her; here someone of unknown name and sex speaks for her, in four words.
8. **Two numbers for one birth:** 105 in the workhouse's register, 113 in the civil register. Nothing to make of it.
9. **Things not to make anything of:** the twins on the page (Kate Curd's, 24 June: an echo of 1883 the record does
   not make; ours would be planted); "Setterfield" at entry 110 (not Setterington); the register's heading "in
   Lambeth Workhouse" against the certificate's "Workhouse Infirmary, Brook Street" (two documents' own usages, both
   quotable as they stand); the spelling "Goldston" (ruled).
10. **Legal status (general knowledge, NOT for prose):** Elizabeth was still William's wife in 1887, and the register
    and certificate enter the child as a single woman's. The law of legitimacy that bears on this (presumption, and
    the husband's non-access) is outside the repo; the prose needs none of it, since the reader knows where William
    is. Stillbirths were not then registrable (hence no initials on 114, 116, 118): also general knowledge, also not
    for prose.

## THE ELIZABETH THREAD

The thread inverted (thread file, section 6): no one writes about her at all. She is a name in two columns, entered
by others: "Elizabeth Goldston" as mother, in a book kept by the workhouse and in a register signed by George Hearson,
on the word of S. Frost. She has no age, no occupation, no husband and no maiden name on either page. She is
"Elizabeth" in our narration and nowhere does our narration say what she felt, chose, or knew. The four names of Part
Four (plan, thread section) begin here with the plainest: no "formerly".

## DECIDE POINTS

**DECIDE A. Length: is Lambeth a chapter?** (a) **Keep Chapter Eighteen, short, about 400 to 650 words**
(recommended: it is all the record holds, and a short chapter after three years and eight months of nothing does the
silence's work without a word of ours). (b) Reopen Part Four DECIDE 1: Lambeth becomes the opening movement of a
single Part Four chapter. (c) Hold Chapter Eighteen until LMA LABG/174/01 is seen (it could add an admission line;
it could add nothing).

**DECIDE B. How much of the opening.** (a) **Albert's line in full; the line above it (Mary Pratt, the same day)
named; the rest by count only** (recommended: one neighbour shows that he was not alone on the day; the counts show
the page; naming sixteen women would turn the chapter into a list). (b) Name every mother on the opening. (c) Albert's
line only, with the count.

**DECIDE C. The form of the lines (finding 3).** (a) **One plain sentence: the legitimate lines give both parents and
the father's trade; the illegitimate give the mother's name and nothing else** (recommended: it shows, without
comment, that Albert's blanks are the book's form for every child like him). (b) Leave it out; the count alone.

**DECIDE D. No maiden surname (finding 5).** (a) **Not remarked in Chapter Eighteen; Chapter Nineteen's "late
Goldstone formerly Stock" then stands on its own, and a reader who looks back sees the difference** (recommended:
Elizabeth was not the informant, so any sentence about the absence edges towards a motive). (b) State it once, as a
fact of the column, no comment. (c) Hold it for Chapter Nineteen and set the two entries side by side there.

**DECIDE E. The Prologue's facts.** Ruled not retold. Confirm that this includes the address, 2 John Street, Hercules
Buildings, and the blank father's column (both in the Prologue). (a) **Both left out** (recommended). (b) The address
given once, as the certificate gives it, because it is the only place her own life in Lambeth touches the record.

**DECIDE F. The ending.** (a) **On the registration: the informant's four words and George Hearson's name**
(recommended; chapters need not end on Elizabeth). (b) On the opening's last line, the eighth of July, the register
going on without her. (c) On the name, "Albert Jennet", in the column headed "In what Name Baptized".

**DECIDE G. The weekday.** (a) **"Sunday" for the nineteenth of June may be used** (calendar computation, recorded
above) (recommended, only if a sentence needs it). (b) No weekday. Nothing else from outside the record: the Golden
Jubilee celebrations of that week are general knowledge with no source in the repo, and would be decoration
(recommended out under the ruled rule, whatever is decided here).

**DECIDE H. Movements.** (a) **One movement, shown whole** (recommended at this length). (b) Two: the page; the
registration.
