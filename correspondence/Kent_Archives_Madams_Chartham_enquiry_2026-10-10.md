Subject: Kent County Lunatic Asylum, Chartham: a patient admitted in 1901 (William Madams)

Dear Archives team,

I am researching my family for a work of narrative non-fiction and would be grateful for help with the records of
the Kent County Lunatic Asylum at Chartham (later St Augustine's Hospital), if you hold them.

The patient:
- William Madams (William James Madams), a labourer of Whitstable, born in Whitstable about 1860.
- Admitted to the Kent County Lunatic Asylum, Chartham, in 1901 (4 September 1901, according to the index to the
  Lunacy Commission's admission registers, MH 94/37).
- Died in the asylum on 23 November 1901; cause given on his death certificate as general paralysis of the insane.

My questions:
1. Do you hold the asylum's admission register, case books and reception orders for 1901, and if so, could you
   tell me the references for his entries?
2. Are records of this date open to the public, or would any access conditions apply?
3. Do you offer a paid search and copying service for researchers working remotely, and what would it cost to have
   his admission entry, reception order and case book entry copied?
4. If the records are held elsewhere, could you point me to the right repository?

Any of these would be of great help: in particular who signed the reception order, the address he was admitted
from, and what was recorded of his family.

With thanks and best wishes,

Rik Ferguson


## REPLY, received 14 October 2026 (Dr Helen Wicker, Kent Archives; pasted by Rik into the session)

Dear Mr Ferguson,

Thank you for contacting Kent Archives regarding the records that we hold from Chartham Asylum/St Augustine's Hospital.

Kent Archives holds quite comprehensive patient records from this former hospital, including detailed medical records recording the history and progress of each patient's illness in the form of case notes. There are no surviving patient reception orders within the St Augustine's hospital collection, but the statements recorded on the reception order relating to the patient's mental state were copied into the case notes. Details of the patient's previous address and next of kin are usually also recorded. Case notes from St Augustine's at this date also often contain a photograph of the patient that were take on admission. Please note that due to the nature of the records (especially their detail and some of the terminology used), you may find their content upsetting, although they can also be very enlightening.

Prior to 1914, all patient case notes were compiled chronologically by date of admission. I would therefore anticipate that William Madams' case notes would be contained in one of the following volumes:

* MH/T3/Mc44 (1900-1901): Case book K: male patients (indexed)
* MH/T3/Mc45 (1901-1903): Case book L: male patients (indexed)

We also hold an Admission Register [ref. MH/T3/Ka7 (1897-1902)] and Patient Index Registers [refs. MH/T3/Kp14 ((1875)-1901), MH/T3/Kp15 ((1875)-1912)], although the details recorded here are likely to be replicated in the case notes. If you also wished to establish if William was buried in the Hospital's cemetery, then we hold a Burial Register covering the date of his death under ref. MH/T3/Kz2 (1875-1917).

As these records are over 100 years old, they are not subject to access restrictions and could be viewed in person in our archive searchroom, if you wished to visit. You can find advice about visiting and how to make a booking on our website, or we can advise by return email.

Alternatively, we can search the records and digitally copy the case notes as part of our paid research and reprographics service. We charge £50 per hour/£12.50 per 15 minutes for research. Depending upon how many of the records you would like to be covered from those that I have mentioned above, I would estimate that a search would take 15-30 minutes (£12.50-£25). Digital copies are charged at £10 for the first image and £4 for each subsequent image from the same record. I can let you know how much copies would cost once the Case Books have been searched, but as William was only at the hospital for a few months, I would not anticipate that his notes would cover more than a couple of pages.

If you would like to proceed, then could I please ask that you return a completed copy of the research form that I have attached? It is acceptable to type your name instead of signing the form if you wish to return it by email attachment. Payment is required before the full results of your research are released. You are welcome to pay by either cheque (made payable to 'Kent County Council') or credit/debit card. If you would like to give your card details over the telephone, then I can arrange to call you at a convenient time when the research is complete to process the payment.

Please let me know if you have any further questions.

Kind regards,
Helen
