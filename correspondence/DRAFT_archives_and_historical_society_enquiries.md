Companion to the cemeteries enquiry. Both written to be sent as they stand.

## 1. Waltham Forest Archives and Local Studies Library
**To:** wfa.enquiries@walthamforest.gov.uk
**Subject:** Research enquiry: Walthamstow, 1867 to 1883 — Helen Cottages, Courtenay Place, and the 1883 Gouldstone case

Dear Archives Team,

I am researching a book about a Walthamstow family and I would be grateful for your help with several
things held in your collections. I am happy to come in and do the work myself if an appointment is the
right way to go about it, and equally happy to pay for copies.

**Courtenay Place, St James's Street.** This is the one I would most like to get to the bottom of. Number
8 was the house in which five children of the Gouldstone family were killed on 8 August 1883, and it no
longer exists. What I have been able to establish so far is that the street does not appear in the 1881
census, that number 8 is there in 1891, that by 1901 the numbering runs 7 to 9 with nothing between, and
that the street has gone entirely by 1911. So it seems to have been built in the early 1880s and
demolished in the 1890s. I would be glad of anything that would confirm or correct that: rate books,
building control or drainage records, large-scale plans, street directories, photographs, and anything in
the W28 St James's Street buildings file. I have also seen it said that Courtenay Place was built on land
belonging to the Frampton family, and I would be grateful to know whether your records bear that out and
who in fact owned and developed that ground.

The house itself is worth describing, in case a plan or a rate-book entry can be matched to it. It was
occupied by three households. A carpenter and his wife held the tenancy and lived on the ground floor; one
family had a back room, a kitchen and a scullery on the first floor; and a third couple lodged in the front
room on the same floor. The scullery contained a cistern whose top stood about six feet from the floor.
Any description of the accommodation at that address, from any source, would be useful.

**Newspapers.** I would like the Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian for Saturday 11 August 1883. I have the
issues for 18 August, 1 September, 22 September, 29 September and 20 October, but not that one, which
should carry the opening of the inquest into the deaths of the five Gouldstone children.

**Helen Cottages.** A related family lived here from at least 1867. A baptism of that year gives their
address as **5 Helen Cottages**; the 1881 census has them at **2 Helen Cottages, James Road**; and a death
certificate of December 1882 gives the address simply as **Queens Road**. A much later official record
then attaches Helen Cottages to the Courtenay Place family, which is part of what I am trying to
disentangle. Anything that would fix the terrace on the ground would be valuable: **rate books for 1865 to
1885**, plans, estate maps and directories. I would also like to know how many houses the terrace
contained, when it went, and whether Queens Road, James Road and St James's Street ever overlapped in
naming.

**The cemetery buildings.** I am trying to establish which building at Queen's Road Cemetery was used for
inquests in the 1880s. Contemporary reports call it both "the board room of the Walthamstow Cemetery" and
"the lodge", and I understand there was an inquest room attached to the entrance lodge. **Walthamstow
Burial Board minutes** for the 1870s and 1880s, or any plan of the cemetery buildings, would settle it. I
would also like to know where the **mortuary** stood in 1883, since an Ordnance Survey sheet of the 1890s
marks one at the north-east corner off Exmouth Road, some distance from the entrance.

**Two biography files.** I understand you hold biography files on the **Gouldstone** case and on the
**Rev. Thomas Harrison Grantham Robinson**, first vicar of St Saviour's, who died in March 1884. I would
be glad of anything in either.

**A juror.** I am trying to identify **Joseph Blythe**, who sat on the coroner's jury in August 1883 and
whose occupation and address I do not have. If the rate books or directories would place him, that would
help. The full jury was William Smith (foreman), David Casey, Joseph Blythe, Walter Rollick, John
Patchett, Joseph Day, George Foxwell, George Banks, George Lloyd, Thomas Mumford, Stephen Archer, Edwin
Lee, William Haggett and William Weston.

**Parish registers.** Finally, if you hold them or can tell me who does, the **St Saviour's baptism
registers** for the 1870s, and any surviving parish papers of Grantham Robinson's.

I realise that is a long list. I am not expecting it answered in one go, and I would be glad of guidance
on what is best done in person.

With thanks for your time,

Richard Ferguson

## 2. Walthamstow Historical Society
**To:** whsenquiries@gmail.com
**Subject:** The Gouldstone case of 1883, and Helen Cottages

Dear Walthamstow Historical Society,

I am writing a book about a Walthamstow family and I am hoping some of your members may know things that
the records do not hold.

In August 1883 a whitesmith named William Gouldstone killed his five children at 8 Courtenay Place, off
St James's Street. The case filled the national press for two months, the inquest sat in the cemetery at
Queen's Road, and the children were buried there on 13 August. I have the contemporary newspaper
coverage, the trial papers and the coroner's depositions, so what I am short of is local knowledge rather
than documents.

Three things in particular.

**Helen Cottages.** A branch of the same family lived there from at least 1867, first at number 5 and
later at number 2. The 1881 census puts Helen Cottages in James Road; a death certificate of 1882 gives
the address as Queens Road. I would like to know exactly where that terrace stood, how many houses it
had, and when it went. Any photograph of it would be a considerable find.

**Courtenay Place.** It seems to have been built in the early 1880s and to have gone by the 1890s, and
the site is now a bus depot. I have seen it said that the ground belonged to the Frampton family, but I
have not been able to confirm that and would be glad to know either way. Again, any photograph, plan or
description would be very welcome.

**The cemetery buildings as they were in the 1880s.** Specifically which room the coroner used, and where
the mortuary stood.

If any of your members have worked on the case itself, I would be glad to hear from them, and glad to
share what I have. I have the press coverage transcribed, including the Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian's
own account of the funeral, which names the mourners.

With thanks,

Richard Ferguson

## Note on the Frampton question
"Built on the Framptons' estate" appears in this project's orientation document and **nowhere in the
notes**, so it is unverified and the email says "I have seen it said" rather than asserting it. Note also
that the only Frampton in our sourced records is **88 Frampton Park Road, Hackney**, the Graves household
from which William and Elizabeth married in 1879 and in which the three eldest boys were born. That is
Hackney, and a separate enquiry to Hackney Archives, not Waltham Forest.

## Note on what not to send
Neither of these mentions the book's argument, the family line, or my own descent. That stays back.
"Researching a book about a Walthamstow family" is enough to explain the questions and to make any later
request to reproduce a photograph honest.
