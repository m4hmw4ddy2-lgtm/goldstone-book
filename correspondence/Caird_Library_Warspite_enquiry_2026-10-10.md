# Caird Library (National Maritime Museum): Marine Society archive, training ship Warspite, 1901
Sent by Rik, 10 October 2026, to library@rmg.co.uk (text as drafted in session; Rik may have edited before sending). No reply by 22 October 2026; CHASER SENT by Rik, 22 October 2026 (below). REPLY RECEIVED 23 October 2026 (below).
Purpose: live_lists 4, item 45; CHAPTER_18_skeleton.md, finding 12.

Subject: Marine Society archive (MSY): a boy entered on the training ship Warspite, April 1901

Dear Caird Library team,

I am researching my great-grandfather, Albert Goldstone, for a work of narrative non-fiction, and I believe he passed through the Marine Society's training ship Warspite in 1901. I would be grateful for your help in finding his entry in the Society's records (MSY), on loan to the Museum.

What I know:
- Name: Albert Goldstone (he also appears in Whitstable records of the 1890s as Albert Madams).
- Born 19 June 1887, Lambeth. He was 13 in April 1901, though the court reports give his age as 14.
- Home: Middle Wall, Whitstable, Kent.
- On Saturday 13 April 1901, at St Augustine's Petty Sessions, he was bound over to come up for judgment when called upon. The Herne Bay Press of 20 April 1901 reports that Mr J. Bray, the police court missionary, had "got the boy on the training-ship Warspite".
- He entered the Royal Navy as a Boy 2nd Class on HMS Impregnable on 11 November 1901 (ADM 188/383, official number 218159).

My questions:
1. The AIM25 description of the MSY collection mentions registers of boys admitted for 1889 to 1958. Could you tell me the reference of the volume covering April to November 1901, and whether there is a separate entry register for the Warspite herself?
2. Is any of this material closed, given that it names individuals? If so, would my being his direct descendant make a difference? I can supply the birth, marriage and death certificates that prove the line.
3. Do you offer a paid search or copying service for researchers working remotely?
4. Could the Society's records also show how he came to the Royal Navy from the Warspite (a discharge or transfer entry), and what they say of his parents or birthplace?

Any guidance would be much appreciated, including a note of anything else in the collection worth looking at for a boy of 1901.

With thanks and best wishes,

Rik Ferguson

## Reply received 23 October 2026 (Sophie Seeyave, Archives Assistant, Royal Museums Greenwich; supplied by Rik)

Summary (her words quoted where they matter):
- The MSY collection: https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/archive/rmgc-object-492005 (expandable hierarchy).
- For 1901, check BOTH, "Sometimes, people are recorded in the registers but not the indexes (or vice versa)":
  - Registers of Admission, 1899-1915, Marine Society: "name, dates of entering and leaving the Society's Ship, to what
    ship (with RN indicating for Royal Navy), and date of visiting ticket".
  - Alphabetical indexes to MSY/L: registers of admission, 1889-1914: "name, age and height, former occupation, family
    connections (e.g. parents) and where they lived, date of leaving the Society's Ship (with RN indicating for Royal
    Navy)".
  - Also: WARSPITE Old Boys Association register (chronological), 1917-49; a few boys discharged as early as 1900,
    "dispersed across the volume".
- Access: open, in person at the Reading Room, Tuesday to Friday, 10:00 to 16:45; no proof of descent needed; request
  through "Request this item" on each item page; register to visit:
  https://www.rmg.co.uk/collections/caird-library/register-to-visit.
- Remote: no scanning (the collection is on loan, use restricted); BUT the enquiry lookup service can check items and
  give "a couple of quick reference photos". Publishing any photo needs the lender's permission, obtained through them.
- Paid searches: independent researchers (TNA and RMG lists, links in her email).
WHAT IT CHANGES: the index entry may give Albert's parents ("family connections") and address, seven months before the
naval "Germany" (live_lists 4, item 45). The register gives his dates on the ship and "RN" if he went to the Navy from
it. Neither is needed for the Chapter Eighteen gap (what the ship was and where she lay), which wants a published
source. Item reference numbers for the two volumes to be copied from the item pages before any request.

## Reply received 29 October 2026 (Sophie Seeyave, Archives Assistant, Royal Museums Greenwich; supplied by Rik): THE SEARCH
"MSY/L/5 is organised according to date, and MSY/M/5 is organised according to surnames (alphabetical). I have checked
MSY/L/5 for the years 1899 - 1901, and MSY/M/5 for surnames starting with G and M, but unfortunately did not see any
mention of Albert Goldstone/Goldston/Gouldstone/Madams."
Further resources she gave: Simon Fowler, "Tracing Your Naval Ancestors" (RMG library rmgl-150751), pages on training ships,
photographed and attached (saved: correspondence/images/Fowler_Tracing_Your_Naval_Ancestors_p38.jpg to _p41.jpg); the Guide
to Naval Records at The National Archives (PDF, https://www.exeter.ac.uk/research/centres/maritime/resources/naval_records/);
RMG Collections Online for context on WARSPITE and IMPREGNABLE.
WHAT IT CHANGES:
1. A NEGATIVE SEARCH by the archive itself: neither the Marine Society's register of admissions (by date, 1899-1901) nor its
   alphabetical index (G and M) names Albert under Goldstone, Goldston, Gouldstone or Madams. It is a search by an archive
   assistant of the two volumes she named, as reported: it covers his months (April to November 1901).
