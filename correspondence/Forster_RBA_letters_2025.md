## WHAT THE SECOND LETTER CHANGES — three corrections, 22 September 2026

**1. The access route is not closed. It is unlikely, and the archive has already said so.**
MASTER_NOTES was written earlier this evening to say that nobody can satisfy the descent test and that
"the silence that protects him is absolute". The first half stands: William left no descendants, and Rik
descends from Elizabeth, not from him. The second half is now too strong as a statement about ACCESS.
Forster's position, in writing, is that Broadmoor's answer "may still be no... as you yourself are not
related to the patient", that she "cannot say this with 100% certainty", that the archive "would still be
willing to put your application forward to the hospital and ask", and that "there is always a possibility
they might agree to release some information." Corrected in MASTER_NOTES.

The barrier that IS close to absolute is the other one, from the first letter: re-use in print, which she
says they are "very unlikely" to grant while the file is closed. So the honest position is that some
summary information might conceivably be released to Rik personally, and that almost none of it could
lawfully appear in the book before 2036.

**2. The descent point remains true and remains Coda material, but it is an argument about lineage, not
about archive policy.** William killed the only people who could ever have inherited the right to ask.
That is arithmetic and it is worth stating. What must NOT be written, now, is that the archive's rules
make the file unreachable in principle, because the archive itself has said it would ask on Rik's behalf.
Keep the two claims apart in the prose.

**3. Rik had already told her he had "a lead on William Gouldstone's family", and she had already answered
the no-living-family argument.** The draft letter written earlier this evening proposed to explain to her
that nobody can meet the descent test. She got there first, fifteen months ago. That paragraph has been
cut; see the revised draft.

## OUTSTANDING, AND CHEAP

Forster offered, in June 2025, a £22 transcription of page three of the coroner's papers, which is
Milne's deposition, because the scan had been smoothed by the software. Page three has since been
transcribed in this project from the PDF (see
`transcribed/Broadmoor_Inquest_COR_R1_7_3_1935_Transcription.txt`) and only two readings remain
uncertain: the doctor's middle name, read as "Oswald", and "in poor bodily health for the last six
[possibly nine] years". Neither is load-bearing. **Recommendation: do not pay £22 for two words. Ask her
for those two readings in an ordinary enquiry instead, where she may simply answer them.** Whether the
2025 offer still stands is unknown.
