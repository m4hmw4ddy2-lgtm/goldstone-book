# DRAFT: letter to Emily Forster, Archives Assistant, Royal Berkshire Archives
# REVISED 22 September 2026, after Rik supplied her second letter of 25 June 2025.
# The first draft explained to her that nobody can meet the descent test. She had already answered that
# point fifteen months ago, and answered it more favourably than the first draft assumed. Paragraph cut.
# Three asks now: the case book, the researcher route, and two words on page three.
# Kept short on purpose. Short enquiries get answered.

---

Dear Emily,

Thank you again for the scans of the coroner's papers, which you sent in June last year. I have been
working through them and they have been valuable.

You offered a transcription of the third page, Dr Milne's deposition, because the scan had been
smoothed. I have managed to read it, with two exceptions, and rather than trouble you for a full
transcription I wonder whether you would tell me those two words. The first is the doctor's own middle
name, which I have read as Kenneth Oswald Milne. The second is in the sentence "he has been in poor
bodily health for the last ___ years", where I cannot decide between six and nine.

Second, the case book you identified in your earlier letter, D/H14/D2/1/1/4, males admitted 1877 to
1884, patient numbers 901 to 1200. Is that volume closed on the same terms as the patient file itself,
or can an individual entry be consulted separately? You noted that a few entries include photographs of
patients, and I would be glad to know whether his is one of them.

Third, you were kind enough to say that although Broadmoor's answer might still be no, you would be
willing to put an application forward and ask. I should like to take you up on that, and to ask a
related question: whether there is any route for a researcher or historian as distinct from a relative.
I am writing a book on the case, working from the trial papers, the coroner's depositions and the
contemporary press. I understand from your earlier letter that permission to use anything from the file
in print is a separate matter and unlikely while the file is closed, and I am not asking you to
anticipate that decision. I would simply rather proceed on a basis that is actually open to me.

I should also like to register a genealogical interest, so that I am notified when the file opens.

With thanks for your help,

Richard Ferguson

---

## Notes on the draft, not for sending

- **The descent paragraph is gone.** Her letter of 25 June 2025 shows she had already been told about the
  family lead and had already ruled on it: the answer may still be no because Rik is not related to the
  patient, she cannot say so with certainty, and the archive would still ask. Explaining the lineage to
  her now would be telling her something she has answered, and would volunteer, in writing, the single
  fact most likely to sink the application. Left out deliberately. If the hospital raises it, answer it
  then.
- **The third paragraph accepts her offer rather than re-arguing the case.** She made it; the useful move
  is to say yes.
- **The two words are worth asking for and not worth £22.** Neither is load-bearing: the doctor's middle
  name is incidental, and six against nine years of declining health changes nothing in the book. But
  they cost nothing to ask, and a transcription fee for two words would be poor value. If she offers the
  paid transcription again, decline politely.
- **The admission number 4953 has been cut** from the first draft. It comes from the criminal lunatic
  register image in the repository, whereas the case file is numbered 1175 and the case book covers
  patient numbers 901 to 1200. Three different numbering systems, and quoting the wrong one would have
  confused the enquiry. Checked and flagged rather than quietly fixed.
- **Not asked, deliberately:** anything about the contents of the sealed file. Her June letter is clear
  that re-use would not be granted while it is closed, so asking now would only invite a no on the
  record.
- **Worth a separate enquiry later, not this one:** whether the Royal Berkshire Archives hold anything on
  the 1883 admission process itself, as distinct from the patient, and whether the criminal lunatic
  register reproduced at `sources/images/broadmoor-documents/Broadmoor admisision register.jpg` is
  theirs. Its series and repository are recorded nowhere in the project's notes and are needed before it
  can be cited.
