# Independent audit, batch B: Chapters 6-10

Method. I read the five chapters against: the Ada Hamilton 9 Aug deposition (images 0007, 0008 viewed; transcription read in full), her coroner deposition of 10 Aug (images 0028-0032 viewed), Emma Clarke (0032, 0033), Cheeseman 18 Aug (0013, 0014, 0015) and coroner (0034, 0035), Folkard 18 Aug (0017, 0018) and coroner (0037, folio 37), Wheatley (0021, 0022), Gould 18 Aug (0023, 0024) and coroner (0038-0041), Smith/Bryant (0036); the Old Bailey account (text, header says collated to print); and press transcriptions (Times 9, 10, 11, 14 Aug and 20 Aug by grep; DT 10, 11 Aug; Daily News 11, 20 Aug; Chronicle 13 Aug; IPN 18 Aug; Guardian 11, 18 Aug in full and 25 Aug by grep; Dover Express; Morning Post; wire copies of 9 Aug). Image file numbers are 1 higher than the folio numbers written on the pages (e.g. file 0014 = folio 13).

Note on shared copy: Times 10 Aug / DT 10 Aug / Guardian 11 Aug / IPN 18 Aug are one report of Ada's evidence of 9 Aug (same sentences, small variants). The Daily News and Evening News of 11 Aug are one report of the inquest. Guardian 25 Aug / IPN 25 Aug / Daily News 20 Aug are partly one copy.

## 0. Where Chapters 6 and 7 get their scene, and how conflicts are handled

Plus Emma Clarke, Cheeseman, Honey, Folkard, Smith, Gould. The chapters never say which version a line comes from, and never tell the reader that the witnesses disagree. Every conflict below is resolved silently, except where noted.

## 1. Errors (text wrong against source)

1.1 **Ch 8, high.** "the other little bugger, I soon settled him. I thought it was getting too hot to have five bloody kids in about three years and a half." Sources: Cheeseman 18 Aug image 0014: "the other little b-" and "five b- kids"; Cheeseman coroner image 0035: "the other little B-gg-r" (hyphens as written) and "5 Children"; Old Bailey print (header: "the page prints the dash"): "b——" twice; Daily News 11, 20 Aug, Times 20 Aug: "——" for both; Evening News: "the other little one". "Bugger" is one editor's reading of the clerk's coroner spelling "B-gg-r" (plausible, but the clerk himself suppressed it and the 18 Aug and OB texts do not complete it). "Bloody" is in no document: the 18 Aug page has only "b-", the coroner has "Children", the OB has "b——". The chapter prints both as the sworn words of Cheeseman, in quotation marks, in the middle of a passage where every other word is verbatim. The reader cannot tell. (Also: "too hot to have five" is the OB/18 Aug transcription reading; image 0014 reads, to me, "hot where/when five b- kids", see 4.3.) Fix: print "b——" as the sources do, or say in text that the word was left blank in the depositions and that the author is supplying one.

1.2 **Ch 8, medium.** "He was perfectly cool, Wheatley thought, and not showy in the least." Wheatley image 0022 (cross-examination): "He appeared perfectly Cool. not [strange/sharp-looking word] in the least". The reading "showy" is the one the transcription header says is wrong ("Earlier transcriptions gave 'not showy' ... both wrong"); the Times 20 Aug has Wheatley "I did not in the least observe any strangeness about him; he was very cool". I cannot read the last word with certainty from the image (the hand is ambiguous), but "showy" is not supported by any source. Chapter still carries the superseded reading.

1.3 **Ch 8, low-medium.** "He did not ramble or talk to himself." Image 0022: "Prisoner did not mumble and talk to himself." Transcription agrees ("mumble"). "Ramble" is a different word.

1.4 **Ch 9, medium.** "Ada was called first, and she repeated the evidence she had given the day before at Stratford. The third time she had sworn it in two days." Thursday 9 Aug (Stratford, signed 9 Aug) and Friday 10 Aug (coroner, sworn 10 Aug, image 0035 foot) is two times. Resworn 11 Aug is after, and the 18 Aug reswearing after that. DN 20 Aug: "already examined at this court and at the inquest". No third oath by Friday found.

1.5 **Ch 6, medium.** "Gould was clear that it had never been drawn tight enough to suffocate anybody." Gould coroner, image 0041: "I don't think the cord had been tied sufficiently tight of itself to cause suffocation." (Times 14 Aug: "had not been tied tight enough to cause death of itself".) The chapter turns an opinion about the cord "of itself" causing suffocation into a flat statement about the cord and anybody, and "never drawn".

1.6 **Ch 6, medium.** "It had taken no longer than it took Ada, through the wall, to get Elizabeth out of the bed and remake it." Ada, OB: after seeing the chair she got Elizabeth up, remade the bed, "was there some little time—I might have been there twenty minutes before I saw the prisoner again". The only support for "very short" is the Guardian 11 Aug's own editorial conjecture ("must have been the work of a very short time"). The chapter states it as fact and omits Ada's twenty minutes. "Through the wall" is also the author's: the cistern is in the scullery off the kitchen; no source puts Ada's room against it.

1.7 **Ch 9, low-medium.** "Ada was not asked to speak" (reading of 11 Aug), against the Chronicle 13 Aug ("the hammer ... was also produced and identified by the witness") and the chapter's own later paragraph ("Ada added one thing of her own", from Guardian/IPN 18 Aug).

1.8 **Ch 9, low-medium.** During Ada's evidence of 9 Aug: "William rested his folded arms on the top of the dock and listened." The folded-arms description is from the Telegraph's opening ("When he was first placed in the dock"). Guardian 11 Aug / IPN 18 Aug (same reporter's copy): he folded his arms, was ordered out by Mr Wells after about two minutes for the licence applications, and on returning "placed his hands together and then rested his head on his right hand". So during Ada's evidence he had his head on his hand. Chapter conflates, and drops the removal.

1.9 **Ch 9, medium.** "Two of his brothers, the Illustrated Police News reported, came to him in the cells of the court. The paper had a man there, and printed what they found: 'He seemed calm and perfectly unconcerned at his position.'" Guardian 18 Aug (same copy): "It is said that he was visited in the cell by two of his brothers, and seemed perfectly unconcerned." The report is second-hand. "The paper had a man there" and "what they found" are the chapter's, and make hearsay into an eyewitness observation.

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

2.1 **Ch 6 lines on the drowning, high.** "He held them under, and held them there, for as long as it took." "He did them one at a time. The second went into the water where the first was already lying, and the third where both of them were." "Charles was ... Herbert ... Frederick" as the order. No witness saw it. No document gives an order, or says the bodies lay together, or that he held them. Gould says death was "suffocation, in my opinion from drowning". William's words ("hard job with the two biggest, the other little ... soon settled") imply the youngest was easiest, not an order of three. Searched: all depositions and the press files in this batch for "one at a time", "order", "held". Nothing.

2.2 **Ch 6, high/medium.** "Drowning a conscious child is not quick. He holds his breath ... and then he breathes, because past a certain point the body overrules him, and what comes in is water." No source for the physiology, and "conscious" is assumed (nothing says the boys were awake, unharmed, or unrestrained). "In their stomachs, half-digested food mixed with water, which means they were swallowing while it happened": Gould records the finding for Frederick only (Herbert and Charles "precisely similar"), and draws no such conclusion; it is the author's inference stated as a fact. "The lungs gorged with dark blood, so heavy with it that it ran out" is a gloss on "gorged with dark liquid blood. Which exuded from cutting into them and using pressure" (image 0040).

2.3 **Ch 6, medium.** "no bruising, nothing broken, nothing to say they had been struck or wrestled with. Whatever William had to do to keep them under the water, it did not take enough force to mark a child." Gould (0040): "Externally no marks of violence" (Frederick; the others "precisely similar"). "Nothing broken" and "no bruising" go beyond "no marks"; the inference about force is the author's, and sits against William's own "hard job with the two biggest" which the chapter quotes two lines later.

2.4 **Ch 6, low.** "Round his neck was an indentation about the width of a cord." Gould 18 Aug (0023): "an indentation mark probably caused by a piece of string"; coroner: "an indentation mark round the neck, apparently caused by a piece of cord or stout string". No width.

2.5 **Ch 6, low.** "Dr Twining, the first doctor there". Honey says Twining was there when he arrived; nothing says he was first.

2.6 **Ch 7, medium.** "leaning over the children was a gentleman Ada took to be a chemist. He was Dr Twining." Ada (all versions) never names him. Honey says he fetched Twining about 5:50, yet Ada puts the "chemist" in the kitchen before the constable (Cheeseman, who arrived about 5:40) and Cheeseman mentions no doctor. The identification is the author's and it hides a timing puzzle (the doctor must have been summoned by someone else earlier, or Ada's order is wrong).

2.7 **Ch 7, low.** "Between them, Dr Twining and the constable had removed Charles's clothes" sits inside Honey's paragraph, so reads as Honey. Smith (Morning Post 14 Aug): "The doctor and the constable, thinking it was alive". Gould (DN 20 Aug): "Dr. Twining having taken the clothes off". No constable is named.

2.8 **Ch 7, low.** "the room where the twins had been struck": nobody says where. Plausible (hammer found in that room) but stated as fact.

2.9 **Ch 7, medium.** The Western Daily Press passage is given without any signal that it is false in most particulars (nurse sent out, cistern overflowing, door burst open, babies struck "at their mother's breast", the remark "Never mind, I am happy now, and she is a single woman" which no sworn witness records). Placed straight after the sworn account the reader may take its details as additional evidence. The wire text is a single report (WDP, Nottingham Guardian, Evening Gazette are identical; the Times 9 Aug is a rewrite of the same wire).

2.10 **Ch 7, low.** "the man who had delivered the twins seven days before, was Mr Sutton": letter (Chronicle 13 Aug) says "her medical attendant" and that the fees for the confinement were returned. DN 11 Aug: "attended by a medical man". Nothing says Sutton delivered them.

2.11 **Ch 8, low-medium.** "Cheeseman did not have to ask him anything." Cheeseman says only "prisoner said to me". The unprompted point is in Folkard's cross ("volunteered all this statement") and Wheatley's ("of his own accord", OB). Not Cheeseman.

2.12 **Ch 9, low.** "By the middle of the morning all five of the Gouldstone children were reunited." No source for the time. Bryant gives no hour.

2.13 **Ch 9, low.** "Then they asked her what William had been like before." The Times prints it as continuous narrative, with no question.

2.14 **Ch 9, low.** Blythe "by himself" and "was admitted" on the Sunday: no source says he was alone or how he got in (Guardian: he went to see the cistern, then called).

2.15 **Ch 10, low.** "It was not filled in until three o'clock." Only the Morning Post (14 Aug) says "At three o'clock the grave ... was filled up". Guardian/Dover/Times: "as soon as the grave was filled up it was covered with flowers", with the three o'clock crowds arriving "to find, however, that [the interment] had taken place". Single paper.

2.16 **Ch 10, not checked.** "Martha Gouldstone, William's aunt ... Her husband, William's uncle Robert, had died at Walthamstow the December before." Guardian: "Mrs. Gouldstone, the prisoner's aunt, accompanied by her daughter"; Times: "wife of one of the prisoner's uncles". Forename, husband and death date are in none of the files I read (1871 census transcription has a Martha aged 13, not an aunt). See section 6.

2.17 **Ch 9 line 15 (and 11), low.** "The Telegraph had a man on the press bench ... added in a line of his own, in square brackets." The same bracketed insertion appears in Guardian 11 Aug ("[Here the witness began to cry.]"), IPN 18 Aug, and the Times ("At this point the witness seemed to be much affected"). It is one agency or reporter's copy; the DT is not shown to be the author.

## 3. Attribution problems

3.1 **Ch 8 and Ch 6/7, high-medium.** The chapters quote the Old Bailey (14 Sept) as if it were the earlier depositions without saying so. OB-only material used as scene or quotation: "Didn't you tell Mr Graves?" (Ch 6); Cheeseman "Yes, a little", "leaned over the foot of the bed", "simply Good-bye", "I said nothing" (Ch 7); Cheeseman's note "three or four minutes afterwards ... I have got it with me" (Ch 8, correctly tied to "the Old Bailey"); "Now I am happy, I thought it was time to put a stop to it" as a separate remark (Ch 8: in OB only, as "he added"; 18 Aug and coroner have only "I thought it was time to put a stop to it"); "of his own accord" (Wheatley, OB); "missis" for Folkard's "Missus" (image 0037) and "is the missis in" for "was" (OB print). The chapters name the Old Bailey only once.

3.2 **Ch 8, low.** Wheatley's first remark: "I have had an extra drop of drink to accomplish the job; there is five of them gone to glory, and it is a good job." Image 0021: "I have had one extra draft of drink today to accomplish the job / there is five of them gone to Glory and it is a good job too". The chapter is the OB print (and Times 20 Aug has "extra drop ... to-day ... this job"), not the deposition. "Today" and "too" are dropped. Also "preying" (chapter) is the Old Bailey correction and the Times; the 18 Aug deposition image 0021 reads "playing". Chapter gives no indication; defensible, but a reader checking the deposition will find "playing".

3.3 **Ch 7, low.** Cheeseman's greeting: "I have done it. Now I am happy and am ready for the rope." Image 0013/0035: "I have done it now. I am happy and am ready for the rope" ("now" ends the first clause). The chapter's punctuation follows Daily News 11 Aug. Words the same.

3.4 **Ch 9, medium.** "The last thing the paper recorded of her evidence was that his wife was 'now dangerously ill in bed'." The words "now dangerously ill in bed" are the Times 10 Aug; Guardian and IPN have "now dangerously ill"; the DT transcription in the repo breaks off mid-sentence ("unkind to the childr"), so I could not confirm the DT has it.

3.5 **Ch 9, low-medium.** "the one thing the reporters in the room could not agree on" (scarf / neck handkerchief / cord). The Guardian 18 Aug and IPN 18 Aug are one report of the Saturday hearing with the nouns changed ("tartan scarf in a copy of the Walthamstow Guardian", "Let me put it around my neck" vs "neck handkerchief", "That is mine"). The chapter says so ("printing the same copy") and then treats them as disagreeing reporters. One reporter's text altered, or one reporter and a sub-editor. The Chronicle is the only independent report, and it mentions the cord.

3.6 **Ch 9 and Ch 10, low-medium.** "His visit was of a kindly nature, to see if he could be of any service" (Guardian, Dover) and Ch 10 "his visit, he said, had been 'of a kindly nature'". The Guardian prints indirect speech by the reporter; Blythe's words are not given. The chapter uses quotation marks for the reporter's paraphrase and, in Ch 10, adds "he said".

3.7 **Ch 9, low.** "What Lyle set down in quotation ... was this". Dr Lyle's letter is not in the repo. The text comes from the Guardian's report of the letter as read aloud (Dover Express, later in the week, copies it word for word). The Times and Morning Post give a different account of what Blythe was alleged to have said ("no use ... to screen her husband"). Chapter notes the Morning Post but treats the Guardian's wording as the letter's.

3.8 **Ch 10, low-medium.** "The Dover Express gave the reason for the change: 'in order to prevent the assemblage of a large crowd while the proceedings at the inquest were progressing.'" The Dover paragraph is the Times 14 Aug text with one phrase altered (Times: "while the proceedings were progressing"; also Mrs Graves, "wife of one of the prisoner's uncles", elm coffins). The Dover's last funeral sentences are the Guardian 18 Aug word for word. Dover also gives a 6ft grave and "not less than 1500" mourners, where Guardian has 10ft and "over 1,000" and Morning Post "several hundred". Chapter takes 10ft without mention. Dover is not an independent witness.

3.9 **Ch 10, low.** Lyle "had wanted Mr Lewis to read it out, but in the end read it himself". Morning Post says so. Times says Lewis "requested Dr. Lyle to read it". Chapter chooses one; silent.

3.10 **Ch 9, low.** Emma's first-person words ("My husband rented the house ... quite a stranger to me ... calm, placid man") are DT 11 Aug verbatim (Times differs: "hired this house"). The signed coroner deposition says "is the tenant", "perfect strangers", "a calm and placid man" (image 0032). Chapter does not name which; fine, but a reader checking the deposition will find different words.

## 4. Source problems (transcription vs image, header notes)

4.1 **Emma Clarke, image 0033 (folio 32), medium.** Transcription: "They then ran downstairs and called for the Police." Image: "I then ran down stairs and called for the Police. And then I returned to Mrs Gouldstone's bed room." Matches the Times/DT press and OB ("I ran downstairs and called for the police"). Ch 7 is right; the transcription is wrong and would mislead.

4.2 **Emma Clarke, image 0033, medium.** Transcription: "last Wednesday after about ¼ past 5." Image: "Aft about ½ past 5" (same "½" glyph as "about ½ past 10" on the previous page). The press and OB give "half-past five". The transcription has her in the garden a quarter hour earlier than she says.

4.3 **Cheeseman 18 Aug, image 0014 (folio 13), low-medium.** Transcription: "I thought it was getting too hot to have five b— kids within three years and a half." Image: "I thought it was getting too hot where [or when] five b- Kids within three years and a half". "To have" is the Old Bailey printed reading, not what I read on the page. The coroner image 0035 reads "too hot. I have 5 Children in about three years and an half" (the transcription matches).

4.4 **Wheatley, image 0022, low.** The transcription header says the last words read "not strange in the least" (Rik's reading, accepted); the earlier "showy"/"shaky" are said to be wrong. On the image the last word is not clearly "strange" either. Unverified; the header is more confident than the hand allows.

4.5 **Ada coroner, image 0030, no error but note.** "He asked him what he had done." The pronoun is "He" (as in "he replied" on the next line); it is ambiguous who asked (the constable, or Elizabeth). Cheeseman gives the sentence to Elizabeth. The transcription is right; the ambiguity is not flagged.

4.6 **Filing.** Image 0038 is in both the Folkard (coroner) folder and the Gould (coroner) folder (folio 37, end of Folkard and start of Gould). Image 0032 is in both the Ada Hamilton and Emma Clarke coroner folders (folio 31). Not an error but easy to miscite.

4.7 **Times/Morning Post 14 Aug vs image.** Both give Gould's post-mortem at "3.40 p.m."; image 0039 reads "3.30 P.M.". Chapter 9 "half past three" follows the image. Correct choice.

## Five findings that matter most

1. Ch 8 prints "bugger" and "bloody" as Cheeseman's sworn words; the deposition (0014), the coroner (0035: "B-gg-r"), the Old Bailey print and the papers all have dashes. "Bloody" appears nowhere.
2. Ch 6's drowning paragraphs (held under, one at a time, bodies lying together, what a conscious child feels, "swallowing") have no source and are stated as fact; the "no longer than it took Ada to remake the bed" contradicts Ada's own "twenty minutes".
3. Ch 6 and 7 are a silent blend of four versions of Ada's evidence plus Emma, Cheeseman, and the Old Bailey. Most visible: William's words at the door (Ada swore at trial he said nothing about the other children), and the babies and Elizabeth appearing in the front room with no mover.
4. Ch 9 "The third time she had sworn it in two days" is two; "the paper had a man there" (cells) is hearsay in the sources; "Telegraph's man" bracket and "reporters could not agree" both rest on shared copy.
5. Image errors in the Emma Clarke transcription (¼ vs ½ past 5; "They" vs "I" ran downstairs); the Wheatley "showy" reading the transcription header itself says is wrong is still in Ch 8, and "mumble" has become "ramble".
