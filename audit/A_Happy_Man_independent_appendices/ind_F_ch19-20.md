# Independent audit F: Chapters 19, 20 and the naval statements in 21

Scope: Albert Goldstone's naval service, 1901-1919. Method: I rendered the service-sheet PDF (`sources/images/albert-goldstone/Albert Goldstone Naval Record.pdf`, ADM 188/383, page 2) at 200 dpi and read the ship, date, character, cells, class-for-conduct and discharge cells against the full transcription. I also viewed the medal-roll image (ADM_171_103_p131...jpeg), the War Badges register image, and the 1939 Register image (`census-stuff/Goldstone Family 1939 Register.png`). Everything else was checked against the press and book transcriptions in `transcribed/`. For Newbolt I also read `sources/books/Newbolt_..._part1/part2` HTML. I did not read `notes/` as evidence, apart from noticing that it is where the daughters' birth dates are first sourced.

## 0. Sheet versus transcription

The service-sheet transcription agrees with the image on every ship, date, rating, cells entry and class-for-conduct entry I checked:

- Ships and dates: Impregnable 11-25 Nov 01; Lion 26 Nov 01 (no close) and 28 Aug 02-23 Jun 03; Agincourt 24 Jun-8 Sep 03; Prince George 9 Sep 03, Ord. 19 Jun-14 Jul 04; Majestic 15 Jul-20 Dec 04 and 4 Jan-28 Feb 05; Firequeen I and Victory (Mar-14 Aug 05); Jupiter 15 Aug 05-1 Mar 06, 12 Mar 06-4 Aug 07, 8 Aug 07 (no close), AB 1 Jan-3 Feb 08; Pembroke 4 Feb-9 Mar 08; Shannon 10 Mar 08-28 Feb 10; Pembroke I 1 Mar-6 Jun 10; Dominion 7 Jun 10-23 May 11; Pembroke I 24 May-7 Aug 11; Crescent 8 Aug-15 Sep 11; **Powerful 16 Sep-16 Dec 11**; Pioneer 17 Dec 11-23 May 12; Drake 24 May 12-25 Feb 13; Pembroke I 26 Feb-26 Oct 13; Tyne (T.B.31) from 27 Oct 13 (Ldg Smn 1 Jul-27 Jul 16); Pembroke I 28 Jul-3 Nov 16; Courageous 4 Nov 16-31 Dec 17; Pembroke I 1 Jan-26 Feb 18; Alexander from 27 Feb 18 (P.O. (ns) 1 Sep 18, no close date); Pembroke I 17 Jan-26 Mar 19.
- Cells: "Cells 14 days" sits beside the Majestic rows, "Cells 10 d" beside the first Jupiter row, "3 days cells" beside the next.
- Class for conduct: 2nd 21.12.04 and 1st 4.7.05; 2nd 2.3.06 and 1st 12.3.07.
- Discharge: "Invalided / Org Heart Disease".

Specific points on the sheet:

- **Tyne letter.** The letter before ".B.31" is a looped capital that could be T, I or L. The chapter asserts "Tyne (T.B.31)" flatly. RMG also reads "TB 31", and the TB35/TB36 logs name "TB31" and "the Tyne", so the identification is probably right. It rests on an uncertain letter plus the number.
- **Dating of the cells spells.** The sheet gives no dates for any cells spell. The cells durations match the unexplained gaps exactly:
  - 21 Dec 04 to 3 Jan 05 is 14 days.
  - 2-11 Mar 06 is 10 days.
  - 5-7 Aug 07 is 3 days.
  
  This is a sound inference and the chapter does not over-date it, but it never says it is an inference.
- **Age on entry.** The sheet's "Age 16" at entry is not consistent with the sheet's own date of birth (19 June 1886, which would make him 15y5m on 11 Nov 1901). It is two years above the true age (14y4m), not "a year older". See B2.
- **Medal roll.** The row "GOLDSTONE, Albert | P.O. | 218159 | St V B | S." is confirmed. The column headings carry no expansion of "St V B" or "S.".
- **War Badges register.** "Gladstone A. | 218159 | P.O. | 36842 | 28.2.19". This is a month before the 26 Mar 1919 discharge date and equals the date written beside the number on the head of the sheet ("26/2/19" or "28/2/19"). The chapters do not use it. See section 5.

## 1. Errors

**E1 (high). Ch 20: Albert "went into the Pioneer" on Friday 15 September 1911 and she sailed on the 17th.**
- Text: "The ships were recommissioned on Friday the fifteenth, and the crews changed over. Albert went into the Pioneer, a third-class cruiser, and on Sunday the seventeenth she sailed for Australia... He would not see England again for eighteen months."
- Sheet (image, p.2): Crescent closes 15 Sept 11; **Powerful 16 Sept 11 to 16 Dec 11**; **Pioneer opens 17 Dec 11**. There is no break and no cells entry.
- So the sheet does not show Albert borne on the Pioneer across any event between 17 Sept and 16 Dec 1911: the Colombo-Australia passage, her arrival at Melbourne, or anything else in that period. For those three months he is on the books of the Australian flagship Powerful.
- Why Powerful: a flagship's books are a plausible place to carry a relief draft, but no document in the repo says so.
- The chapter never mentions the Powerful at all. Its own source file `powerful_transcriptions.txt` (for example Lyttelton Times 6 Dec 1911; Lloyd's Weekly 10 Dec 1911) has the Powerful returning to be a training ship.
- A reader of Ch 20 will believe he sailed from Colombo in the Pioneer on 17 Sept 1911. The sheet says he was borne on the Powerful, and only came onto the Pioneer's books exactly three months after the 17 Sept sailing.
- The Pioneer events the chapter then narrates (Feb to Mar 1912, Himitangi) fall within his Pioneer entry (17 Dec 11 to 23 May 12) and pass the test.

**E2 (medium). Ch 19: "That day came on the nineteenth of June 1904" (the eighteenth birthday that starts the 12 years).**
- The chapter's first sentence says the Navy had him down a year older. His real birth is 19 June 1887 (birth certificate transcription).
- His real 18th birthday was 19 June 1905. On 19 June 1904 he was 17.
- The 1904 date is the Navy's false birthday. The chapter presents it as his birthday, and says (correctly) that he was "sixteen" in Oct 1903, which makes 18 on 19 June 1904 impossible.
- The same problem sits behind "His twelve years ran out on the nineteenth of June 1916" in Ch 20. That is the Navy's reckoning, and on true dates he was 29 and the engagement had a year to run.
- Source for the rule itself: Hansard 7 Aug 1947 (see U1).
- Also, the engagement year on the sheet is blotted. Only the final "4" is visible. "1904" is inference (reasonable, since it matches Ord. 19 June 04).

**E3 (medium). Ch 20: Pioneer "by the new year had crossed to New Zealand".**
- Lyttelton Times 10 Jan 1912 reads: "An expected arrival at Bluff on Saturday, January 20, is the drillship H.M.S. Pioneer, from Australia."
- At 10 January she was still expected from Australia. The first firm New Zealand position in the files is Bluff on 12 Feb 1912.

**E4 (medium). Ch 20: Drake "sailed on the fifth with the Admiral aboard, for six weeks among the islands".**
- Lyttelton Times 6 Jul 1912: "has sailed for a six weeks' cruise among the islands" (a forecast, from a Sydney telegram dated July 5).
- Lyttelton Times 30 Jul 1912 shows her sailing from Auckland on 29 July "for Sydney". The cruise was about four weeks, not six.

**E5 (low). Ch 20: "Four months in the barracks followed" (after Tyne).**
- Sheet: Pembroke I 28 Jul 16 to 3 Nov 16, which is 98 days or about 3 months 1 week. "Four months" is wrong. The professional exam on 20 Oct 16 does fall inside the spell.

**E6 (low). Ch 20: Courageous's enemy were minesweepers and destroyers "with four light cruisers in support".**
- Newbolt: "three or four light cruisers". The chapter also drops the central group of submarines.
- "at work on the minefield" is not in the extract. The nearby text supports minesweepers working the barrier.

**E7 (low). Ch 20: Pioneer "at two o'clock the next afternoon she left her moorings at Gladstone Pier".**
- Lyttelton Times 6 Mar 1912: "the cruiser left her moorings at Gladstone Pier, and at 2 p.m. sailed". She left her moorings in the morning ("Yesterday morning the final arrangements were made") and sailed at 2 p.m.

**E8 (low). Ch 19: Majestic "was commissioned at Portsmouth... and he joined her that same day".**
- The press item (Hampshire Telegraph 2 Jul 1904) is a forecast: "is to be commissioned at Portsmouth on July 15th".
- The sheet fixes his own date (15 Jul) but the commissioning itself is an announcement, not a record. The Prince George replacement ("in place of the Prince George") is as printed.

**E9 (low). Ch 20: "Rear-Admiral Kemp, who reached Archangel two days later".**
- Newbolt (extract and full part 2): "Admiral Kemp" or "Commodore Kemp". The text says "Early in July Admiral Kemp went to Archangel" and that the demonstration was "shortly before his arrival". "Two days" and "Rear-" are not in the repo.
- "Henry Hurt" (first name): Newbolt says only "Captain Hurt". "Henry" is not in any repo source (grepped transcribed/, sources/books).

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

**U1 (high). Alexander, 1918: "Two months later he was in a very different ship... helped two ships of food... through the ice to Archangel", with "He stayed in the Alexander for the rest of the war".**
- The sheet shows Albert borne on the Alexander from 27 Feb 1918 with no closing date. A second "Alexander" row (5I / 5) has no dates. RMG's 16 Jan 1919 end date is its own inference (the day before the next entry).
- Nothing in the repo places him aboard the icebreaker, at Archangel, or in Russia. The Newbolt text says "early spring" for the food ships, with no dates.
- The sheet's date of joining (27 Feb) is before the food ships arrived. The chapter turns "borne on the books" into "was in". At a minimum the sentence needs "his name was on the Alexander's books".
- The July "ugly demonstration" narrative is the official history's, not Albert's.
- Same family of problem: the sheet shows no physical movement from Pembroke (Chatham) to Alexander.

**U2 (medium). "Tyne (T.B.31)" in 1914: Albert aboard TB31 when she grounded on 29 Aug 1914.**
- Source test: Tyne entry 27 Oct 13 to 27 Jul 16 (no break; his cells are earlier). The 29 Aug 1914 grounding falls inside his borne period.
- The sheet says "borne on the books of Tyne" ("whose men were carried on the books of HMS Tyne", Ch 20). The logs say "Tyne's boat" and "One rating joined from Tyne". The inference that the sheet's "(T.B.31)" means the boat Albert was in is probable but unproven.
- On "aboard": nothing says he was aboard on the night. The chapter narrates it as his boat's experience, which is fair, but the sentence "Boats came out to her..." etc is the log's, not his.

**U3 (medium). Ch 19: "HMS Jupiter, the battleship he joined at Chatham... was manned for the Channel Fleet that same day".**
- North Star 16 Aug 1905: "manned at Chatham yesterday". Naval & Military Record 31 Aug 1905 lists Jupiter under "Ships in commission with nucleus crews". Western Morning News 2 Sep and N&MR 14 Sep: she was to go to Portsmouth "to join the Reserve Division until her crew is completed".
- Albert was on Victory (Portsmouth) to 14 Aug. The sheet says nothing about Chatham for him. "Joined at Chatham" is an inference, and "manned" overstates the nucleus crew.
- Morning Advertiser 16 Aug: "to be transferred to the Portsmouth command for manning and refitting purposes". Of his own movements for Aug-Sep 1905 nothing is known.
- The £60,000 and "recommissioned at Chatham" are correct.

**U4 (medium). Ch 19: "he was rated boy first class, his character 'V.G.'" (Aug 1902).**
- The first V.G. in the character column is undated; it stands beside the B1cl row (28 Aug 02 to 23 Jun 03). The second V.G. (also undated) stands beside the Prince George row. Tying V.G. to August 1902 is plausible and unproven.

**U5 (medium). Ch 20 and 21: the expansion of sheet marks.**
- "Under the King's proclamation of 1914 he was kept on" and "gratuity ... that went to every man so detained": Hansard 17 May 1916 supports the proclamation and the gratuity ("paid to all ratings entitled thereto"). That "£3.10 G C + B June 16" is that gratuity, and that Albert was "kept on", is inference, but a good one.
- D1 and R1 in the badge column are read as "taken from him" and "given back" (Ch 20), and the second D1 31.5.17 as "taken away again". The sheet shows only D1 and R1 with dates. No document in the repo explains the letters.
- Ch 21: "issued with the King's Certificate of Discharge" from the stamp "K.C. ... ISSUED 17/4/19". The sheet gives "K.C." only. The transcription notes this ("NOT on the sheet"), and the chapter states the expansion as fact.
- Ch 21: "awarded the 1914-15 Star, the British War Medal and the Victory Medal" from "St V B". The roll image shows only the letters under "Medals, &c., earned"; the expansion is general knowledge, not in the repo.

**U6 (medium). General naval history with no source in the repo**
- "so that an enemy's torpedo boats could not find her... as she would be in wartime" (Ch 19, night manoeuvres). The press says "without lights" only.
- "from which her after torpedoes were fired" (Ch 19): the sources say "aft submerged torpedo flat... filled with water".
- HMS Impregnable and Lion as boys' training ships at Devonport: Lion is supported by the Navy List July 1903 ("Training Ships for Boys... Devonport... two Ships... tender Liberty"). Note that this is a 1903 list applied to 1901-1903. The Impregnable-as-training-ship is supported only by Lloyd's Weekly 10 Dec 1911 ("old training ship Impregnable").
- Firequeen I and Victory "at Portsmouth": Victory is general knowledge; Firequeen I has no source.
- Berehaven "on the coast of County Cork": general knowledge.
- Parkeston "by Harwich": general knowledge.
- Heligoland action, New Zealand and Invincible to the Forth: Corbett supports "after the Heligoland action the two battle cruisers were ordered to the Forth" (no date for arrival). TB36 gives the date.
- "Archangel, on the White Sea" and "Britain went to war on the fourth of August": general knowledge, no repo source.
- Ch 21: "The Yard Craft Service was the Admiralty's civilian service for the tugs, lighters, launches and harbour ferries" has no source in the repo (grepped transcribed/ and sources/; the term appears only as the occupation entries in the 1921 census and Vera's birth certificate).
- "Boy entrants' terms of service" (Ch 19): supported (see section 6, U1). The Hansard 1947 text is a 1947 statement of a rule it dates to 1884. It does not show what applied in 1901 beyond that.

**U7 (low). Ch 19: Morning Leader quote order.**
- "At Ferrol, the Morning Leader's Madrid correspondent reported, 'An immense multitude are on the pier awaiting the squadron.'" The paragraph then says the Prince George was made fit "while eight battleships and three cruisers waited". The report is from before the fleet arrived ("expected to arrive this afternoon"). Quote is verbatim; context is earlier than the chapter's sequence implies.

**U8 (low). Ch 20: "The Crescent... two of them were to take the men she carried".**
- Times of Ceylon 12 Sep and Ceylon Observer 14 Sep: Crescent carried reliefs for Flora (China), Psyche and Pioneer. "Two of them" is right for the four Australian cruisers, but the Crescent also carried Flora's crew, who are not mentioned. The Times of Ceylon of 18 Sept says Gibraltar brought the reliefs for Cambrian, Pioneer and Psyche, which conflicts with the other three items; the chapter does not note this.
- "ships were recommissioned on Friday the fifteenth": the press items are forecasts ("will probably take place on Friday"). The sheet's Crescent close on 15 Sept agrees but is not independent of the plan.

**U9 (low). Ch 20: Drake statement "for July" and the Navy League statement.**
- LT 28 Jun 1912: "Drake leaves Sydney about July 2... returning to Sydney about August 4". The chapter calls it the statement for July and says she "sailed on the fifth", which is right. The source file itself notes it is a programme. The chapter's "Each month the naval authorities sent..." is from "monthly statement"; fine.

## 3. Attribution and witness problems

- **A1.** Prince George / Ferrol: the Reuter text is one witness running in four papers (the transcription header says so). The chapter uses only the Morning Leader (Madrid), the Standard (Admiral's telegram) and the Western Times (men on board). The Morning Leader and Western Evening Herald share one Madrid text; the chapter names only the Morning Leader. No false multiplication. Note that the Admiral's telegram is quoted from the Standard; the Western Evening Herald's version differs slightly ("the Hannibal having collided with the Prince George... October 17"). The chapter's text matches the Standard's, which is the better text. Fine.
- **A2.** Ch 19: "Lloyd's List had a correspondent in HMS Russell". The piece is in "Shipping Gazette and Lloyd's List" of 5 Jul 1906, headed with the Russell and Captain Lowry. The correspondent is never named as being in the Russell in so many words; "every man on board the Russell thrilled" is the evidence. Reasonable.
- **A3.** Ch 19: the Jupiter burial. The paper says "the hauling out of her station of H.M.S. Jupiter, for the purpose of the burial of a member of her crew... it was on this occasion undesirable for the battle squadron to slow down" ("for reasons immediately to be explained"). The reasons are given in the next section (enemy report at 5 p.m.). The chapter's "because the enemy had been reported close by" is a fair reading, but the burial is reported before the 5 p.m. enemy report.
- **A4.** Shannon commissioning: the chapter says "Tuesday the tenth of March 1908... on the day she was commissioned", from Western Mail 11 Mar 1908 ("on Tuesday commissioned"). Sheerness Guardian 14 Mar 1908 says "On Wednesday H.M.S. Shannon was commissioned at Chatham". The two disagree (Tuesday 10 versus Wednesday 11). The sheet's 10 March agrees with the Western Mail. The chapter does not mention the conflict.
- **A5.** Ch 20 Drake: the Naval and Military Record quote is attributed to "The Naval and Military Record's correspondent" and is verbatim. The "Home, sweet home" quote is right.

## 4. Source problems

- **S1.** Service-record transcription, N5 and engagement. The sheet's year is blotted. The transcription says so. Nothing wrong.
- **S2.** The transcription header (Tyne): the note "T possible" and later "READ AS T.B.31 by Rik" is an editorial choice. My view of the crop: I cannot read it as clearly T. The chapter's certainty exceeds the image.
- **S3.** `powerful_transcriptions.txt`, `drake_transcriptions.txt`: header says only part of the clips was image-checked. The items used by the chapters (LT 6 Jul, 25 Jul, 12 Dec 1912; N&MR 19 Feb 1913; Ceylon Observer 30 Jan 1913; LT 28 Jun, 29 Aug, 9 Sep 1912) are flagged as checked or added with checks.
- **S4.** `jupiter_transcriptions.txt`: the Lloyd's List 5 Jul 1906 note says Jupiter "is NOT named on this page" (part two) and is named once, on 27 June. Fine for the chapter, which only claims the burial on 27 June and the Needles on 5 July. The Needles list entry (Lloyd's List 6 July, bracket [July 5]) is a screenshot with the date in brackets; reading it as 5 July is an inference.
- **S5.** 1911 census transcription: the Dominion crew total on the cover reads 764 on the ship's books plus 5 passengers etc = 769. The chapter's "769 people on board" is right. Albert's row is on page 11, aged 24, "aB". Fine.
- **S6.** The 1939 Register (image viewed): Lily "14 Dec 13" (year reads as "13" or "74", consistent with 1913), Clarie "21 Oct 18", Albert "19 June 86". Chapter 21 quotes "19 Jun 86"; the image reads "19 June" as best I can see. Low.
- **S7.** Clarie Irene's age on the 1921 census is "2 years 8 months" (transcription) at 19 June 1921. A birth on 21 Oct 1918 gives 2y7m, not 2y8m. A birth in the 20 Sep to 19 Oct 1918 window fits the census. The chapter's 21 October rests on the 1939 Register only. Low, but worth one more document (GRO or baptism).

## 5. Unused material worth the author's attention

1. **Silver War Badge (or the war badge register)**: `War_Badges_register_Albert_Goldstone_badge_36842.jpeg`, "Gladstone A. | 218159 | P.O. | 36842 | 28.2.19", with "GC" at the top of the facing page. The badge is dated 28 Feb 1919, a month before the discharge (26 Mar 1919), and equals the date written under the number at the head of the sheet. The chapters never mention a badge, and the date suggests the invaliding decision was made in late Feb.
2. **Cells timeline**: the 14 days (21 Dec 04 to 3 Jan 05) fit exactly between Majestic's two entries and the conduct drop on 21.12.04, i.e. the Christmas leave of the fleet's return in Dec 1904 (the Echo story) was spent in the cells if the inference holds. Likewise the 10 days (2 to 11 Mar 1906) fall just before Berehaven's mining operations week (Evening Irish Times 4 Apr 1906: "An E M mine-field was laid by the Jupiter").
3. **Jupiter at Berehaven**: Stamford Mercury 2 Feb 1906: a sailor named Corke killed on the Jupiter on Friday 26 Jan 1906, at Berehaven. Inside his first Jupiter entry.
4. **Jupiter, Aug 1905**: Lloyd's List 26 Aug 1905 (Jupiter ran into the brigantine Caroline off Dover) and Chatham, Rochester and Brompton 26 Aug 1905 (Jupiter AB Chant, drunk in Gillingham). Both within his entry.
5. **Prince George court-martial**: Daily Express 27 Oct 1903 and Western Times 27 Oct 1903: Captain Campbell acquitted after a two-day court-martial; the ventilators "failed to act"; "over 800 tons" of water; Southern Daily Mail 3 Nov 1903: Lord Selborne conveyed the King's message to the Prince George's crew while she was in dock. Albert was borne on her.
6. **Whitstable theft case**: Folkestone Chronicle 13 Apr 1901 (boy "Albert Goldstone, aged 14" charged with stealing sea boots; the police court missionary offered to get him on a training ship). It is the likeliest route to Impregnable. The transcription notes the outcome is not found, so the chapter rightly does not state it.
7. **Shannon**: West Lothian Courier 20 Mar 1908 and Chatham, Rochester and Brompton 21 Mar 1908 (Fifth Cruiser Squadron programme, Queensferry, May gunlayer's tests at the Nore). Two years of his service, one paragraph.
8. **Powerful**: Lloyd's Weekly 10 Dec 1911 says she was "returning to Devonport to take the place of the old training ship Impregnable", which closes Albert's naval loop (Impregnable 1901 and Powerful 1911). The Powerful was at Lyttelton on 6 Dec 1911 with the Admiral, and left for Colombo to meet the Drake. If the chapter admits the Powerful row, there is a story.
9. **Alexander**: the sheet's "(P.O. (ns))" and the second Alexander book number (5I / 5) with no dates.
10. **Drake**: Ceylon Observer 30 Jan 1913 (crossing the line, desertions at Fremantle, "nearly 150 Australian recruits", the Orvieto wireless messages on the Balkan War), Lyttelton Times 23 Dec 1912 (Drake to be relieved by Cambrian at Hobart on 4 Jan).
11. **TB31 log items**: TB35 log 29 Nov 1914, 21-22 Jun 1916; TB36 log 10 Jul 1916, which date Albert's boat at sea with TBs 32 and 31 inside his entry.

## 6. What I could not check

- ADM 53 logs and Old Weather page images (outside the repo; the transcription is by volunteers).
- Newbolt "two hours" chase and "smoke" are in the full part 1 text, which I read; the "at work on the minefield" and the order of battle are as given. I did not verify the printed volume.
- Corbett's printed volume (archive.org OCR only).
- The Naval Enlistment Act 1884 itself; only the 1947 Hansard speech is in the repo.
- The church register of the 1910 marriage (the GRO certified copy is the clergy's quarterly return). Bride's age: the chapter's "twenty-two" for her is the entry's text. The marriage transcription itself notes it is two years over (she was born 7 June 1889 per the 1939 Register, so 20 in March 1910; the Setterington census gives age 1 in 1891 and 12 in 1901). The chapter prints both ages as the register has them and does not flag the bride's, though the book has the evidence. Low-medium omission, but it is a statement, not an error.
- Dates of Albert's physical movements to and from ships: the sheet shows only the books. No document in the repo places him aboard the Pioneer in 1911, the Alexander in 1918, or elsewhere.
- Bride's birth place (Kingston) and the colour sergeant's rank: per the Setterington 1891/1901 census transcription; I did not open the images.
- Whether "Firequeen I" is a Portsmouth establishment.

## Arithmetic and calendar rechecks (all correct unless listed above)

- 21 Oct to 11 Nov 1918: three weeks, correct.
