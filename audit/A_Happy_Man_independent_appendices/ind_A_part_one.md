# Independent audit A: Prologue and Chapters 1-5 (Part One)

Method: read all six chapters; checked against transcriptions, trial-doc texts, GED trees (as indexes only), the agricultural lock-out PDF, and the images listed under "Images viewed". Notes/ and ChatGPT-archive were not used as evidence (I looked at notes only to see what a claim was resting on, and say so where it matters).

Images viewed (Read tool): Albert's 1887 birth certificate; the 1879 marriage entry (St Luke's, Hackney); 1871 census 115 Albion Road; 1881 census 88 Frampton Park Road, pp. 1 and 2; Great Sampford 1861 p. 1; Brewery House 1861 (Stock household); 1854 Stock/Ansell marriage; 1827 Stock/Hockley marriage; William's 1856 birth entry; death entries for Sarah, Arthur, John, Elizabeth (Eliza), Alfred, Edith, Nathan, George, Emma Ansell, Jemima and Thomas Stock, Ruth Gouldstone; Raven monument (two faces only); Courtney Place map crop.
Not viewed as images (transcription relied on): Hibblethwaite clip, 1866 cholera clips, 1867 sale, 1861 sale, City Press, Mercury clips, directories, 1861 Finchingfield/Newman census, 1871 Great Sampford, Albert's service record, 1910 marriage, 1921 census, Broadmoor documents, Chelsea/Whitstable material.

**There is no image or transcription anywhere in the repo of Elizabeth's 1855 birth registration** (see Unsupported U1). That was one of the five items I was asked to look at; I could not.

## 1. Errors (text wrong against source)

**E1. Ch 1, George Ansell burial date. Medium.**
Manuscript: "George Ansell died of typhus fever, aged nineteen. He was buried on the eleventh of July."
Source: `death-certificates/George Ansell Death Cert.jpg` (viewed): "Fifteenth July 1858, The Village Weathersfield ... George Ansell, Male, 19 years, Agricultural Labourer, Typhus Fever 14 days, Certified ... Sixteenth July 1858". Registered 16 July. The 11 July burial date comes from the MyHeritage GED index (`BURI 11 JUL 1858`), not from an image. A burial four days before the death is impossible. Either the certificate date or the burial index is wrong; the chapter prints the burial date and omits the death date, so the reader never sees the clash. (Same pattern for Nathan: cert death 14 July 1856, registered 19 July; the chapter's "buried 19 July" is the GED burial date and coincides with the registration date. Plausible, but check the register image.)

**E2. Ch 1, Jemima's age at remarriage and Thomas's baptism. Medium.**
Manuscript: "She married again on the sixteenth of June 1854 ... She was forty-two. Her new husband, Thomas Stock ... had been baptised at Finchingfield in 1802." Then "The following February, at forty-three".
Source: marriage entry (image `Thomas Stock Jemima Ansell M 1854.jpg`, viewed): ages "49" (Thomas) and "43" (Jemima), widower/widow, Thomas's father "James Stock, Labourer", Jemima's "Edward Suckling, Labourer". The chapter's 42 is computed from a baptism date (15 Dec 1811; GED only), and contradicts the register's 43. The register's 49 for Thomas contradicts a 1802 baptism (that makes him 51; and 71, not the stated "seventy-two", at his December 1873 death; the death entry itself, viewed, reads 72). Neither conflict is acknowledged. The chapter should either quote the register's ages or say the baptism implies otherwise.

**E3. Ch 1, "across a little over three years". Low.**
Sarah died 29 Feb 1844, Edith 19 Feb 1847 (both certificates viewed): 2 years 11 months and 3 weeks, just under three.

**E4. Ch 2, "the auctioneer William Rand sold thirty-three lots". Low.**
Chelmsford Chronicle 23 Aug 1867 (transcription): Lots 2, 4, 5, 16, 18, 22, 23, 24 are "Not sold". The other figures (Lot 26 to Mr G. Wright, £102; Lot 32 Orchard to Mr Cutts, £58, with W. Phillpott; 16th inst. = 16 Aug; Rose and Crown) are right.

**E5. Ch 2, "two doors from the Red Lion". Low.**
Newman household transcription: schedule 95; Red Lion is schedule 97, and 95 has no entry in the "Inhabited" column. "Two schedules" is what the page gives; "two doors" is an inference.

(Everything else I could test in these chapters came out right. Verified correct, with image: all ages and relationships on 1861 Brewery House, 1861 Great Sampford, 1871 Albion Road, 1881 Frampton Park Road (including Lewis 6 months, Herbert 4 months, Bennett 19 boarder, "William G. Stone"); death certificate causes, ages, informants and "mark" for Sarah, Arthur, John, Eliza, Alfred, Edith, Emma, Nathan, Jemima, Thomas Stock, Ruth; Elizabeth Carter "five times" (Sarah, Arthur, John, Eliza, Alfred; Edith's informant was Phebe French); "four at Hudson's Hill" (Sarah, John, Eliza, Alfred); 1879 marriage entry word for word including "(dead)", the dash, the 88 Frampton Park Rd address, Emily's signature, Thomas's mark; William's 1856 registration (3 Nov, Emily informant). Arithmetic: Elizabeth 16 in April 1871; 17 months at Nathan's death; 24 at marriage; William 22/not quite two years younger; Jemima 61; Thomas 8 weeks after; Mabel registered exactly four weeks after birth; 51+ years in Broadmoor; Albert 14 in Nov 1901; twenty years 1901 to 1921. All Raven monument dates on the two faces photographed.)

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

**U1. Ch 1 opening: Elizabeth's birth registration. High (unverifiable, and it is the book's first scene).**
Claims: registered 30 March 1855 by Thomas Stock with his mark; "the mark of Thomas Stock, Father"; registrar "for the Finchingfield district"; born 18 February "at 'The Village, Weathersfield'"; father "an agricultural labourer"; "Jemima Stock, late Ansell formerly Suckling". Searched: all of `transcribed/`, `sources/images/birth-certificates/`, whole image tree by filename, both GEDs. No such document. Three of the 22 script-unmatched strings are these quotations. The only trace of the birth date is the MyHeritage GED `BIRT 18 FEB 1855, The Village, Weathersfield` (sourced there to censuses) and a transcription note saying "GRO certificate". The chapter quotes a document the repo does not hold. Same for Elizabeth being "six weeks" old at registration (40 days; fine if the date is right).

**U2. Ch 3, Isabella Chilton "had been a governess herself before her marriage". Medium.**
1871 census (viewed) gives only "Do Wife", born Oundle. Searched chapters, transcribed, GEDs: the only "governess" hit in a GED is a 1921 Cowes entry for a different woman (Ancestry GED line ~32617). No source.

**U3. Ch 3, "By 1874 the Chiltons had left London for Eltham House, at Westbury on Trym ... the census found them there in 1881 ... three servants". Medium.**
No 1874 or 1881 Chilton document in the repo (grep Chilton/Eltham/Westbury: nothing outside chapters/notes). The "nothing yet found places Elizabeth anywhere" caveat that follows is honest; the Chilton move is not shown.

**U4. Ch 4, William Graves's biography. Medium.**
"baptised at Radwinter ... January 1829", "a lodger in Tower Hamlets at twenty-two", "a married man in Bethnal Green with five children at thirty-two", "By 1871 ... 4 Mare Street ... Martha kept a chandler's shop", two boys of 1861 missing in 1871. Only 1881 (viewed: Graves 52, Martha 40, Lewis 6m) and Old Bailey evidence are in the repo. Searched Radwinter, Mare Street, Graves in transcribed and GEDs (no Graves individual in either GED). Also "Martha ... his second wife" is unsupported.

**U5. Ch 4, Susan Newman's marriage and address. Medium.**
"That Christmas Day, at Penge, Susan Newman married a man named Arthur John Davis, and by the spring they were living in Lambeth, at 362 Clayton Road." No source in repo (grep Clayton Road / Arthur John Davis: nothing). Also tangential to the narrative.

**U6. Ch 4, children's full names. Low-medium.**
"Charles Thomas", "Herbert Ernest", "Frederick William". No birth certificate for any child. "Herbert Ernest" appears in the MyHeritage tree; "Charles Thomas" only in the Ancestry tree. The trial papers say Charles, Herbert, Frederick.

**U7. Ch 4, "Elizabeth had no parents ... Every brother and sister she had ever had was dead before them. She had no near relatives at all". Medium.**
Proven only for the Ansell half-siblings (eight children, all dead by 1861 except Emma, d. Oct 1861; certificates viewed). Thomas Stock was a widower: a Thomas Stock, bachelor, married Sarah Hockley at Finchingfield 20 Oct 1827 (image in repo, viewed). Any children of that marriage would be Elizabeth's half-siblings; the book never mentions the first marriage. Jemima's brother Robert Suckling (witness 1854) and brother John with wife Ann and baby (1861 Finchingfield) are named in Chs 1-2, so uncles and a cousin existed. "No near relatives at all" is the narrator's assertion and conflicts with the book's own earlier pages.

**U8. Ch 4, the 1879 witnesses were "his parents". Medium.**
The register shows "Thomas Goldstone his X mark" and "Emily Goldstone". William also had a brother Thomas (b. c.1859) and a sister Emily (the Old Bailey Emily, "in service in London"). Probably the parents, but stated as fact; and "Elizabeth stood at the front of the church with her own side of it empty" is narration with no source for who attended.

**U9. Ch 3, Mabel Frances. Medium.**
Certificate (transcription of image): mother "Emily Gouldstone" with no maiden surname, father blank, informant "Emily Gouldstone, Mother" (signed). The chapter follows with "William's sister Emily was twenty-one, and unmarried", which tells the reader the mother is the sister. The certificate cannot show that: William's mother, also Emily Gouldstone (aged about 43, married), is equally "Emily Gouldstone", and nothing on the entry names the daughter. The author's own transcription note says "very probably". The chapter should say "probably".

**U10. Ch 2-3, local facts with no repo source. Medium (as a group).**
- Raven's brewery "established in 1855"; description "a brewhouse, a malthouse, the brewer's house and, next door, the tap"; "the Dog Inn was the Ravens' too"; Westwood "lived on a plot he owned" (census gives no ownership column; I viewed it); "Nobody in the village had water in the house ... it would until 1937".
- 1842 tithe survey: John Ansell the brewer as occupier of "a cottage and ... a row of tenements, both in the same lane"; Joseph Gouldstone "the plot beside a smithy near the Bull Inn"; "the Gouldstones had been in the village for at least four generations".
- 1851 Great Sampford census (Thomas "working as a smith on his own account", neighbours "a journeyman miller's and a blacksmith's", sisters Lydia and Ruth straw plaiters). No 1851 page for Sampford in repo (only GED mentions).
- White's 1863 directory, "Joseph Linsell and Son, straw plait manufacturers, a plait dealer and a straw hat maker" at Finchingfield: only Post Office directories (Sampford, 1862-78) are in `directories/`.
- "At Moor End ... White House ... built in the seventeenth century, timber-framed and plastered, two gables ... a square chimney stack with a heart carved at its base": no Historic England text or photograph in repo.
- "In Great Sampford that year [1871] the census found nine people living in sheds": no 1871 summary in repo; the notes cite Adrian Gray (book not in repo).
- "Hudson's Hill, where the High Street runs out of the village into the fields" and the Ch 1 geography of Dog Chase.
- Enumerator "John Burton": not on the page I viewed (the transcription says it comes from an index).
- Jemima's Hornchurch baptism (15 Dec 1811), Thomas's Finchingfield baptism (3 Oct 1802), the 1833 marriage at which "the parish clerk, Frederick Smee" witnessed (index shows witnesses Samuel Pentler and Frederick Smee, nothing about "parish clerk"), the Independent chapel's single date for the twins (4 Aug 1835), and the 1841 and 1851 household details: all from GED indexes, no images.

**U11. Ch 3, population and trade statistics. Medium.**
Wethersfield 1,770 and Finchingfield 2,594 (peaks "in the same year, 1851"); losses of 281 and 434; Great Sampford "nearly a third" lost, "one house in six" empty in 1881, "one in five" in 1901; Hertfordshire plaiters "almost halved in the 1870s and halved again in the 1880s"; Luton rateable value "up nearly half between 1876 and 1888"; plait from China and Japan "from about 1870". No census abstract or secondary source for these in `sources/`. (The lock-out PDF and the 1871/1878 directory lines "The straw-plait manufacture is carried on here" do support the existence of the trade.) I could not verify any of the numbers.

**U12. Ch 3, narrator inferences presented flatly. Low.**
"Elizabeth had somebody beside her who did not need her history explained" (nothing shows the two girls' relationship; Susan was in fact 15, entered as 16); "She had gone the way most people went, into service, a servant among servants" (no evidence from 1871 to Hackney 1879; the chapter then says so itself); "The one family in these pages that never had to leave Wethersfield emptied all the same" (Raven; the third and fourth tomb faces were not photographed, so "only Edith outlived her mother", Charlotte Mary d. Edinburgh 1900 aged 28, and Jane Raven's age 47 are unchecked).

**U13. Ch 5, Courtenay Place setting and travel. Medium.**
"a terrace of thirteen bay-fronted cottages ... gone up the year before, on streets that had not even existed when William and Elizabeth married"; "Tuppence covered the return fare on the workmen's train"; "reach Upper Thames Street by half past seven"; "the platforms of St James's Street were the first thing visible from the front step". Only the map crop is in `sources/images` (the notes cite an OS 1891-95 revision for the count of thirteen; that is a decade later and cannot show bay fronts or build dates). No fare, timetable or build-date source. "By half past seven" is a misuse of Ada's testimony, which gives 7.30 as his usual time of coming HOME.

**U14. Ch 5, Charles and Emma Clarke detail rests on one newspaper reporter. Medium (see also A1).**
"kept the ground floor with his wife Emma and let the rooms above" is not in any deposition (Emma says her husband is "the tenant"; the Gouldstones had back room, kitchen and scullery on the first floor, Hamiltons the front room). "without fuss" (killing rabbits) and "a friendship that asks little of either man" are the author's words; the source says "a disposition to be friendly with Clark, in a reserved sort of way".

**U15. Ch 5, drink night: sources disagree and the chapter does not say. Medium.**
Chapter: Friday (pay day), "the only time in her ten weeks that Ada ever saw him so". Ada at the Old Bailey: "on the Friday after the babies were born" (supports). Emma Clarke, coroner deposition (`Coroner Depositions/Emma Clarke/Emma Clarke.txt`): "The following night (Thursday) he got the worse for drink, and this is the only time I ever saw him so." Times 11 Aug: Emma said the drink was on "Bank Holiday" and also "the next night, August 2". Three accounts, two nights. The chapter picks Ada's day silently.

**U16. Ch 5, "On one of those two days Clarke remarked to his own wife how fortunate they were in their lodgers." Low.**
Guardian 18 Aug: "only a day or two before". Narrowing to Monday or Tuesday is the author's. Holiday Monday and Tuesday is supported (Ada at the inquest, as reported by the Guardian 18 Aug; Graves at the Old Bailey says Tuesday he "ought to have been there", so the firm did NOT treat Tuesday as a holiday; "so far as the household understood it" is a careful hedge, and defensible).

**U17. Prologue, small overstatements. Low.**
"The false birthplace remained with him for the rest of his life": shown for 1901, 1911 (HMS Dominion transcription: "Hukening, Honyllon, Germany") and 1921 only. "There is no such place": a negative; not sourced. The Prologue spells the mother "Elizabeth Goldstone"; the certificate image (viewed) reads "Goldston" and carries no maiden name ("She had been born Elizabeth Stock" is the author's knowledge, not the certificate's).

**U18. Ch 1, "Emily was ill in her mind when he was born". Supported, but cite.**
Thomas at the Old Bailey: "she was in a very bad state of mind when the prisoner was born" and "18 years ago ... business troubles ... I kept a woman with her". That also supports Ch 2's "In the middle of the decade ... the smithy ran into difficulties ... Thomas brought a woman into the house". Emily the sister: "through trouble in business ... attempted to commit suicide several times". Ch 2 "tried at least once" is a fair reading ("tried to grain herself with a scarf"). Not an error; the book gives no source line.

**U19. Ch 2 cholera: selective use. Low.**
The Chronicle (9 Nov) says the drainage report came from "our correspondent was informed at Sampford", that the summons "was allowed to be withdrawn on his undertaking to remedy the nuisance", and that the boy was "certainly of a very delicate constitution" and Winterflood "a very ailing individual" and a pugilist. The chapter gives only the foul-drainage point, as though the paper had stated it. "The first to die was a stranger": the paper does not say first. Essex Standard 28 Nov ("details ... very much exaggerated", attacks "did not exceed 18") is the other paper; Chronicle's 18 cases/7-8 deaths is consistent with it.

**U20. Ch 1, Joseph's age. Low.**
Quote "I am 65" is faithful to the Chronicle (transcription). Baptism (GED) 17 Mar 1793 makes him 63. The chapter later gives 80 (1871) and 83 (burial 1876). Defensible as his own words; a short note would pre-empt readers doing the sums.

**U21. Ch 3, Spooner letter. Low.**
"two shillings a week more for horsekeepers and stockmen": the figure is "2[s]." with a broken "s" by the transcriber's own note. And the Mercury's "fewer than fifty" from Mr Moxon answers "our correspondent's statements of last week", not the "between 50 and 60" printed in the same issue; the chapter presents the secretary as correcting the 50-60 figure.

## 3. Attribution problems

**A1. Ch 5 passes a newspaper reporter's profile off as narrative fact.** The opening paragraphs (Clarke's walks by the river, long silences, "boyish games", rabbits, "There's my Charley / There's my Herbert", the 6.04 and "as good as a timepiece", the landlord remark) all come from one unsigned passage in the Walthamstow and Leyton Guardian, 18 August 1883 (transcription lines ~350-395). That passage also contains errors the book does not repeat (William "the son of a country farrier", the "Kirkley wharf", "a great amateur musician playing the cornet, the flute") and is a reporter's paraphrase of the landlord ("Mr. Clark ... stated"), not testimony. The chapter never names the paper. "His landlord would later say" is accurate to the paper's "His landlord says".

**A2. Ch 5 "He still had the rupture from Finchingfield, and it distressed him" sits inside a paragraph about what Ada saw.** Ada never mentions a rupture (deposition, Old Bailey, inquest). The rupture is from Robert, Bennett Gouldstone and John Clark. Move it or attribute it.

**A3. Ch 4 and 5, Elizabeth's words.** Every quotation attributed to her ("We commenced housekeeping...", "one of our own family", "painfully aware...", "During our five years married life...", "strange and wandering") is from one statement: her letter written about 15-19 September 1883, printed in the Daily Telegraph (20 Sept, "Statement of his Wife"), reproduced in Essex Weekly News 21 Sept, Western Daily Press 21 Sept, Walthamstow Guardian 22 Sept, Dundee Courier. One witness, written after conviction to save his life ("I am certain he was insane"). The chapter says "Elizabeth wrote" without date or occasion, and "Long afterwards she would say" (four years after the events, a week after the verdict). Quote check: "burden" in the chapter; Essex Weekly News prints "burthen" and the Western Daily Press "burden" (the transcription's own note); chapter follows the latter. "Strange and wandering" is the Telegraph's wording; Ada's "despondent" is from the 18 Aug cross-examination ("desponding" in the transcription).
Also: her "five years married life" is used twice (Ch 5) while the book's own dates give 4 years 5 months (the Guardian says "four years and a half"); no comment.

**A4. Ch 2 Chelmsford Chronicle emigration paragraph.** Correctly attributed ("The Chronicle had the figures from the agent, Charles Jay"; the transcription confirms one shared text across four papers). "Charles" is not in the Chronicle text ("Mr. Jay"). The on-board scene is a London paper's, which the chapter says. Fine, but note the scene quoted is a second-hand picture of the ship, and the same paragraph says "Very few among the passengers are locked-out men", which the chapter does quote; this undercuts using the voyage as part of "the lock-out" story, and it would be fair to say the chapter's lead-in ("In the middle of it, others left altogether") is the author's framing.

## 4. Source problems (transcription vs image, header errors)

**S1. George Ansell: certificate (death 15 July 1858) vs GED burial (11 July 1858).** See E1. The GED is an index of the parish register; the image of the register is not in the repo.

**S2. 1851 Wethersfield household (GED index text only).** Index reads Jemima 39, John Nathan 16, George 11, Emma 9, "Father in Law" John. The chapter says "George and Emma, eleven and ten". Emma (d. Oct 1861 at 21; baptised 30 May 1841) would be 10 on 30 March 1851; the index's 9 and the chapter's 10 cannot both be the enumerator's figure and no 1851 image is held. Low.

**S3. Marriage 1879 and 1854.** No transcription files exist; I read the images directly and the chapter matches. (The repo header for the 1879 entry, if any, is not in `transcribed/`.)

**S4. 1861 Great Sampford transcription.** Header says "Enumerator's name not on this page: 'John Burton' is from the index". The chapter states Burton as fact. Also schedule 5 is not labelled "the smithy" on the page ("Village" throughout); identification is by Thomas's occupation and the 1867 sale.

**S5. 1871 Stoke Newington transcription.** Matches the image I viewed (Chilton 35, Isabella 32 Oundle/Northamptonshire, Guy 5, Stanley 4, Reginald 11 [months], Susan 16, Elizabeth 18; General Servant; born Wethersfield). The transcription's note that Susan was really 15 is useful and not in the chapter. The age cells for Chilton and Elizabeth are heavily struck through on the image; the readings 35 and 18 are the only plausible ones.

**S6. Chelmsford Chronicle 13 March 1857 transcription.** Partial; the chapter says "The next witness was Thomas" (the transcription marks "[...]" omissions elsewhere but none between Joseph's cross-examination and Thomas). The hearing date is unconfirmed (notes say probably 6 March; chapter says only "March 1857", which is safe). Chapter quotes with silent omissions in the first quote ("I am 65, and knew ... never could explain what they were to be done to") are contiguous in the source; fine.

**S7. Albert's service record.** Date of birth entered as 19 June 1886, first engagement 11 Nov 1901 (Impregnable), confirming the Prologue. 1910 marriage entry age 22 matches the birth certificate year (1887), the service record's 1886 would make him 23; the Prologue's "a year older" is therefore right.

## 5. Unused material worth the author's attention

1. **Joseph Gouldstone, insolvent, in Chelmsford gaol, January 1839** (London Gazette 24 Jan 1839, issue 19698, p. 158; discharged, Essex Standard 8 Mar 1839). Transcriptions and images exist in `transcribed/` and `Media-coverage/Part-One/` and are not used in Chs 1-5, although Chapter 2 deals with the smithy's later failure and the Old Bailey evidence ties "business troubles" to the family.
2. **John Nathan Ansell was baptised 14 Sept 1834 and born 19 May 1834** (GED, RG4/1768 p. 44), four months and a week after the Christmas Day 1833 wedding.
3. **Thomas Stock and Sarah Hockley, 20 Oct 1827, Finchingfield** (`marriage-certificates/1827 ...jpg`, viewed; both made marks). An unexplored first family for Thomas Stock; bears on "no near relatives" (U7).
4. **Old Bailey, Thomas Gouldstone**: "I had business troubles about 18 years ago; it was at that time that my wife tried to strangle herself" (the jury explains "grain" = Essex for strangle); also that William's father's sister "wore a strait jacket for some years", and a second cousin died in a madhouse. This is the only source for Ch 1-2 on Emily, and it dates the crisis to c.1865.
5. **E. V. King, 29, "Farmers Daug.", born Gt Bardfield, Visitor at Thomas Raven's Brewery House, April 1861** (viewed). Raven's widow, Elizabeth Victoria, was 69 on the tomb in 1901 (b. c.1832). A likely identification; the chapters say nothing.
6. **Eliza Stock, 17, servant, pleaded guilty at Chelmsford Adjourned Sessions, February 1869** to stealing a silk jacket and lace at Great Dunmow (transcription, flagged "not identified"). The ages do not fit Elizabeth (14 in Feb 1869), which is one reason to keep it out; but it is a live gap-year lead the author has closed on his own authority.
7. **James Gouldstone, blacksmith, licensed to the Three Horseshoes, Great Bardfield, 30 Nov 1874** (Chelmsford Chronicle 4 Dec 1874 clip and transcription), Thomas's brother.
8. **Great Sampford directories 1871 and 1878 both say "The straw-plait manufacture is carried on here"**: a primary line for the Ch 1/3 plaiting point.
9. Chelmsford Chronicle 30 Aug 1861 sale also lists "a large garden occupied by Goldstone" in Wethersfield (unidentified).
10. Ada Hamilton at the Walthamstow inquest (Guardian 18 Aug): "at noon on the latter day [Tuesday] he seemed as if he had been crying" and that he "had had a holiday Monday and Tuesday": direct support for Ch 5's holiday and crying lines.
