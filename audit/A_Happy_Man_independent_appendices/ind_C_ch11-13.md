# Independent audit C: Chapters 11, 12, 13

Scope: chapters/CHAPTER_11.md, CHAPTER_12.md, CHAPTER_13.md against the Old Bailey Proceedings (website text and the 11 page images), the 18 August depositions, the committal form, and the press transcriptions. Images actually viewed: Old Bailey 188309100134 to 144 (all 11); CRIM 1/19/2 0024 (Gould deposition) and 0025 (statement of the accused); IPN front covers of 18 and 25 August 1883. Not viewed as images: depositions other than Gould's, the 0043 cover sheet, newspaper page images. For those I relied on the transcriptions, and say so.

Headline: the quotations are accurate. Every quoted passage I checked from the Proceedings, the depositions and the press matches its source, and I found no wrong name, wrong date or wrong sum. The weaknesses are (a) silent choices between disagreeing papers, (b) a few narrator statements with no source in the repo, and (c) the order in which defence witnesses are presented.

## 1. Errors (text wrong against source)

None at high severity.

1. Ch13, "Dr Savage of Bethlem" and "physician at Bethlem Hospital". Low. Proceedings p. 615 image prints "physician at Bethlehem Hospital"; the Times (Grain's opening and Savage) prints "Bethlehem". "Bethlem" is the hospital's actual short name, but it is not what any source for this trial prints. Not inside quote marks, so low.
2. Ch13, Honey "had seen the string tied loose round Charles's neck". Fine as to words (Proceedings p. 611: "a piece of string was tied round the neck of the eldest; it was loose"). No error; listed only because Ch11 attributes the noose answer to the deposition (correct: Honey deposition, "I saw the noose on the string which was loose").

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

1. Ch11: "Mrs Pelly's Refuge Home, which the wife of the vicar of St John's, Stratford, had founded that year." Medium. Searched all of transcribed/, sources/ and the chapters for "Pelly": only the Times and Morning Advertiser of 20 Aug, which say "a lady who is doing good work ... in connexion with Mrs. Pelly's Refuge Home". Neither says who Mrs Pelly was, her husband's office, the parish, or the founding year. The identification appears only in the author's notes, which are not evidence. Either cite a source (parish or directory record) or cut to what the Times says.
2. Ch11: "the coroner had issued his warrant committing him for trial." Medium. No warrant is in the archive (the committal.txt header says so). The Times of 14 Aug has the verdict and "five verdicts" signed, but no warrant. The Old Bailey heading ("charged on the Coroner's Inquisition") supports an inquisition, not a warrant on 13 August. Say "the inquisition" or cite.
3. Ch12: the grand-jury paragraph ("at least twelve ... not more than twenty-three ... If twelve of them agreed ... 'a true bill' on the back of the indictment ... Only then was he brought into court to plead"). Medium. No source is cited, and none in the repo is clearly tied to it (an Archbold HTML file is in transcribed/; I did not find a passage confirming these numbers). The numbers are correct for 1883 as I understand it. But "Only then was he brought into court to plead" is too strong: Ch13 itself says he was "charged on the Coroner's Inquisition", and a coroner's inquisition needed no grand jury bill. Suggest "to be tried on an indictment".
4. Ch12: "Before he was tried, Elizabeth went to see him three times at the House of Detention." Low. Source is Elizabeth's own statement printed after the trial (Essex Weekly News 21 Sep, "three times at the House of Detention, once at the Old Bailey, and on Tuesday last in the Prison at Chelmsford"). Sound inference, but it is a post-conviction statement and is not named in the chapter.
5. Ch13: "The eldest of Thomas's seven children, Emily". Low-medium. Proceedings: Thomas says "the eldest is a daughter"; Robert says he is "the eldest son"; Emily says only "I am the prisoner's sister". Nothing identifies Emily as that daughter. Inference presented as fact.
6. Ch13: "His brother Bennett worked in the same shop." Low. Proceedings (p. 613): "I am employed at the Falkirk Ironworks". "Same shop" is not stated.
7. Ch13: Sunderland "said that neither woman had been certified or confined as a lunatic." Low. Proceedings p. 615 (cross-exam) covers certification and confinement for the mother only; for Mrs Andrews it says only that she "lives at home with her husband". The Times says "neither the mother nor her sister had been certified". The chapter blends the two without saying so.
8. Ch13: Poland "told them ... the wilful killing of a human being was murder, and it was for the person charged to show that he was not guilty of it." Medium (legal framing). This is reported correctly from the Times, and the chapter says "he told them". But it is Poland's advocacy, and the reader is not told it is stated as law by the prosecutor only. It conflicts with Day's later direction as the Telegraph, Chronicle and Times print it (the question was knowledge of the nature and quality of the act and that it was wrong). Not Ch13's job to resolve, but a sentence flagging it as the Crown's formulation would protect the reader.
9. Ch12: IPN "on the morning of the committal". Low. The 18 Aug IPN is dated 18 Aug, and its text covers the 9 and 11 Aug hearings (transcribed IPN p2 18 Aug), so the committal could not be in it. "Morning of the committal" assumes publication timing. I viewed both covers: layout matches the chapter's description (house, bedroom scene, portraits labelled Craggs, Mrs Hamilton the nurse, Mrs Clark the landlady; 25 Aug oval portrait, mortuary, jury viewing bodies, mother drowning child, Ipswich throat-cutting). The cover scan is low-resolution, so I could not read the label "A Witness" or confirm the hammer.

## 3. Attribution problems and silent choices between papers

Ch11 (18 August hearing)

1. Who said Gould "was not well up in insanity". Times: Gould said it ("a speciality"). Daily News (p6), Guardian and IPN (same copy): the clerk Wells said it and Atkinson "would not press the question". Chapter follows the Times (Gould) silently. Note too the Times's own image is clipped at that line (transcription header), and the Daily News/Guardian/IPN say nothing of the Bench dissenting or of Atkinson's "long extract". The chapter's "bench dissented" is Times-only. Medium.
2. Gould's cross-examination. Deposition (image 0024, viewed): "I have seen prisoner several times". Times and IPN/Guardian: "only seen the prisoner in connexion with this case". The chapter says the deposition "gives his cross-examination in a single line" but does not quote it or note that the line contradicts the press. Medium. This matters later if anyone suggests Gould knew William.
3. Honey's time. Deposition: "about 10 minutes to 6" (chapter uses this). Guardian prints "ten o'clock" (IPN too, with "August 9"). Chapter silently takes the deposition. Fine choice, but the disagreement is unmentioned. Low.
4. "Crowds at all three stations", followed by the Guardian quote. The three stations come from the Morning Advertiser ("several hundreds ... at each of the three stations"). The Guardian names two (Stratford Main and Maryland Point). The chapter attributes the disappointment sentence to the Guardian correctly but the "all three" to nobody. Low.
5. Cheeseman, "hadn't a piece of string in his hand". Deposition says "string"; the Times says "cord", Daily News/Guardian/IPN "rope" and place it at the moment he said "ready for the rope". Chapter quotes the deposition without that context. Low.
6. Wording of Ada's "never heard the wife say". Deposition (clerk's garble: "the prisoner say she wished"); Times: "the wife". Chapter correctly uses the Times and says so. Good practice.
7. Mug of water. Guardian says only "was brought"; Advertiser says Honey brought it. Chapter follows Guardian. No issue.
8. Prisoner's statement form. Chapter quotes the printed address and stops after "upon your Trial". The printed form (image 0025, viewed) continues "And you are clearly to understand that you have nothing to hope from any Promise of Favour ...". Chapter says "in the words printed" but gives only the first sentences. Low. Image confirms the rest: "I reserve my defence", empty signature space, Powell alone, footnote (b) "Get him to sign it if he will", and (Gould deposition image) the ink note "Insp. Craggs bound over to prosecute". All correct.
9. Daily News wording: Daily News says "Mr. Atkinson saying he would reserve his defence"; Guardian "intimating that the accused would reserve his defence". Both quoted correctly.

Ch12

10. Guardian 15 Sep also says "the prisoner will be defended by Mr. Forrest Fulton", but at trial it was Grain and Elliott (Proceedings; Times). Chapter silently drops Fulton and gives only the prosecution counsel. Low-medium (a change of counsel or a paper error, not resolved anywhere).
11. "'The trial will take place during the week,' The Times told its readers ... Mr Poland and Mr Montagu Williams were to prosecute." The Times item (12 Sep) says only the true bill and that quote. The counsel come from the Guardian. Read as one source, it misattributes. Low.
12. Graves's notice "dated the tenth of September". Low-medium. The transcription header says the date "reads as '10th Sept., 1883' but only the tops of the figures show", the clip is 507 x 90 pixels, and the Times issue date comes from a filename. The chapter states the date without hedging. I did not see the image.
13. Vicar's "ten months" in Walthamstow (Guardian) vs Emma Clark "about eleven months" (Proceedings p. 609). The vicar's remark is quoted as his; no problem.
14. Money story: Graves's 5 Sep letter says "we can see our way to get £20"; Guardian 15 Sep says "have raised ... £20". Chapter reports both in order. Correct.

Ch13

15. Order of defence witnesses. Proceedings order: Thomas, Robert, Emily, Graves, Cakebread, Bennett, John Clark, Skelton, Westbrook, Kinnaird, Charles Gouldstone (Epping), Byford, Sunderland, Savage. Chapter order: Thomas, Robert, Emily, Byford, Charles of Epping, Sunderland, then Graves, Cakebread, Bennett, Clark, Skelton, Westbrook, Kennard, Savage. The chapter says only first (Thomas) and last (Savage) explicitly, and signals regrouping ("For the father's side ..."). A reader will still take it as sequence. Medium. Say once that the family are grouped, not in calling order.
16. "Kennard" vs the Proceedings' "KINNAIRD" (printed twice, p. 614). Chapter follows the Times ("Mr. Kennard") and the man's own signature "H. J. KENNARD" on his Daily Telegraph letter of 19 Sep. That is the better spelling, but the chapter does not tell the reader that the sworn record prints Kinnaird. Low.
17. Savage's "last patient at Bethlem ... a woman who had killed her whole family". Times only. The Proceedings (the sworn record) has nothing like it. The chapter presents it in the same paragraph as sworn evidence ("He told the court one thing more"). The Telegraph and Chronicle summaries speak of Savage admitting knowledge of crime and penalty, not this. Medium: label it "the Times reports".
18. Day's interruption of Savage ("the very question the jury had to decide") is Times-only and is correctly marked "according to the Times".
19. Savage "saw the prisoner once, on the Thursday, the day before the trial". Proceedings cross-exam: "yesterday" and, later in the same answer, "what I saw of him on Saturday" (p. 616, confirmed on the image: a Sessions Paper slip). Times: "Thursday". Chapter chooses Thursday, which is consistent with a Friday trial, but does not mention the printed "Saturday". Low.
20. Which indictment. Chapter: "several indictments ... he was tried on one" (Charles). Supported: Proceedings heading; Times ("first tried upon the indictment ... Charles Gouldstone"); Telegraph and Essex Weekly News agree. But the Times's opening line and Day's summing-up speak of "an indictment charging ... his five children" and "indicted for the wilful murder of his five children". So the papers disagree with the Proceedings on what the indictment named; chapter silently takes the Proceedings (correct choice, as the primary record). The chapter also does not say the Proceedings print "charged on the Coroner's Inquisition" as something not covered by a grand-jury bill (see 2.3).
21. Poland's "I shall be hung, and I am ready for the rope". The Times transcription header (checked against the image) says this is ONE quotation in the paper, Poland's own joining of two separate sayings (Cheeseman: "ready for the rope"; "I shall be hanged" said to his wife). The chapter presents it as William's words ("for he had said, ..."). Reader could take it as a single utterance. Medium-low. Also the spelling "hung" is the Times's.
22. Poland's remark that William knew "the law made it a crime" rests on the Times's report of counsel, not on any witness. Chapter gets this right ("Poland said").
23. Counsel's client: Chapter says Poland and Williams prosecuted "on behalf of the Director of Public Prosecutions" (Times). Telegraph says "Public Prosecutor"; Essex Weekly News "the Treasury". Chapter silently takes the Times. No error, as the Times quotes Poland himself.
24. Wheatley "preying" / "playing". Proceedings p. 610 (image): "I have had this *playing* on my mind" then "Cross-examined. He said 'preying,' not 'playing.'" The chapter's "single answer" is exact. The Times of 15 Sep prints "playing"; Essex Weekly News also "playing"; the Times of 20 Aug (magistrates) printed "preying". Chapter's "The Times printed 'playing' the next morning" is correct. (The line is ambiguous: "He" could be Wheatley reporting what William said, as the chapter implies, or Wheatley correcting himself. The chapter does not say which.)
25. Grain's words on the new Act and "detained during her Majesty's pleasure": Essex Weekly News only, and the chapter says so. The Times's version of Grain's opening has no mention of the Act (it appears only in Day's summing-up). Fine.

## 4. Source problems

1. Website text vs printed page (11 images). I read every page. For all passages the chapters quote (Ada, Emma, Cheeseman, Folkard, Wheatley, Gould, Honey, Thomas, Robert, Emily, Graves, Cakebread, Bennett, John Clark, Skelton, Westbrook, Kinnaird, Charles G., Byford, Sunderland, Savage), the page and the website file agree word for word, after the corrections the file header lists. One observation not in the header: p. 609's running head reads "Eleventh Session, 1881-82" (printer's slip; other pages "1882-83"). It does not affect the text. Resolution is too low to confirm that the dash in Cheeseman's "b——" is two dashes; the page prints at least one.
2. Header of the website file says Thomas's "Charles Gouldstone, a second cousin" is "William" in the Epping witness's own evidence. The chapter wisely avoids naming the cousin in Thomas's evidence. Also the Times names him "William Gouldstone, a second cousin of mine". So Proceedings (Charles) and Times (William) disagree on the second cousin's name inside Thomas's evidence.
3. Daily News 20 Aug p6 says Cheeseman "I made a note of the conversation a few minutes afterwards" and Gould's account with "Dr. Twining having taken the clothes off". Not used by the chapter; consistent with the Proceedings.
4. Deposition header note on Cheeseman and Wheatley attribute meanings to the press; consistent with my reading. No transcription-vs-image disagreement found in the Gould deposition (viewed). It reads "I have seen prisoner several times" (cross), and the ink footnote reads "Commit[ted] for Trial next Sess[ions] Cen. Crim. Ct / Insp. Craggs bound over to prosecute". Matches the transcription.
5. Times 20 Aug transcription says the Times image's left margin is clipped; the claim of who spoke the "not well up" line is therefore unverifiable from the Times alone (see 3.1).

## 5. Unused material worth the author's attention

- Ada at the door: Proceedings p. 607: "Mr. Gouldstone, what have you done, you wicked man?" - "No, I am a happy man." The Times (15 Sep) has the same ("No, I am a happy man"). The 9 Aug deposition has "I have killed the children. I am a happy man now." Three versions of the book's central phrase. Ch13 skips it.
- Thomas Gouldstone's own words on the asylum: "my father had a sister who wore a strait jacket for some years" (p. 611); the Juryman's gloss "Grain is the Essex word for strangle" (p. 611), which explains Thomas's "grain herself with a scarf". The chapter says "tried to strangle herself" and omits the gloss; only the strait-jacket line and the Times's "William" vs Proceedings "Charles" are interesting here.
- Skelton, cross-exam: William "dancing and singing foolish things, what they call the Salvation Army hymns" (p. 614). The chapter uses only the lift remark.
- Bennett's examination in chief ("kill himself out of his misery", p. 613) is skipped; the chapter quotes only his cross-examination about absences.
- Emma Clark: "I was examined before the Coroner, but not before the Magistrate" (p. 609), which confirms the 18 Aug hearing did not call her (Ch11 correctly lists six witnesses).
- Elizabeth's statement (Essex Weekly News 21 Sep): visits at "House of Detention", "Old Bailey", "Chelmsford"; and "You will take care of her, won't you?". Outside this batch but Ch12 touches it.
- Time of verdict: jury retired "twenty minutes past five" (Telegraph, Chronicle, Morning Post) and returned in about a quarter of an hour. Ch13 has no clock time for the trial at all.
- Morning Post and Essex Weekly News both have "The Prisoner: I am not guilty" after the verdict; the Times says he "replied in the negative". Not in these chapters but flagged by transcriber for Ch14.
- IPN 18 Aug text of the 11 Aug hearing: "Colonel Howard, a justice" and "Mr. Lister left the bench" for about five minutes. The Times on 9 Aug has Lister in the chair with Glenny; the Daily Chronicle of 13 Aug names Powell, Spicer, Glenny, Lister for 11 Aug. Ch11's description of Powell and Lister on 18 Aug (Daily News p6) is right.

## 6. What I could not check

- Whether the Times 20 Aug line about Gould saying he was "not well up" is Gould's or Wells's: the image is clipped (per transcription header) and I did not see the page image.
- The 0043 cover sheet, other depositions' images and newspaper page images: not viewed. I rely on the transcriptions' own checked-against-image notes for those.
- Graves's notice date (10 Sep) and house number (57 or 67): clip too small per header; not viewed.
- The "shortly after eleven o'clock" start of the 18 Aug hearing: Guardian/IPN only; no second source.
- The legal statements in Ch12 (grand jury numbers) and Poland's statement of law: no legal source in the repo that I could tie to them.
- Whether the "A Witness" portrait label (Ch12) and the hammer in the bedroom sketch are as described: the 18 Aug cover scan is too small.
- Composition of the bench on 18 Aug beyond Powell and Lister: depositions are signed by Powell alone; Lister's presence rests on the Daily News and the Times ("Mr. Lister").
