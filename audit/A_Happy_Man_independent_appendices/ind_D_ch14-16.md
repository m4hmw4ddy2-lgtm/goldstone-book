## 1. Errors

**E1. Ch14, para 1 vs later paras (internal contradiction). Medium.**
"apart from his plea, no report has him say a word." Ch14 later quotes the Essex Weekly News: "The Prisoner: I am not guilty." So at least one report does. In addition the Morning Post, 15 Sep (`Morning_Post_15_September_1883_trial_close_transcription.txt`), prints "The Prisoner—I am not guilty." So two papers, not "a line of its own" in the EWN. The Times says he replied "in the negative". The chapter should say the papers differ.

**E2. Ch16, "Harcourt signed the order for Broadmoor on the Monday, the day to which the hanging had been put back." Medium.**
By the chapter's own account the respite was made indefinite on 1 Oct ("The respite no longer had an end"; Times 2 Oct: "until further signification of Her Majesty's pleasure"). The 8th was no longer the day of any execution. The warrants (images viewed) say the execution "was respited until further signification of Her Majesty's Pleasure". Delete or recast the clause.

**E3. Ch16 line 1, "The Home Secretary ... took a week." Low-medium.**
The letter reached the Sheriff on Tue 25 Sep (EWN 28 Sep p5 and p8), eleven days after sentence. Nothing in the sources puts a week on it. The Central News paragraph is 22 Sep (eight days after). Unclear which interval is meant.

**E4. Ch16, BMJ 6 Oct: "It closed on a warning about where the system it complained of could lead: 'the rich murderer might go to Broadmoor, and the poor one to the gallows'." Medium.**
`BMJ_6_October_1883_pp682-683`, last paragraph: the sentence continues "...if the present duplex system of dealing with prisoners alleged to be insane were permitted to continue". The context is the Benn (Matlock) case: officials removing untried prisoners to asylums, "a time might come when the State had less immaculate servants". It is not a warning about the system that nearly hanged Gouldstone. The words are exact; the gloss reverses the target. The article does not close there only on that point either; the chapter's "closed on" is fair for position, not for meaning.

**E5. Ch16, "Dr Clarke, from London" asserted as the second examiner. Medium (unreconciled conflict).**
EWN 28 Sep, Sunday Dispatch 30 Sep, Times 2 Oct all say Clarke. BMJ 29 Sep guesses "Dr. Gower" (as printed). Nicolson's 1917 BMJ obituary of Orange (`sources/pdfs/Nicolson-WilliamOrangeCB-1917.pdf`, col. 2 of the page shown) says of Gouldstone: "Here Drs. Orange and Gover found distinct evidence of insanity." The chapter should flag that the later source names Gover. Nothing in the repo identifies Clarke beyond the surname.

**E6. Ch16, Sunday Dispatch "six or seven hours" vs EWN. Medium.**
Ch16 gives only the Dispatch figure. EWN 28 Sep p8 and W&L Guardian 6 Oct (shared copy, one witness) say "nearly three hours", with the gaol surgeon Hunt Carter and the chaplain present for part, and that the doctors also saw "the convict's brother". The EWN is local and specific; the Dispatch is a Sunday London weekly. The chapter attributes ("It put their time...") so it is not wrong, but it hides a 2x disagreement the reader should see.

**E7. Ch15, Elizabeth's visit companions. Low.**
Ch15: "with his sister and his aunt" (her own words: "His sister and aunt accompanied me"). W&L Guardian 22 Sep: "visited on Tuesday by the High Sheriff ... and also by his wife, mother, and sister." Not noted. Defensible to follow her own words; the conflict should be known.

**E8. Ch15, "burden of the family" inside Elizabeth's printed statement. Low.**
The Telegraph (20 Sep) and EWN print "burthen". Script diff of the whole statement against the Telegraph: this is the only difference. The chapter says the statement is "as the Daily Telegraph printed it" only for William's letter, but the block is presented as a quotation. Either restore "burthen" or note modernisation. William's letter in the chapter is word-for-word the Telegraph's (diff clean, including "Dreadfull", "Happey", "Chappeling").

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

**U1. Ch14 para 1, "he was not put on oath at his own trial, because to swear him was thought a form of compulsion."** No source in the repo gives the reason. Searched: `Full text of "Archbold's ..."` html for "Married Women" (no hits), the Criminal Evidence Act 1898 PDF (text extraction fails after the preamble: pdftotext XRef errors; I could read only s.1 opening and proviso (a)/(b)). The usual explanation was the rule of interest-disqualification, not compulsion. Unsupported narrator claim. Medium.

**U2. Ch14, MWPA exception ("since the first of January, a wife could give evidence against her husband in proceedings to protect her own property").** No source in the repo (grep for "Married Women" in transcribed/ returned none relevant). It is correct in substance (Married Women's Property Act 1882, in force 1 Jan 1883) but unsourced. Low.

**U3. Ch14, "fifteen years".** The Act is dated 12 Aug 1898 (PDF), i.e. 14 years 11 months after 14 Sep 1883. Fine as rounding; "the prisoner could still address the court without being sworn" is not checkable from the PDF (the saving proviso is on the unreadable page). Low.

**U4. Ch15, Leeds Mercury paragraph: "In April the Attorney General ... Sir Henry James ... come through committee ... on the twenty-first of August ... Gladstone's government withdrew it."** The only source in the repo for the Leeds Mercury is the Dundee Courier 21 Sep (`Dundee_Courier_and_Argus_Sep_21_1883.txt`), which says only that there was feeling in medical circles and that a Court of Appeal would have heard the case "if Parliament had done its duty last session". All Bill details (April, James, committee, 21 Aug, withdrawn) come from Hansard, which is not in the repo; the notes cite URLs but I could not verify (fetch timed out). Blend of paper's statement and author's research presented as "the correspondent blamed Parliament for that". Also "A man condemned to death in England had no court to appeal to" is the author's statement and omits the Court for Crown Cases Reserved (points of law). Also the paper says "strong representations will be made"; chapter's "predicted a campaign" is fair. Medium.

**U5. Ch15, Walthamstow Guardian count: "The Daily Telegraph, the Morning Post and the Daily News called for his state of mind to be further examined."** That is the Guardian's framing ("an equal number pleading for further inquiry"). The Morning Post extract in the Guardian does not ask for inquiry: it says guilt "never for a moment in doubt" under M'Naghten and predicts public opinion will turn. The chapter then describes the Post accurately, so the chapter contradicts its own sentence. Attribute the grouping to the Guardian. Low-medium.

**U6. Ch15, Wood: "that William had chosen a madman's way of going about it."** Wood: "he adopted an irrational method". "Madman's way" is the author's gloss. Low.

**U7. Ch15, "The Daily News, writing after the letters of the Monday".** Daily News extract (quoted in Guardian 22 Sep) is undated; the dating is inferred from its content (quotes Telegraph juror letter and Savage's Times letter). Defensible inference; the Daily News 22 Sep file is only the Central News paragraph. Low.

**U8. Ch15, Savage "In the box, Savage had said: 'I believe that he knew he was killing the children, and that the penalty for that act was death.'"** Verbatim from Old Bailey account (`sources/trial-docs/Trial Account Old Bailey website.txt`, line 108), but it is the last clause of cross-examination. Immediately before it Savage says: "if he knew I do not think it would have influenced his actions". Omitting that sentence makes the quote harder than the witness's whole answer, in a chapter whose point is Savage being misunderstood. Medium (context). Note too the Old Bailey text has Savage seeing him "on Saturday" in one place and "yesterday" in another; the chapter's "Thursday" follows the Times, Savage's Lancet letter (via "Thursday" in Telegraph juror letter) and is sound.

**U9. Ch15, workmen's complaint.** The chapter reports "we were not allowed to put this evidence before the jury" without noting the Times court report (15 Sep) shows "a number of fellow workmen also gave evidence" and Kennard "stated... the general feeling among his fellow workmen was that he was insane", and Weatherly 1894 (`Weatherly-DiscussionLawRelation-1894.pdf`, discussion section) says Day "allowed the fellow-workmen of the prisoner to state their belief in his insanity." The workmen's grievance and the record conflict; Kennard's own Telegraph letter (19 Sep) says he was "prevented"/"not permitted". The chapter should say what exactly was refused. Medium.

**U10. Ch16, "Nothing records how, or indeed if, Elizabeth was officially informed."** A statement of absence; I found no record in the batch's sources, but it is unprovable. Low. Ch16 does not mention that the Home Office letter of 3 Oct was already public (Times, Daily News 6 Oct) four days before William was told (10 Oct); not wrong, just unremarked.

**U11. Ch16, "The money collected ... exact total £35 14s 4d".** Image of Times 12 Oct (viewed): the shillings read "£35 1?s. 4d." and are too low-resolution to settle between 1s and 14s. The transcription chooses 14s by inference from Graves's "£36". Chapter calls it "the exact total". Low-medium.

**U12. Ch14, "to nerve himself".** Poland/IPN say "nerved himself, as he afterwards stated, by taking drink"; William's recorded words are "an extra drop of drink to accomplish the job" (no "nerve"). Chapter says "by his own account, to nerve himself": it merges counsel's gloss into William's account. Low.

## 3. Attribution problems

**A1.** Ch14 "The Telegraph's shorter report" quotes language that is the shared agency text of the Telegraph, IPN (22 Sep) and W&L Guardian (22 Sep) (Telegraph header says so). Fine as it names only the Telegraph; but the IPN, which the chapter also quotes ("ridiculous"), is the same text from the summing-up on. No false multiplication of witnesses in Ch14. For the speeches, Times and IPN are one reporter's text cut differently (IPN header note); the chapter draws "ridiculous" from the IPN and "dangerous precedent" from the Times and joins them in one paragraph without saying so. Low.

**A2.** Ch14: jury "retired at twenty minutes past five" with no source named; the time is in the Telegraph, Morning Post and IPN/Guardian, not the Times (which the surrounding paragraph relies on). Low.

**A3.** Ch14, Telegraph: "one doctor's admission". The Telegraph says "one medical gentleman"; the IPN version of the same text names Dr Savage. Chapter's anonymity is faithful.

**A4.** Ch15, Manchester Guardian: the clip bears no title or date; identification as the Manchester Guardian of 26 Sep rests on the Newspapers.com label ("The Guardian", filed under London) and word shapes (transcription header). Reasonable, but not visible on the clip. Low. Ch16 "Liverpool Daily Post, the same day": date and title are from the filename only. The Times 1 Oct paragraph's opening is hidden; the Evening Standard of 1 Oct (Monday evening) is named as the source in W&L Guardian 6 Oct and EWN 5 Oct, so the "Times on the Monday morning" and "Evening Standard Monday" are probably one copied paragraph, not two witnesses; chapter uses one, fine.

**A5.** Ch16 Sunday Dispatch quotes: the paper prints "Dr. Orringe" twice. Chapter silently corrects to "Orange". Acceptable if footnoted. Low.

**A6.** Ch15 Sunderland "on the Saturday": the Times clip carries no date (header: filename and Times search); EWN 28 Sep reprints it. Plausible. Low.

## 4. Source problems

- **Warrants transcription vs images (viewed Removal p2, Reception p2).** Handwriting agrees: "William Gouldstone was at the Central Criminal Court on the 10th day of September 1883 convicted of Murder and sentenced to Death"; "duly certified to me under the provisions of the 27th & 28th Vic. Cap. 29 to be of unsound mind and thereupon the execution of the sentence of death was respited until further signification of Her Majesty's Pleasure"; Reception: "keep him therein until he shall be lawfully removed or discharged" (the printed words). Quotation in Ch16 ("until he shall be lawfully removed or discharged") is correct (this is the string the script flagged; it is printed form text, reading "until he shall be lawfully removed or discharged"). The Removal warrant clause on the certificate also matches. Note: the transcription's header says "Page 3" signature/date "8th. day of October 1883" – I did not view page 3 images, so the date 8 Oct is not verified by me.
- **Broadmoor admission register (viewed):** "4953 Gouldstone Wm ... 15 Oct Broadmoor", death entry "19.1.35". Supports Ch16 "admitted ... the same day" and the 15 Oct date.
- **The warrants recite certification before the indefinite respite** ("duly certified ... and thereupon ... respited until further signification"). Ch16 puts the respite on 1 Oct and the "certified" news on 10 Oct and does not note that the warrants imply the certificate existed first. The transcription's own finding 4 flags this as inference. Ch16 sequence is consistent with press; but it is a gap worth a line.
- Ch15 transcriptions: Daily Telegraph 15 Sep header says the EWN, etc. agree; no error found. Essex Weekly News 21 Sep header's own correction list includes "burthen".
- Sunday Dispatch header: "the 8th" partly lost; fine.

## 5. Unused material worth attention

- **Savage's JMS, Jan 1884, pp 534-539 ("The Case of Gouldstone")** (read closely). It agrees with the chapters' account of Savage's view: he says "I could only say 'yes' when asked if the man knew he had killed ... and again ... the punishment"; "I distinctly added that I believed him to be insane at the time the act was committed"; counsel "strictly bound me down to answer simply and solely as to facts observed by myself"; the Judge told him the opinion "was not for me, but for the jury". Adds facts the chapters lack: William would tell his workmates strange dreams "like Joseph of old", "his dreams displayed heaven opened, and he heard God speaking to him" (workmen's account); "melancholia ... so said the doctor [of the House of Detention], though for some reason his evidence was never given in Court"; he says William was "said to have taken more drink than usual to nerve him" (so "nerve" is Savage's too); the brothers who appeared "dull men" (Savage's impression). Savage there recommends a months-long observation before trial where the prison medical officer has doubts. Second JMS piece (pp 603-607) is a reprint of the Times report of 15 Sep and is not new.
- **Kennard, Winslow, Foreman of the Jury (Telegraph 19 Sep)**: the employer writes that he, as juror, "should have felt compelled" to return the verdict; the foreman says "We did not misunderstand Dr. Savage", the jury "quite agreed", and offers to subscribe for Elizabeth. This directly answers Sunderland's and Savage's charge that the jury misunderstood him. A strong counterweight absent from Ch15.
- **Peel letter, Times 26 Sep**: a barrister's plea for mercy (M'Naghten-era acquittal at Lancaster), same column as the respite.
- **Times 1 Oct / Evening Standard**: "His wife ... says that he has shown signs of madness for the last 12 months" differs from Elizabeth's own statement (nothing before the twins; "I never knew till very lately"). Ch16 quotes the paragraph but omits this sentence.
- **Morning Post 15 Sep**: the prisoner "looking on as they left the box with [a] smile upon his face", and "looking upon the court unconcernedly" at sentence; EWN: "stood calmly, with his thumbs in the sleeveholes of his waistcoat". Not in Ch14.
- **Bucknill 1884** reproduces Day's direction ("the nature and quality of the act meant that the man knew what it was that he was doing ... a fellow-creature"), an independent confirmation of Ch14's wording; **Weatherly 1894** says Day allowed workmen's belief in his insanity to be stated; **Nicolson 1917** gives Orange and Gover as the examiners.
- **Daily Chronicle 15 Sep** and the Chronicle's leader (via Guardian 22 Sep): "fifteen minutes' consideration".
- **EWN 28 Sep leader "The Fate of Gouldstone"**: predicted commutation and life confinement "for we believe that is what it will come to"; **EWN 5 Oct leader** announces commutation before the 8 Oct warrants (runs ahead of fact).
