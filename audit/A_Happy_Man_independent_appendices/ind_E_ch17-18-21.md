# Independent audit E: Chapters 17, 18 and 21

Scope: `chapters/CHAPTER_17.md`, `CHAPTER_18.md`, `CHAPTER_21.md`, checked against `transcribed/` and images. Notes/ChatGPT-archive were not used as evidence (a few notes lines are cited only where they state what the repo holds).

Images I actually viewed: Lambeth Workhouse register pp. 95 and 96 (including an enlarged crop of the legitimacy column across the gutter); Albert's 1887 GRO birth certificate; Whitstable 1891 and 1901 census pages; Lilian 1890 birth certificate; Eliza Jane 1891 birth certificate; both 1891 baptism crops; William Madams 1901 death certificate; Elizabeth Madams 1906 death certificate; Brian's 1937 death certificate; Broadmoor inquest page 1 (PNG only); Broadmoor 1921 census; Evening Standard 21 Jan 1935 clip; Chatham burial extract and Palmerston Road MI extract; 1939 Register (Chatham page); Herne Bay Press 2 May 1896; Whitstable Times 21 May 1898 (PDF, p. 8, rendered at 500 dpi); Folkestone Chronicle 13 Apr 1901; Canterbury Journal 6 Apr 1901. Not viewed: see section 6.

Overall: the three chapters are accurate on almost every date, name, figure and quotation I could test. Every transcription I compared with its image agrees with it. The problems are (a) one unfinished placeholder, (b) several identity inferences stated as fact, (c) a few statements with no source in the repo, (d) a handful of small slips.

## 1. Errors (text wrong against source)

**E1. Ch 18, "the mother's column holds the same three surnames she had given the year before."** (low-medium)
Lilian's birth was registered 19 May 1890 (`Lilian_Madams_birth_certificate_1890`; image `Lilliam Madams Birth Cert.jpg`, col. 8 "Nineteenth May 1890"). Eliza Jane's was registered 29 Oct 1891 (image `1891 Eliza Jane Madams Birth Cerificate.jpg`). That is 17 months later, not "the year before". Both images read "Elizabeth Madams late Goldstone formerly Stock", so the "three surnames" point is right; only the interval is wrong. Fix: "the year before" to "seventeen months before" or "on Lilian's certificate".

**E2. Ch 18, unfinished placeholder in the manuscript:** "[WARSPITE: one or two sentences on what the ship was and where she lay in April 1901, once sourced (F (c)).]" (high for publication; no factual effect). Source for the Warspite itself is only the Herne Bay Press 20 Apr 1901; no source in the repo on the ship.

**E3. Ch 21, Albert's age at the 1962 burial.** The chapter says the register "entered him as 'Retired', aged '76'". Quoted accurately (KFHS transcript extract, image viewed: "76", "Retired"). Albert's birth certificate gives 19 June 1887, so he was 75 on 21 June 1962. The chapter quotes the 76 (and "44" on the Orbita list, true age 43; "19 Jun 86" on the 1939 Register; image of Register viewed) without telling the reader these carry his naval false year 1886. A reader will assume the book has made a slip. (Low; defensible if the false birth year is explained elsewhere in Part Five, but not in these chapters.)

**E4. Ch 21, "Clarie and three of the girls were with him. Lily was a civil servant and Clarie a shop assistant in a grocer's".** 1939 Register image (viewed): schedule 230 lists Albert (ship's rigger), Clarie I. b. 7 Jun 89 (unpaid domestic duties: the mother), Lily A.R. (civil servant), "Clarie I (jnr)" (shop assistant, grocery), Doris M. (at school). The chapter gives two different women the name Clarie in consecutive sentences with no distinction; the mother's own entry ("unpaid domestic duties") is dropped. Clarity error, not a fact error. (low)

**E5. Ch 21, sequence.** "Clarie and Albert's last child was a boy, Brian ... born in 1932. In her turn Vera, who was eleven, sat the entrance examination ... and in June ... awarded one of its scholarships." The Observer item (24 June 1932, p. 11) is June 1932; the GRO index puts Brian's birth in July-Sept 1932 (per `dramatis_personae`, index text only). So Vera's June award precedes Brian's birth; the "In her turn" order is backwards by a few weeks. (low)

No arithmetic errors found in: 17 entries / 11 illegitimate / 3 stillborn (see section 3); 17 months; 18 months and "a month old"; Albert 3 in April 1891 (3 yr 9 mo); Lilian "eleven months" (11 mo 29 d); Lilian 10 and Eliza 9 on 31 Mar 1901; William Madams 4 Sept to 23 Nov 1901 (80 days, "about 3 months" certificate); 26 Mar to 17 Apr 1919 (22 days = "three weeks"); William aged 78 on 19 Jan 1935 (b. 10 Oct 1856 per notes' birth certificate; the 1921 census age 64 agrees); Vera 16 in June 1937; Clarie b. 7 June 1889, 68 in Jan 1958; Brian buried 20 May 1937 to 7 Jan 1958 ("twenty years before": 20 y 7 m); 35 votes (700-665) and 327 (832-505) match the printed returns; "Saturday 28 March 1942", "Sunday 19 June 1921", "Monday 21 Jan 1935" weekdays are right.

## 2. Unsupported or overstated

## 3. Attribution problems

**A1. Ch 18: charge wording from the wrong hearing.** The chapter says the constable arrested him on Monday 1 April and that he was "charged 'with stealing a pair of sea boots, value 10s., the property of Alfred Baker'". The words are from the Folkestone Chronicle's report of the 6 April remand hearing ("charged on remand with stealing a pair of sea boots, value 10s., the property of Alfred Baker"). The Whitstable Times 6 Apr 1901 (first hearing, Tuesday 2 April, Canterbury) words it differently. Fine as a charge description, but the quotation is placed as if it were the arrest-day charge. (low)

**A2. Ch 18: the 1901 shoe-boots story.** The chapter correctly names the Folkestone Chronicle ("gives the story as the witnesses told it") and attributes "his father wanted 3s." to Pout's evidence. Good. But the chapter then merges the two reports on facts that differ: the vessel is "Christabel" (Folkestone, image viewed: "Christabel") against "brigantine Christabelle" (Whitstable Times); Pout "paid it" for 3s. in both. The conflicts the transcriber lists (last seen 22/23 March; sale 28th/30th vs "next day"; "Corporal Lambkin" v "P.C. Lampkin") do not touch the chapter's text except the vessel's spelling, which follows the Folkestone paper. Not an error; note that only one paper supports "the thirtieth".

**A3. Ch 21: Evening Standard.** The chapter quotes the Standard correctly (image viewed) and juxtaposes it with the inquest ("seventy-eight"), but does not tell the reader the Standard's "80 years of age" and "52 years" are both wrong (inquest age 78; 1883 to Jan 1935 is 51 years 4 months). An unflagged discrepancy. (low)

**A4. Ch 21, Milne quotations.** All quotes verified against the transcription (`Broadmoor_Inquest_COR_R1_7_3_1935`): "he was a married man & a blacksmith by occupation"; "poor bodily health for the last six years"; "during the last 3 months he gradually became weaker"; correctly attributed to Kenneth Oswald Milne; Priest is Edward John Priest; death at 12.5 a.m. 19 Jan (typed slip and deposition). **A5. Ch 18 "the Canterbury Journal described Elizabeth's part in that day"** and then "At St Augustine's Petty Sessions on Saturday the sixth of April": the Folkestone paper says "on Saturday" in its own 13 April issue; "6 April" is inferred (corroborated by the Whitstable Times "Remanded to the Petty Sessions on Saturday" and Herne Bay Press "previous week"). Fine.

## 7. Identity inferences (what supports them, what would settle them)

**I1. "Elizabeth Goldston" in the Lambeth Workhouse register (Ch 17) = Elizabeth Stock, William Gouldstone's wife (the book's Elizabeth).**
Ch 17 begins "After the October of 1883, the record holds nothing of Elizabeth until a Sunday in June 1887", and presents entry 105 as hers with no qualification; Ch 18's title adds "formerly Stock". Evidence for: (a) 1887 register and certificate: "Elizabeth Goldston", mother, illegitimate boy, no father, no age, no maiden name (images viewed). (b) 1891 census: a household at Whitstable with "Elizabeth do" (Madams), 34, born Essex "Weathersfield", and "Albert Goldstone", stepson, 3, born Lambeth (image viewed): the boy's name, age and birthplace match the 1887 entry (Albert, b. 19 June 1887, Lambeth). (c) Birth certificates 1890 and 1891: "Elizabeth Madams late Goldstone formerly Stock" (images viewed), given by the informant herself, which is the only document linking this woman by her own words to the Stock maiden name. (d) 1901 press: "Elizabeth Goldstone of Middle Wall ... mother of the boy in custody", the boy named "Albert Goldstone". (e) Her birthplace in both censuses is Wethersfield (in Essex), the Stock family's village. Weaknesses: the 1887 documents do not mention Stock, Walthamstow or Wethersfield; "Goldston" is a variant spelling; her age on every Whitstable document (34, 43, 47) is 2 to 4 years below the 1855 birth date the author's notes give; and no document names her (or Albert) in Lambeth other than the register and certificate. The chain is strong (it ends in a daughter's own words on a certificate), but each link is a name, age or birthplace match, not a document stating "the wife of William Gouldstone". What would settle: Albert's own baptism or later records giving his mother's maiden name; DNA match between descendants of Albert and a Stock/Gouldstone line (the notes mention DNA, not examined); Lambeth Guardians' records (admission/discharge register, LMA) giving her age, birthplace and religion; the 1881 census (image present: `Goldstone Family 1881 Census`) to follow her from the Walthamstow years toward Lambeth. Recommended wording for the book: an explicit sentence in Ch 17 or 18 stating that the identification rests on the 1890/1891 certificates and the 1891 census, not on the 1887 register.

**I2. The "Albert Madams" of the School Board items (Whitstable Times 21 May 1898, 25 June 1898; Canterbury Journal 19 Aug 1899) = Albert.** Evidence: name; Whitstable; same period; "Albert Goldstone" is "Albert do" under William Madams in 1901 (the 1901 census calls him "Son", the surname by ditto = Madams, image viewed); the 1896 Herne Bay Press item names William Madams and "his son aged eight" (Albert turned 8 in June 1895 and was 8 until 19 June 1896; Madams's own children by Elizabeth were girls). Weakness: none of the 1898-99 items gives an age, a parent or an address; the 1896 item gives no first name. The transcriber's own note says other Madams households lived in Whitstable (`Whitstable_Times_Aug_1899...`). Highly probable but not established by a document that links name and child. Needs: the Whitstable Board School admission/attendance registers (Kent History Centre), the Board's minute books naming the parent, or the Whitstable Times 5 Aug 1899 court report naming the defendant. Ch 18 states "The reports in both years call him 'Albert Madams'" as fact, which is accurate; the reader is left to assume the identity.

**I3. The 1896 summons concerns Albert.** The summons is in the name "William Madams" "in respect to his son aged eight"; the mother "pleaded poverty". Age 8 on the paper's date (2 May 1896: Albert 8 yr 10½ mo) fits Albert. Girls Lilian (6) and Eliza (4) do not fit. So the one boy of the right age in that household is Albert, and the 1891 census calls him "Stepson" while the school-board report calls him Madams's "son". Weakness: no names for the child or mother in the clip (the book names neither; it says "his son" and "The mother of the child"); the identification of this William Madams as the Whitstable labourer rests on the name and the age. Needs: the court register (Whitstable/St Augustine's petty sessions, May 1896) or a Board minute naming the child.

**I4. The "Albert Goldstone, aged 14" in the 1901 boots case = Albert, son of Elizabeth and stepson of William Madams.** Strong, from the document: the Canterbury Journal states the woman charged is "Elizabeth Goldstone, of Middle Wall ... mother of the boy in custody charged with stealing a pair of sea boots"; the census of two days before has the Madams household at Middle Wall including Albert. The chapter does not say the boy's age (14) in the papers is wrong (13 or 15 in other documents); fine.

**I5. "his father" in the Pout evidence.** The chapter says nothing about whom the boy meant, which is right (the transcriber notes "his father" is Pout's report of the boy's words; "Madams is the obvious reading; not for prose"). The chapter's "Pout told the court that the boy had said ... Two days later Albert came back 'and said his father wanted 3s. for them'" is correct and appropriately attributed. No problem; flagged only so the author knows this needs to remain open.

**I6. Elizabeth's marital status and surnames across documents (Ch 17 and 18 statements).**
- Ch 17: the 1887 register and certificate give "Elizabeth Goldston", mother alone, "Illegitimate", no father. The chapter does not state or imply a marriage at Lambeth. Correct.
